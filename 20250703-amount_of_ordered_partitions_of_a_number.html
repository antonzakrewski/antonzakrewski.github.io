amount of ordered partitions of a number

Proposition

Let $n,k \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{P}_{n,k}}$ the set of ordered partitions. Then

\begin{align*} \tilde{\mathcal{P}_{n,k}} = \binom{n-1}{k-1} \end{align*}

Proof

Consider $n$ ordered points, i.e. $[n] = \{1,...,n\}$. Then an ordered partition consists of a partition of those $n$ into connected intervals.

Such a partition is equivalently described by choosing $k-1$ many gap, i.e. $(i,i+1)$. Here we have $n-1$ gaps and therefore

\begin{align*} \binom{n-1}{k-1} \end{align*}
