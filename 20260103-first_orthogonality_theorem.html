first orthogonality theorem

Proposition

Let $V_1,V_2$ be irreducible, complex representations of a finite group. Then

\begin{align*} \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \sum_{g \in G} \overline{\chi_{V_1}(g)} \chi_{V_2}(g) = \begin{cases} 1 & \mbox{if } V_1 \cong V_2 \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

Proof

using hom of representations is G-invariant of tensor product

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V_1,V_2) \cong& (V_1^{*} \otimes V_2)^G \\ \cong& \begin{cases} \mathbb{C} & \mbox{if } V_1 \cong V_2 \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

where we use schur lemma for finite complex representations

Now consider the projection to the fixed points for a representation of a finite group

\begin{align*} \Pi: V_1^{*} \otimes v_2 \rightarrow (V_1^* \otimes V_2)^G \end{align*}

where

\begin{align*} \Pi = \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \rho^1_g^* \otimes (\rho^2_g) \end{align*}

But

\begin{align*} \mathrm{tr}_{V_1^* \otimes V_2}(\Pi) =& \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \sum_{g \in G} \chi_{V_1^* \otimes V_2}(g) \\ =& \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \sum_{g \in G} \chi_{V_1^*} \cdot \chi_{V_2}(g) \\ =& \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \sum_{g \in G} \overline{\chi_{V_1}}(g) \cdot \chi_{V_2}(g) \\ =& \begin{cases} 1& \mbox{if } V_1 \cong V_2\\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

and isomorphic representations have isomorphic characters

Now trace of a projection to a 1-dimensional space is given by 1
