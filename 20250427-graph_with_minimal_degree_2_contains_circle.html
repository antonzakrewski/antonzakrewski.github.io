graph with minimal degree 2 contains circle

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph with minimal degree of a graph $\geq 2$. Then $G$ contains a circle graph $C_{l}$ with $l \geq \partial(G) + 1$.

Proof

Choose a vertex $v_0$ (which exists as $\partial(G) \geq 2$). Then proceed inductively by choosing an edge to vertex $v_{i+1}$, which wasn't chosen previously. By assumption, this can be done at least $l$ times. Repeat until no new vertex can be chosen this way. Note that a graph here is by assumption finite, hence this algorithm terminates. If you can't choose a new vertex anymore, then choose the first vertex $v_n$ connected to the current edge $v_{m}$. It remains to show, that the circle $v_n \rightarrow v_{n+1} ... \rightarrow v_{m} \rightarrow v_n$ contains at least $l+1$ vertices. Note that $v_m$ contains at least $l$ vertices connected to $v_m$, which lie by construction in the path from $v_n$ to $v_m$ as they have already been marked (otherwise we wouldn't have terminated) and are choosen after $v_{n-1}$ (otherwise the vertex would have been the smallest). Hence $v_n \rightarrow ... \rightarrow v_m \rightarrow v_n$ contains at least $l+1$ vertices: namely the $l$ many connected to $v_m$ and $v_m$ itself.
