classification of two sided ideals of the q-weyl algebra over the complex numbers

Proposition

Let $q \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$ be an element which is not a root of unity. Then there exist only the trivial and nontrivial two sided ideal.

Proof

Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a nontrivial ideal and let $f = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i y^{n_i} x^{m_i}$ be a polynomial. w.l.o.g. after multiplication with $x$ from the right, we may assume that $0 = n_1 < ... n_n$. Then

\begin{align*} (f - x^{-1}f x) =& \sum_{i=1}^n 1 - q^{\pm n_i} \alpha_i y^{n_i} x^{m_i} \\ =& \sum_{i=2}^n (1 - q^{\pm n_i}) \alpha_i y^{n_i} x^{m_i} \end{align*}

where $(1 - q^{\pm n_i)) \neq 0$ by assumption. Hence we may induct and get some $\alpha_i y^{n} x^m \in \mathfrak{a}$, which is a unit with inverse $\alpha_i^{-1} x^{-n} y^{-m}$ and therefore the ideal is then the whole ring.
