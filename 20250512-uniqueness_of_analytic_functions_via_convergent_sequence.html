uniqueness of analytic functions via convergent sequence

Proposition

Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be open and connected and $f,g: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ be analytic functions Suppose there exists a convergent sequence $w_k \in \Omega$ with $w_k \neq w_j$ for $k\neq j$ and $\mathrm{lim} w_k \in \Omega$

\begin{align*} g(w_k) = f(w_k) \end{align*}

Then

\begin{align*} f = g \end{align*}

Proof

w.l.o.g. assume that $f' = f - g, g = 0$. Then it suffices to show that $f' = 0$ Let $z_0 = \mathrm{lim} w_k$.

Then since $z_0 \in \Omega$ we may write

\begin{align*} f'(z) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} a_n(z - z_0)^n \end{align*}

on some disc.

Assume that there exists some $a_n \neq 0$, let $a_m \neq 0$ be the one with smallest index $m$. Then

\begin{align*} f'(z) = a_m(z - z_0)^m(1 + r(z)) \end{align*}

with $r(z) \rightarrow 0$ as $z \rightarrow z_0$ - which is to be regarded as remainder . Thus we may assume that $r(z) > - 0.5$ for some small disk $D_{R'}(z_0)$ around $z_0$.

Now within that disk we still find a convergent sequence $w_k \rightarrow z_0$. Then by assumption

\begin{align*} f'(w_k) = 0 \end{align*}

but also

\begin{align*} a_m(w_k - z_0)^m (1 + r(w_k)) \neq 0 \end{align*}

This shows a contradiction, hence $a_n = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$

This shows that $f' = 0$ on the original disk of convergence of $f'$. Hence $(f')^{-1}[0]$ has nonempty interior, i.e. $\mathrm{int}((f')^{-1}[0]) \neq \emptyset$.

Now we will show that $\mathrm{int}((f')^{-1}[0]) \cap \Omega$ is also closed in $\Omega$ Let $w_k \rightarrow z$ be a sequence with $w_k \in\mathrm{int}((f')^{-1}[0])$.

Then we may repeat the argument above and get a small disk around $z$ with $f_{D_R(z)} = 0$. Hence $z \in \mathrm{int}((f')^{-1}[0]) \cap \Omega$

Now by assumption $\mathrm{int}((f')^{-1}[0]) \cap \Omega \subseteq \Omega$ is both open (intersection of two opens), closed and nonempty, thus by connectivity of $\Omega$ it has to be $\Omega$
