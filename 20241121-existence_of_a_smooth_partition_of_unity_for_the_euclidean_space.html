existence of a smooth partition of unity for the euclidean space

Proposition

There exists a smooth partition of unity for $\mathbb{R}^d$ for $B_{\varepsilon}(p)$ where $p \in \mathbb{Z}^d$

Proof

Define for a fixed $\varepsilon > 0$

\begin{align*} \gamma_{p,\varepsilon}(x) \coloneqq \prod_{j=1}^d \gamma(\frac{x_j}{\varepsilon} - p_j) \end{align*}

where

\begin{align*} \gamma(t) = \frac{\psi(t)}{\sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \psi(t - k)} \end{align*}

and

\begin{align*} \psi: \mathbb{R} \rightarrow& [0,1] \\ t \mapsto& \begin{cases} \mathrm{exp}(-\frac{1}{1 - t^2}) & \mbox{if } \vert t \vert < 1 \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}
