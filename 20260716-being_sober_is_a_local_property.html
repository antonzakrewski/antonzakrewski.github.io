being sober is a local property

Proposition

Let $X$ be a topological space and $U_i$ an open cover. Suppose that each $U_i$ is sober, then so is $X$.

Proof

Let $A \subseteq X$ be an irreducible, closed subspace of $X$, in particular nonempty. Then $A \cap U_i$ - if nonempty - is also irreducible (cf. open subset of an irreducible space as irreducible space).

Hence by assumption, each nonempty $A \cap U_i$ is the closure of a single point $a_i \in A \cap U_i$. Moreover, $U_1 \cap U_2$ is also sober as open subspace of $U_1$ (cf. open subspace of a sober space is sober). Hence $a_i$ and $a_j$ either agree or lie in $U_i \setminus U_j$ and $U_j \setminus U_i$.

Suppose $a_i \in U_i \setminus U_j$, then $\overline{a_i} \subseteq X \setminus U_j$ since $a_i$ is contained in the closed subset $X \setminus U_j$. Hence $\overline{a_i} = \overline{A \cap U_i} \subseteq X \setminus U_j$ and a similar argument for $a_j \in U_j \setminus U_i$ would imply that $A \cap (U_i \cup U_j)$ would not be irreducible (cf. irreducible subspace and closure, open subset of an irreducible space as irreducible space), hence there can't exist an $a_j \in U_j \setminus U_i$ if there already exists an $a_i \in U_i \setminus U_j$.
