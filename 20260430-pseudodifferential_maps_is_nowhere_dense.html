pseudo n-lipschitz maps are nowhere dense

Proposition

The set of pseudo n-lipschitz map $A_n$ is nowhere dense

Proof

Let $f$ be in $\overline{A_n} = A_n$ and $\varepsilon > 0$.. Then it suffices to show that there exists an $h$ with $\| h - f \|_{\infty} < \varepsilon$ with $h \not\in A_n$.

We first define

\begin{align*} h': \mathbb{R} \rightarrow& [0,1] \\ x \mapsto& \begin{cases} x - \lceil x \rceil & \mbox{if } \lceil x \rceil \equiv 1 \mathrm{mod} 2 \\ 1 + \lceil x \rceil - x & \mbox{if } \lceil x \rceil \equiv 1 \mathrm{mod} 2 \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

Observe that this map is a continuous map by some glueing lemma (cf. glueing lemma for a locally finite, closed covering) and $\| h' \|_{\infty} = 1$.

Let $\tilde{h}(x) \coloneqq \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \cdot h'(4 \cdot \frac{n+1}{\varepsilon} \cdot x)$.

Then we claim that for each $x$ in $[0,1]$, there exists some $y$ such that

\begin{align*} \vert \tilde{h}(x) - \tilde{h}(y) \vert \geq (2n + 1) \vert x - y \vert \end{align*}

Namely it suffices to see that $\tilde{h}$ is piecewise linear with differential $\pm 2 (n+1)$ (whereever differentiable). Moreover for the/any continuous map $\tilde{h}$, the subspace $B_{\tilde{h}} = \{x \in [0,1] \vert \forall y \in [0,1] : \vert \tilde{h}(x) - \tilde{h}(y) \vert \leq (2n+1) \vert x - y \vert\}$ is a closed subspace: Suppose $x_n \xrightarrow{n \rightarrow \infty} x$ is a convergent sequence in $B_{\tilde{h}}$, then we may use that $\vert - \vert$ and $\tilde{h}(-)$ are sequentially continuous and estimate

\begin{align*} \vert \tilde{h}(x) - \tilde{h}(y) \vert = \mathrm{lim}_{n} \vert \tilde{h}(x_n) - \tilde{h}(y) \vert \leq \mathrm{lim}_{n} (2n+1) \vert x_n - y \vert = (2n+1) \vert x - y \vert \end{align*}

This shows that the closed subset $B_{\tilde{h}}$ is given by $[0,1]$, since it at least contains all points, where $\tilde{h}(-)$ is differentiable (which is $[0,1]$ except for finitely many points), which is a dense subset.

Then we claim that

\begin{align*} h: [0,1] \rightarrow& \mathbb{R} \\ x \mapsto& f(x) + \tilde{h}(x) \end{align*}

suffices: It is again a continuous map as composition of continuous maps.

Moreover, it follows immediately that $\| h - f \|_{\infty} \leq \frac{\varepsilon}{2} < \varepsilon$ and we claim that $h \not\in A_n$: Here we use the inverse triangle inequality and get

\begin{align*} \vert h(x) - h(y) \vert =& \vert f(x) - f(y) + \tilde{h}(x) - \tilde{h}(y) \vert \\ \geq& \vert \tilde{h}(x) - \tilde{h}(y) \vert - \vert f(x) - f(y) \vert \\ \geq& \vert \tilde{h}(x) - \tilde{h}(y) \vert - n \vert x - y \vert \\ \overset{*}{\geq}& (2n+1) \vert x - y \vert - n \vert x - y \vert \\ \geq& n+1 \vert x - y \vert \end{align*}

where (*) follows for some $y$ as we have shown previously, that for each $x$ in $[0,1]$, there exists some $y$ with $\vert \tilde{h}(x) - \tilde{h}(y) \geq 2n+1 \vert x - y \vert$.
