intersection theorem for the jacobson radical

Proposition

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $J \subseteq \mathcal{J}_A$ an ideal (cf. Jacobson radical). Then

\begin{align*} \bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} J^n = 0 \end{align*}

Proof

note that $A,J$ are Noetherian modules (cf. noetherian ring as noetherian module, submodule of a noetherian module as noetherian module) Furthermore

\begin{align*} J \cdot \bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} J^n = \bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} J^n \end{align*}

(cf. product with infinite intersection of an ideal)

Hence this follows from the Nakayama Lemma for Jacobson Radical
