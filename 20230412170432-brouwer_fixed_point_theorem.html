Brouwer fixed-point theorem

Proposition

Let $D^n$ be the \(n\)-dimensional disk and $f: D^n \rightarrow D^n$ a continuous map. Then there exists a fixed point

Proof

Assume there exists no fixed point for $f$. Then the map

\begin{align*} g: D^n \rightarrow& S^{n-1} \\ x \mapsto& \mathrm{intersection} \{tx + (1 - t) f(x) \vert t \geq \frac{1}{2}\} \mathrm{with } S^{n-1} \end{align*}

is continuous <todo>

Note that by construction

\begin{align*} g_{\vert S^{n-1}} = \mathrm{id}_{S^{n-1}} \end{align*}

Hence functorality of the singular homology functor results in contradiction, more explicitly

$n > 1$

$n = 1$
