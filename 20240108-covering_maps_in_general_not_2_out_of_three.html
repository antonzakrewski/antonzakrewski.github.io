covering maps in general not two out of three

Proposition

Let $E,E',B$ be topological spaces and $f: E \rightarrow E'$, $g: E \rightarrow B$ continuous maps. Suppose $f$ and $g \circ f$ are covering maps, then $g$ is in general not.

Proof

Let $E = \mathbb{Z}, E' = \mathbb{Z} \coprod \{*\}$ and $B = \mathbb{Z}$ with

\begin{align*} f =& \iota \\ g =& \mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Z}} \coprod 0 \end{align*}

Then we get

\begin{align*} f, g \circ f \end{align*}

as identity morphisms and hence trivial covering maps. But for $g: \mathbb{Z} \coprod 0 \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}$, we get for each neighbourhood of $0$

\begin{align*} g^{-1}[B_{\varepsilon}(0)] = B_{\varepsilon}(0) \coprod \{*\} \end{align*}

where $\{*\} \not\cong B_{\varepsilon}(0)$
