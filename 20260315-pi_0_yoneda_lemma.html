pi_0 yoneda lemma

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an \((\infty,1)\)-category and $\eta: \pi_0(\mathrm{Hom}(-,c)) \Rightarrow \pi_0(\mathrm{Hom}(-,c'))$ be a natural transformation of functors to category set Then there exists a (not unique) map $c \rightarrow c'$

Proof

existince

observe that we get a map

\begin{align*} \eta(c): \pi_0(\mathrm{Hom}(c,c)) \rightarrow \pi_0(\mathrm{Hom}(c,d)) \end{align*}

so tracing $\mathrm{id}$ suffices.

not unique

follows as we are passing to $\pi_0(-)$. see e.g. identity morphism of an anima not detectable on pi_0
