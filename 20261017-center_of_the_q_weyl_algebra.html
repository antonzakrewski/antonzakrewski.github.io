center of the q-weyl algebra

Proposition

Let $q \in k \setminus \{0\}$ be an element and $A$ the q-Weyl algebra. Then the center is given by polynomials $\sum \alpha_i x^{n_i} y^{m_i}$ such that $q^{n_i} = q^{m_i} =0$

Proof

First observe that it suffices to show that the generators $x,y,x^{-1},y^{-1}$ commute with a monomial $x^{n_i} \cdot y^{m_i)$. Here $x x^{n_i} \cdot y^{m_i} = q^{\pm m_i} x^{n_i} \cdot y^{m_i} \cdot x$ where both are equal if and only if $q^{\pm m_i} = 1$. Similarly for $y \cdot -$ and the $x^{-1},y^{-1}$ case follows as the multiplicative inverse of an element in the center lies in the center.

Now let $g = \sum \alpha_i x^{n_i} y^{m_i}$ be an element in the center. Then consider the k-linear maps $f \mapsto x f - fx$ and $f \mapsto y f - f y$.

Then the intersection of the kernels is the center and both k-linear morphisms map standard basis vectors $x^n y^m$ to $(1 - q^{\pm n}) x^n y^{m+1}$ and $(1 - q^{\pm m}) x^{n+1} y^{m}$. In particular, if two different standard basis vectors map to nonzero elements, then they land in the span of different standard basis vectors. Hence the kernel of the map is the span of the fiber of the restriction to the standard basis, which is precisely the span of monomials $x^n y^m$ such that $q^{\pm n} = q^{\pm m} = 1$.
