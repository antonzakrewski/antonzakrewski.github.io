minimal degree implies connected graph

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph with $n$ vertices and

\begin{align*} \partial(G) \geq \mathrm{floor}( \frac{n}{2}) \end{align*}

Then $G$ is a connected graph.

Furthermore this bound is optimal.

see: minimal degree of a graph

Proof

bound

Let $G'$ be a connected component. We will show, that it contains at least $\mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2}) + 1 > \frac{n}{2}$ vertices: Since the connected components form a partition, we will then conclude that it only has one connected component. Choose a vertex $v \in G'$, then we may connect it to at least $\mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2})$ many other vertices. Hence $G'$ contains at least $v$ and the connected edges, namely $\mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2}) + 1$ many edges.

optimal

Consider $G = K_{\mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2})} \coprod K_{\mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2})}$ where $G$ is not connected but has

\begin{align*} \partial(G) = \mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2}) - 1 \end{align*}
