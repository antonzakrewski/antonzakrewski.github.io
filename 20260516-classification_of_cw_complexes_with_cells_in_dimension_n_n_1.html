classification of cw complexes with a single cell in dimension n, n+1

Proposition

Let $X$ be a CW Complex with a single cell in dimension $n,n+1$ for $n \geq 1$. Then

\begin{align*} X \simeq M(\mathbb{Z}/n \mathbb{Z}) \end{align*}

and each of these exist.

Warning

In the case $n=1$, any cw complex with cells in dimension $1,2$ is precisely an analogue of $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^2$, but being a $1$ moore space is not a uniquely characterizing property, since it is not unique (cf. guess: no nilpotent moore space for F2 and 1, existence of a moore space / nilpotent moore space is unique up to non unique equivalence)

Proof

using classification technique for low cell cw complexes and $\pi_n(S^n) = \mathbb{Z}$. Then the long exact sequence of homology gives the desired output.
