localization of an idempotent element

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $e \in R$ an idempotent element. Then the localization $R_{e}$ is isomorphic to the ring $(1 - e)R \subseteq eR \times (1-e) R$ under the isomorphism $R \simeq eR \times (1-e)R$ (cf. idempotent elements and product of rings).

Proof

Observe that there exists a canonical map $eR \rightarrow R$ and hence we get a map $eR \rightarrow R_{e}$. Now it is injective: Let $er$ be an element such that $er = 0$ in $R_{e}$, then there exists an $e^n$ such that $e^n (er - 0) = 0$ and hence $er = 0$. Furthermore, it is surjective: Any element is of the form $\frac{(1-e)r_1, er_2}{e^n} = e \cdot \frac{((1-e)r_1, er_2)}{e^{n+1}} = \frac{e^2 r_2}{e^{n+1}} = \frac{e^2r_2}{e} = er_2$ and hence lies in the image of $eR$ Note that $e$ becomes equal to $1$ once localized
