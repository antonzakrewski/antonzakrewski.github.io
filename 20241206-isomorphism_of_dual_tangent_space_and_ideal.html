isomorphism of dual tangent space and ideal for an affine variety

Proposition

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field, $\mathbb{A}_{K}^n$ the affine space and $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}_{K}^n$ be an affine variety. Let $P \in X$, $M_P \subesteq A(X)$ the maximal ideal (cf. affine coordinate ring and points as maximal ideals)

Then the map

\begin{align*} \tilde{d}: M_P \rightarrow& T_P(X)^{\vee} F \mapsto (dF)_p \end{align*}

induces a vectorspace-isomorphism

\begin{align*} M_P/M_P^2 \cong T_P(X)^{\vee} \end{align*}

Proof

welldefined

note that $(\mathrm{d} F)_P$ is a linear polynomial and is \(k\)-linear. we have to show that $\tilde{d}(G) = 0$ for all $G \in I(X)$. Let $I(X) = (F_1,...,F_r)$ and $G = \sum_{i=1}^r H_i F_i$, then it follows from the product rule

\begin{align*} \mathrm{d}(H_i F_i)_P =& H_i(P) (\mathrm{d}F_i)_P + F_i(P) (\mathrm{d} H_i)_P \\ =& H_i(P) (\mathrm{d} F_i)_P \\ =& c (\mathrm{d}F_i)_P \end{align*}

for some constant $c \in K$

Suppose $Q \in T_P(X) = Z((\mathrm{d}F_1)_p,...,(\mathrm{d}F_r)_p)) \subseteq \mathbb{A}_k^m$, then

\begin{align*} c \cdot (\mathrm{d} F_i)_P(Q) = 0 \end{align*}

hence $\tilde{d}(H_iF_i) = 0$.

surjective

Let $P \in X$, w.l.o.g. $P = (0,...,0)$ after linear coordinate transformation. Let $X_i \in K[X_1,...,X_m]$.

Then $\tilde{d} X_i = (d X_i)_P = \frac{X_i}{\partial X_i} (P) (X_i) = X_i)$ and since $X_1,...,X_m$ generate the \(k\)-vector space $T_p(X)^{\vee} = \mathrm{Hom}_{k}(T_p(X), k)$ we conclude that

\begin{align*} M_P \rightarrow T_p(X)^{\vee} \end{align*}

is surjective

kernel, fundamental theorem of vectorspace homomorphisms

It remains to show that $M_p^2 = \mathrm{ker}(\tilde{d})$.

$\subseteq$

We know, that $M_p^2$ is generated by $X_i \cdot X_j$ for $i,j \leq m$. Using the product rule for the formal derivation we may conclude that

\begin{align*} \mathrm{d}(X_i X_j) =& X_i(P) X_j + X_j(P) X_i \\ =& 0 \cdot X_j + 0 X_i \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

$\supseteq$

Let $F \in \mathrm{ker}(\tilde{d})$, i.e. $F \in M_P$ such that $F(P) = 0$. Because $F(P) = 0$ we may conclude, that $F$ does not contain constant terms.

Let

\begin{align*} F = L + G \end{align*}

where $L$ is linear, $G \in M_P^2$.

This shows that

\begin{align*} \tilde{d}(F) =& \tilde{d}(L) + \tilde{d}(G) \\ =& \tilde{d}(L) \\ =& L \end{align*}

Then $L$ regarded as map $T_p(X) \rightarrow k$ is trivial, since $L \in \mathrm{ker}(\tilde{d})$.

Consider $I(X) = (F_1,...,F_r)$. This shows that

\begin{align*} L \in I(T_p(X)) \end{align*}

with

\begin{align*} I(T_P(X)) =& I(Z((\mathrm{d}F_i)_P, ..., (\mathrm{d}F_r)_P)) = ((\mathrm{d} F_1)_P,..., (\mathrm{d} F_r)_P) \end{align*}

Let

\begin{align*} L = \sum_{i=1}^r c_i (\mathrm{d} F_i)_P \end{align*}

with $c_i \in k$

Consider

\begin{align*} L - \sum_{i=1}^r c_i F_i \in A(X) \end{align*}

This is a regular morphism on $X$ without constant resp. linear term. Hence $L - \sum_{i=1}^r c_i F_i \in M_p^2$.

Since

\begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^r c_i (F_i)_{\vert X} = 0 \end{align*}

is the constant zero map.

This shows that

\begin{align*} L = M_P^2 \end{align*}

and hence also $F = L + G$
