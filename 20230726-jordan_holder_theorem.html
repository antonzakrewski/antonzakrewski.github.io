Jordan-Hölder theorem

Proposition

Let $A$ be a K-Algebra and $V$ a finite dimensional representation Let $0 = V_0 \subseteq V_1 \subseteq ... \subseteq v_n = V$ and $0 = V_0' \subseteq V_1' \subseteq ... \subseteq V_m' = V$ be two filtrations of $V$.

Thne $n = m$ and there exists a Permutation $\sigma$ such that

\begin{align*} W_i \cong W_{\sigma(i)}' \end{align*}

for $W_i \cong V_{i}/V_{i-1}, W_i' = V_{i}'/V_{i-1}'$

Proof

we argue by induction on $\mathrm{dim}(V) = d < \infty$. The case of $n = 0$ or $n = 1$ is clear, since every 1 dimensional representation is simple.

If $W_1 = W_1'$ then we are done by induction (take $V/V_{1}$ and apply some isomorphism theorem). So assume that $W_1 \neq W_1'$, then $W_1 \cap W_1' = 0$ so we find an injection $W_1 \oplus W_1' \rightarrow V$. Consider the quotient $U = V/W_1 \oplus W_1'$ and pick a filtration of $U$ and call it $U_i$ with simple quotient $Z_i = U_i/U_{i-1}$. Then we observe that $V/W_1$ has a filtaritons with quotients $(W_2,Z_p,...,Z_p) and $(W_2,...,W_n)$. So there exists by induction hypothesis a $\tau$ mathcing the permutations. Similarly $V/W_2$ has two filtartions $(W_1,.Z_1,...,Z_p)$ and $(W_2',...,W_n')$

Therefore, up to permutation $(W_1,W_1',Z_1,...,Z_p)$ and $(W_1,...,W_n)$ agree up to permutation. Similarly $(W_1,W_1',Z_1,...,Z_p)$ and $(W_1',...,W_n')$ agree up to permutation. Hence $(W_1',...,W_n')$ and $(W_1,...,W_n)$.
