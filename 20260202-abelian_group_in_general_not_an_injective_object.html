abelian group in general not an injective object

Proposition

Let $G$ be an abelian group, then $G$ is in general not an injective object.

Proof

Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}$. Then we need to find a group $H$ and a subgroup $U$ and a group homomorphism $\varphi: U \rightarrow G$ which does not extend to $H$

So for example consider $H = \mathbb{Q}$ and $U = \mathbb{Z}$ and

where $\mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}$ is the projection.

we claim that there does not exist an extension: Assume there exists a $\overline{\varphi}: \mathbb{Q} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}$ with $\overline{\varphi}(1) = 1$. Then in particular

\begin{align*} \overline{\varphi(1)} =& \overline{\varphi(\sum_{i=1}^p \frac{1}{p})} \\ =& \sum_{i=1}^p \overline{\varphi}(\frac{1}{p}) \\ =& p \cdot [\varphi(\frac{1}{p})] \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

which is a contradiction..
