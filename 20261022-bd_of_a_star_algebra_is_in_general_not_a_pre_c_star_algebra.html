Bd of a star algebra is in general not a pre c star algebra

Proposition

Let $A$ be a nonunital star algebra. Then $\mathrm{Bd}(A)$ is in general not a pre C star algebra.

Proof

Consdier $A = \mathbb{C}[x,y,z,t]/ (t^2 - z^2 - 1, y^2 - t^2 x(1-x)$ for $x,y,z,t$ self-adjoint. Then $\mathrm{Bd}(A) = \mathbb{C}[x]$

Now let $f \in \mathrm{Bd}(A) \subseteq A$. Now apply $t^2 = z^2 + 1$ as often as necessary so that each instance of $z$ appears with multiplicity $1$. Then we have a polynomial of the form

\begin{align*} f = t^n( \alpha_1 z + \alpha_2 t) + g \end{align*}

for $g \in \mathbb{C}[z,t]$.

Then we claim that $t^n( \alpha_1 z + \alpha_2 t) = 0$: For this we can use write down for $r \in \mathbb{R}^+$ the representation $\rho_r: A \rightarrow \mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C})$

\begin{align*} x,y \mapsto& 0 \\ t \mapsto& \mathrm{sgn}_t \sqrt{r^2 + 1} \\ z \mapsto& r \end{align*}

where $\mathrm{sgn}_t \in \{\pm 1\}$ is choosen such that $\| \alpha_1 z + \alpha_2 t \| \geq \mathrm{max}(\alpha_1,\alpha_2) \vert z \vert$ which exists by handwaving and elementary geometry (choose a sign such that the smallest angle between $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ is at most $90$ degree). Then we get $\| \rho_r(f) \| \geq \mathrm{max}(\vert \alpha_1 \vert, \vert \alpha_2 \vert) \| r^{n+1} \| - \| g(0,0,0,0) \|$ which implies $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = 0$

So we may now assumet that $f \in \mathbb{C}[x,y]$. But here we can write down the representation $\rho_{r}'$

\begin{align*} x \mapsto& 0.25 \\ t \mapsto& 2r \\ y \mapsto& r \\ z \mapsto& \sqrt{4r - 1} \end{align*}

which then implies that $f$ can not have any $z$ term, as otherwise the norm of the polynomial would grow to infinity.

Hence $f \in \mathbb{C}[x]$

Now we want to show that $x \in \mathbb{C}[x]$. So let $\rho$ be any representation. We first claim that $\rho(t^2)$ is invertible: It is self adjoint and we claim that it is also bounded below: It is bonuded below because it is of the form $1 + \rho(z^2)$ (cf. 1 plus square of a self adjoint operator is bounded below) and self adjoint since $z$ is self-adjoint.

Hence bounded below, self adjoint operator is invertible implies that it is invertible.

Now for any representation, we have

\begin{align*} 0 =& \langle (\frac{\rho(y)}{\rho(t)})^2 - \rho(x) (\rho(1) - \rho(x)) v, v\rangle \\ =& \| \frac{\rho(y)}{\rho(t)}^2 v \| - \langle \rho(x) v, v\rangle + \langle \rho(x) \rho(1) v, \rho(x) \rho(1) v \rangle \\ \geq& \| \frac{\rho(y)}{\rho(t)}^2 v \|^2 + \| \rho(x) \rho(1) v \|^2 - \| \rho(x) \| \| \rho(1) v \|^2 \\ \end{align*}

where we have used $1^2 = 1 = 1^*$ and hence $\rho(1)^2 = \rho(1) = \rho(1)^*$

now for any $v$ which maximizes $\| \rho(x) \rho(1) v \|$ up to an $\varepsilon$ implies, this implies that $\| \rho(x) \|$ is bounded by $1$ todo

Now $\mathrm{Bd}(\mathbb{C}[x]) = \mathbb{C}$ follows from the representation given by $x \mapsto r$.
