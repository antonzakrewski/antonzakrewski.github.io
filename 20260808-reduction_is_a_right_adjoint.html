reduction of schemes is a right adjoint

Proposition

Let $\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{red}}$ be the full subcategory of reduced schemes Then the inclusion $\mathrm{Sch}^\mathrm{red} \rightarrow \mathrm{Sch}$ admits a right adjoint given by the reduction of a scheme.

Proof

Let $f: Y \rightarrow X$ be map where $Y$ is already a reduced scheme. Then it suffices to show that $f$ factors through $X^{\mathrm{red}}$, since $X^{\mathrm{red}} \rightarrow Y$ is a monomorphism or in other words, that the dashed map

is an isomorphism

Here $Y \times_{X} X^{\mathrm{red}} \subseteq Y$ is a closed subfunctor, so classified by a quasi-coherent ideal - so we want to show that it is the zero ideal.

Then for an affine open $\mathrm{Spec}(S) \subseteq X$ and then we have a diagram

where $\mathrm{Spec}(R)$ is an open cover of $D$ and hence open in $Y$, since $D$ is the pullback of the open $\mathrm{Spec}(S) \subseteq X$.

Now using zariski descent of quasi-coherent ideals it suffices to show that the image of the quasi-coherent ideal $\sqrt{0}$ in $\mathrm{Spec}(R_i)$ is the zero ideal. As reduced is a local property of schemes, we get that $\mathrm{Spec}(R_i)$ is a reduced scheme, i.e. $R_i$ is a reduced ring and thus the image of the nilradical must be zero.

This - together with zariski descent for the pullback of the open cover (cf. pullback of an open cover) - shows that the ideal classifying $Y \times_{X} X^{\mathrm{red}} \subseteq Y$ must be the zero ideal, hence the map is an isomorphism
