sigma algebra generated by singletons

Proposition

Let $X$ be a set. Then the sigma algebra $\mathcal{F}$ generated by all the singletons $\{x\} $ is given by the set containing

  1. the countable sets
  2. the cocountable sets, i.e. $A^{C}$ is countable

Proof

$\subseteq$

Let $A$ be countable or cocountable. Then it (or the complement) is the countable union of singletons. Hence it (or the complement) lies in $\mathcal{F}$, as each singleton lies in $\mathcal{F}$.

If the complement lies in $\mathcal{F}$, then also $A$ has to lie in $\mathcal{F}$, as $\mathcal{F}$ is closed under taking the complement.

$\supseteq$

We show that it is a sigma algebra.

Note that $X \in \mathcal{F}$ follows as $X^{\mathrm{c}} = \emptyset$ is countable.

Furthermore it is closed under taking the complement, as $A$ is countable iff $A^{c}$ is cocountable.

Let $\bigcup_{n} A_n$ be the countable union. Suppose there exists some cocountable $A_n$, then $A_n$ contains all but countably many elements. Hence by $\bigcup A_n \supseteq A_n$ it follosw that $\bigcup A_n$ contains all but countably many elements, i.e. is cocountable.

Suppose there does not exist a cocountable $A_n$, then it is the countable union of countable sets, hence countable.
