hopf maps as essential map

Proposition

The following continuous maps are essential maps:

  1. hopf fibration from S^3
  2. hopf fibration from S^7
  3. hopf fibration from S^15

Proof

$S^3$

using the singular cohomology ring of the complex projective space

Note that

\begin{align*} C(\eta) = \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^2 \end{align*}

(cf. mapping cone of the hopf fibration)

Then since reduced suspension commute with mapping cones

\begin{align*} C(\Sigma^n(\eta)) =& \Sigma^n(C(\eta)) =& \Sigma \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^2 \end{align*}

Let $x \in H^2(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^2, \mathbb{F}_2)$. Then by stability of steenrod operations

\begin{align*} \sigma^n(x) \in H^{n+2}(\Sigma^n \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^2, \mathbb{Z}/2 \mathbb{Z}) \end{align*}

is the nontrivial element

Furthremore $x^2 \in H^4(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^2, \mathbb{Z}/2 \mathbb{Z})$ is the nontrivial element (cf. cup product pairing non degenerate)

Then by square-likeness of steenrood operations

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Sq}^2(\sigma^n(x)) =& \sigma^n(\mathrm{Sq}^2(x)) \\ =& \sigma^n(x^2) \end{align*}

hence again by stability $\mathrm{Sq}^2(\sigma^n(x))$ is the nontrivial element.

Thus the map

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Sq}^2: H^{n+2}(\Sigma^n \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^2; \mathbb{Z}/2 \mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow H^{n+4}(\Sigma^n \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^2; \mathbb{Z}/2 \mathbb{Z}) \end{align*}

is nontrivial.

Assume that

\begin{align*} \Sigma^n \eta \end{align*}

is nullhomotopic.

Then by mapping cone of a nullhomotopic map (?)

\begin{align*} C(\eta) \cong S^{n+2} \vee S^{n+4} \end{align*}

But

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Sq}^2(x) = x^2 = 0 \in S^2 \vee S^4 \end{align*}

for $x \in H^2(S^2 \vee S^4, \mathbb{Z}/2 \mathbb{Z})$ by cf. cup product on a wedge & stability of steenrod operations
