Proposition

Let $R$ be a ring. Then $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{LMod}_{R}}(R)$ is equivalently given by $R^{\mathrm{op}}$.

Proof

We claim that there exists an isomorphism

\begin{align*} \varphi: R^{\mathrm{op}} \rightarrow& \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{LMod}_{R}}(R) r \mapsto& (- \cdot r) \\ \end{align*}

This map is a ringhomomorphism: It is additive by distributivity and for $a \in R$ and $r_1,r_2$ we have $\varphi(r_1 \cdot r_2)(a) = ((- \cdot r_1) \cdot r_2) = \varphi(r_2) \circ \varphi(r_1)(a)$. This shows, that the map $R \rightarrow \mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{LMod}_{R}(R))}(R)$ is antimultiplicative and hence multiplicative for $R^{\mathrm{op}}$. It is injective, since the kernel is trivial, as can be detected by $\varphi(r)(1) = r$. It is surjective: any morphism $\varphi: R \rightarrow R$ maps $1$ to some element in $R$ and is then determined by \(R\)-linearity by this element.
