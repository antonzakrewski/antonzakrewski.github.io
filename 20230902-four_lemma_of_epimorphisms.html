Four-Lemma of modules of epimorphisms

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an abelian category (resp. category Group) and the following commutative diagram in $\mathcal{A}$

where $m,p$ are epimorphisms and $q$ is a monomorphism. Suppose the rows are exact, then $n$ is an epimorphism

Proof

w.l.o.g. we continue to assume that $\mathcal{A} = \mathrm{RMod}$. The general case follows from Mitchell's embedding theorem

$x \in \mathrm{ker}(f_2')$

Then by exactnesss and $\mathrm{im}(f_1') = \mathrm{ker}(f_2')$ there exists an $a' \in A'$ such that

\begin{align*} f_1'(a') = x \end{align*}

By surjectivity of $m$, there exists an $a \in A$ with $m(a) = a'$.

Then by commutativity,

\begin{align*} n(f_1(a)) =& f_1'(m(a)) =& x \end{align*}

hence $\mathrm{ker}(f_2') \subseteq \mathrm{im}(n)$

$x \not\in \mathrm{ker}(f_2')$

Suppose $x\not\in \mathrm{ker}(f_2')$, then $f_2'(x) \neq 0$ and thus by surjectivity of $p$ there exists an $c \neq 0$ such that $p(c) = f_2'(x)$ Furthermore by exactness, it holds that $f_3' \circ f_2'(x) = 0$ and thus also by commutativity

\begin{align*} q \circ f_3(c) =& f_3' p(c) \\ =& f_3' f_2'(x) \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

Since $\mathrm{ker}(q) = 0$, as $q$ is injective, we conclude that $f_3(c) = 0$ and therefore $c \in \mathrm{ker}(f_3)$ or by exactness

\begin{align*} c \in \mathrm{im}(f_2) \end{align*}

Thus there exists an $b \in B$ such that

\begin{align*} f_2(b) = c \end{align*}

or by commutativity

\begin{align*} f_2' \circ n(b) =& p(f_2(b)) \\ =& p(c) \\ =& f_2'(x) \end{align*}

Hence

\begin{align*} x - n(b) \in \mathrm{ker}(f_2') \end{align*}

and by choosing $b'$ with $n(b') = x - n(b)$ we get

\begin{align*} n(b + b') =& n(b) + x - n(b) \\ =& x \end{align*}

or $x \in \mathrm{im}(n)$
