Satz von Frobenius über Divisionsalgebren

Proposition

Every finite dimensional, associative \(\mathbb{R}\)-division algebra $A$ is isomorphic to one of

  1. $\mathbb{R}$
  2. $\mathbb{C}$
  3. $\mathbb{H}$

Proof

Let $n$ be the dimension of $A$. Let $V \coloneqq \{a \in A \vert a^2 \in \mathbb{R}, a^2 \leq 0\}$ ?.

Then $V \cap \mathbb{R} = 0$.

Moroevor $V = \mathrm{ker}(A \rightarrow \mathbb{R})$ is the kernel of the trace map. Hence $\mathrm{dim}(V) = n-1$ and $V \oplus \mathbb{R} = A$

For $a,b \in V$ and $ab + ba = (a+b)^2 - a^2 - b^2$ we get $(a+b)^2, a^2, b^2 \in \mathbb{R}$.

Let

\begin{align*} B: V \times V \rightarrow& \mathbb{R} \\ (a,b) \mapsto& - \frac{1}{2} (ab + ba) \end{align*}

and observe that $B(a,a) = - a^2 \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ and equal to $0$ if and only if $a = 0$.

Hence $B$ is a positive definite symmetric bilinear form.

Let $W$ be the subspace of $V$ that generates $A$ as an algebra and minimal with this property.

Let $e_1,...,e_k$ be an ortho-normal basis of $W$ with respect to $B_{\vert W}$

Thne $e_i^2 = - B(e_i,e_i) = -1$ and $0 = B(e_i,e_j) = 0 = - \frac{1}{2} (e_i e_j - e_j e_i)$ which shows $e_i e_j = - e_j e_i$.

Hence we want to count the number of orthonormal elements $e_i,...,e_j$.

$k = 0$

Then $A$ is generated by the zero space as $\mathbb{R}$ algebra

$k = 1$

Then $A$ is generated by $1$ and $e_1$ such that $e_1^2 = -1$. Hence $\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}(A) = 2$ and $A$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$

$k = 2$

Then $A$ is generated by $1,e_1,e_2$ and $e_i^2 = -1$ and $e_1 e_2 = - e_2 e_1$ and $(e_1 e_2)^2 = -1$

Then define

\begin{align*} \mathbb{H} \rightarrow A I \mapsto& e_1 \\ J \mapsto& e_2 \\ k \mapsto& e_1 e_2 \end{align*}

the map which is a ring homomorphism (it satisfies the relations)

It is surjective, as $A$ is generated by $e_1,e_2$. Moreover it is injective (cf. morphism between skew fields is injective).

$k > 2$

Then consider $u_i = e_1 \cdot e_2 \cdot e_k$ and consider $u^2$. It follows $u^2 = e_1 e_2 e_k e_1 e_2 e_k = -e_1^2 e_2^2 e_k^2 = 1$ by anticommutativity. Hence $0 = u^2 - 1 = (u-1) (u+1)$ and therefore $u \in \{\pm 1\}$ as $A$ is a Integral domain.

This shows that $e_k = \mp e_1 e_2$ or $e_1,...,e_{k-1}$ generate $A$ as algebra, which contradicts the minimality of $e_1,...,e_k$.
