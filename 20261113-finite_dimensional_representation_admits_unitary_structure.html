finite dimensional representation admits unique unitary structure

Proposition

Let $V$ be a $\mathbb{C}$ representation. Then $V$ admits a \(G\)-equivariant, hermitian product, which is unique up to real scalars.

Proof

a) existence

pick any hermitian product $\langle -,-\rangle$ and define consider

\begin{align*} (v,w) \coloneqq \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \sum_{g \in G} \langle gv, gw\rangle \end{align*}

b) uniqueness

let $B_1,B_2$ be hemritian forms on $V$. Then there exists an $A: V \rightarrow V$ such that

\begin{align*} B_1(v,w) = B_2(Av,w) \end{align*}

Moreover, $A$ is \(G\)-equivariant.

\begin{align*} A v = \sum_{i=1}^n B_i(v e_1)e_i \end{align*}

where $e_i$ is a norhtonormal basis of $B_2$. This implies $A = \lambda \mathrm{id}$ by schur lemma over algebraically closed fields.

Moreover,

\begin{align*} B_1(v,v) = \lambda B_2(v,v) \end{align*}

where both $B_1(v,v), B_2(v,v)$ are positive real numbers.
