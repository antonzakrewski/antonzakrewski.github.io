power of a diagonalizable matrix

Proposition

Let $A \in K^{n \times n}$ be a diagonalizable Matrix with

\begin{align*} A =& S \cdot D \cdot S^{-1} \end{align*}

where

Then the power $A^n$ is given by

\begin{align*} A^n = S \cdot D^{n} \cdot S^{-1} \end{align*}

where

In particular $A^n$ is also diagonalizable and one can give an explicit description of $A^n$ by computing $S,S^{-1},D^n$ and then taking the product.

Proof

Proof by induction

induction basis

for $n = 1$ (or $n = 0$) this follows immediately

induction step

Suppose

\begin{align*} A^n = S \cdot D^n \cdot S^{-1} \end{align*}

then

\begin{align*} A^{n+1} =& A \cdot A^n \\ =& (S \cdot D \cdot S^{-1}) (S \cdot D^n \cdot S^{-1}) \\ =& S \cdot D \cdot (S^{-1} \cdot S) \cdot D^n \cdot S^{-1} \\ =& S \cdot D \cdot D^n \cdot S^{-1} \\ =& S \cdot D^{n+1} \cdot S^{-1} \end{align*}
