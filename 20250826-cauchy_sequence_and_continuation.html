cauchy sequence and continuation

Proposition

Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space, $(x_n)$ a sequence TFAE:

  1. $x_n$ is a cauchy sequence.
  2. let $\mathbb{N}^+ \times \mathbb{N}^b \hookrightarrow \{(\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{m} \vert n,m \in \mathbb{N}^+\} \cup \{0\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ be the inclusion.

Then the map

\begin{align*} f: \{ \left(\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{m} \right) \vert n,m \in \mathbb{N}^+\} \cup \{0\} \rightarrow& [0,\infty) \\ \left(\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{m} \right) \mapsto& d(x_n,x_m) \\ 0 \mapsto& 0 \end{align*}

is continuous

Note that $\{ \left(\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{m} \right) \vert n,m \in \mathbb{N}^+\}$ carries the discrete topology. think of $\mathbb{R}^2$ carrying the Maximumsnorm, which is topologically equivalent to the standard metric.

Proof

Note that $x$ is cauchy if and only if for each $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists an $n_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $d(x_n,x_m) < \varepsilon$ for $n,m \geq n_0$. Then $\{ \left(\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{m} \right) \vert n,m \geq n_0\} \cup \{0\} \subseteq f^{-1}(B_{\varepsilon})$ is open, 1) $\implies$ 2)

If the map is continuous, then for $\varepsilon > 0$ the preimage $f^{-1}[B_{\varepsilon}]$ contains a subset of the subbasis, i.e. $\{ \left(\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{m} \right) \vert n,m \geq n_0\} \cup \{0\}$. Hence take $n_0$.
