complement of a ring extension multiplicatively closed and integrally closed

Proposition

Let $A \hookrightarrow B$ be a ring extension. Suppose $B \setminus A \cup \{1\}$ is multiplicatively closed

Then $A$ is integrally closed in $B$

Proof

Proof by contraposition

Suppose $A$ is not integrally closed, then there exists an $b \in B \setminus A$ such that

\begin{align*} 0 = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i b^i \end{align*}

with $\alpha_i \in A$ and $\alpha_n = 1$

w.l.o.g. take the monic polynomial with smallest degree

Then we get

\begin{align*} - \alpha_0 = \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_{i} b^{i-1} \right) \cdot b \end{align*}

with $b \not\in A$ and $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_{i} b^{i-1} \not\in A$ as otherwise

\begin{align*} g \coloneqq \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_{i} b^{i-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_{i} X^{i-1} \end{align*}

is a polynomial in $A[X]$ with degree $n-1$ satisfying

\begin{align*} g(b) = 0 \end{align*}
