colimit and morphisms defined by component

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category, $I: \mathcal{I} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$ a diagram and $\colim_{I}$ the colimit

Then a morphism $f: \colim_{I} \rightarrow B$ for an object $B$ is precisely and uniquely defined by morphisms $f_j: A_j \rightarrow B$ such that

commutes

Proof

a) morphism $f_i: \colim \rightarrow B$ induces $f_i$

commuting follows from definition of a colimit, as 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
A_i \arrow[]{rd}{} \arrow[]{rr}{} & \arrow[phantom, center ]{d}{\circlearrowleft} & A_j \arrow[]{ld}{}\\
& B & 
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

commutes

[label={[label distance=-2mm]-45:\ulcorner}]

b) $f_i$ induces morphisms $f$

follows from universal property

c) uniqueness

follows for a) from construction and for b) from universal property, where uniqueness is required
