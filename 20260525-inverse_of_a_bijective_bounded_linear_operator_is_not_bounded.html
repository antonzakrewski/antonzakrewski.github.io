inverse of a bijective, bounded, linear operator with norm 1 is not bounded

Proposition

Let $X,Y$ be normed vector spaces and $\varphi: X \rightarrow Y$ a bijective, bounded operator. Then $\varphi^{-1}$ is in general not bounded.

Proof

Let $p \in [1,\infty]$. Then consider the map

\begin{align*} \varphi: l_p \rightarrow& l_p \\ (x_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mapsto& ( \frac{x_n}{n})_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \end{align*}

It is linear in each component and it is bounded: We claim that it is bounded: For any $(x_n)$ we get

\begin{align*} \| x \|_{p} \sqrt[p]{\sum_{n=0}^\infty x_n^p} \leq& \sqrt[p]{\sum_{n=0}^\infty (\frac{x_n}{n})^p} = \| x_n \|_n \end{align*}

and it has norm $1$ (cosnider $(1,0,...)$.

But the inverse is given by

\begin{align*} \varphi^{-1}: l_p \rightarrow& l_p \\ (x_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mapsto (n \cdot x_n_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \end{align*}

which is not bounded: for any $\| e_n \|_{p}=1$ we get

\begin{align*} \| \varphi(e_n) \| = \| n \cdot e_n \| = n \end{align*}
