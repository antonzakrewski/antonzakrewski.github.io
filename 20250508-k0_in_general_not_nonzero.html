k0 in general not nonzero

Proposition

Let $R \neq 0$ be a ring Then $K_0(R)$ is in general not nonzero.

Proof

Let $K$ be a field, $V = K^{\mathbb{N}}$ a countably infinite dimensional vector space. Then by the example we have rings in general do not satisfy the invariant basis property.

In particular we get

\begin{align*} [V] = [V \oplus V] = 2 [V] \end{align*}

or $[V] = 0$

Then it remains to show that

\begin{align*} [M] = 0 \end{align*}

for arbitrary finite projectives $M$.

But

\begin{align*} M \cong& V \otimes_{V} M \\ \cong& (V \oplus V) \otimes_{V} M \\ \cong& V \otimes_{V} M \oplus V \otimes_{V} M \end{align*}

or

\begin{align*} [M] = 0 \end{align*}
