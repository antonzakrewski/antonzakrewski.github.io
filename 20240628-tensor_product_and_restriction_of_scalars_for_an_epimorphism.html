tensor product and restriction of scalars for an epimorphism as isomorphism

Proposition

Let \(A,B\) be rings, $f: A \rightarrow B$ an epimorphism and $M,N$ \(B\)-modules. Then the map

\begin{align*} \varphi: M \otimes_A N \rightarrow& M \otimes_B N \\ m \otimes n \mapsto& m \otimes n \end{align*}

induced by

\begin{align*} M \times N \rightarrow& M \otimes_{B} N \\ (m,n) \mapsto& m \otimes n \end{align*}

is an isomorphism of \(A\)-modules

Proof

Let $m,n \in M \times N$. Then consider the \(A\)-linear map

\begin{align*} \cdot_{m,n}: B \otimes_{A} B \rightarrow& M \otimes_{A} N \\ (b_1,b_2) \mapsto& (b_1 \cdot_{b} m) \otimes (n \cdot b_2) \end{align*}

where $M \otimes_A N$ is seen as \(A\)-module.

It follows that $M \otimes_A N$ inherits a \(B\)-module structure via.

\begin{align*} b \cdot (m \otimes n) =& bm \otimes n \\ =& m \otimes bn \end{align*}

(cf. symmetry of the tensor product for a ring epimorphism)

Therefore

\begin{align*} M \times N \rightarrow& M \otimes_{A} N \\ (m,n) \mapsto& m \otimes n \end{align*}

is a \(B\)-bilinear map, showing that the map

\begin{align*} M \otimes_{B} N \rightarrow& M \otimes_{A} N \\ (m,n) \mapsto& m \otimes n \end{align*}

is welldefined and an inverse.
