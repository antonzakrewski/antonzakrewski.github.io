direct product of rings as categorical product in Ring

Proposition

Let $R_i$ be rings and $\prod R_i$ the direct product of rings. Then $\prod R_i$ with the projections

\begin{align*} \pi_i: \prod R_i \rightarrow& R_i \\ (r_i) \mapsto& r_i \end{align*}

is the categorical product

Proof

Suppose $S$ is another ring and

\begin{align*} \varphi_i: S \rightarrow R_i \end{align*}

are ring-homomorphisms

Then the map

\begin{align*} \varphi: S \rightarrow \prod R_i s \mapsto& (\varphi_i(s)) \end{align*}

is a welldefined ring homomorphism, since

\begin{align*} \varphi(s_1 + s_2 \cdot s_3) =& (\varphi_i(s_1 + s_2 \cdot s_3) \\ =& (\varphi_i(s_1) + \varphi_i(s_2 \cdot s_3)) \\ =& (\varphi_i(s_1)) + (\varphi_i(s_2 \cdot s_3)) \\ =& (\varphi_i(s_1)) + (\varphi_i(s_2) \cdot \varphi_i(s_3)) \\ =& \varphi(s_1) + (\varphi_i(s_2)) \cdot (\varphi_i(s_3)) \\ =& \varphi(s_1) + \varphi(s_2) \cdot \varphi(s_3) \\ \end{align*}

and

\begin{align*} \varphi(0) =& (\varphi_i(0)) \\ =& (0) \\ \varphi(1) =& (\varphi_i(1)) \\ =& (1) \end{align*}

furthermore, it makes by construction following diagram commute

At last, this map is unique, as each component of $\varphi(s)$ is forced by $\pi_i(\varphi(s)) = \varphi_i(s)$
