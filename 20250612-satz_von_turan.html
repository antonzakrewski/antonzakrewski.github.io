turan's theorem

Proposition

Let $G = ([n],E)$ be a graph without $K_{r+1}$ and maximal amount of edges. Then

\begin{align*} G \simeq K_n^{(r)} \end{align*}

Proof

Define

\begin{align*} x \sim y :\Leftrightarrow xy \not\in E \end{align*}

This is an equivalence relation:

  • symmetry and reflexivity follow immediately, transitivity follows frommaximal K_r free graph and transitivity of missing edges.

Hence this defines a partition $V_1,...,V_k$ with the properties

  1. each $V_i$ is an independent set of vertices
  2. for vertices $v_i \in V_i, v_j \in V_j$ with $i \neq j$ we may conclude that $v_i v_j \in E$

Hence $G$ is isomorphic to some complete r-bipartite graph.

We show that

\begin{align*} \vert \vert V_i \vert - \vert V_j \vert \vert \leq 1 \end{align*}

so that $G$ is also an r-th turan graph.

Assume otherwise, i.e. w.l.o.g. $\vert V_i \vert - \vert V_j \vert > 1$. Then remove a knot of $V_i$ and add it to $V_j$ (and add all necessary edges for it to be a r-bipartite graph).

Then this increases the amount of edges, so contradiction to the assumption that $G$ has a maximal amount of edges (note that the r-th turan graph and similar graphs still satisfy the assumption of being $K_{r+1}$ free)
