bounded metric space in general not compact

Proposition

Let $(M,d)$ be a bounded metric space. Then $M$ is in general not compact

Proof

consider the discrete metric on $\mathbb{N}$. Then $d(x,y) \leq 1$, hence it is bounded. But the cover $\{ \{n\} \vert n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ does not contain a finite subcover.
