extension of a lipschitz map from a dense subspace to a complete space

Proposition

Let $X,Y$ be metric spaces, $A \subseteq X$ a dense subset and $Y$ complete metric space. Let $f: A \rightarrow Y$ be a L-lipschitz map, then there exists a unique, L-lipschitz extension $\overline{f}$ 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
A \rar{f} \arrow[hookrightarrow, ]{d}{} & Y \\
X \arrow[]{rd}{\overline{f}}& \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

Proof

Let $x \in X$, then choose a convergent sequence $x_n \rightarrow x$. We define

\begin{align*} \overline{f}(x) = \mathrm{lim} f(x_n) \end{align*}

We need to show that it is welldefined, so let $y_n \rightarrow x$ be another convergent sequence. Then

\begin{align*} \frac{1}{L} d(\mathrm{lim} f(x_n), \mathrm{lim} f(y_n)) \leq \mathrm{lim} d(x_n, y_n) = 0 \end{align*}

since $x_n,y_n$ converge to the same $x$ (we have implicitly used, that $d(-,-)$ is continuous in both variables).

In particular, the diagram 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
A \rar{f} \arrow[hookrightarrow, ]{d}{} & Y \\
X \arrow[]{rd}{\overline{f}}& \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

commutes, since we may choose the constant sequence for $a \in A$.

Now it suffices to show that it is \(L\)-lipschitz. Let $x,y\in X$, then choose convergent sequences $x_n,y_n$ and observe

\begin{align*} d(x,y) =& \mathrm{lim} d(x_n, y_n) \\ =& \mathrm{lim} L \cdot d(f(x_n), f(y_n)) \\ =& L \cdot \mathrm{lim} d(f(x_n), f(y_n)) \\ =& L \cdot d(f(x), f(y)) \end{align*}
