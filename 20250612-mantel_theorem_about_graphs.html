mantel theorem about graphs without triangle

Proposition

Let $G = ([n],E)$ be a graph without $K_3$ as subgraph. Then

\begin{align*} e(G) \leq \frac{n^2}{4} \end{align*}

Furthermore this bound is optimal

Proof

optimal

Consider the complete bipartite graph

\begin{align*} K_{\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \rfloor, \lceil \frac{n}{2} \rfloor, } \end{align*}

bound

Let $xy \in E$ be an edge.

Then

\begin{align*} N(x) \cap N(y) = \emptyset \end{align*}

since otherwise we would have a triangle.

Thus

\begin{align*} \vert N(x) \cup N(y) \vert \leq n \end{align*}

consider

\begin{align*} \sum_{x \in [n]} d^2(x) =& \sum_{x \in [n]} \vert N(x) \vert \cdot d(x) \\ =& \sum_{x \in [n]} \sum_{y \in N(x)} d(x) \\ =& \sum_{xy \in E} d(x) + d(y) \\ \end{align*}

Then as $N(x) \cap N(y) = \emptyset $

\begin{align*} \sum_{xy \in E} d(x) + d(y) \leq& \sum_{xy \in E} n \\ =& e(G) \cdot n \end{align*}

but then the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality tells us

\begin{align*} \sum_{x \in [n] } d(x)^2 \geq \frac{(\sum_{x \in [n]} d(x))^2}{n} = \frac{4 e(G)^2}{n} \end{align*}

or equivalently

\begin{align*} \frac{4 e(G)^2}{n} \leq n \cdot e(G) \end{align*}

resp.

\begin{align*} e(G) \leq \frac{n^2}{4} \end{align*}
