differentiation operator is not bounded

Proposition

Let

\begin{align*} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}: C^{\infty}([0,1]) \rightarrow& C^{\infty}([0,1]) \\ f \mapsto& f' \end{align*}

be the differentiation operator. Then it is an unbounded operator.

Proof

consider

\begin{align*} f_n = x^n \end{align*}

Then $\| x^n \|_{\infty} = 1$ but $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} x^n = n \cdot x^{n-1}$ with

\begin{align*} \| n \cdot x^{n-1} \|_{\infty} = n \end{align*}

hence $\| T \| \geq n$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$
