projective vanishing locus for the integers and rationals

Proposition

Let $X$ be a projective vanishing locus of $\mathbb{P}^n$. Then the inclusion $\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}$ induces a Bijection

\begin{align*} X(\mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow X(\mathbb{Q}) \end{align*}

Proof

observe that $X(\mathbb{Z})$ is given by the map

\begin{align*} [a_0 : ... : a_{n}] \mapsto& [a_0 : ... : a_{n}] \end{align*}

now it is surjective: write any solution

\begin{align*} [ \frac{a_0}{b_0} : ... : \frac{a_{n}}{b_n}] =& [a_0 \cdot \prod_{i \neq 0} b_i : ... : a_n \cdot \prod_{i \neq n} b_i] \end{align*}

here we have used projective coordinates of the projective space since any line over $\mathbb{Z}$ / $\mathbb{Q}$ is trivial (cf. picard group of a dedekind domain as invertible ideals).
