canonical map induced between colimits for a subdiagram

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{E}$ be categories and $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{L}: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{E}$ be functors where $\mathcal{F}$ is a Suppose $\mathrm{colim} \mathcal{L}, \mathrm{colim} \mathcal{LF}$ exists.

Then there exists a canonical morphism

\begin{align*} \mathrm{colim} \mathcal{LF} \rightarrow \mathrm{colim}(\mathcal{L}) \end{align*}

Proof

Consider the map induced by 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathcal{LF}(\alpha) \arrow[]{rd}{\pi} \arrow[]{rr}{\mathrm{id}} &  & \mathcal{L}( \mathcal{F}(\alpha)) \arrow[]{rd}{\pi}& 
& \mathrm{colim} \mathcal{LF} \arrow[dashed]{rr}{\exists! } & & \mathrm{colim} \mathcal{L}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

Note that

\begin{align*} \eta: \mathcal{L} \rightarrow \mathrm{colim} \mathcal{L} \end{align*}

is a natural transformation, hence also the right whiskering / left whiskering
