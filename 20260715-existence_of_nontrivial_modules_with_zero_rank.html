existence of nontrivial modules with zero rank

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M$ a \(R\)-module of constant rank $0$. Then $M$ is in general not trivial.

Proof

consider $R = \mathbb{Z}$ and $M = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$. Then for characteristic $p$, we get $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} k = 0$ since any element in $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is $p$-divisible. For characteristic $0$, we may use that every element in $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion, i.e. $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$ vanishes.
