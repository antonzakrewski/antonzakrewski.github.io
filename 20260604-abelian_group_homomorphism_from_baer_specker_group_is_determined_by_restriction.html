abelian group homomorphism from baer-specker group to a finitely generated group is determined by restriction

Proposition

Let $G$ be a finitely generated abelian group and $f,f': \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow G$ be group-homomorphisms such that $f_{\vert \coprod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}} = f'_{\vert \coprod_{n \in \mathbb{N}}}$. Then $f = f'$.

Proof

w.l.o.g. we may assume that $f' = 0$. So let $f$ be a group homomorphism, which vanishes on $\coprod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}$. Let $(x_n) \in \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}$ be a arbitrary, then we can find $(y_n), (z_n) \in \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}$ such that $2^n y_n + 3^n z_n = x_n$, since $2^n,3^n$ are coprime.

Then

\begin{align*} f((x_n)) =& f((2^n y_n) + (3^n z_n)) \\ =& f((2^n y_n)) + f((3^n z_n)) \end{align*}

and it suffices to show that $f((2^n y_n)), f((3^n z_n))$ vanish.

We claim that $f((2^n y_n))$ ($f((3^n z_n))$) is divisible by $2^n$ ($3^n$)$ for any $n \in $\mathbb{N}$ and hence zero (cf. classification of finitely generated abelian groups).

So let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ be arbitrary, then

\begin{align*} f((2^n y_n)) =& f((2^0 y_0,...,2^{n-1} y_{n-1}),0,...) + f((0,...,0,2^n y_n,2^{n+1} y_{n+1})) \\ =& 0 + 2^n f((y_n,2 y_{n+1},...)) \end{align*}

which shows the claim
