injectivity as point local property

Proposition

Let \(R\) be a ring and $\mathrm{RMod}$ the category RMod and $f: M \rightarrow N$ a module-homomorphism of \(R\)-modules \(M,N\).

Then injectivity is a point local property

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

corollary of:

  • localization as exact functor
  • exact functor preserves monomorphisms

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be an arbitrary maximal ideal 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathrm{ker}(f) \arrow[]{r}{} & M \arrow[]{r}{f} & N \arrow[]{r}{} 
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

be a sequence such that

is injective. Then $\mathrm{ker}(f_{\mathfrak{m}}) = 0$

Then as localization commutes with kernel we conclude that

\begin{align*} \mathrm{ker}(f_{\mathfrak{m}}) = \mathrm{ker}(f)_{\mathfrak{m}} \end{align*}

Thus we conclude that $\mathrm{ker}(f) = 0$ (cf. zero module and localization at a maximal ideal)
