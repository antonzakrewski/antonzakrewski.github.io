gelfand duals of profinite spaces

Proposition

Let $A$ be a commutative unital C-star Algebras. TFAE:

  1. the Gelfand dual of $A$ is a profinite space
  2. $A$ is generated as a $C^*$ algebra by a family of self-adjoint, idempotent elements

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

use that any profinite space is the cofiltered limit of finite, discrete spaces, hence after applying the equivalence we get a filtered colimit of $\mathrm{Hom}( \{1,...,n\}, \mathbb{C}) \cong \mathbb{C}^n$ where the elements $e_i$ are self-adjoint and idempotent and generate $\mathbb{C}^n$ as $C^*$ Algebra.

2) $\implies$ 1

We need to show that $A$ is a filtered colimit of objects which are isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^n$. First observe that $A$ is the filtered colimit of the finitely presented, commutative, unital $C^*$ Algebras.

Now let $A' = \langle p_1,...,p_n\rangle$ be the generated, unital subalgebra by self-adjoint, idempotent elements (i.e., projections). Then we need to show $A' \cong \mathbb{C}^m$ for some $m$, as then $A$ is the filtered colimit of objects of the form $\mathrm{Hom}( \{1,...,m\}^{\delta}, \mathbb{C})$ and hence after applying the gelfand dual a cofiltered limit of discrete, finite sets. First observe that if $A' = \langle p_1\rangle$, then $A' \cong p_1 \mathbb{C} \times (1 - p_1) \mathbb{C} \hookrightarrow A$ where the inclusion is unital and contains $p_1$.

Now suppose $A'$ is generated by $p_1,...,p_n$ with $n$ minimal. Then define

\begin{align*} A' \rightarrow p_1 A' \times (1 - p_1) A' a \mapsto (p_1 a), (1 - p_1 a) \end{align*}

where this map is a $\mathbb{C}$ algebra map by a standard argument and compatible with the pointwise star operation, since $p_1$ and $(1 - p_1)$ are both self-adjoint.

Now $p_1 A'$ is generated by $p_1 \cdot p_2,...,p_1 \cdot p_n$ as unital subalgebra and $(1 - p_1)A'$ is generated by $(1 - p_1) p_2,...,(1 - p_1) p_n$ as unital subalgebra. We can continue this in each component to show that $A'$ is the product of unital subalgebras, which are generated by a single projection, hence of the form $\mathbb{C}$ or $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{C}$ with the pointwise multiplication. In particular, we have shown that $A' \cong \mathbb{C}^m$ for some $m$
