pi_n of S^n wedge S^1

Proposition

Let $n \geq 2$. Then there exists an isomorphism

\begin{align*} \pi_n(S^n \wedge S^1) \cong \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z} \end{align*}

Proof

think of:

  • use the description of universal cover and higher homotopy groups, i.e. $\bigvee S^n \rightarrow S^1 \vee S^n$ induces an isomorphism $\pi_n(\bigvee S^n) \rightarrow \pi_n(S^1 \vee S^n)$
  • use absolute Hurewicz theorem for $\pi_n(\bigvee S^n) \cong H_n(\bigvee S^n) \cong \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}$

Remark

an element is precisely characterized by finitely many "degrees" (after winding some amount)

  • higher homotopy groups are abelian, i.e. we may permute, and then cancel windings appropriately.
