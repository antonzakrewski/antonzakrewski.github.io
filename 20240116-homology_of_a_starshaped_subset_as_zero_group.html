homology of a starshaped subset as zero group

Proposition

Let $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be a starshaped subset. Then each singular homology group is the zero group

Proof

higher level (albeit circular)

follows from:

  • relative singular homology as homotopy invariant functor
  • starshaped subset as contractible set

geometric proof

Let $x \in X$. Consider the map

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Top}}(\Delta^n, X) \rightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Top}}(\Delta^{n+1}, X) \end{align*}

given by sending the point $[n+1] \mapsto x$ and linearly interpolating the rest.

This gives a group-homomorphism

\begin{align*} h_n: C_n^{\mathrm{sing}}(X) \rightarrow C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{sing}}(X) \end{align*}

which for $n \geq 0$ satisfies the relation

\begin{align*} d_{n+1} h_n + h_{n-1} d_n = \mathrm{id} \end{align*}

hence for $\sigma \in Z_n(X)$ a cycle we conclude that

\begin{align*} \sigma = d_{n+1}h_n(\sigma) + h_{n-1} d_n(\sigma) = d_{n+1} h_n(\sigma) \in B_n(X) \end{align*}

as $h_{n-1} d_n(\sigma) = 0$ as $\sigma \in \mathrm{ker}$, which shows that $H_n(x) = 0$
