secondcountable space is separable

Proposition

Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a secondcountable topological space. Then $X$ is separable.

Proof

Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a countable basis Then choose for each subset $U$ in $\mathcal{B}$ a point $y \in U$ and let $Y$ denote the set of these choosen points $y$. Observe that $Y$ is countable, as it contains at most $\vert \mathcal{B} \vert$ many elements. We claim that $\overline{Y} = X$.

Let $x \in X$ be arbitrary and $O$ an open neighbourhood of $x$ (and hence nonempty). Then $O$ is the union of subsets $U_i$ in $\mathcal{B}$. So choose an $U_i$ with $y \in U_i$ and $y \in Y$. Then $y \in U_i \cap Y \subseteq O \cap Y$ and hence $x \in \overline{Y}$.

Therefore $\overline{Y} = X$
