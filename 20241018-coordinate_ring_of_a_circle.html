coordinate ring of a circle

Proposition

Let $K$ be a field and $\mathbb{A}_{K}^2$ the affine space. Let $C \coloneqq Z(X^2 + Y^2 - 1)$ be the circle.

Then the coordinate ring is given by

\begin{align*} A(C) \cong \begin{cases} K[T] & \mbox{if } \mathrm{char}(K) = 2 \\ K[T,T^{-1}] & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

Proof

Let $\mathrm{i}$ denote a root of $-1$.

$\mathrm{char}(K) = 2$

Consider the \(K\)-Algebra homomorphsim

\begin{align*} K[X,Y] \rightarrow& K[T] \\ X \mapsto& \mathrm{i} T = T\\ Y \mapsto& T + 1 \end{align*}

Then it follows that the map is surjective, as the image of $\mathrm{i} T$ generates it.

Furthermore it follows that

\begin{align*} X^2 + Y^2 =& - T^2 + T^2 + 2T + 1 \\ =& 1 \end{align*}

hence $(X^2 + Y^2 - 1) \subseteq \mathrm{ker}$

By reasons of dimension this map has to be injective todo

\begin{align*} K[X,Y] \rightarrow& K[T] \\ X \mapsto& \frac{1}{2} (T + T^{-1}) \\ Y \mapsto& - \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{i} (T - T^{-1}) \\ \end{align*}

Then it follows that

\begin{align*} (X + \mathrm{i} Y) \mapsto& T \\ (X - \mathrm{i} Y) \mapsto& T^{-1} \end{align*}

hence the map is surjectiev.

Furthermore

\begin{align*} X^2 + Y^2 - 1 = (X + \mathrm{i} Y) ( X - \mathrm{i} Y) - 1 = 0 \end{align*}

thus showing $(X^2 + Y^2 - 1) \subseteq \mathrm{ker}$.
