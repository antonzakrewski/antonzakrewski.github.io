Artin-Wedderburn

Proposition

Let $A$ be a finite dimensional k-Algebra over a field $k$. Then $A$ has finitely many isomorphism classes simple representations, each of which is finite dimensional.

Moreover, if $\{V_i\}$ is the collection of simple represntation of $A$, then the map

\begin{align*} A/\mathrm{Rad}(A) \xrightarrow{\oplus p_i} \bigoplus \mathrm{End}_{D_i}(V_i) \end{align*}

is an isomorphism

Proof

Let $V$ be a simple \(A\)-representation. then $A \rightarrow V, a \mapsto av$ is surjective so $\mathrm{dim}(V) < \mathrm{dim}(A)$ is finite. Now let $\{V_i\}$ be a collection of pairwise non-isomorphic \(A\)-representations.

Then $A \rightarrow \bigoplus \mathrm{End}_{D_i}(V_i)$ is surjective But $\mathrm{dim} \mathrm{End}_{D_i}(V_i) \geq 1$, so

\begin{align*} \mathrm{dim}(\bigoplus_{r} \mathrm{End}_{D_i}(V_i) \geq r \end{align*}

So $r \leq \mathrm{dim}(A) < \infty$. So there exists only finitely many simple \(A\)-representations.

pick an enumeration $\{v_i\}_r$ of thm. Then

\begin{align*} A \twoheadrightarrow \bigoplus \mathrm{End}_{D_i}(V_i) \end{align*}

is surjective by the density theorem for representations.

Now the kernel of it is by defintiion the radical, hence the fundamental theorem of ring homomorphism implies that

\begin{align*} A/\mathrm{Rad}(A) \simeq \bigoplus \mathrm{End}_{D_i}(V_i) \end{align*}
