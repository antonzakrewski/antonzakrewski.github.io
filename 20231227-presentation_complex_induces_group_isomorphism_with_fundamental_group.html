presentation complex induces group isomorphism of pi_1

Proposition

Let $G$ be a group, $(S,R)$ a presentation of $G$ and

be a presentation complex

Then there exitss a canonical group-isomorphism

\begin{align*} \pi_1(C(S,R)) \cong $G$ \end{align*}

Proof

Let $r \in R$ Then consider the point $\overline{r}(0) \in C(S,R)$ given by the map $\overline{r}$ from $(0,0) \in D^{2}_r$. Thus there exists an open cover of $C(S,R)$ given by

\begin{align*} \bigcup_{r \in R} \overline{r}( \mathrm{int}(D^2)) \cup C(S,R) \setminus \left( \bigcup_{r \in R} \overline{r}(0) \right) \end{align*}

Then the intersection is given by

\begin{align*} \coprod{r \in R} D^2 \end{align*}

then the Seifert van Kampen about groupoids implies, that the map

\begin{align*} \mathcal{F}_S \cong \pi_1( \bigvee_{S} S^1} \rightarrow \pi_1(C(S,R)) \end{align*}

is a algebraic group-epimorphism with kernel generated by $R$

Hence by Homomorphiesatz für Gruppen there exists a canonical group isomorphism

\begin{align*} G = \mathcal{F}_S/ \langle R\rangle \rightarrow \pi_1(C(S,R)) \end{align*}
