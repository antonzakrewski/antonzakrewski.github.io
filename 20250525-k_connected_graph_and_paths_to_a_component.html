k-connected graph and paths to a component

Proposition

Let $k \geq 2$ and $G = (V,E)$ be a k knot-connected Graph. Let $a \in V$ and $B \subseteq V \setminus \{a\}$ with $\vert B \vert \geq k$.

Then there exists at least $k$ many paths from $a$ to $B$ which intersect only in $a$

Proof

consider the graph

\begin{align*} G' = \overline{b} \coprod G \end{align*}

where $\overline{b}$ is connected by an edge to each element in $B$.

This graph is still \(k\)-connected (by pigeonhole principle type arguments) and hence using menger theorem about knot connectivity of a graph we conclude that there exist at least $k$ many vertex disjoint paths from $a$ to $\overline{b}$. those intersect only in $\overline{b}$ and $a$, hence restricted to $G$ only intersect in $a$
