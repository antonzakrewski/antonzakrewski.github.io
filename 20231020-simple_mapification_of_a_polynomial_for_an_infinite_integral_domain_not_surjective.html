simple mapification of a polynomial for an infinite integral domain not surjective

Proposition

Let $R$ be an Integral domain with $\vert R \vert = \infty$, $R[X_i]$ the polynomial ring and

\begin{align*} f: R[X_i] \rightarrow \mathrm{Maps}(\prod X_i \mapsto R) \end{align*}

the mapification Then $f$ is not surjective

Proof

$\vert I \vert = 1$

to many zeros Grad eines Polynoms und maximale Anzahl an Nullstellen

general case $I$

there exists a canonical algebrabic ring-epimorphism, restriction to $X_1$,

\begin{align*} \varphi: R[X_i] \rightarrow& R[X_1] \\ \alpha \mapsto& \alpha \\ X_i \mapsto& \begin{cases} 1 & \mbox{if } i = 1 \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

Suppose the mapification is surjective, then there exists a polynomial, such that

\begin{align*} \varphi(R[X_i]) = \begin{cases} 1 & \mbox{if } (X_1,...,X_n) = 0 \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

Therefore, there exists the restriction

\begin{align*} h(x) \mapsto& h(x,0,...,0) \end{align*}

contradiction to $n=1$
