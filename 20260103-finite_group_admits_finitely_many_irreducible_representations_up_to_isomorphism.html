finite group admits finitely many irreducible complex representations up to isomorphism

Proposition

Let $G$ be a finite group. Then there exist only finitely many isomorphism classes $\mathbb{C}[G]$ of irreducible representations $V_1,...,V_k$ and furthermore

\begin{align*} (\mathbb{C}[G], L) \cong \bigoplus_{i = 1}^k V_i^{\mathrm{dim}(V_i)} \end{align*}

Proof

a)

  • first orthogonality theorem

b)

using bilinearity of the inner product on class functions for a finite group

\begin{align*} \langle \Chi_{v_i} , \Chi_L\rangle = \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \sum_{g \in G} \overline{\Chi_{V_i}(g)} \Chi_{L}(g) \end{align*}

But

\begin{align*} \Chi_L(g) =& \mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}[G]}(\rho_g) \\ =& \begin{cases} \vert G \vert & \mbox{if } g = e \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

So

\begin{align*} \langle \Chi_{v_i} , \Chi_L\rangle =& \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \sum_{g \in G} \overline{\Chi_{V_i}(g)} \Chi_{L}(g)\\ =& \frac{1}{\vert G \vert} \overline{\Chi_{V_i}(e)} \Chi_L(e)\\ =& \frac{1}{\vert G \vert } \overline{\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{id}_{V_i})} \vert G \vert \\ =& \overline{\mathrm{dim}(V_i)} \\ =& \mathrm{dim}(V_i) \end{align*}
