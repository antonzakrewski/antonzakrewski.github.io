localized ideal generated by numerator

Proposition

Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $S \subseteq A$ multiplicatively closed and $S^{-1}A$ the localization. then an ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ is precisely generated by the denominators, i.e.

\begin{align*} \mathfrak{a} = \langle \frac{\alpha}{1} \vert \exists s \in S : \frac{\alpha}{s} \in \mathfrak{a} \rangle \end{align*}

Proof

Let $\frac{\alpha}{s} \in \mathfrak{a}$, then

\begin{align*} \frac{\alpha}{s} = \frac{\alpha}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{s} \in \langle \frac{\alpha}{1}}\rangle \end{align*}

Furthermore, note that

\begin{align*} \frac{\alpha}{s} \in \mathfrak{a} \Rightarrow \frac{\alpha}{1} \in \mathfrak{a} \end{align*}

as it follows from

\begin{align*} \frac{\alpha}{1} = \frac{\alpha}{s} \cdot \frac{s}{1} \end{align*}
