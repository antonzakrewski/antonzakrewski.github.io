compact operator and convergent subsequence

Proposition

Let $T: X \rightarrow Y$ be a bounded operator TFAE:

  1. $T$ is a compact operator
  2. for every sequence $x_n$ with $\mathrm{sup} \| x_n \| < \infty$ the sequence $T(x_n)$ has a Convergent subsequence

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

follows from:

  • first countable, compact space as sequentially compact space

where $T(x_n)$ lies in $T(B_{R}(0))$ which is relatively compact.

2) $\implies$ 1)

follows from

  • Kompaktheit und Folgenkompaktheit in metrischen Räumen.

Suppose there exstis a bounded subset $A$ such that $T(A)$ is not relatively compact. Then there exists a sequence $b_n \in \overline{T(A)}$ without accumulation point. Then choose $a_{m,n}$ with $T(a_{m,n}) \xrightarrow{m \rightarrow \infty} b_n$ for $a_{m,n} \in A$ and $\| T(a_{n,n}) - b_n \| < \frac{1}{n+1}\|$. Now consider the sequence $a_{n,n}$ which lies in $A$, hence is bounded and has some convergent subsequence. But $b_n$ does not have any accumulation point, which is a contradiction, since any accumulation point of $T(a_{n,n})$ would be an accumulation point of the sequence $b_n$ (using the assumption $\| T(a_{n,n}) - b_n \| < \frac{1}{n+1}\|$).

Remark

we have have implicitly used, that each point in $\overline{A}$ is the limit of a convergent sequence.
