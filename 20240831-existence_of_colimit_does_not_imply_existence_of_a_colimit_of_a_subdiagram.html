existence of colimit does not imply existence of a colimit for a subdiagram

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{E}$ be categories, $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{G}: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{E}$ be functors Suppose

\begin{align*} \mathrm{colim} \mathcal{G} \end{align*}

exists.

Then $\mathrm{colim} \mathcal{FG}$ does not exists in general

Proof

Consider the partially ordered set

\begin{align*} [0, \sqrt{2}) \coprod ( \sqrt{2}, 2] \end{align*}

as category

Then a colimit is precisely a supremum (cf. supremum as colimit) Consider the identity functor and the inclusion

\begin{align*} \iota: [0,\sqrt{2}) \hookrightarrow [0, \sqrt{2}) \coprod ( \sqrt{2}, 2] \end{align*}

Then

  1. $\mathrm{colim}(\mathrm{id}) = 2$
  2. $\mathrm{colim}(\iota)$ does not exists, as $\sqrt{2} \not \in [0, \sqrt{2}) \coprod ( \sqrt{2}, 2]$
