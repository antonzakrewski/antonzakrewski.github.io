category of pointed objects is product preserving functors

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category with finite products Thne there exists an equivalence

\begin{align*} \mathcal{C}_{*/} \simeq \mathrm{Fun}^{\prod}(\mathrm{Fin}_*^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathcal{C}) \end{align*}

Proof

Recall that $\mathcal{C}_{*/}$ is the full subcategory of the arrow category $\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$, since fully faithful functors are closed under pullbacks

Now we have a (non-full) inclusion $\Delta^1 \rightarrow \mathrm{Fin}_*^{\mathrm{op}}$ mapping the arrow to the unique morphism $\{*\} \rightarrow \{*,1\}$, where $\{*\}$ is the terminal object in $\mathrm{Fin}_*$ and therefore the initial object in $\mathrm{Fin}_*^{\mathrm{op}}$.

  2. right kan extension restricts to $\mathcal{C}_{*/} \rightarrow \mathrm{Fun}^{\prod}(\mathrm{Fin}_*^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathcal{C})$
  1. follows as any product preserving functor $\mathcal{F}$ must map $\{*\}$ to the terminal object, i.e. the essential image lies in $\mathcal{C}_{*/} \subseteq \mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C})$
  2. follows from some pointwise right kan extension computation:

For any set $\{*,1,...,n\}$, the category $\Delta^{1}_{ \{*,1,...,n\}}$ consists of objects given by morphisms $\{*,1,...,n\} \rightarrow \{1,*\}$ or $\{*,1,...,n\} \rightarrow \{*\}$ in $\mathrm{Fin}_*^{\mathrm{op}}$. Therefore there exist n many objects corresponding to $\{*,1\} \rightarrow \{*,1,...,n\}$ in $\mathrm{Fin}_*$ and the unique zero morphism.
