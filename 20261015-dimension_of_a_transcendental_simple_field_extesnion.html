dimension of a transcendental, simple field extension

Proposition

Let $k$ be an infinite field and $k(x)$ a simple transcendental field extension. Then $k(x)$ has dimension $\vert k \vert$.

Proof

We claim that $(x - \lambda)^{-1}$ is linearly independent for $\lambda \in k$. For this suppose we have an equation $\sum \alpha_i (x - \lambda_i)^{-1} = 0$, then we can multiply with $\prod (x - \lambda_i)$ and get

\begin{align*} 0 = \prod (x - \lambda_i) \cdot \sum \alpha_i (x - \lambda_i)^{-1} \end{align*}

which shows that the RHS is zero. But this implies that the polynomial on the RHS is the zero or $(x - \lambda_i)$.
