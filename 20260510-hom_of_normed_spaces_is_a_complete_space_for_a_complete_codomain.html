bounded hom of normed spaces is a complete space for a complete codomain

Proposition

Let $X,Y$ be normed vector spaces, where $Y$ is complete. Then the bounded hom is also complete.

Proof

Let $T_n$ be a Cauchy sequence in $\mathrm{BL}(X,Y)$. Then $T_n(x)$ is a cauchy sequence in $Y$ for $x \in X$: By assumption,

\begin{align*} \| T_n(x) - T_m(x) \| \leq \| T_n - T_m \| \cdot \| x \| \end{align*}

where $\| x\|$ is constant and $\|T_n - T_m \|$ is a cauchy sequence (and everything is nonnegative).

So define

\begin{align*} T \colon X \rightarrow& Y \\ x \mapsto& \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} T_n(x) \end{align*}

where the limit exists, as $Y$ is cauchy.

well-defined

linear operator

for $x_1,x_2 \in X$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb{K}$ we get

\begin{align*} \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}( T_n(x_1 + \lambda x_2)) =& \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}( T_n(x_1) + \lambda T_n(x_2)) \\ =& \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}( T_n(x_1)) + \lambda \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}(T_n(x_2)) \\ =& T(x_1) + \lambda T(x_2) \end{align*}

bounded operator

Observe that $\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \| T_n \|$ is a cauchy sequence in $\mathbb{R}^+$, so in particular convergent and bounded. So let $x \in X$, then

\begin{align*} \frac{\| \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} T_n(x) \||}{\| x \|} =& \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\| T_n(x) \|}{\| x \|} \leq \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \| T_n \| \end{align*}

so passing to supremum on the left side shows

\begin{align*} \| T \| \leq \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \| T_n \| \end{align*}

where the RHS is bounded.

limit point

Let $\varepsilon > 0$, then we need to show that there exists an $n_0 >> 0$ such that

\begin{align*} \mathrm{sup}_{\| x \| = 1} \frac{\| T(x) - T_n(x) \|}{\| x \|} < \varepsilon \end{align*}

for all $n > n_0$.

Recall taht for every $x \in X$ and $k,l \in \mathbb{N}$ we have $\| T_k(x) - T_l(x) \leq \| T_k - T_l \| \cdot \| x \|$ (by the definition of the operator norm) Thus

\begin{align*} \forall \varepsilon > 0 \exists N \in \mathbb{N} \forall k, l \geq N \forall x \in X : \| T_k x - T_l x\| \leq \varepsilon \| x \| \end{align*}

as $T_k$ is cauchy.

In particular, passing to $\lim_{l \rightarrow \infty}$ shows

\begin{align*} \| T_k x - T x \| \leq& \varepsilon \| x \| \end{align*}

and passing to supremum shows

\begin{align*} \| T_k - T \| \leq& \varepsilon \end{align*}

for the operator norm

In other words, $T_k \xrightarrow{n \rightarrow \infty} T$.
