limit point in the cofinite topology

Proposition

Let $\mathbb{R}$ be the real numbers with the cofinite topology and $(x_n)$ a sequence in $\mathbb{R}$. Then $x$ is a limit point of $x_n$ if and only if $x_n$ contains at most a constant subsequence consisting of $x$

Proof

other constant subsequence

Suppose there exists a constant subsequence $y$ with $y \neq x$. Then $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{y\}$ is a neighbourhood of $x$ but there exist infinitely many $x_n$ such that $x_n \not\in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{y\}$.

"no other constant subsequence" limit point

Let $O = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{y_1,..,y_m\}$ be a neighbourhood $x$. Then there exists only finitely many $x_{n_i}$ with $x_{n_i} = y_i$. Hence after finitely many steps, $x_n$ has to lie in $O$.
