index n in general not a normal subgroup

Proposition

Let $G$ be a group, $H \subseteq G$ a subgroup of index $n$ for $n > 2$. Then $H$ is in general not a normal subgroup

Proof

Consider

\begin{align*} S_{n-1} \hookrightarrow S_n \end{align*}

Then the index is

\begin{align*} \vert S_n : S_{n-1}\vert =& n! : (n-1)! \\ =& n \end{align*}

Furthermore,

\begin{align*} (1,...,n)(1,n-1)(n,...,1) =& (2,n) \not\in S_{n-1} \end{align*}

Hence $S_{n-1}$ is not a normal subgroup
