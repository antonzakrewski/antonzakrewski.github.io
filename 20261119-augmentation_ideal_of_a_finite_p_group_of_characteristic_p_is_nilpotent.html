augmentation ideal of a finite p-group of characteristic p is nilpotent

Proposition

Let $G$ be a finite p-group and $k$ a field of characteristic $p$. Then the augmentation ideal $I_G \subseteq k[G]$ is a nilpotent ideal.

Proof

Proof by induction, induction on $p$

induction basis

$G$ commutative

We show this for the case of $G$ commutative, which covers in particular $p = 0,1$. In that case, $k[G]$ is a commutative algebra and the $\vert G \vert$-frobenius endomorphism is well-defined

\begin{align*} k[G] \rightarrow& k[G] \\ \alpha g \mapsto (\alpha g)^{\vert G \vert} \end{align*}

i.e., extends additive and multiplicative.

But for this $\vert G \vert$ frobenius endomorphism also factors as

\begin{align*} k[G] \rightarrow k \rightarrow k[G] \end{align*}

since $g^{\vert G \vert} = 1$.

In particular, this shows that $(I_G)^{\vert G \vert} = 0$ and hence $I_G$ is contained in the nilradical. As $I_G$ is finitely generated and $k[G]$ is commutative, this implies that $I_G$ is a nilpotent ideal (cf. ideal finitely generated by nilpotent elements is a nilpotent ideal in a commutative ring).

induction step

choose a nontrivial normal subgroup $G'$ and consider the short exact sequence

Then we can apply $k[-]$ and get a short exact sequence living over $\mathbb{C}$d

and taking the kernel of the vertical map happens to give a short exact sequence of augmentation ideals

We claim that $I_G^{\vert G \vert} = I_{G}^{\vert G'} \cdot I_{G}^{\vert G/G' \vert} = 0$. So let $x_1 \cdot ... \cdot x_{\vert G \vert} = (x_{1} \cdot ... x_{\vert G/G' \vert }) \cdot (... \cdot x_{\vert G \vert})$ be a product of elements in $I_{G}$. Then we can project to $I_{G/G'}$ and get by induction hypothesis that $(x_{1} \cdot ... x_{\vert G/G' \vert }) = 1$ or equivalently that $(x_{1} \cdot ... x_{\vert G/G' \vert }), ... \in I_{G'}$. But the product of these elements lives in $I_{G'}^{\vert G' \vert}$ which is by induction hypothesis also zero.

Thus $x_1 \cdot ... \cdot x_{\vert G \vert} = 0$ which shows $I_G^{\vert G \vert} = 0$.
