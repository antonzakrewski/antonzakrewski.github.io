reflector sends an object back

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}$ be categories, such that $\iota = \mathcal{R} \mathcal{C} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}$ is a reflective subcategory with reflector $\mathcal{L}: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$. Then for $C \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$, it follows, that there exists a canonical isomorphism

\begin{align*} C \cong \mathcal{R}(\iota(C)) \end{align*}

Proof

since $\iota: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ is fully faithful, we conclude that there exists a natural isomorphism for hom functors

\begin{align*} \mathcal{R}(-): \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}}(-,-) \cong \mathcal{R}(\mathrm{Hom}(-,-)) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R}(-), \mathcal{R}(-)) \end{align*}

where

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{C}}}(-,-), \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R}(-), \mathcal{R}(-)): \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{Set} \end{align*}

Furthermore, there exists the natural isomorphism

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{R}(-), \mathcal{R}(-)) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{R}(-)), -) \end{align*}

of functors

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{}(\mathcal{L}(-),-), \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(-,\mathcal{R}(-)): \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{Set} \end{align*}

and hence by precomposition with $\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}: \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \mathcal{C}$ preserves the isomorphism, hence

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}(L(R(-)), -) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{R}(-), \mathcal{R}(-)) \end{align*}

as functors

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}(L(R(-)), -), \mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{R}(-), \mathcal{R}(-)): \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \times \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \rightarrow \mathcal{C} \end{align*}

hence composing those isomorphisms we get an isomorphism

and by fixing an $A$ through

\begin{align*} \mathcal{H}: \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \rightarrow& \mathcal{C}^{ \times} \times \mathcal{C} \\ C \mapsto& (C,A) (f) \mapsto& (f,\mathrm{id}_{A}) \end{align*}

also a natural iso

By Yoneda this natural transformation is given by an element in $f \in \mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{L}\mathcal{R}(-), A)$ and precomposition and therefore by precomposition with an isomorphism as isomorphism it follows, that $f$ is an isoa.d
