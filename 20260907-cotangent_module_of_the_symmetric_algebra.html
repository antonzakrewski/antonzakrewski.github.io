cotangent module of the symmetric algebra

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M$ an \(R\)-module. Then the cotangent module of $R \rightarrow \mathrm{Sym}_{R}(M)$ is given by $M \otimes_{R} \mathrm{Sym}(M)$.

Proof

follows as

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Der}(\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(M), -) \cong& \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{R}}(M,-) \\ \cong& \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(M)}(M \otimes_{R} \mathrm{Sym}_R(M),-) \end{align*}

A derivation $\partial: \mathrm{Sym}_{R}(M) \rightarrow N$ into an $\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(M)$ module is uniquely determined by the restriction to $M \subseteq \mathrm{Sym}_{R}(M)$. In particular, any \(R\)-module homomorphism $f: M \rightarrow N$ induces a derivation $\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(M) \rightarrow N$.

This map can inductively defined via the universal property of the tensor product using

\begin{align*} M \rightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,N) \\ m \mapsto (- \cdot f(m) + f(-) \cdot m) \end{align*}

where we use that the unit map is \(R\)-linear

\begin{align*} M \rightarrow \mathrm{Sym}_{R}(M) \end{align*}
