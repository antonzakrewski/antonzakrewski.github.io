chromatic number of the complement of the circle

Proposition

Let $\overline{C_n}$ be the complement of the circle graph. Then its chromatic number is given by

\begin{align*} \Chi(\overline{C}_n) = \mathrm{min}(1,\mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2})) \end{align*}

Proof

$n \leq 3$

then the circle graphs are complete, hence the complement is totally disconnected. Thus it has chromatic number $1$

$\Chi(\overline{C}_n) \geq \mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2})$, $n \geq 4$

We claim that its independence number is at most $2$ (even $2$). then use chromatic number and independence number to get the inequality suppose we have $3$ vertices and assume that one - namely the vertex $i$ - is not connected to the other. Then by construction the other vertices are necessarily $i-1,i+1$. since we assume $n \geq 4$ there has to be a fourth vertex $l$ with $l < i - 1$ or $i+1<l$. Hence $i-1,i+1$ are connected

$\Chi(\overline{C}_n) \leq \mathrm{floor}(\frac{n}{2})$

consider the coloring

\begin{align*} i \mapsto \mathrm{floor}(\frac{i}{2} + 1) \end{align*}

which is a coloring since $i$ is not connected to $i+1$
