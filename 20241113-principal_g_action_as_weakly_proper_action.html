principal g-action as weakly proper action

Proposition

Let $p: E \times G \rightarrow E$ be a principal G action for a topological group $G$. Then $p$ is weakly proper action

Proof

Let $e \in E$, then choose a neighbourhood $U_i$ of $E/G$ such that $e \in E/G$.

Then a neihgbourhood is up to compatible homeomorphism of the form

\begin{align*} U_i \times G \end{align*}

and it follows that the translation map restricts to

\begin{align*} (U_i \times G) \times (U_i \times G) \rightarrow& G \\ ((u_i,g), (u_i),g') \mapsto& g' \cdot g^{-1} \end{align*}

this is given by projecting to $G \times G$, taking the inverse on the left, switching and multiplying. As $G$ is a topological group, all of those maps are continuous.

Then the translation map is locally continuous and can be glued to a global map, as the maps are compatible on the intersection (the homeo $U_i \times G \rightarrow \mathrm{copr}^{-1}[?]$ is \(G\)-equivariant) topological space as colimit of an open covering
