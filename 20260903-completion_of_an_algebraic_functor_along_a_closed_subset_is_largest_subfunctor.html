completion of an algebraic functor along a closed subset is largest subfunctor

Proposition

Let $X$ be an algebraic functor and $K \susbeteq \vert X \vert$ a closed subfunctor. Then the completion of an algebraic functor $X_{K}^{\wedge}$ of $X$ along $K$ is the largest subfunctor of $X$ whose underlying space is $K$.

Proof

Recall that an R-point induces map from Spec(R), so let $Y$ be the algebraic functor with $Y(R) = \{x \in X(R) \vert \vert x \vert: \vert \mathrm{Spec}(R) \vert \rightarrow \vert X \vert \textrm{ restricts to } K \}$. Then $Y(R)$ is a Subfunctor, since any map $\varphi: R \rightarrow S$ gives a commutative diagram

hence for any $x \in Y(R)$ and any $\varphi: R \rightarrow S$, the element $\varphi(x)$ lies in $Y(S)$.

Now the underlying space of $Y$ maps to $K$: In fact any element $k \in K$ is represented by a map $\mathrm{Spec}(\kappa) \rightarrow X$ which then by assumption restricts to $K$ on underlying spaces, i.e. $\vert Y \vert \supseteq \vert K \vert$; similarly any element $y$ in $\vert Y \vert$ maps to $K$.

Let $Y'$ be any functor such that $\vert Y' \vert \subseteq K$. Then for any $y' \in Y'(R)$, we have construction $y' \in Y(R)$.

Now it remains to show that

\begin{align*} X^{\wedge}_{K}(R) =& \{x \in X(R) \vert I(x) = \sqrt{0}\} \\ \overset{!}{=}& Y(R) \end{align*}

for the quasi-coherent ideal classifying the open subfunctor corresponding to $O = \vert X \vert \setminus K$.

Now we have the following equivalent conditions

  1. $x \in Y(R)$
  2. $\mathrm{spec}(R) \rightarrow \vert X \vert$ restricts to $K$
  3. $x^{-1}[U] = \emptyset$
  4. the underlying space of $D(I(x))$ is the $\emptyset$ (cf. underlying space for a pullback with a monomorphism).
  1. $D(I(x))$ is the open subfunctor classified by the emptyset / nilradical
