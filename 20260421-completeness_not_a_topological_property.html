completeness not a topological property

Proposition

Let $(M,d)$ be a complete metric space and $(M',d)$ be any metric space. Suppose there exists a homeomorphism $M \simeq M'$, then $M'$ is in general not complete

Proof

observe that $\mathbb{R}$ is complete, but $(- \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$ (regarded as metric subspace of $\mathbb{R}$) is not, as we may choose any sequence, which (in $\mathbb{R}$) converges to $\frac{\pi}{2}$ and hence is in particular Cauchy. Now $\mathrm{tan}$ and $\mathrm{atan}$ are homeomorphisms between $\mathbb{R}$ and $(- \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$
