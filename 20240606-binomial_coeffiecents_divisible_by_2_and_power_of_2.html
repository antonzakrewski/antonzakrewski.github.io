binomial coeffiecents divisible by 2 and power of 2

Proposition

Proof

Consider the polynomial ring

\begin{align*} \mathbb{F}_2[x] \end{align*}

Suppose that

\begin{align*} (1 + x)^n = (1 + x^n) \end{align*}

Let $I \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ be a finite subset and

\begin{align*} n = \sum_{k \in I} 2^k \end{align*}

i.e. write $n$ in binary

Then

\begin{align*} (1 + x)^n = \prod_{k \in I} (1 + x)^{2^k} \end{align*}

as squaring is additive in $\mathbb{F}_2$ this shows that

\begin{align*} \prod_{k \in I} (1 + x)^{2^k} =& \prod (1^{2^k} + x^{2^k}) \\ =& \prod 1 + x^{2^k} \\ =& x^{\sum 2^k} \end{align*}

Note that $\sum 2^k$ is a unique expansion.

Since

\begin{align*} 1 + x^n = \sum X^{\sum 2^k} \end{align*}

this implies that $i$ contains only a single element

Therefore $n$ is a power of $2$
