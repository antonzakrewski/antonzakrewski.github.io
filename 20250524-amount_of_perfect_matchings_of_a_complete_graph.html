amount of perfect matchings of a complete graph

Proposition

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, then there exist

\begin{align*} \begin{cases} \prod_{i=1}^{\frac{n}{2}} (n - 2i + 1) & \mbox{if } n \mathrm{ even} \\ 0 & \mbox{if } n \mathrm{ odd} \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

perfect matchings in the complete graph $K_n$

Proof

odd case

  • perfect matching and even amount of vertices in a component

even case

We show an induction formula. Proof by induction

induction basis $n = 2$

Then the formula gives $1$, which is precisely what we want.

induction step

Let $1 \in V(K_n)$. Then there exist $n-1$ possible choices (the remaining vertices) - each symmetric - for matching $1$. Let $i_1$ be the matching with $1$.

Then it remains to construct a perfect matching on $K_{n} \setminus \{1,i_1\} = K_{n-2}$ hence
