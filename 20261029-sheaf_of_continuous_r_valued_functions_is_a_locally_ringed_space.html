sheaf of continuous, R valued functions is a locally ringed space

Definition / Proposition

Let $X$ be a topological space and $(X, \mathcal{C}_{X, \mathbb{R}})$ the sheaf of continuous functions. Then it is a locally ringed space

Proof

it is a sheaf and we claim that

\begin{align*} \mathfrak{m}_{X} = \{[(f,U)] \in \mathcal{C}_{X, \mathbb{R}, x} \vert f(x) = 0\} \end{align*}

is the unique maximal ideal of the stalk

It is well-defined, closed under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication and contains every non-invertible element. Suppose there exists an element $[f]$ wiht $f(x) \neq 0$, then there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such thta $f(U) \neq 0$. In particular, $f$ is invertible for $f^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ when restricted to $U$ and it is a preimage of $[f]$ under the map $\mathcal{C}_{X, \mathbb{R}}(U) \rightarrow \mathcal{O}_{X, \mathbb{R},x}$ of ringes, hence the image is also a unit.
