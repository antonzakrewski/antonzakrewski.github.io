borsuk-ulam theorem

Proposition

Let $fs S^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$ be a continuous map. Then there exists an $x \in S^n$ such that

\begin{align*} f(-x) = f(x) \end{align*}

Proof

by singular homology

Assume that for arbitrary $x \in S^n$ it holds, that

\begin{align*} f(x) \neq f(-x) \end{align*}

Then the map

\begin{align*} g: S^n \rightarrow& S^{n-1} \\ x \mapsto& \frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{\| f(x) - f(-x) \|} \end{align*}

is continuous and satisfies

\begin{align*} g(-x) = -g(x) \end{align*}

Thus $g$ is an equivariant map with respect to $C_2 = \{1,-1\}$

$n = 1$

Note that $S^1$ is connected, hence by image of a connected set connected $\mathrm{im}(g) \subseteq S^0 = \{1\} \coprod \{-1\}$ is connected. But since $g$ is \(C_2\)-equivariant, we conculde, that $\mathrm{im}(g) = S^0$, contradiction

$n \geq 2$

Proof by contraposition

Suppose $f(x) \neq f(-x)$ for all $x \in S^n$. Assume that $n \geq 2$

Consider

\begin{align*} g: S^n \rightarrow& S^{n-1} \\ x \mapsto& \frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{\vert f(x) - f(-x) \vert} \end{align*}

Then

\begin{align*} g(-x) =& =& -g(x) \end{align*}

Hence there exists an induced map

\begin{align*} h: \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \end{align*}

\begin{align*} g(\tilde{\lambda}(0)) = g(- \tilde{\lambda}(1)) \\ =& g(\lambda(1)) \end{align*}
