universal property of the semidirect product

Proposition

Let $\varphi: N \rightarrow \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Grp}}(H)$ be a group-homomorphism and $H \ltimes N$ the semidirect product (sitting in $H \rightarrow H \ltimes N \rightarrow N$). Then there exists a canonical bijection between

  1. pairs of group-homomorphisms $f: N \rightarrow G,g: H \rightarrow G$ such that $g(\varphi_n(h)) = f(n) g(h) f(n^{-1})$
  2. group-homomorphisms $\psi: H \ltimes N \rightarrow G$

given by

\begin{align*} (f: N \rightarrow G,g: H \rightarrow G) \mapsto (H \ltimes N \rightarrow G, (h,n) \mapsto f(n) \cdot g(h)) \\ (\psi: H \ltimes N \rightarrow G) \mapsto (\psi_{\vert N}: N \rightarrow G, \psi_{\vert H}: H \rightarrow G) \end{align*}

where under the (implicit) splitting we may regard $N$ as subgroup of $H \ltimes N$

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Define the map as indicated

\begin{align*} H \ltimes N \rightarrow& G \\ (h,n) \mapsto& f(n) \cdot g(h) \end{align*}

Then we may calculate

\begin{align*} (g \ltimes f)((h,n), (h',n')) =& (g \ltimes f)(h \cdot \varphi_n(h), n \cdot n') \\ =& g(h) \cdot g(\varphi_n(h')) \cdot f(n) \cdot f(n') \\ =& g(h) \cdot f(n) \cdot g(h) \cdot f(n^{-1}) \cdot f(n) \cdot f(n') \\ =& g(h) \cdot f(n) \cdot g(h) \cdot f(n') \\ =& (g \ltimes f)((h,n)) \cdot (g \ltimes f)(h',n') \end{align*}

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let $\psi: H \ltimes N $\rightarrow G$ be a homomorphism. Then define as above

\begin{align*} \psi_{\vert N}: N \rightarrow& G \\ n \mapsto& \psi(1,n) \\ \psi_{\vert H}: H \rightarrow& G h \mapsto \psi(h,1) \end{align*}

where

Note that in particular $\psi(n,h) = \psi((n,1) \cdot (1,h))$ so in particular the construction (if welldefined) is bijective So it remains to show that

\begin{align*} \psi_{\vert H}(\varphi_n(h)) = \psi_{\vert N}(n) \psi_{\vert H}(h) \psi_{\vert N}(n^{-1}) \end{align*}

Here we note that

\begin{align*} (\varphi_n(h), 1) =& (\varphi_n(h), n) \cdot (1, n^{-1}) \\ =& (1,n) \cdot (h,1) \cdot (1, n^{-1}) \end{align*}

and hence as $\psi$ is a group homomorphism

\begin{align*} \psi_{\vert H}(\varphi_n(h)) =& \psi(\varphi_n(h),1) \\ =& \psi((1,n) \cdot (h,1) \cdot (1,n^{-1})) \\ =& \psi(1,n) \cdot \psi(h,1) \cdot \psi(1,n^{-1}) \\ =& \psi_{\vert N}(n) \cdot \psi_{\vert H}(h) \cdot \psi_{\vert N}(n^{-1}) \\ \end{align*}
