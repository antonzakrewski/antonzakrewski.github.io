artins theorem

Proposition

Let $G$ be a finite group and $\mathcal{X}$ a set of subgroups of $G$ closed under conjugation, i.e. for $H \in \mathcal{X}$ and $g \in G$, we have $g H g^{-1} \in \mathcal{X}$. TFAE:

  1. any element $g \in G$ is an element of some $H \in \mathcal{X}$
  2. for every finite dimensional $\mathbb{C}[G]$ representation, $\chi_{V}$ is a linear sum with rational coefficients of characters $\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(W)}$ where $H \in X$ and $W$ is simple.

2') for every finite dimensional $\mathbb{C}[G]$ representation, $\chi_{V}$ is a linear sum with $\mathbb{C}$ coefficients of characters $\chi_{\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(W)}$ where $H \in X$ and $W$ is simple.

  1. the map of representation rings $\mathrm{Ind}: \bigoplus_{H \in \mathcal{X}} R(H) \rightarrow R(G)$ has finite cokernel
  2. the map $\mathrm{Ind}: \bigoplus_{H \in \mathcal{X}} R(H) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \rightarrow R(G) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}$ is surjective

Proof

2) $\implies$ 1)

Suppose there exists a $g \in G$ such that $g \not\in H$ for all $H \in \mathcal{X}$. Then frobenius formula over C

\begin{align*} \chi_{\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(v)}(g) = \sum \chi(x^{-1} g x) = 0 \end{align*}

for all $H \in \mathcal{X}$ and $V$ a simple of $H$.

But then by 2), $\chi_{W}(g) = 0$ for any finite dimensional G-representation $W$. But this contradicts the fact that characters of simple \(G\)-representations span $\mathrm{Cl}(G,k)$, so e.g. any nonzero, constant class function.

1) $\implies$ 2)

Suppose that $U$ is a virtual representation of $G$ such that $\langle \chi_{U}, \chi_{\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(V)}\rangle = 0$ for all $H \in \mathcal{X}$ and all simples $V$ of $H$. Then

\begin{align*} \langle \chi_{U_{\vert H}}, \chi_{V}\rangle = 0 \end{align*}

for all simples $\chi_{V}$. So orthogonality of simple characters implies that $\chi_{U_{\vert H}} = 0$. But then $\chi_{U}$ is identically zero by i.

2) $\implies$ 3)

We have $R[G] = \bigoplus_{S_i} \mathbb{Z}$ a finite direct sum.

Hence there exsits a $d$ such that $d S_i \in \mathrm{Im}(I)$. So $R(G)/\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Ind}))$ is a quotient of $\bigoplus_{S_i} \mathbb{Z}/ d_i \mathbb{Z}$ so it is finite.
