reduction modulo p in general not necessary for irreducibility

Proposition

Let $R$ be a UFD, $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq R$ a prime ideal and $f = \sum_{i=0}^{n} \alpha_i X^i$ a polynomial such that $\alpha_n \not\in \mathfrak{p}$ Then

Proof

Consider $\mathbb{Z}[X]$ with $x^2 + 2$. Then by irreducible polynomial of degree less than 3 and root, it follows, that $x^2 + 2$ is irreducible.

But reduction modulo $2$ results in $x^2 = x \cdot x$, which is not irreducible.
