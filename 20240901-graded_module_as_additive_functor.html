graded module as additive functor

Proposition

Let \(R = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}_0} R_n\) be a graded ring and $M = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}_0$ a graded module. Then $M$ is equivalently described as additive functor

\begin{align*} M: BGR \rightarrow& \mathrm{Ab} \\ m \mapsto& M_m \\ (r: m \rightarrow n) \mapsto& (r \cdot: M_m \rightarrow M_n) end{align*} see: - [[id:c2ada5de-6f39-4851-a087-f801f1f91283][categorification of a graded ring]] * Proof ** abelian group Note that each $M_m$ is an [[id:7281c9cf-564f-4c4c-8c3f-fc1424ac85f8][abelian]] [[id:b6023bc8-458a-44e4-982a-6623378f4b4f][group]]. Hence also the $\bigoplus M_m$ ** module Let $r = \sum r_i \in R = \bigoplus R_m$. Then for $m = \sum m_j$ define \begin{align*} r \cdot m = \sum \sum \mathcal{L}_j(r_i) m_j \in \bigoplus M_m = M \end{align*}

unit

Note that $\mathcal{L}_j(1) = \mathrm{id}$

compatibly graded

Note that for $r_i \in R_i, m_j \in M_j$ we have

\begin{align*} M(\mathcal{L}(r_i)) \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ab}}(M_j, M_{j+i}) \end{align*}

hence $r_i \cdot m_j \in M_{j+1}$
