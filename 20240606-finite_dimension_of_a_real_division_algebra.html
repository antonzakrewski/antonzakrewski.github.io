dimension of a finite dimensional real division algebra

Definition

Let $\mu: \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$ a real division algebra Then $n = 2^k$

Proof

As $\mu(-,-)$ is bilinear, it is continuous (cf. bilinear map as continuous map)

Then there exists

\begin{align*} f: S^{n-1} \times S^{n-1} \rightarrow& S^{n-1} \\ (x,y) \mapsto& \frac{\mu(x,y)}{\| \mu(x,y) \|} \end{align*}

Let $p: S^{n-1} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ be the projection. Then

\begin{align*} f(-x,y) =& \frac{\mu(-x,y)}{ \| \mu(-x,y) \|} \\ =& - \frac{\mu(x,y)}{\| \mu(x,y) \|} \\ =& - f(x,yr \end{align*}

and similarly

\begin{align*} f(x,-y) = - f(x,y) \end{align*}

Therefore we get an induced map $g: \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$

Let

\begin{align*} \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \cong \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \times \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \end{align*}

Let $[ \lambda] \neq 0 \in \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1})$ and $c$ the constant loop at the basepoint

Let

\begin{align*} \tilde{\lambda}: [0,1] \rightarrow S^{n-1} \end{align*}

be the unique lift of $\tilde{\lambda}$ along $p$.

Then

\begin{align*} f(\tilde{\lambda}(0), c(0)) = \frac{\mu(\tilde{\lambda}(0), c(0))}{\| \mu(\tilde{\lambda}(0), c(0) \|} = \frac{\mu(- \tilde{\lambda}(1), c(1)}{\| \mu(- \tilde{\lambda}(1), c(1) \|} = - f(\tilde{\lambda}(1), c(1)) \end{align*}

using an analogous argument shows that it maps a generator to

Therefore the induced map on the product is given by

\begin{align*} \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \cong \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \times \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \rightarrow \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \end{align*}

This gives

\begin{align*} H_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \oplus H_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \rightarrow H_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) \end{align*}

using the universal coefficient theorem

As the choices of basepoints induces inculsion maps

\begin{align*} \iota_0: \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \\ (x) \mapsto& (x,b) \iota_1: \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} \\ (x) \mapsto& (b,x) \end{align*}

Those induce maps on cohomology

Let $w \neq 0$ with $w \in H^1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1})$ a generator

Then

\begin{align*} H^1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}, \mathbb{F}_2) \otimes H^1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}, \mathbb{F}_2) \oplus H^0(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}, \mathbb{F}_2) \otimes H^1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}, \mathbb{F}_2) \end{align*}

This shows that

\begin{align*} g^*(w) =& w \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes w \end{align*}

As $g^*$ is multiplicative, it follows that

\begin{align*} 0 =& g^*(w^n) \\ =& g^*(w)^n \\ =& (w \otimes p + 1 \otimes w)^n \\ =& \sum_{j=0}^n \binom_{n}{j} w^j \otimes w^{n-j} \\ =& \sum_{j=1}^{n-1} \binom_{n}{j} w^j \otimes w^{n-j} \\ \end{align*}

since $w^n = 0$

Therefore all binomial coefficients $\binom{n}{j}$ are zero for all $1 \leq j \leq n-1$.
