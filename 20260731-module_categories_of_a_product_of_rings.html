module categories of a finite product of rings

Proposition

The functor from commutative rings to modules in Ab for extension of scalars preserves finite products

Proof

Let $R_1,...,R_n$ be commutative rings and $\prod_{i = 1}^n R_i$ the product. Then we need to show that the canonical map

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Mod}_{\prod_{i = 1}^n R_i} \rightarrow \prod_{i=1}^n \mathrm{Mod}_{R_i} \end{align*}

is an equivalence

Then take $e_1,...,e_n$ where $e_j = (0,0,...,1,0,...,0)$. In particular, $\sum_{i=1}^n e_i = 1$ and $(\prod_{i = 1}^n R_i)_{e_j} = R_j$ (cf. idempotent elements and product of rings and localization of an idempotent element). Moreover, $e_{i,j} = \partial_{i,j}$ and hence by zariski descent for modules we get that the further terms vanish as $\mathrm{Mod}_{R_{0}} = \mathrm{Mod}_{0}$ is the terminal category.
