integral domain integral over a field and field

Proposition

Let $k$ be a field, $A$ an integral k-Algebra.

TFAE:

  1. $A$ is a field
  2. $A$ is an Integral domain

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

corollary of field as integral domain

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let $\alpha \in A \setminus \{0\}$ then it remains to show that there exists a multiplicative inverse. Since $\alpha$ is integral over $k$ there exists an irreducible polynomial (cf. integral element in an integral domain and irreducible polynomial)

\begin{align*} f = \sum \alpha_i X^i \end{align*}

Since $f$ is irreducible we may assume that $\alpha_0 \neq 0$.

Thus consider

\begin{align*} \alpha_0 =& \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i (\alpha)^i \\ =& \alpha \cdot \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i (\alpha)^{i-1} \end{align*}

Therefore

\begin{align*} \frac{1}{\alpha_0} \cdot \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i (\alpha)^{i-1} \end{align*}

is an inverse
