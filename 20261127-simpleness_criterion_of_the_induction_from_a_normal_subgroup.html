simpleness criterion for the induction from a normal subgroup

Proposition

Let $G$ be a finite group and $H$ a normal subgroup Let $W$ be a representation of $H$ .

TFAE:

  1. $\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(W)$ is a simple
  2. $W$ is a simple representation and for $s \in G \setminus H$, $\mathrm{conj}_{s}(W)$ and $W$ are non isomorphic

Proof

special case of

  • simpleness criterion for the induction of a group, where normality of $H$ implies that $H_s = H \cap s H s^{-1} = H$.

Now therefore the criterion is that no simple representation of $H$ occurs as a summand of $\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{H}(\mathrm{conj}_{s}(W)) = \mathrm{conj}_{s}(W)$ and $\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{H}(W)$. Since $W$ is assumed or implied to be a simple repreesntation, this is equivalent to saying that $\mathrm{conj}_{s}(W)$ and $W$ are non isomorphic, since any nontrivial summand of $W$ would be $W$ itself.

Remark

$\mathrm{conj}_{s}(-)$ is an isomorphism of categories, so it preserves and detects simpleness.
