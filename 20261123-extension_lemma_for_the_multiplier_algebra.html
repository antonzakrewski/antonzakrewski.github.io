extension lemma for the multiplier algebra

Proposition

Let $\varphi: A \rightarrow M(B)$ be a non degenerate star homomorphism. Then there exists a unique unital $\overline{\varphi}: M(A) \rightarrow M(B)$.

Moreover, $\overline{\varphi}$ is strictly continuous (on bounded subsets).

Proof

uniqueness

Suppose there exists an extension, then for $m \in M(A)$

Then

\begin{align*} \overline{\varphi(m)} \varphi(a) b =& \overline{\varphi}(') \overline{\varphi}(a) b \\ =& \overline{\varphi}(m a) b \\ =& \varphi(m a) b \end{align*}

which shows uniqueness by denseness

existence

Let $\overline{\varphi}(m) = (L_m, R_m)$. Then

\begin{align*} L_m(\varphi(a) b) \overset{!}{=} \varphi(m a) b \end{align*}

We want to define

\begin{align*} L_m(\sum_{i} \varphi(a_i) b) \coloneqq \sum_{i} \varphi(m a_i) b_i \end{align*}

Lte $e_{\lambda}$ be an approximate unit of $A$. Then

\begin{align*} \sum_{i} \varphi(m a_i) b_i =& \lim_{\lambda} \sum_{i}\varphi(m e_{\lambda} a_i) b_i \\ =& \lim_{\lambda} \sum_{i} \varphi(m e_{\lambda}) \varphi(a_i) b_i \\ =& \lim_{\lambda} \varphi(m e_{\lambda}) (\sum_{i} \varphi(a_i) b_i) \end{align*}

This shows that $L_m$ is linear and well-defined.

It follows that $\| L_m \| \leq \| m \|$ and then we can extend to $B$.

Now define

\begin{align*} R_{m}(\sum_{i} b \varphi(a_i)) \coloneqq \sum b \varphi(a_i m) \end{align*}

strictly continuous (on norm bounded subsets)

Let $m_{\nu} \rightarrow m$ conevrge strictly in $M(A)$. Then we need to show that $\overline{\varphi}(m_{\nu}) \rightarrow \overline{\varphi}(m)$. we can apply this to $\varphi(a) \cdot b$ and egt

\begin{align*} \overline{\varphi}(m_{\nu}) (\varphi(a) b) =& \varphi(m_{\nu} a) b \\ \rightarrow& \varphi(m a) b \\ =& \overline{\varphi}(m) \varphi(a) b \end{align*}

Now since $m_{\nu}$ is bounded, we get pointwise convergence.
