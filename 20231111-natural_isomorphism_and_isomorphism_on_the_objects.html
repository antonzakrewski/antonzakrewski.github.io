natural isomorphism and pointwise isomorphism

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}$ be categories, $\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ be functors and $\eta: \mathcal{F} \rightarrow \mathcal{G}$ a natural transformation.

TFAE:

  1. $\eta$ is a natural isomorphism with a canonical inverse
  2. for $A \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$ it holds, that $\eta(A): \mathcal{F}(A) \rightarrow \mathcal{G}(A)$ is an isomorphism in $\mathcal{D}$

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

By assumption, there exists an $\eta^{-1}: \mathcal{G} \rightarrow \mathcal{F}$, such that

\begin{align*} \eta \circ \eta^{-1} =& \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}} \\ (\eta \circ \eta^{-1})(A) =& \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}(A)} \end{align*}

Analougosly for \eta^{-1} \circ \eta =& \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}} \\ (\eta^{-1} \circ \eta)(A) =& \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let

\begin{align*} \eta^{-1}: \mathcal{G} \rightarrow& \mathcal{F} \\ A \mapsto& (\eta(A))^{-1}: \mathcal{G}(A) \rightarrow \mathcal{F}(A) \end{align*}

be the unique inverse morphism.

Then following diagram commutes,

hence

\begin{align*} \mathcal{G}(f) \circ \eta(A) =& \eta(B) \circ \mathcal{F} \end{align*}

Thus following diagram commutes 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
A \arrow[]{d}{f} & & \arrow[bend left]{rr}{\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}(A)} } \mrthcal{F}(A) \arrow[]{d}{\mathcal{F}(f)} \arrow[]{r}{\eta(A)} & \mathcal{G}(A) \arrow[]{r}{\eta(A)^{-1}} \arrow[]{d}{\mathcal{G}(f)}&  \mathcal{F}(A) \arrow[]{d}{\mathcal{F}(A)}\\
B & & \arrow[bend right]{rr}{\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F(B)}} }\mathcal{F}(B) \arrow[]{r}{\eta(B)} & \mathcal{G}(B) \arrow[]{r}{\eta(B)^{-1}} & \mathcal{F}(B) \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

hence since $\eta(A)$ is an isomorphism and thus especially also an epimorphism

by diagram and pasting epimorphism, it follows that $\eta^{-1}$ is natural
