connectivity of a complete bipartite graph

Proposition

Let $n,m \geq 2$, then the complete bipartite graph $K_{n,m}$ has

  1. knot connectivity $\mathrm{min}(m,n)$
  2. edge connectivity $\mathrm{min}(m,n)$
  3. minimal degree $\mathrm{min}(m,n)$

Proof

Let $d \coloneqq \mathrm{min}(m,n)$, w.l.o.g. $

1)

Note that removing $d$ knots - $d_1$ and $d_2$ many from the part with $n$ resp. $m$ many vertices - gives

\begin{align*} K_{n-d_1,m - d_2} \end{align*}

where we interpret $K_{0,l}$ as $l$ disjoint vertices.

Then removing $d$ from the part with $d$ many vertices shows that $K_{n,m}$ is at most $d$ knot connected. Removing less than $d$ gives $K_{n',m'}$ with $n',m' > 0$, in particular a connected graph. Hence it $d$ knot connected.

2)

w.l.o.g. $n < m$. Removing all outgoing edges of a vertex in the $m$ part shows that $K_{n,m}$ is at most $n = d$ edge connected. By knot connectivity smaller than edge connectivity this shows that it is $d$ edge connected

3)

Let $v \in V(K_{n,m})$ be a vertex. Depending where $v$ lies it has degree $n$ or $m$. Hence the minimal degree is given by the minimum.
