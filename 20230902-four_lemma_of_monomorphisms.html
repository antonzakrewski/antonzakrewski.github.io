Four-Lemma of modules of monomorphisms

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an abelian category and the following commutative diagram in $\mathcal{A}$

where $m,p$ are monomorphisms and $l$ is an epimorphisms. Suppose the rows are exact, then $n$ is a monomorphism

Proof

w.l.o.g. for $\mathcal{A} = \mathrm{RMod}$, the general case follows from Mitchell's embedding theorem.

Let $c \in \mathrm{ker}(n)$. Then it remains to show, that $c = 0$.

Then by commutavity, we get

\begin{align*} 0 =& f_3'(0) \\ =& f_3'(n(c)) \\ =& p(f_3(c)) \end{align*}

and since $p$ is a monomorphism, we get

\begin{align*} f_3(c) = 0 \end{align*}

or $c \in \mathrm{ker}(f_3)$.

Thus by exactness, there exists an $b \in B$ with $f_2(b) = c$.

Suppose w.l.o.g. that $b \neq 0$, otherwise $f_2(0) = c = 0$

Then by injectivity, $m(b) \neq 0$ with

\begin{align*} f_2'(m(b)) =& n(f_2(b)) \\ =& n(c) \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

and thus $m(b) \in \mathrm{ker}(f_2')$. Hence by exactness, there exists an $a'$ with $f_1'(a') = m(b)$ and by surjectivity of $l$ there exists an $a \in A$ with $l(a) = a'$.

Note that

\begin{align*} m(f_1(a)) =& f_1'(l(a)) \\ =& f_1(a') \\ =& m(b) \end{align*}

and thus $f_1(a) = b$.

Furthermore, since exactness implies

\begin{align*} f_2 \circ f_1 = 0 \end{align*}

it follows, that

\begin{align*} c =& f_2(b) \\ =& f_2 f_1 (a) \\ =& 0(a) \\ =& 0 \end{align*}
