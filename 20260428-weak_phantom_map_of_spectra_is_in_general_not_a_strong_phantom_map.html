weak phantom map of spectra is in general not a strong phantom map

Proposition

Let $\varphi: X \rightarrow Y$ be a weak phantom map of spectra. Then $\varphi$ is in general not a strong phantom map

Proof

consider the bockstein map $\mathbb{F}_2 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Sq}^1} \mathbb{F}_2[1]$. Then it is a weak phantom map, as $\mathbb{F}_2$ and $\mathbb{F}_2[-1]$ only have homotopy groups in degree $0$ and $1$, resp.

Let $\mathbb{S}[\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^2]$ be the suspension spectrum of the real projective space. Then let $\mathbb{S}[\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^2][-1] \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_2 \in \pi_{-1}(\mathrm{map}(\mathbb{S}[\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^2][1]),\mathbb{F}_2)$ be the nontrivial element. Then postcomposition with the bockstein map is given by the first steenrod square (cf. Sq1 is bockstein modulo 2).

But $\mathrm{Sq}^1(x) = x^2 \neq 0$ in $H^*(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^2,\mathbb{F}_2)$, which shows that the composition $\mathbb{S}[\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^2][-1] \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_2[1]$ is nontrivial.
