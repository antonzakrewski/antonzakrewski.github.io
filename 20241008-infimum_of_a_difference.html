infimum of a difference

Proposition

Let $X,Y \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be nonempty bounded subsets. Then the Infimum is given by

\begin{align*} \mathrm{inf}(X - Y) = \mathrm{inf}(X) - \mathrm{sup}(Y) \end{align*}

Proof

Note that for each element $z$ we find $x \in X, y \in Y$ with $z = x - y$ Hence as

\begin{align*} \mathrm{inf}(X) \leq x \\ -\mathrm{sup}(Y) \leq -y \end{align*}

it follows that

\begin{align*} z = x - y \geq \mathrm{inf}(X) - \mathrm{sup}(Y) \end{align*}

Hence $\mathrm{inf}(X) - \mathrm{sup}(Y) \leq \mathrm{inf}(X - Y)$.

To see the other direction we may choose sequences $x_n \rightarrow \mathrm{inf}(X), y_n \rightarrow \mathrm{sup}(Y)$ and consider

\begin{align*} x_n - y_n \end{align*}

which converges against $\mathrm{inf}(X) - \mathrm{sup}(Y)$ as $-$ is continuous.

Hence $\mathrm{inf}(X - Y) \leq \mathrm{inf}(X) - \mathrm{sup}(Y)$
