universal property of the mapping cone

Proposition

Let $f: X \rightarrow Y$ be a continuous map and $Z$ a topological space. Then maps out of the mapping cone $C(f)$ are precisely given by

  1. a map $g: Y \rightarrow Z$
  2. a point $z \in Z$
  3. and a nullhomotopy of $g \circ f$ to the constant map $\mathrm{const}_z$

Proof

Note that by definition of the mapping cone as a pushout, a map out of the mapping cone corresponds to

using the universal property mapping cylinder, a map out of $\mathrm{Cyl}(f)$ corresponds to a homotopy

\begin{align*} X \times [0,1] \rightarrow Z \end{align*}

where one end is given by the composition $X \rightarrow Y \rightarrow Z$

Here the other end is given by a constant map, since it factorizes through the point

\begin{align*} X \rightarrow \{*\} \rightarrow Z \end{align*}
