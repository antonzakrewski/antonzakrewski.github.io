analytic fredholm theorem

Proposition

Let $D \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be open and connected space, $\mathcal{H}$ a separabler Hilbert space. Let $f: D \rightarrow \mathrm{BL}(\mathcal{H})$ be a norm-analytic map such that $f(z)$ is a compact operator. Then either

  1. $(f(z) - \mathbbm{1})^{-1}$ does not exist for any $z \in D$.
  2. there exists a discrete (and hence countable) subset $S \subseteq D$ without Accumulation point in $D$ such that the mapping
\begin{align*} D \setminus S \rightarrow& \mathrm{BL}(\mathcal{H}) \\ z \mapsto& ( f(xz) - \mathbbm{1})^{-1} \end{align*}

is well-defined and norm-analytic map Moreover, $z \in S$ if and only if $f(z) \psi = \psi$ has a solution $0 \neq \psi \in \mathcal{H}$.
