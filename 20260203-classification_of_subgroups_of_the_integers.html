classification of subgroups of the integers

Proposition

Let $H \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ be a subgroup of the integers. Then $H = n \mathbb{Z} = \{z \cdot n \vert z \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ for some $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$.

Proof

more elementary

Assume w.l.o.g. that $H \neq 0 = 0 \mathbb{Z}$, then let $x \in H \setminus \{0\}$ be an element with minimal absolute value.

Then in particular $\vert x \vert \in H$ since either $\vert x \vert = x$ or $x = -x$ and $H$ is a subgroup, so w.l.o.g. assume that $x \geq 0$. We claim that $H = x \mathbb{Z}$:

Furthermore $(1 + 1 + ... + 1) \cdot x = z \cdot x \in H$ for any $z \in \mathbb{Z}$, so $x \mathbb{Z} \subseteq H$.

It remains to show the converse, so let $y \in H$ be arbitrary, w.l.o.g. $y > 0$. Then consider $z := y - x \cdot \mathrm{floor}(\frac{y}{x}) \in \mathbb{N}_0$ (which is precisely the remainder of "$\frac{y}{x}$"). In particular $y \in H, x \cdot \mathrm{floor}(\frac{y}{x}) \in x \mathbb{Z} \subseteq H$ so $z \in H$. Furthermore $z \in \{0,1,...,x-1\}$ and therfore $z = 0$, since otherwise $z$ would be an element with smaller absolute value in $H$, contradiction.

Hence $y = x \cdot \mathrm{floor}(\frac{y}{x})$ or $x \mathbb{Z} \supseteq H$.
