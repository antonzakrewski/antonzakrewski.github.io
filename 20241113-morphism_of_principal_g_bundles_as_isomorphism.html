morphism of principal G-bundles as isomorphism

Lemma

Let $p: E \rightarrow B, p' E' \rightarrow B$ be principal g-bundles and $\varphi: E \rightarrow E'$ a morphism of principal bundles Then $\varphi$ is an isomorphism

Proof

$\varphi$ as bijection

surjective

Let $e' \in E'$. Then consider $p'(e') \in B$. As $p: E \rightarrow B$ is surjective (cf. principal g-bundle as quotient map), we may choose an element $e \in E$ such that $p(e) = p'(e')$ As $G$ acts transitively on fibres (cf. fiber of a principal g-bundle as g-torsor), there exists an $g \in G$ such that

\begin{align*} \varphi(e) g = e' \end{align*}

As $\varphi$ is \(G\)-equivariant, it follows that $e \cdot g$ suffices as preimage.

injective

Assume that $e_0,e_1 \in E$ such that $\varphi(e_0) = \varphi(e_1)$. Then by commutativity of the diagram we conclude that

\begin{align*} p'(\varphi(e_0)) =& p'(\varphi(e_1)) \\ p(e_0) =& p(e_1) \end{align*}

But it follows that $\varphi_{p^{-1}(b)}$ is \(G\)-equivariant between $p^{-1}(p(e_0)), p'^{-1}(p(e_0)) \cong G$ Thus it is also injective, as for $g,g' \in G$ with $g \neq g'$ (under the identification of the fibres with $G$) it holds

\begin{align*} \varphi(g') = g' \cdot g^{-1} \cdot \varphi(g) \end{align*}

where $g' \cdot g^{-1} \neq 1$

$\varphi$ as homeo

  • glueing lemma for an open map

Pick a trivializing cover $U_i$ of $B$, such that $p,p'$ are trivial over $U_i$ (e.g. the intersection of a trivializing cover of $p$ with a trivializing cover of $p'$). Then

where $\varphi_{\vert p^{-1}[U_i])$ is (sloppily) identified with $U_i \times G \rightarrow p^{-1}[U_i] \rightarrow p'^{-1}[U_i] \rightarrow U_i \times G$

Then

\begin{align*} \varphi_{\vert p^{-1}(U_i)}: U_i \times G \rightarrow U_i \times G \end{align*}

corresponds to a non-equivariant map

\begin{align*} q: U_i \rightarrow G \end{align*}

as $\varphi$ is compatible with the projection of $U_i$ and equivariant.

Thus the map $\varphi_{\vert p^{-1}[U_i]}$ is given by

\begin{align*} (u,g) \mapsto (u,q(u)g) \end{align*}

This map has an inverse given by $(u,h) \mapsto (u,q(u)^{-1}h)$ where by Thus it is locally open onto its image. But $p'^{-1}[U_i]$ is open in $B'$, hence it follows that it is also locally open with respect to $B'$

Hence by glueing lemma for an open covering it is an open map
