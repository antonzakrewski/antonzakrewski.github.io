Proposition

there exists an K-Algebra $A$ such that for each natural number $n$, there exists a . Then there exists an representation $V$ of $A$ such that $\mathrm{dim}_{k}(V)$ is arbitrary large.

Proof

finite dimensional algebra

Let $A = k\{x,e_1,...,e_n \}/(x^{n+1} - 1, e_i^2 - e_i, x e_i - e_{i+1})$ be the free noncommutative algebra modulo the relations (where one has to read it as $xe_{n} = e_{n+1} = e_0$). It satisfies the relations by construction. (or do not quotient)

We claim that any nonzero element in $k^n$ generates $k^n$: For this let $v = \sum_{i=1} \alpha_i v_i$ with w.l.o.g. $\alpha_1 \neq 0$. Then $v_1 \in \langle v\rangle$ since $v_1 = \frac{1}{\alpha_1} e_1(v)$ and $v_n = x^{n-1} v_1$. Hence a k-basis of $k^n$ is generated.

infinite dimensional

Let $X$ be any set Then consider $k(X)$ the function field generated by $X$ and $k(X)$ as $k(X)$ module. Then $k(X)$ is a field, so any nonzero element generates $k(X)$ as $k(X)$ algebra, i.e., $k(X)$ is irreducible. But $k(X)$ has dimension at least $\vert X \vert$.
