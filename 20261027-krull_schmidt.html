krull schmidt

Proposition

Let $A$ be a K-Algebra. Then any finite dimensional representation $V$ of $A$ admits a decomposition $V = V_1 \oplus ... V_nq$ where each $V_i$ is indecomposable. Moreover, this decomposition is unique up to an isomorphism in $\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{Mod}_{A})$.

Proof

existence

follows from $\mathrm{dim}(V) < \infty$

uniqueness

Let $V = V_1 \oplus ... \oplus V_n$ and $V = V_1' \oplus ... V_m'$. Define $i_{s}: V_s \hookrightarrow V, i_{t}': V_t' \rightarrow V$ and $p_s: V \rightarrow V_s, p_t': V \rightarrow V_t$. Then

\begin{align*} \mathrm{id}_{V} = \sum_{s,t} i_{s} p_t \end{align*}

But we can also consider $\theta_t: p_i s_t' p_t' i_i: V_i \rightarrow V_i$. Then $\sum_{s} \theta_s = 1_V_1$ since we can use distributivity in both variables.

By the fitting lemma some $\theta_s$ must be an isomorphism Let asume $\theta_1$ is an isomorphism. Now $V_1' = \mathrm{im}(p_{1} i_{i}) \oplus \mathrm{ker}(p_1 i_{p'})$ so $V'$ is equal to one or the other.

Hence $V_1$ is isomorphism to $V_1$.

Now we can quotient by the direct summand and further argue inductively.
