Z equivariant isomorphisms on \bigoplus_Z Z

Proposition

Let $\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}$ be the sum with the $\mathbb{Z}$ action given by shifting the factors Then the $\mathbb{Z}$ equivariant automorphisms are precisely the maps

\begin{align*} f: \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow& \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z} \\ e_i \mapsto \mathrm{sgn}(f) e_{i + k} \end{align*}

for $\mathrm{sgn}(f) \in \{\pm 1\}$.

Proof

Observe that these maps are $\mathbb{Z}$ equivariant automorphisms. Now let $f: \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}} \rightarrow \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}$ be $\mathbb{Z}$ equivariant, then $f(e_{n+k}) = f(e_n)_{+k}$. Now assume that $f: \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}} \rightarrow \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}$ is an isomorphism. Hence let $v = (v_{i},...,v_{j}) \in \bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}$ be the vector such that $f(e_0) = v$ and w.l.o.g. $v_{i}, v_j \neq 0$ (we may assume this as $f$ is injective). We proceed to show that $i = j$: As $f$ is surjective, there exists an equation $\sum_{l=0}^k \lambda_l f(e_{m + l}) = e_0$ for $\lambda_l \in \mathbb{Z}$ and w.l.o.g. $\lambda_0 \neq 0, \lambda_k \neq 0$. Then the $i+m$ th component is precisely $\lambda_l \cdot v_i \neq 0$ and hence $i + m = 0$.

Now suppose that $j > i$ or $k > 0$. Then The \(m + k + j\)-th component is precisely $\lambda_l \cdot v_j = (e_0)_{m+k + j} = 0$ for $m + j + l > i + m$, contradiction to $\lambda_k, v_j \neq 0$. Hence we have $\lambda_l f(e_{m+l}) = e_0$.
