pointwise cauchy sequence of endomorphisms

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a Hilbert space and $T_n \in \mathrm{BL}(\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{H})$ a sequence such that for each $x \in \mathcal{H}$, the sequence $T_n(x)$ is Cauchy. Then there exists an $T$ such that $T_n \rightarrow T$ converges strongly.

Proof

Define

\begin{align*} T: \mathcal{H} \rightarrow& \mathcal{H} \\ x \mapsto \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} T_n(x) \end{align*}

It is well-defiend by assumption, linear by elementary computation and bounded by the uniform boundedness principle: The boundedness principle shows that $\mathrm{sup}_{n} \| T_n \| < \infty$ and hence

\begin{align*} \| T \| =& \sup_{\| x \| =1} \| T(x) \| \\ =& \sup_{\| x \| = 1} \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \| T_n(x)\| \\ \leq& \sup_{\| x \| = 1} \sup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \| T_n(x) \| \\ =& \sup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \sup_{\| x \| = 1} \| T_n(x) \| \\ =& \sup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \| T\| <& \infty \end{align*}

where we have used that suprema commute with suprema (or equivalently: coproducts commute with coproducts / colimits commute with colimits).
