local ring of regular functions at a point of an affine variety

Proposition

Let $K$ be a field, $\mathbb{A}_{K}^n$ the affine space and $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}_{K}^n$ be an affine variety. Then the ring of regular functions on $P$ is a local ring

Proof

  • local ring and non-units as maximal ideal

Let $\varphi = \frac{f}{g} \in \mathcal{O}_P^K$ be a regular function, w.l.o.g. with $g(P) \neq 0$. Then $\varphi$ is a unit if and only if $f(P) \neq 0$.

Assume that $f(p) \neq 0$, then $\frac{g}{f}$ is a regular function at $P$ and the inverse.

Otherwise assume that $\varphi$ is a unit, then there exists an inverse $\varphi'$ which is welldefined on $P$ then we may conclude by the evaluation homomorphism that

\begin{align*} 1 =& 1(P) \\ =& (\varphi \cdot \varphi)(P) \\ =& (\frac{f}{g} \cdot \frac{g'}{f'})(P) \\ =& \frac{f}{g}(P) \cdot \frac{g'}{f'}(P) \\ \end{align*}

showing that $f(P) \neq 0$.

The functions, which are zero at $P$, form clearly an ideal and are precisely the non-units.
