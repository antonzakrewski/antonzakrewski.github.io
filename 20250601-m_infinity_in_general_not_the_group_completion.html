M infinity in general not the group completion

Proposition

Let $M \in \mathrm{CMon}(\mathrm{An})$ be a anima valued commutative monoid. Then

\begin{align*} M_{\infty} \rightarrow M^{\mathrm{grp}} \end{align*}

is in general not an equivalence.

See: infinite group completion

Proof

Conside $\mathrm{Fin}$ as the Category FinSet with the cocartesian monoidal structure. Then let $\mathrm{Fin}^{\simeq}$ be the groupoid core functor of a symmetric monoidal categories. In particular

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Fin}^{\simeq} \simeq \coprod_{i =0}^n BS_n \end{align*}

where $S_n$ is the symmetric group.

consider $\{*\}$ which generates $\coprod_{i =0}^n BS_n$ (?) Then

\begin{align*} M_{\infty} = \mathbb{Z} \times BS_{\infty} \end{align*}

where $S_{\infty}$ is the infinite symmetric group.

Furthermore the multiplication map

\begin{align*} 1 \cdot - \end{align*}

is a functor which maps

\begin{align*} p \mapsto (\mathrm{id} \coprod p) \end{align*}

But this is not an equivalence (cf. equivalence of infinity categories and equivalence on connected components) since it is for example not essentially surjective on the mapping space - the mapping space is discrete and it fails to hit for example $(1,2)$. contradiction to multiplication in anima valued groups as equivalence on underlying anima
