fundamental theorem of group homomorphism

Proposition

Let $G$ be a group and $N \trianglelefteq G$ a normal subgroup with $\pi: G \twoheadrightarrow G/N$ as canonical projection to the quotient group Given another group and a group-homomorphism $\varphi: G \rightarrow H$ such that $N \subseteq \mathrm{ker}(\varphi)$, there exists a unique homomorphism $h: G/N \rightarrow H$ such that following diagram commutes

Proof

uniqueness

Suppose we have $\psi_1,\psi_2$ satisfying the condition. then we get

\begin{align*} \psi_1 \circ \pi = \psi_2 \circ \pi \end{align*}

Since $\pi$ is surjective, by surjective map as epimorphism we conclude, that $\pi$ is an epimorphism. Hence it follows, that

\begin{align*} \psi_1 = \psi_2 \end{align*}

existence

Let

\begin{align*} \psi: G/N \rightarrow& H \\ gN \mapsto& \varphi(g) \end{align*}

welldefined map

Let $mN = m'N$. Then there exists an $n \in N$ such that $g \cdot n = g'$. Therefore,

\begin{align*} \psi(g \cdot N) =& \varphi(m) \\ =& \varphi(g) \cdot 1 \\ =& \varphi(g) \cdot \varphi(n) \\ =& \varphi(g \cdot n) \\ =& \varphi(g') \\ =& \psi(g'N) \end{align*}

group homomorphism

\begin{align*} \psi(gN \cdot g'N) =& \psi(gg'N) \\ =& \varphi(g \cdot g') \\ =& \varphi(g) \cdot \varphi(g') \\ =& \psi(gN) \cdot \psi(g'N) \end{align*}

commutes

follows from construction
