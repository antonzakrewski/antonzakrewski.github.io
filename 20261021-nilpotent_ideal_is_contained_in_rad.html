Proposition

Let $I$ be a nilpotent ideal, i.e. $I^n = 0$. Then $I \subseteq \mathrm{Rad}(A)$ is contained in the Rad

Proof

Let $V$ be a simple representation and let $v \in V$ be nonzero. Then $I_{v} = \{av \vert a \in I\} \subseteq V$ is a subrepresentation. Suppose $I_v \neq 0$, then $I_v = V$ by simpleness of $V$.

But then there exists an $a \in I$ such that $av = v$, but then $a^n\neq 0$ for all $n$, contradicting the nilpotence of $I$.
