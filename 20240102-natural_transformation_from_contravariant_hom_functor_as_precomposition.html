natural transformation from contravariant hom functor as element

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a locally small category, $A \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$ an object and $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{Set}$ a contravariant functor and $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,A)$ the contravariant hom-functor. Then a natural transformation

\begin{align*} \eta: \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, A)\rightarrow \mathcal{F} \end{align*}

is naturally given by an element $\eta(id) \in \mathcal{F}(A)$, hence

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, A), \mathcal{F}) \rightarrow& \mathcal{F}(A) \\ \eta \mapsto& \eta(\mathrm{id}) \end{align*}

is an isomorphism

Proof

we show, that

construction

a)

\begin{align*} \varphi: \mathrm{NatTrans}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, A), \mathcal{F}) \rightarrow& \mathcal{F}(A) \\ \theta \mapsto& \theta(A) (\mathrm{id}_{A}) \end{align*}

b)

\begin{align*} \varphi^{-1}: \mathcal{F}(A) \rightarrow& \mathrm{NatTrans}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, A), \mathcal{F}) \\ u \mapsto& (\theta^u(-): \left(\alpha \mapsto \mathcal{F}(\alpha)(u) \right) \end{align*}

welldefined

a)

note that $\theta(A): \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,A) \rightarrow \mathcal{F}(A)$ is a morphism, hence map with $\mathrm{id}_{A}$ as element in the domain, thus

\begin{align*} \theta(A) (\mathrm{id}_{A}) \in \mathcal{F}(A) \end{align*}

b)

naturality

we get

since by construction, it holds, that

\begin{align*} \theta^u(X) = \theta^u(Y) \end{align*}

we may write (using sloppy notation) $\theta^u$

Hence it remains to show, that

\begin{align*} \theta^u \circ f^{ \rightarrow} = \mathcal{F}(f) \circ \theta^u \end{align*}

Since we are working in set, we may element chase, hence for $g: Y \rightarrow A$

\begin{align*} \theta^u \circ f^{ \rightarrow}(g) =& \mathcal{F}(f) \circ \theta^u(g) \\ \theta^u \circ g \circ f =& \mathcal{F}(f) \circ \mathcal{F}(g)(u) \\ \theta^u \circ (g \circ f) =& \mathcal{F}(g \circ f)(u) \\ \mathcal{F}(g \circ f)(u) =& \mathcal{F}(f \circ g)(u) \\ \end{align*}

where since $\mathcal{F}$ is contravariant, it holds, that

\begin{align*} \mathcal{F}(f) \circ \mathcal{F}(g) = \mathcal{F}(g \circ f) \end{align*}

bijection

a)

Given a natural transformation $\theta$, we get

\begin{align*} \varphi^{-1} \circ \varphi(\theta) =& \varphi^{-1}(\theta(A)(\mathrm{id}_{A})) \\ =& \theta^{\theta(A)(\mathrm{id}_{A})}: f \mapsto \mathcal{F}(f) \circ \theta(A)(\mathrm{id}_{A}) \end{align*}

Here by element chasing for $f \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(B,A)$ and pseudo commutativity of a natural transformation we get

\begin{align*} \theta^{\theta(B)(\mathrm{id}_{A})}(X)(f) = \mathcal{F}(f) \circ \theta_A(\mathrm{id}_{A}) \\ =& (\theta(X) \circ \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,A)(f))(\mathrm{id}_{A}) \\ =& (\theta(X) \circ f^{ \rightarrow})(\mathrm{id}_{A}) \\ =& \theta(X) \circ \mathrm{id}_{A} \circ f \\ =& \theta(X) \circ f \end{align*}

b)

for $u \in \mathcal{F}(A)$, it holds, that

\begin{align*} \varphi \circ \varphi^{-1}(u) =& \varphi(\theta^u) \\ =& \theta^u(A)(\mathrm{id}_{A}) \\ =& \mathcal{F}(\mathrm{id}_{A})(u) \\ =& \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}(u) \\ =& u \end{align*}
