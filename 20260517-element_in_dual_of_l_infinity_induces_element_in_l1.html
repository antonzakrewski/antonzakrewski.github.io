element in dual of l infinity induces element in l1

Proposition

Let $f \in (l^{\infty})^*$, then $(f(e_n))_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ lies in $l^1$ (cf. L unendlich, l1 of sequences).

Proof

Observe that $x = (\mathrm{sgn}(f(e_1) \cdot 1, \mathrm{sgn}(f(e_2) \cdot 1,...)$ is a bounded sequence (with $\|x\|_{\infty} = 1$) and of the form $x = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mathrm{sgn}(f(e_1) \cdot e_n$. Now $f$ is a bounded linear operator (in particular continuous), so $f(x) < \infty$. But we also have

\begin{align*} f(x) =& f(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathrm{sgn}(f(e_n) \cdot e_n) \\ =& \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathrm{sgn}(f(e_n)) \cdot f(e_n) \\ =& \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \vert f(e_n) \vert \\ =& \| (f(e_n))_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \|_{1} \end{align*}

which shows $(f(e_n))_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \in l^1$.
