riemann mapping theorem

Proposition

Let $\Sigma \subsetneq \mathbb{C}$ be open, nonempty and simply connected space. Then for every $z_0 \in \Omega$ there exists a unique conformal map $F: \Omega \rightarrow D_1(0)$ with

\begin{align*} F(z_0) =& 0 \\ F'(z_0) \in \mathbb{R}^+ = (0,\infty) \end{align*}

Proof

uniqueness

Assume $F,G: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{D}$ satisfy $F(z_0) = G(z_0) = 0, F'(z_0), G'(z_0) > 0$ then $H \coloneqq F \circ G^{-1}$ is a conformal automorphism of $\mathbb{D}$ that fixes the origin. Hence by pointed conformal automorphisms of the disk we get $H(-) = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \theta} w$ for some $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$.

This shows

\begin{align*} H(w) = e^{\mathrm{i}\theta} w \end{align*}

or

\begin{align*} H'(w) = e^{\mathrm{i} \theta} \end{align*}

Thus using hte Kettenregel & Ableitung der Umkehrfunktion

\begin{align*} 1 =& H'(0) \\ =& F'(G^{-1}(0)) \cdot (G^{-1})'(0) \\ =& F'(G^{-1}(0)) \>dot \frac{1}{G'(G^{-1}(0))} \\ =& \frac{F'(z_0)}{G'(z_0)} > 0 \end{align*}

Thus $F = G$

existence

reduction to $\mathbb{D}$

Let $\Omega \subsetneq \mathbb{C}$ be open, nonempty and simply connected space. Then there exists an open and simply connected $\tilde{\Omega} \subseteq \mathbb{D}$ with $0\in \tilde{\Omega}$ and a conformal map $F: \Omega \rightarrow \tilde{\Omega}$ such that $F(z_0) = 0$ and $F'(z_0) > 0$.

$\mathbb{D}$

Let $\Omega\subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be open with $0\in \Omega$. Then we show that

\begin{align*} s \coloneqq \mathrm{sup} \{\vert f'(0) \vert : f: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{D}, \mathrm{ conformal and} f(0) = 0\} \end{align*}

attained

Let $f_n$ be a maximizing sequence. Since $f_n(\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{D}$ we may conclude that $f_n$ is uniformly bounded on $\Omega$. Thusby montel theorem we may assume that $f_n$ uniformly converges $f_n \rightarrow f$ on compact subsets in $\Omega$.

In particular note that $f(0) = \mathrm{lim}_{n \rightarrow \infty} f_n(0) = 0$.

By the uniform convergence of holomorphic functions as holomorphic function we note that $f$ is holomorphic on $\Omega$ and we also have $f_n' $\rightarrow f'$ on compact subsets of $\Omega$ (cf. uniform convergence of holomorphic functions and derivative) and thus

\begin{align*} \vert f'(0) \vert = \mathrm{lim}_{n \rightarrow \infty} \vert f_n'(0) \vert = s \end{align*}

In particular $f'(0) \neq 0$.
