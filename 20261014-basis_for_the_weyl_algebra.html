PBW-basis theorem for the weyl algebra

Proposition

A basis for the Weyl algebra is given by $x^i y^i$ for $i,j \geq 0$.

Proof

First we show that the $x^i y^i$ span $W$. So supose $xyx = x(xy - 1) = x^2y - x$. You can prove that by applying the defining relations always decreases the length of a monomial in $k<x,y>$.

so it remains to show that this set is linearly independent. For this consider the following vector space $E = k[a][t,t^{-1}]$ and let $W$ act on $E$ by $xf =tf$ and $yf = \frac{df}{dt} f$ where this is the \(K[a]\) linear extension of $\frac{d t^n}{dt} = (a + n) t^{n-1})$.

So here we have

\begin{align*} (xy + yx) f =& t \frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d} t} - \frac{\mathrm{d} t f}{\mathrm{d} t} \\ =& t \cdot \frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d} t} - \frac{\mathrm{d} t f}{\mathrm{d} t} \\ =& t \cdot \frac{\mathrm{d} f}{\mathrm{d} t} - ( t \cdot \frac{\mathrm{d} f}{\mathrm{d} t} - \frac{\mathrm{d} t}{\mathrm{d} t} f) \\ =& -f \\ = -1 f \end{align*}

Suppose $\sum_{i,j} c_{i,j} x^i y^i = 0$ in the weil algebra. Then the operator $L = \sum_{i,j} c_{i,j} t^i (\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t})^j$ acts by zero. Now write $L$ as $\sum_{j}^{r} Q_j(t) \left( \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} t}\right)^j$ where $Q_j$ are polynomials and $r$ is choosen such that $Q_r \neq 0$.

Then $L(1) = \sum_{j=0}^n Q_j(t) a(a-1) (a - j + i) t^{-j} = 0$ in $k[a][t,t^{-1}]$. But then the leading term in the a's must be zero, so $Q_r(t) = 0$, hence $Q_j(t) = 0$.
