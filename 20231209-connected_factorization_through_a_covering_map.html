uniqueness of connected factorization through a covering map

Proposition

Let $E,B,Z$ be topological spaces, where $Z$ is connected, $p: E \rightarrow B$ a covering map and $f,g: Z \rightarrow E$ continuous maps such that there exists a $z \in Z$ with

\begin{align*} p \circ f(z) = p \circ g(z) \end{align*}

Then

\begin{align*} f = g \end{align*}

Proof

Proofsketch

using equalizer and equal maps it suffices to show, that $\mathrm{Eq}(f,g) = Z$: Here we show, that $\mathrm{Eq}(f,g) \subseteq Z$ is nonempty and clopen, hence by connectedness of $Z$ it follows, that $\mathrm{Eq}(f,g) = Z$

actual proof

Consider the equalizer (cf. category Top as complete category)

It is the subspace $\{z \in Z \vert f(z) = g(z)\}$ and nonempty because of $z \in \mathrm{Eq}(f,g)$.

closed

Consider the mediating morphism

\begin{align*} (f,g): Z \rightarrow& E \times_B E z \mapsto& (f(z), g(z)) \end{align*}

given by the universal property:

And

Here the equalizer is given as subspace

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Eq}(f,g) =& \{z \in Z \vert f(z) = g(z) \} \\ =& \{z \in Z \vert z \in (f,g)^{-1}[(e,e)] \} =& (f,g)^{-1}[(e,e)] \\ =& (f,g)^{-1}[\Delta(E)] \subseteq Z \end{align*}

by continuity of $(f,g)$ it suffices to show, that $\Delta E \subseteq E \times_B E$ is closed.

This is the case, as by separation of distinct points in a fibre and pullback and closed diagonal, we conclude that $\mathrm{Eq}(f,g) \subseteq Z$ is closed

open

Suppose $z \in \mathrm{Eq}(f,g)$, then there exists an open neighbourhoods $V$ of $f(z) = g(z)$ such that

\begin{align*} p^{-1}[p[V]] \cong V \times F \end{align*}

Hence it follows, that $p_{\vert V}$ is injective. Then $V' \coloneqq f^{-1}[V] \cap g^{-1}[V] \subseteq Z$ is an open neighbourhood of $z$.

Since $p_{\vert V}$ is injective and hence mono (cf. injective map as monomorphism) and since $f_{\vert V'}, g_{\vert V'}: V' \rightarrow V$ has compatible codomain, it follows, that

\begin{align*} f_{\vert V'} = g_{\vert V'} \Leftrightarrow p_{\vert V} \circ f_{\vert V'} = p_{\vert V} \circ g_{\vert V'} \end{align*}

Here it follows, from the assumption $p \circ f = p \circ g$, hence $\mathrm{Eq}(f,g) \subseteq Z$ is an neighbourhood of all points and thus open (cf. openness and neighbourhood for all points)

Thus $\mathrm{Eq}(f,g)$ is clopen and by connected space and clopen set we conclude, that

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Eq}(f,g) = Z \end{align*}

since it is by assumption nonempty

Thus by equalizer and equal maps, we conclude, that $f = g$
