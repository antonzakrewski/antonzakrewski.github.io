A1 invariant presheaf

Definition

Let $S$ be a scheme Then a presheaf $\mathcal{F} \in \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})$ is said to be $\mathbb{A}^1$ invariant, if for all $X \in \mathrm{Sm}_{S}$, the projection

\begin{align*} X \times \mathbb{A}^1 \rightarrow X \end{align*}

induces an equivalence

\begin{align*} \mathcal{F}(X) \rightarrow \mathcal{F}(X \times \mathbb{A}^1) \end{align*}

Remark

These are by definition the local objects with respect to $よ(X \times \mathbb{A}^1) \rightarrow よ(X)$.
