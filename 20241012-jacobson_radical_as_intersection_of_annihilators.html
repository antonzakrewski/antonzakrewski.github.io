jacobson radical as intersection of annihilators

Proposition

Let $R$ be a ring. Then the Jacobson radical is given by the intersection

\begin{align*} \mathfrak{J} = \bigcap_{M \textrm{simple}} \mathrm{Ann}(M) \end{align*}

of the Annihilators of simple modules

Proof

$\subseteq$

Let $r \in \bigcap \mathrm{Ann}(M)$ and $\mathfrak{m}$ an arbitrary ideal. Then $R/\mathfrak{m}$ is a simple \(R\)-module (todo) Furthremore

\begin{align*} r \cdot (1 + \mathfrak{m}) = r + \mathfrak{m} = 0 \end{align*}

as $r \in \mathrm{Ann}(R/\mathfrak{m})$ showing that $r \in \mathfrak{m}$

$\supseteq$

Assume that $\mathfrak{J} \cdot M \neq 0$, then choose an $m \in M$ with $\mathfrak{J} \cdot m \neq 0$. It follows that $\mathfrak{J} \cdot m = M$ as $M$ is a simple module. Thus there exists an $r \in \mathfrak{J}$ with $r \cdot m = m$ or $(r - 1) \cdot m = 0$.

Hence $(r - 1) \in \mathrm{Ann}(M)$ but as the annihilator is a strict ideal there exists some maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ containing $\mathrm{Ann}(M)$ and thus especially $(r-1)$. But $r \in \mathfrak{m} \supseteq \mathfrak{J}$ and $r - (r - 1) = 1$, contradiction
