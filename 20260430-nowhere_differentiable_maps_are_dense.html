nowhere differentiable maps are dense

Proposition

Let $N \subseteq C([0,1])$ be the subspace of continuous maps, which are not differentiable at any point. Then $N$ is dense

Proof

Let $A_n$ be subspace of pseudo n-lipschitz map. We claim that $N \supseteq C([0,1]) \setminus (\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n)$.

Let $f \not\in N$, i.e. assume that $f$ is differentiable at a point $x$ with differential $f'(x) < n$ for $n >> 0$. Then there exists an $\varepsilon > 0$ such that

\begin{align*} \vert f(x) - f(y) \vert \leq (n+1) \vert x - y \vert \end{align*}

for $y \in [0,1] \setminus (x - \varepsilon, x + \varepsilon)$ as $f$ can be linearly approximated at $x$ with sublinear error term.

Now consider the continuous map

\begin{align*} g: [0,1] \setminus (x - \varepsilon, x + \varepsilon) \rightarrow& \mathbb{R} \\ y \mapsto \frac{\vert f(x) - f(y) \vert }{\vert x - y \vert} \end{align*}

Which is welldefined since $x \not\in [0,1] \setminus (x - \varepsilon, x + \varepsilon)$. Moreover, $[0,1] \setminus (x - \varepsilon, x + \varepsilon)$ is compact, so $g$ attains a maximum $m$.

In particular,

\begin{align*} \vert f(x) - f(y) \vert \leq l \cdot \vert x - y \vert \end{align*}

for $l \in \mathbb{N}$ with $l > \mathrm{max}(m,n+1)$. In other words, $f$ lies in $A_l$ and hence $f \not\in C([0,1]) \setminus (\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n)$.

Now recall that $A_n$ is nowhere dense (i.e. $C([0,1] \setminus A_n$ is dense, cf. nowhere differentiable maps are dense & nirgends dicht und Komplement enthält offene dichte Menge) and that $C([0,1])$ is a complete metric space. So baires theorem shows that $C([0,1]) \setminus (\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n) = \bigcap_{n \in \mathbb{N}} C([0,1]) \setminus A_n$ is dense and hence in particular so is $N$.
