1-monad of an adjunction

Definition / Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}$ be categories and $\mathcal{L} \dashv \mathcal{R}$ an adjunction for a functors $\mathcal{L}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{R}: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$. Then there exists a monad on $\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{C})$ with

  1. the endofunctor category as strict monoidal category (as always)
  2. $T = \mathcal{RL}$ as object
  3. the unit $\eta: \mathrm{id} \Rightarrow \mathcal{RL}$ as unit of the monoid object
  4. the counit as multiplication after left and right whiskering: $T \otimes T = \mathcal{RL} \circ \mathcal{RL} \Rightarrow \mathcal{RL}$

Proof

follows from the Adjunction and triangle identity)
