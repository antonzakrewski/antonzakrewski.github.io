natural transformation as functor from the product with the interval category

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}$ be categories, $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ be functors.

Then a natural transformation $\epsilon$ is precisely defined by a functor

\begin{align*} \eta: \mathcal{C} \times \Delta^1 \rightarrow \mathcal{D} \end{align*}

making following diagram commutes

see: product category

Proof

natural transformation $\implies$ functor

We define

\begin{align*} \eta: \mathcal{C} \times \Delta^1 \rightarrow& \mathcal{D} \\ (A,0) \mapsto& \mathcal{F}(A) \\ (A,1) \mapsto& \mathcal{G}(A) \\ (f: A \rightarrow B, 0 \rightarrow 1) \mapsto& \mathcal{G}(f) \circ \epsilon(A) = \epsilon(B) \circ \mathcal{F}(f) \\ (f: A \rightarrow B, \mathrm{id}_{0}) \mapsto& \mathcal{F}(f) \\ (f: A \rightarrow B, \mathrm{id}_{1}) \mapsto& \mathcal{G}(f) \end{align*}

Note that by naturality $\mathcal{G}(f) \circ \epsilon(A) = \epsilon(B) \circ \mathcal{F}(f)$, hence the mapping is welldefined

Then by construction, $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}$ factor through $\mathcal{C} \times \Delta^1$

now about functorality of $\eta$:

identity

follows from

\begin{align*} \eta(\mathrm{id}_{A}, \mathrm{id}_{0}) =& \mathcal{F}(\mathrm{id}_{A}) \\ =& \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}(A)} \\ \eta(\mathrm{id}_{A}, \mathrm{id}_{1}) =& \mathcal{G}(\mathrm{id}_{A}) \\ =& \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}(A)} \end{align*}

composition

For composition within $\mathcal{C} \times \{0\}$ and $\mathcal{C} \times \{1\}$, this follows from functorality of $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}$

For $(f, 0 \rightarrow 1) \circ (g, \mathrm{id}_{0}) = (f \circ g, 0 \rightarrow 1)$ we get

where applying functorality of $\mathcal{F}$ results in

\begin{align*} \epsilon(B) \circ \mathcal{F}(f \circ g) = \epsilon(b) \circ \mathcal{F}(f) \circ \mathcal{F}(g) \end{align*}

For $(f, \mathrm{id}_{1}) \circ (g, 0 \rightarrow 1) = (f \circ g, 0 \rightarrow 1) $ we get

where applying functorality of $\mathcal{G}$ results in

\begin{align*} \mathcal{G}(f) \circ \mathcal{G}(g) \circ \epsilon(A) = \mathcal{G}(f \circ g) \circ \epsilon(A) \end{align*}

functor $\implies$ natural transformation

We define

\begin{align*} \epsilon(A) \coloneqq \eta((\mathrm{id}_{A}, 0 \rightarrow 1)) \end{align*}

Thus we get a diagram

Note that the Right half commutes, since the first half commutes. Furthermore, by assumption of factorization, we get $\eta(f, 0) = \mathcal{F}(f), \eta(f,1) = \mathcal{G}(f)$

therefore this construction satisfies naturality
