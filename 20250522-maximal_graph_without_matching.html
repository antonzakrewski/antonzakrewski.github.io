maximal graph without matching

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph without perfect matching and maximal with respect to this property. then

Proof

Let $C \subseteq G \setminus S$ be a connected component which is not a complete graph. Let $a,a' \in V(C)$ be different vertices such that $aa' \not\in E$. Now choose a minimal path $\gamma: a \leadsto a'$. By assumption $\gamma$ traveses through at least one other vertex $b \in V(C)$, let $b \neq a'$ denote the neighbour of $a$. Let $b'$ be the neighbour of $b$ (possibly $a'$)

Then $ab'\not\in E$ since $\gamma$ is minimal.

Now $G$ is maximal, i.e. $G \cup ab'$ contains a perfect matching $M_1$ with necessarily $ab' \in M_1$.

Furthermore there exists a vertex $v \in V$ with $bv \not\in E$. otherwise $b$ would be connected to each other vertex in $V$ and hence $b \in S, b \not\in C \subseteq V \setminus S$.

Now we may add the vertex $bv$ which provides another perfect matching $M_2$ with $bv \in M_2$.

Now consider a maximal path $Q \in G$ with $Q = e_1,...,e_l$ with $v \in e_1$ which is alternating $e_{2n+ 1} \in M_1, e_{2n} \in M_2$

Let $u = e_l$ be the last edge. Now do a case distinction

$u \in M_1$

Then there does not exist a matching $u$ to something in $M_2$. Thus $u = b$ or $u = v$, where we can exclude the possibility $u = v$ since the path is by assumption disjoint.

Now consider $C = Q \cup \{bv\}$

$u \in M_2$

Then there does not exist a matching $u$ to something in $M_1$. Then $u \in \{a,b'\}$

Now consider $C = Q \cup \{ub, bv\}$

In both cases we have an even amount of vertices in our circles.. Then apply the perfect matching and circle which provides a perfect matching
