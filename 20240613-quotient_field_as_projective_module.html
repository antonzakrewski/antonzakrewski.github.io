quotient field as projective module and original field as ring

Proposition

Let \(R\) be an Integral domain, $Q(R)$ the quotient field.

TFAE:

  1. $R = Q(R)$ is a field
  2. $Q(R)$ is projective

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

  • free module as projective module

2) $\implies$ 1)

Proof by contraposition

Suppose $Q(R)$ is projective, then by projective module and sum as free module there exists a free module $R^\kappa$ and

\begin{align*} Q(R) \otimes P' \simeq R^\kappa \end{align*}

then there exists a nontrivial map

by projection of a nontrivial component (note that $Q(R)$ is a field, hence not the zero ring)

Let $r \in Q(R)$ such that

\begin{align*} \varphi(r) \neq 0 \end{align*}

and $s \in R$ arbitrary. Then

\begin{align*} s \cdot \varphi(r) \cdot \varphi(s^{-1} \varphi(r)^{-1} s) =& \varphi(r) \end{align*}

As $R$ is an integral domain, this shows that $s = 1$ as $R$ is cancellable ring.

Since $s \in R \setminus \{0\}$ was arbitrary, it follows that $R$ is a field, hence $Q(R) = R$
