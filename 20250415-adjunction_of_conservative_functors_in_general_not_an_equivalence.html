adjunction of conservative functors in general not an equivalence

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}$ be infinity categories (or \(1\)-categories), $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{G}: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$ infinity functors and $\mathcal{F} \dashv \mathcal{G}$ an infinity adjunction Suppose both $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}$ are conservative functors.

Then neither $\mathcal{F}$ nor $\mathcal{G}$ are in general an equivalence

Proof

consider the free vector space adjunction.

  • free vector space functor as left adjoint
  • then an isomorphism betwee vector spaces is given precisely by a bijection of the canonical basis (of the free vector space)

But $\mathrm{Vect}_K$ and $\mathrm{Set}$ are clearly not equivalent, e.g. because $\mathrm{Vect}_K$ has a zero object.
