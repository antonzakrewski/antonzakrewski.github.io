unitary structure for a finite dimensional representation of a finite group

Proposition

Let $G$ be a finite group and $\rho: G \rightarrow \mathrm{Gl}_n(V)$ a finite dimensional representation. Then there exists a unitary structure on $V$ such that $\rho$ is an unitary representation.

Proof

Let $(-,-): V \times V \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ be any inner product, (e.g. after choosing an isomorphism $\mathbb{C}^n \cong V$). Then define

\begin{align*} \langle v,w\rangle \coloneqq \sum_{g \in G} \langle \rho_g(v), \rho_g(w) \rangle \end{align*}

Then the inner product is \(G\)-equivariant.
