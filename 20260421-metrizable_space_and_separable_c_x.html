metrizable space and separable C(X)

Proposition

Let $X$ be compact hausdorff and $C(X)$ the space of continuous maps with the Supremumsmetrik. TFAE:

  1. $C(X)$ is a separable space
  2. $X$ is metrizable space

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let $d$ be a metric. Then for $m,n \in \mathbb{N}$ define

\begin{align*} G_{mn} \coloneqq \{f \in C(X) : f(B_{\frac{1}{m}}(x)) \subseteq B_{\frac{1}{n}}(f(x)) \forall x \in X\} \end{align*}

As $X$ is compact, it follows that every $f$ in $C(X)$ is uniformly continuous, hence for each $n$ (and $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{n}$) there exists some $m >> 0$ such that $\frac{1}{m} \leq \delta$ and hence $f \in G_{mn}$. Therefore $C(X)$ is the union $\bigcup_{m} G_{mn}$

Moreover, for any $m$ in $\mathbb{N}$ we can find a $K_{m}$ in $\mathbb{N}$ and $a_1,...,a_{K_m}$ in $X$ such that $X$ can be written as union of open balsl of radius $\frac{1}{m}$:

\begin{align*} X = \bigcup_{k=1}^{K_m} B_{\frac{1}{m}}(a_k) \end{align*}

Now $\mathbb{K} \in \{\mathbb{C}, \mathbb{R}\}$ is separable, so there exists a countable dense subset $\{k_v \vert v \in \mathbb{N}\}$. For $m \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\varphi = (\varphi(1),...,\varphi(K_m))$ in $\mathbb{N}^{K_{m}}$ define

\begin{align*} G_{mn}^{\varphi} \coloneqq \{g \in G_{mn}: \vert g(a_k) - v_{\varphi(k)} \vert < \frac{1}{n}\ \forall k = 1,...,K_m} \end{align*}

We only want to consider those $\varphi$ with $G_{mn}^{\varphi} \neq \emptyset$ so we set $\Phi_{mn} = \{\varphi \in \mathbb{N}^{K_m}: G_{mn}^{\varphi} \neq \emptyset\}$.

Then $\Phi_{mn}$ is not empty (for example, the constant function lies in $G_{m,n}^{\varphi}$ for the constant $\varphi(-)$).

For every $\varphi \in \Phi_{mn}$ pick some $g_{\varphi} \in g_{mn}^\varphi$ and define $L_{mn} = \{g_{\varphi} : \varphi \in \Phi_{mn}\}$ and $L = \bigcup_{m,n} L_{mn}$. Then $L_{mn}$ is countable, because $\Phi_{mn} \subseteq \mathbb{N}^{K_m}$ and hence so is $L$.

We claim that $\overline{L}$ is $C(X)$. So let $f$ in $C(X)$ be arbitrary and $n$ in $\mathbb{N}$.

Then there exists some $m$ such that $f$ lies in $G_{mn}$. There also exists a $\varphi_{f} \in \Phi_{mn}$ such that $f$ lies in $G_{mn}^{\varphi_f}$ (as $\{k_v\}$ is dense, so we may choose sufficiently close points).

Hence for all $x$ in $X$ there exists some $a_{k_x}$ such that $x \in B_{\frac{1}{m}}(a_{k_x})$. Then take $g_{\varphi_f}$ in $L_{mn} \subseteq L$ as approximant. Since $f,g_{\varphi_f} \in G_{mn}^{\varphi_f}$ we have

\begin{align*} \vert f(x) - g_{\varphi_f}(x) \vert \leq& \vert f(x) - f(a_{k_x}) \vert + \vert f(a_{k_x}) - x_{\varphi_f(k_x)} \vert + \vert x_{\varphi_f}(k_x) - g_{\varphi_f}(a_{k_x}) \vert + \vert g_{\varphi_{f}}(a_{k_x}) - g_{\varphi_f}(x) \vert \\ <& \frac{4}{n} \end{align*}

as each term is smaller than $\frac{1}{n}$:

  1. $\vert f(x) - f(a_{k_x}) \vert$
  2. $\vert f(a_{k_x}) - x_{\varphi_f(k_x)} \vert$
  3. $\vert x_{\varphi_f}(k_x) - g_{\varphi_f}(a_{k_x}) \vert$
  4. $\vert g_{\varphi_{f}}(a_{k_x}) - g_{\varphi_f}(x) \vert$
