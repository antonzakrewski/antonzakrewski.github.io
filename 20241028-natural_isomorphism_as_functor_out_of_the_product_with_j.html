natural isomorphism as functor out of the product with J

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}$ be categories and $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ functors. Then a natural transformation $\eta: \mathcal{F} \rightarrow \mathcal{G}$ is a natural isomorphism if and only if it extends to a functor

\begin{align*} J \times \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{G} \end{align*}

restricting appropriately to $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}$ and $\eta$

Proof

follows from category cat as cartesian closed category and

\begin{align*} (J \times \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{G}) \sim (J \rightarrow \mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}) \end{align*}

and universal property of the isomorphism classifier
