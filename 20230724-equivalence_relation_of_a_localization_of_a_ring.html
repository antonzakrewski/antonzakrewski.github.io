equivalence relation of a localization of a ring

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $S \subseteq R$ a multiplicatively closed set. Then the relation

\begin{align*} (a,s_1) \sim (b,s_2) :\Leftrightarrow \exists s : s(a s_2 - b s_1) = 0 \end{align*}

is an equivalence relation

Proof

reflexive

for $1 \in S$, we conclude

\begin{align*} 1 \cdot (a s - a s) =& 1 \cdot 0 \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

symmetric

follows from Multiplication with $-1$:

\begin{align*} sas_2 - sbs_1 =& 0 && \vert \cdot -1 \\ sbs_1 - sas_2 =& 0 \end{align*}

transitive

It holds true:

\begin{align*} s(as_2 - bs_1) =& 0 \\ s'(bs_3 - cs_2) =& 0 \\ \end{align*}

there we conclude

\begin{align*} s(as_2 - bs_1) \cdot s's_3 =& 0 \\ ss' (as_2s_3 - bs_1s_3) =& 0 \\ s'(bs_3 - cs_2) \cdot ss_1 =& 0 \\ ss' (bs_1s_3 - cs_1s_2) =& 0 \end{align*}

and adding them together results in

\begin{align*} ss'( as_2 s_3 - bs_1s_3 + bs_1s_3 - cs_1s_2) =& 0 \\ ss'( as_2 s_3 - cs_1s_2) =& 0 \\ ss's_2( a s_3 - cs_1) =& 0 \end{align*}
