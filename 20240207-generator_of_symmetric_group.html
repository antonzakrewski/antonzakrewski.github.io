generator of a finite symmetric group

Proposition

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, then the symmetric group $S_n$ is generated by

\begin{align*} S_n = \langle (1,2), (1,2,3...,n)\rangle \end{align*}

Proof

Let $t = (1,2)$ and $c = (1,2,...,n)$ and

\begin{align*} G = \langle c,t\rangle \subseteq S_n \end{align*}

Then

\begin{align*} ctc^{-1} =& c (12) c^{-1} \\ =& (23) \\ c(2,3)c^{-1} =& (34) \end{align*}

or $(m,m+1) \in G$.

Furthermore,

\begin{align*} (1,2)(2,3) (1,2) =& (1,3) \\ (1,3)(3,4) (1,3) =& (1,4) \\ \end{align*}

and or in general $(1,m) (m, m+1) (1 m) = (1,m+1)$ and therefore $(1,m) \in G$

Since

\begin{align*} (1,i)(1,j)(1,i) = (i,j) \end{align*}

Hence by Darstellung einer Permutation als Produkt von Transpositionen we conclude, that

\begin{align*} G = S_n \end{align*}
