colimits in locales are computed in Pos^op or Cat^op

Proposition

Let $\mathrm{Pos}$ be the category of partially ordered sets. Then the inclusion $\mathrm{Loc} \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Pos}^{\mathrm{op}}$ preserves colimits

Proof

We show that limits in $\mathrm{Loc}^{\mathrm{op}} \subseteq \mathrm{Pos} \subseteq \mathrm{Cat}$ can be computed in either category: For $\mathrm{Pos} \subseteq \mathrm{Cat}$ this follows from category of partially ordered sets is closed under limits in Cat and moreover, limits in a limit category & colimits in a limit category imply that the limit of a locale is again a locale, as can be checked pointwise.
