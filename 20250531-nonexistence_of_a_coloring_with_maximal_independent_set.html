nonexistence of an optimal coloring with maximal independent set

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph. Then in general there does not exist an optimal coloring where the color is an independent set of vertices of maximal cardinality.

Proof

consider the (undirected - I only know tikz-cd :/) graph

Then it follows that the independence number is given by $3$ - namely the cardinality of the set $\{1,4,6\}$. As soon as we have an indepent set containing one of $2,3,5$ its cardinailty is at most $2$. w.l.o.g. let $2$ lie in this mysterious independent set, then another element must be $6$ (and hence unique).

Suppose we have such a coloring where $\alpha^{-1}[1] = \{1,4,6\}$. But the vertices $2,3,5$ must have distinct colors since they form a clique and each of them is connected to at least one of $\{1,4,6\}$. Hence $\alpha$ is at best a 4-coloring (even precisely a 4-coloring by lack of options).

So we have to construct a 3-coloring. But

\begin{align*} 1,5 \mapsto& 1 \\ 2,6 \mapsto& 2 \\ 4,3 \mapsto& 3 \end{align*}

suffices
