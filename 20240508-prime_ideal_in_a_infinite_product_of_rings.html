prime ideal in an infinite product of rings in general not a single prime ideal

Proposition

Let \(R_i\) be rings for an infinite indexing set $I$, $\prod_i R_i$ the direct product of rings and $I$ a prime ideal. Then in general

\begin{align*} I \neq \prod_{i \neq j} R_i \times \mathfrak{p}_i \end{align*}

Proof

Consider

\begin{align*} \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z} \end{align*}

and the prime ideal

\begin{align*} \mathfrak{p} \coloneqq \{(a_n) \in \prod_n \mathbb{Z} \vert a_n \in p \mathbb{Z} \text{for infinitely many n}\} \end{align*}

strict

follows from $(1) \not\in \mathfrak{p}$

prime

Suppose $(a_n) \cdot (b_n) \in \mathfrak{p}$. Then for infinitely many $n \in I$ it follows that $a_n \cdot b_n \in p \mathbb{Z}$ Then we conclude w.l.o.g. that $a_n \in p \mathbb{Z}$ for infinitely many $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Hence $(a_n) \in \mathfrak{p}$
