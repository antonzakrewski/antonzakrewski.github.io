second countable topological manifold implies compact exhaustion

Proposition

Let $M$ be a topological manifold with the assumption, that it is secondcountable. Then it admits a compact exhaustion.

note that we never used hausdorffness

Proof

for $m \in M$ we may choose locally euclidean neighbourhoods $U_m$. Then for each $m \in U_m$ we may choose $V_m \subseteq U_m$ with $\overline{V_m} \subseteq U_m$ compact (e.g. by restricting to an \(\varepsilon\)-ball) Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a countable basis. Then for each $V_m$ we may choose an open subset $W_m \subseteq V_m$ with $W_m \in \mathcal{B}$. Then the countable collection $W_m$ covers $M$ (since we have at least one $V_m$ for each$m \in M$.

Then the closure $\overline{W_m}$ is compact since it is a closed subset of $\overline{V_m}$.

Then enumerate each $\overline{W}_m$ by $\overline{W}_n$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$. so $\bigcup_{i=0}^n \overline{W}_i$ is a compact exhaustion of $M$ since each component is a finite union of compact subsets (cf. finite union of compact subspaces)
