upper bound of edges for a disconnected graph

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph with $\vert V \vert = n$ and $\vert E \vert > \binom{n-1}{2}$ Then $G$ is connected

Proof

optimal

consider

\begin{align*} K_{n-1} \coprod K_1 \end{align*}

which contains $\binom{n-1}{2}$ many edges in the first component and $0$ in the second.

It is clearly not connected.

bound

Let $G$ be a disconnected graph with maximal amount of edges. w.l.o.g. we may assume that each connected component is the complete graph. Furthermore we may assume, that $G$ contains exactly $2$ connceted components, since otherwise we could connect two connected components (and add edges while $G$ stays disconnected).

Hence we have to find an $l \leq n$ such that $K_{l} \coprod K_{n-l}$ contains a maximal amount of edges. But

\begin{align*} \vert E_{K_l \coprod K_{n-l}} \vert = \binom{l}{2} + \binom{n-l}{2} \end{align*}

which is maximal iff $l = 1$ or $l = n-1$
