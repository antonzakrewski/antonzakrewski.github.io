existence of a unique normalization for an affine variety

Proposition

Let $X$ be an affine variety over an algebraically closed field. Then there exists a normalization of $X$ - unique up to iso

Proof

uniqueness

uniqueness of the normalizaiton

construction

let $A(X)$ be the coordinate ring with quotient field $k(X)$ Then we get an embedding 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
A(X) \arrow[]{rd}{} \arrow[hookrightarrow, ]{r}{} & & k(X) \\
& B & \arrow[]{ru}{}\\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

where $B$ is the integral closure of $A(X)$ in $k(X)$

Then by construction $B$ is integrally closed over $A$. and a finite type \(K\)-Algebra, d.h.

\begin{align*} B \cong k[X_1,...,X_n]/I(X) \end{align*}

for some prime ideal $I(X)$.

Here $B$ is an Integral domain

By equivalence between algebraic sets and affine k-algebras we get an affine variety $X'$ with $A(X') \cong B$

Hence

By $\mathrm{Quot}(B) \cong k(X)$ we may conclude that this is a birational equivalence and isomorphic function fields (cf. equivalence between the category of affine varieties and dominant rational maps and finitely generated field extension)
