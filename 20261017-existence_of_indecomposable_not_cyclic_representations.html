existence of indecomposable, non cyclic representations

Proposition

Let $A$ be a K-Algebra, then there an indecomposable representation is in general not a cyclic representation.

Proof

Let $A = \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x^2,y^2,xy)$ and let $V$ be the \(A\)-module $\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(A,k)$. Then $A$ has a basis given by $1,x,y$ First we show that it is indecomposable and then that it is not cyclic

indecomposable

Let $\partial_x,\partial_y, \partial_1$ be the dual basis. Then $x \partial_x = \partial_1 = y \partial_y$ and $x$ and $y$ act by zero on the remaining combinations. Suppose there exist $M_1,M_2$ such that $M_1 \oplus M_2 = V^*$ as \(A\)-modules. Then there exists an vector $v = \alpha_1 \partial_1 + \alpha_x \partial_x + \alpha_y \partial_y \in M_1$ and $1 \partial_1 - v \in M_2$ since $1 \partial_1$ lies in $M_1 + M_2$. In particular $x \cdot v = \alpha_x \partial_1 = x \cdot (v + 1 \partial_1)$ which implies $\alpha_x = 0$ and similarly $\alpha_y = 0$ since $M_1 \cap M_2 =0$.

Hence $v = \alpha_1 \partial_1 \in M_1$ for w.l.o.g. $\alpha_1 \neq 0$ (otherwise proceed with $v + 1 \in M_2$). If $M_1 \oplus M_2 = 0$, then each vector ($\beta_1,\beta_x,\beta_y) \in M_2$ must satisfy $\beta_x = \beta_y = 0$ since we may multiply with $x$ and $y$ resp. to get $\beta_x \partial_1 \in M_2$. At last, similarly $\beta_1 =0$

not cyclic

Let $k$ be the $A$ representation where $x,y$ act by zero. Then $\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(A,k) \cong k$ is a cyclic (1-dimensional) \(k\)-vector space as $x,y \mapsto 0$ and hence an element is determined by $1 \mapsto \lambda$ as a k-vector space. We claim that $\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(V^*,k)$ has dimension $\geq 2$ and that this suffices to see that $V^*$ is not cylcic If $V^*$ were cyclic, i.e. $V^* \cong A/I$, then $\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(V^*,k)$ would be a subvector space of a 1-dimensonal vector space, hence itself $0$ or $1$ dimensional.

But here it suffices to see that $f_1: \partial_1 \mapsto 0, \partial_x \mapsto 1, \partial_y \mapsto 0$ and $f_1: \partial_1 \mapsto 0, \partial_x \mapsto 0, \partial_y \mapsto 1$ are \(A\)-linear and linearly independent over $k$.
