finite dimensional, irreducible, complex representation of an abelian group

Proposition

Let $G$ be an abelian group and $\mathcal{F}: BG \rightarrow \mathrm{FinVect}_{\mathbb{C}}$ a finite dimensional, irreducible representation of a group. Then $\mathcal{F}$ is one dimensional

Proof

Let $g \in G$, then we claim that $\rho_{g} \in \mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V,V)$. This follows as

\begin{align*} (\rho_g \circ \rho_{g'})(-) =& \rho_{g \circ g'}(-) \\ =& \rho_{g' \circ g}(-) \\ =& \rho_{g'} \circ \rho_g(-) \end{align*}

Let $\mathrm{id} \in \mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V,V)$ and $\rho_g$ which is nonzero (since it is an automorphism). then schurs lemma for finite complex representations shows

\begin{align*} \rho_{g} = \alpha \mathrm{id} \end{align*}

for some $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$, in other words $G$ acts by multiplication with some $\alpha$

Therefore each subspace is invariant. Since $\mathcal{F}$ is irreducible, we get that $\mathcal{F}$ must be one dimensional.
