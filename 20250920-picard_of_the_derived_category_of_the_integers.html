picard of the derived category of the integers

Proposition

The Picard group of the derived category of the integers is given by

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{Z}) \simeq \mathbb{Z} \times \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathbb{Z}) \end{align*}

Proof

Let $X$ be an invertible module with inverse $Y$. Then $X \simeq \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}} H_n(X)$ and $X$ is in particular dualizable (cf. invertible object is dualizable).

Now dualizable objects in the derived category of a ring are compact, which implies that $X \simeq \bigoplus_{i=-n}^n H_n(X)$ for finitely presented groups $H_n(X)$. moreover, invertible object in the derived category of a field implies that $X \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{F}_p \simeq \mathbb{F}_p[n_p]$ and $X \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} \simeq \mathbb{Q}[n]$ for $n_p,n \in \mathbb{Z}$. In particular, $H_n(X)$ contains exactly $\mathbb{Z}$ as summand, which then implies $n_p = n$ as $H_n(X, \mathbb{F}_p) \simeq \mathbb{F}_p$. Furthermore, $H_m(X)$ must be zero, as otherwise, there would exist an $p$ with $H_m(X,\mathbb{F}_p) \neq 0$ (cf. classification of finitely generated abelian groups).
