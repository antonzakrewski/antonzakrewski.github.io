stetige lp funktion impliziert im allgemeinen nicht 0 grenzwert

Proposition

Sei $f: \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ eine stetige Abbildung L1 Abbildung. Dann gilt i.A. nicht

\begin{align*} \mathrm{lim}_{ \vert x \vert \rightarrow \infty} f(x)= 0 \end{align*}

Proof

Let

\begin{align*} f_n: [0,1] \rightarrow& [0,1] \\ x \mapsto& \begin{cases} 2^n x & \mbox{if } x \leq \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2^n} \\ \mathrm{max}(0,1 - 2^n x) & \mbox{if } x \geq \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2^n} \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

And define

\begin{align*} f: \mathbb{R}^+ \rightarrow& [0,1] \\ x \mapsto f_{\mathrm{floor}(x)}(x - \mathrm{floor}(x)) \end{align*}

and then extend such that this map is zero on the negative half of $\mathbb{R}$

this is continuous by the glueing lemma for a locally finite, closed covering

Then

\begin{align*} f \leq \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \chi_{[n, n + \frac{1}{2^n}]} \end{align*}

where the RHS is lebesgue integrable, since

\begin{align*} \int_{\mathbb{R}} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \chi_{[n, n + \frac{1}{2^n}]} =& \sum_{n=0}^\infty 2^n \\ =& 2 \end{align*}

But

\begin{align*} f(n + \frac{1}{2^{n+1}}) = 1 \end{align*}

with with

\begin{align*} n + \frac{1}{2^{n+1}} \rightarrow \infty \end{align*}

for $n \rightarrow \infty$

modulo some technical detail
