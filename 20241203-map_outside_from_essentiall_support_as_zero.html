Proposition

Let $f: \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ be a measurable map. Then

\begin{align*} f = 0 \end{align*}

almost everywhere on $\mathbb{R}^d \setminus \mathrm{supp}(f)$

Proof

by definition

\begin{align*} \mathbb{R}^d \setminus \mathrm{supp}(f) = \bigcup A_i \end{align*}

where $A_n$ are the open subsets where $f_{\vert A_n} = 0$ almost everywhere.

We need to show that $\bigcup A_i$ can be written as countable union of $A_n$. Then the set $\{x \in \bigcup A_n \vert f(x) \neq 0 \}$ is a countable union of nullsets, hence a nullset.
