localization of ordinary modules does not commute with infinite products

Proposition

Let $A_i$ be a collection of \(R\)-modules and $S$ a multiplicatively closed subset . Then the localization functor does not preserves (infinite) products

Proof

canonical map

consider e.g. $A_i = \mathbb{Z}$ for $i \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$. Then we claim that the canonical map $(\prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z})_{\mathbb{Q}}) \rightarrow \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Q}$ is not an isomorphism (although both vector spaces are abstractly isomorphic)

For that it suffices to observe that $(\frac{1}{n}) \in \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Q}$ does not lie in the image of the map, i.e. the map is not surjective: Any element in $(\prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z})_{\mathbb{Q}})$ is given by $\frac{1}{m} \cdot (\alpha_i) \mapsto \left(\frac{\alpha_i}{m}\right)$ and hence the \(n\)-th component does not lie in the image for $n >> m$

general

consider $A_i = \mathbb{Z}/i$. Then $(A_i)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is trivial, since it is a torsion group.

But $(\prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}/n)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is nontrivial. Suppose $\frac{(1,1,...,)}{1}$ is the zero element $\frac{0}{1}$, then there exists an $s$ such that $s \cdot (1,1,...,) = 0$.

But the \(n\)-th component is nonzero for $n >> s$.
