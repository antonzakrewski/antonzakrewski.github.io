nilpotent element functor is not an affine scheme

Proposition

The nilpotent element functor

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Nil}: \mathrm{CAlg}_{R} \rightarrow& \mathrm{Set} \\ A \mapsto \{a \in A \vert \exists n \in \mathbb{N} : a^n = 0 \} \end{align*}

is not an affine scheme

Proof

recall that representable functors preserve limits, so in particular also infinite products. But consider $\prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} R[x]/x^n$. Then $(x)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is an element in $\prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathrm{Nil}(R[x]/x^n )$ but not in $\mathrm{Nil}(\prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} R[x]/x^n \mathbb{Z})$.
