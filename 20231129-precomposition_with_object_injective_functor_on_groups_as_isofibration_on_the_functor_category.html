precomposition with object-injective functor on groups as isofibration on the functor category

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}_1,\mathcal{G}_2$ be small (?) groupoids and $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{G}_0 \rightarrow \mathcal{G}_1$ a object-injective functor. Then for the functor category, precomposition

\begin{align*} \mathcal{F}^{ \rightarrow}: \mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{G}_1,\mathcal{G}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{G}_0, \mathcal{G}) \end{align*}

is an isofibration

Proof

Let $\mathcal{F}_1,\mathcal{F}_2: \mathcal{G}_0 \rightarrow \mathcal{G}$ be functors and $\eta: \mathcal{F}_1 \rightarrow \mathcal{F}_2$ a natural isomorphism

Suppose furthermore, that $\mathcal{F}_2 = \mathcal{H}_2 \circ \mathcal{F}$ for a functor

\begin{align*} \mathcal{H}_2: \mathcal{G}_1 \rightarrow \mathcal{G} \end{align*}

Let

\begin{align*} \epsilon(A) = \begin{cases} \eta(A'): \mathcal{F}_1(A') \rightarrow \mathcal{F}_2(A') & \mbox{if } \exists A' \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G}_0) : \mathcal{F}(A') = A \\ \mathrm{id}: \mathcal{H}_2(A) \rightarrow \mathcal{H}_2(A) & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

We then define a functor

\begin{align*} \mathcal{H}_1: \mathcal{G}_1 \rightarrow& \mathcal{G} \\ A \mapsto& \begin{cases} \mathcal{F}_1(A') & \mbox{if } \exists A' \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{G}_0) : \mathcal{F}(A') = A \\ \mathcal{H}_2(A') & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} (f: A \rightarrow B) \mapsto& \epsilon(B)^{-1} \circ f \circ \epsilon(A) \end{align*}

note that $\epsilon(B)$ exists, as $\mathcal{G}$ is a groupoid. hence, by construction, $\mathcal{H}_1$ is a functor.

Let $\epsilon: \mathcal{H}_1 \rightarrow \mathcal{H}_2$ be the natural transformation, a,

commutes Note, that it is an iso (cf. natural isomorphism and isomorphism on the objects)

Let $A', B' \in \mathcal{G}_0$ and $f: A' \rightarrow B'$. then by construction, we get

\begin{align*} \mathcal{H}_1 \circ \mathcal{F}(A) =& \mathcal{F}_1(A) \\ \mathcal{H}_1 \circ \mathcal{F}(B) =& \mathcal{F}_1(B) \end{align*}

and

\begin{align*} \mathcal{H}_1 \circ \mathcal{F}(f') =& \eta(B)^{-1} \circ \mathcal{H}_2(\mathcal{F}(f')) \circ \eta(A) \\ =& \eta(B)^{-1} \circ \mathcal{F}_2(f') \circ \eta(A) \end{align*}

where by naturality of $\eta$ we conclude, that

\begin{align*} \mathcal{F}_2(f') =& \eta(B)^{-1} \circ \mathcal{F}_2(f') \circ \eta(A) \end{align*}
