Proof

we construct a morphism

\begin{align*} h: B \rightarrow A \oplus C \end{align*}

show that $h$ is a bijection (hence an isomorphism) and that the diagrams commute.

morphism

choose a basis $(a_i)$ of $A$. Then by assumption $f$ is injective, hence $(f(a_i))$ is linearly independent (cf. Vektorraummonomorphismus erhält lineare Unabhängigkeit). We may extend $f(a_i)$ to a basis (cf. erweiterung einer linear unabhängigen Menge zu einer Basis) $\{f(a_i),b_j\}$ and define

\begin{align*} \tilde{f}: B \rightarrow& A \\ f(a_i) \mapsto& a_i \\ b_j \mapsto& 0 \end{align*}

where the map $f(a_i) \mapsto a_i$ is welldefined (existence follows from assumption, uniqueness follows from injectivity of $f$).

It follows that

\begin{align*} \tilde{f} \circ f = \mathrm{id} \end{align*}

for example since $(\tilde{f} \circ f)(a_i) = a_i$ (cf. Existenz und Eindeutigkeit eines Homomorphismus durch die Abbildungen der Basis)

Then define

\begin{align*} h = (\tilde{f}, g): B \rightarrow& A \oplus C \\ b \mapsto& (\tilde{f}(b), g(b)) \end{align*}

bijective

injective

Let $b \in \mathrm{ker}(h)$. Then in particular $g(b) = 0$ and we may find a preimage $a \in A$ of $b$ under $f$ (since $\mathrm{ker}(g) = \mathrm{im}(f)$.

It follows that

\begin{align*} (\tilde{f}(b), g(b)) =& h(b) \\ =& h(f(a)) \\ =& (\tilde{f}(f(a)), g(b)) \\ =& (a,g(b)) \end{align*}

which shows $a = 0$ or $b = f(a) = 0$.

Thus we are done by minimal kernel and monomorphism
