universal property of a ring localization

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $S^{-1}R$ a localization along $S \subseteq R$, $\varphi: R \rightarrow S^{-1}R$ the canonical ring homomorphism for a localization and

\begin{align*} \psi: R \rightarrow R' \end{align*}

a ringhomomorphism, such that $\psi(S) \subseteq (R')^{\times}$ Then there exists a unique homomorphism $f: S^{-1}R \rightarrow R'$ such that

Proof

uniqueness

Let $s \in S$, then for $a \in R$ we get by cancelling in a localization

\begin{align*} 1 =& f(\frac{s}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{s}) \\ =& f(\frac{s}{1}) \cdot f(\frac{1}{s}) \end{align*}

hence $f(\frac{1}{s}) = f(\frac{s}{1})^{-1} = \psi(s)^{-1}$

Thus the map is - if existent - uniquely defined by

\begin{align*} f(\frac{a}{s}) = \psi(a) \cdot \psi(s)^{-1} \end{align*}

existence

welldefined map

Let $\frac{a_1}{s_1} = \frac{a_2}{s_2}$, then by construction there exists an $s \in S$ such that

\begin{align*} (a_1s_2 - a_2s_1)t = 0 \end{align*}

Therefore applying $\psi$

\begin{align*} \left(\psi(a_1)\psi(s_2) - \psi(a_2)\psi(s_1) \right) \cdot \psi(t) =& \psi(0)\\ =& 0 \end{align*}

By assumption $\psi(t) \in R'^{*}$ hence since units are no zero divisor we conclude, that

\begin{align*} \psi(a_1)\psi(s_2) - \psi(a_2)\psi(s_1) = 0 \end{align*}

Furthermore, by assumption there exist inverse elements $\psi(s_1)^{-1}, \psi(s_2)^{-1}$, hence

\begin{align*} \psi(a_1)\psi(s_2) - \psi(a_2)\psi(s_1) =& 0 && \vert \cdot \psi(s_1)^{-1} \psi(s_2)^{-1} \\ \psi(a_1)\psi(s_1)^{-1} - \psi(a_2)\psi(s_2) =& 0 \\ \psi(a_1)\psi(s_1)^{-1} =& \psi(a_2)\psi(s_2) \end{align*}

ringhomomorphism

Let $\frac{a_1}{s_1}, \frac{a_2}{s_2} \in S^{-1}R$, then

\begin{align*} f(\frac{a_1}{s_1} \cdot \frac{a_2}{s_2}) =& f(\frac{a_1 \cdot a_2}{s_1 \cdot s_2}) \\ =& \psi(a_1 \cdot a_2) \cdot \psi(s_1 \cdot s_2)^{-1} \\ =& \psi(a_1) \cdot \psi(a_2) \cdot \psi(s_1)^{-1} \cdot \psi(s_2)^{-1} \\ =& \psi(a_1) \cdot \psi(s_1)^{-1} \cdot \psi(a_2) \cdot \psi(s_2)^{-1} \\ =& f(\frac{a_1}{s_1}) \cdot f(\frac{a_2}{s_2}) \end{align*}

or

\begin{align*} f(\frac{a_1}{s_1} + \frac{a_2}{s_2}) =& f(\frac{a_1s_2 + a_2s_1}{s_1s_2}) \\ =& \psi(a_1s_2 + a_2s_1) \cdot \psi(s_1s_2)^{-1} \\ =& \left(\psi(a_1) \psi(s_2) + \psi(a_2)\psi(s_1) \right) \cdot \psi(s_1)^{-1} \psi(s_2)^{-1} \\ =& \psi(a_1)\psi(s_1)^{-1} + \psi(a_2)\psi(s_2)^{-1} \\ =& f(\frac{a_1}{s_1}) + f(\frac{a_2}{s_2}) \end{align*}
