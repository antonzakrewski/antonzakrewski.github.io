terminal, initial and zero object in an Ab-enriched category

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a Ab-enriched category category and $A \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$.

TFAE:

  1. $A$ is an initial object
  2. $A$ is a terminal object
  3. $A$ is a zero object

Proof

1), 2) $\implies$ 3)

By assumption of being initial/terminal and construction of a category, there exists only one morphism $\mathrm{id}_{A} = 0$. Hence $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,A)$ is the trivial abelian group.

Suppose $B \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$, then

1) $\implies$ 3)

for $f \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B)$ and the tensor product

\begin{align*} C: \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,A) \times \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B) \rightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B) \end{align*}

we get

\begin{align*} f =& \\ =& f \circ \mathrm{id}_{A} =& C(0,f) \\ =& 0 \\ \end{align*}

Therefore the morphism is unique and by construction of the trivial map also exists

2) $\implies$ 3)

for $f \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(B,A)$ and the tensor product

\begin{align*} C: \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(B,A) \times \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(B,B) \rightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(B,A) \end{align*}

we get

\begin{align*} f =& \mathrm{id}_{A} \circ f \\ =& C(f,0) \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

Therefore the morphism is unique and by construction of the trivial map also exists

3) $\implies$ 1), 2)
