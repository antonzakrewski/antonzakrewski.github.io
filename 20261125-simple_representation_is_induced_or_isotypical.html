simple representation is induced or isotypical

Proposition

Let $N \subseteq G$ be a normal subgroup of $G$ and let $V$ be a simple representation of $G$ Then at least one of the following statements holds:

  1. there exists an $H$ with $N \subseteq H \subsetneq G$ and a simple $W$ of $H$ such that $V \cong \mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(W)$.
  2. $\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}(V)$ is isotypic

Proof

By maschkes theorem about semisimple group rings, $\mathrm{Res}_{N}^{G} = \bigoplus V_i$ for $V_i$ simples of $N$ where $V_i$ are isotypic.

Normality of $N$ in $G$ implies that each $V_i$ is mapped to some $V_j: W \subseteq V$$ where $W$ is an \(N\)-subrepresentation.

\begin{align*} gW \subseteq V \end{align*}
\begin{align*} n g w = g n' w \subseteq gW \end{align*}

moreover, $gW$ is isomorphic to $W^g$, so $W^g = N \xrightarrow{g \cdot g^{-1}} N \rightarrow \mathrm{End}(W)$.

Moreover, the direct sum of an orbit is necesseraliy a $G$-subrepresentation. So $G$ acts transitively on $V_i$ since $V$ is simple.

If $\vert I \vert = 1$ for $\mathrm{Res}_{N}^{G}(V) = \bigoplus_{i \in I} V_i$, then we are in case ii)$. Otherwise, we pick some $V_{i_0}$ and let $H = $\{g \vert g V_{i_0} \subseteq V_{i_0}\}$.

Here $N \subseteq H$ since each $V_{i_0}$ is an \(N\)-representation and $H$ is a nontrivial subgroup.

Moreover, $V_{i_0}$ is an \(H\)-representation.
