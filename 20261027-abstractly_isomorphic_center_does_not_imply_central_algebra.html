abstractly isomorphic center does not imply central algebra

Proposition

Let $k$ be a field and $A$ a K-Algebra Suppose that the center is abstractly isomorphic to $A$, then $A$ is in general not a central algebra.

Proof

Let $k = \mathbb{Q}(x_{2n} \vert n \in \mathbb{N})$ be the function field and consider $\mathbb{Q}(x_n \vert n \in \mathbb{N})$ with the inclusion as algebra structure. Here $\mathbb{Q}(x_n \vert n \in \mathbb{N})$ is a commutative ring, but the center of $A$ is larger.
