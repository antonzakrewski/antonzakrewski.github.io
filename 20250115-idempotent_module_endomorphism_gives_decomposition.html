idempotent module-endomorphism gives decomposition

Proposition

Let $R$ be a ring and $M$ an \(R\)-module, $M$ a \(R\)-module and

\begin{align*} T: M \rightarrow M \end{align*}

an idempotent module-homomorphism

Then

\begin{align*} T = \langle \mathrm{im}(T), \mathrm{ker}(T)\rangle \end{align*}

with

\begin{align*} \mathrm{im}(T) \cap \mathrm{ker}(T) = (0) \end{align*}

Proof

generates

Let $m \in M$. Then consider

\begin{align*} m = T(m) - (T(m) - m) \end{align*}

with $T(m) \in \mathrm{im}(T)$. it suffices to show that $T(m) - m \in \mathrm{ker}(T)$.

But then

\begin{align*} T(T(m) - m) =& T(T(m)) - T(m) \\ =& T(m) - T(m) \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

trivial intersection

Let $m \in \mathrm{im}(T) \cap \mathrm{ker}(T)$. Then

\begin{align*} m = T(m') \end{align*}

for some $m' \in M$ as $m \in \mathrm{im}(T)$.

Then since $T$ is idempotent,

\begin{align*} T(m) =& T(T(m')) \\ =& T(m') \\ =& m \end{align*}

But furthermore $m \in \mathrm{ker}(T)$ hence

\begin{align*} T(m) = 0 \end{align*}

or

\begin{align*} m = 0 \end{align*}
