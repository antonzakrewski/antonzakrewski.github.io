finite, nontrivial spectrum has infinitely many nontrivial homotopy groups

Proposition

Let $X$ be a finite spectrum with $X \not\simeq 0$. Then $X$ has infinitely many notrivial homotopy groups

Proof

Observe that there exsits an $n$ such that $H_n(X)$ is nontrivial (otherwise: bounded below spectrum with trivial homology is contractible). Then it either contains a free term $\mathbb{Z}$ or a p-primary torsion term $\mathbb{Z}/p^n \mathbb{Z}$ (by the classification of finitely generated abelian groups, finite spectrum and bounded below, finitely many, finitely generated homology). Both cases (and universal coefficient theorem for spectra and ordinary rings) show that $H_*(X, \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z})$ is nontrivial for some prime $p$. Then finite spectrum with nontrivial Z/p homology contains infinitely many p-torsion homotopy groups shows the rest.
