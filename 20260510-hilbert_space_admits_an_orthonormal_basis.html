hilbert space admits an orthonormal basis

Proposition

Let $X \neq 0$ be a Hilbert space. Then $X$ admits an orthonormal basis.

Proof

Let $\mathcal{M} \coloneqq {S \subseteq X \vert S \textrm{ orthonormal}}$. Then $M$ is nonempty: take any $x \neq 0$, then $\{\frac{x}{\| x \|}\}$ is for trivial reasons orthonormal. Moreover, it is a partially ordered set and closed under unions of chains: This follows, as being orthonormal can be checked on finite subsets.

Hence $\mathcal{M}$ admits a maximal element $M$, which is orthonormal: We claim that $M$ is a (fake) basis. Suppose not, then there exists an $x \in X \setminus M^{\bot}$ or equivalently $x \bot m$ for all $m \in M$. Hence $M' = \{\frac{x}{\| x \|}\} \cup M$ is an orthonormal set with $M \subsetneq M'$, contradiction.
