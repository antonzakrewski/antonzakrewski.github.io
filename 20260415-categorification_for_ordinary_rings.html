categorification for ordinary Rings

Example

consider the categorification functor for $\mathcal{C} = \mathrm{Ab}$, i.e.

\begin{align*} \mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Ab}) \rightarrow& \mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Ab}}(\mathrm{Pr^L}_{\kappa})) \\ R \mapsto& \mathrm{Mod}_{R}(\mathrm{Ab}) \\ (f: R \rightarrow S) \mapsto& (- \otimes_{R} S: \mathrm{Mod}_{R}(\mathrm{Ab}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Mod}_{S}(\mathrm{Ab})) \end{align*}

where

  1. $- \otimes_{R} S$ is the extension of scalars - it is a left adjoint and moreover symmetric monoidal (cf. extension of scalars is symmetric monoidal)
  2. $- \otimes_{R} S$ is a left adjoint, hence a valid morphism in $\mathrm{Pr}^L_{\kappa}$
  3. $\mathrm{Mod}_{R}(\mathrm{Ab})$ should be tensored over $\mathrm{Ab}$ via the unique colimit preserving functor $\mathrm{Ab} \rightarrow \mathrm{Mod}_R(\mathrm{Ab})$ mapping $\mathbb{Z}$ to $R$ and therefore $- \otimes_{R} S$ is $\mathrm{Ab}$ linear, as it preserves colimits
  4. this functor is not essentially surjective, as presentable, additive categories should be examples of objects in $\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Ab}}(\mathrm{Pr^L}_{\kappa})$
  5. it is fully faithful, as any ring homomorphism $f: R \rightarrow S$ can then be recovered by

since any element $r \in R$ corresponds to the module endomorphism $\cdot r: R \rightarrow R$ and then gets mapped to $r \otimes_{R} S$ which by elementary calculation of the tensor product structure corresponds to multilpication with $f(s)$.
