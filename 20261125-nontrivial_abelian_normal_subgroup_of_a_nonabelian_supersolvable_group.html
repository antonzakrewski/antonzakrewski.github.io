nontrivial abelian normal subgroup of a nonabelian supersolvable group

Proposition

Suppose $G$ is a nonabelian supersolvable group. Then there exists an abelian normal subgroup $A \triangleleft G$ such that $A$ is not contained in the center of $G$

Proof

Consider $G/Z(G)$, which is supersolvable (cf. quotient of a supersolvable group) and nontrivial. Hence there exists a nontrivial cyclic normal subgroup $C$ of $G/Z(G)$ (the first term of any supersolvable series).

Now define $A \coloneqq \pi^{-1}[C] \subseteq G$ for $\pi: G \rightarrow G/Z(G)$.

Note that $A$ is normal (cf. preimage of normal subgroups as normal subgroup) and picking a preimage of a nontrivial element in $C$ shows that it is not contained in the center. So it remains to show that it is abelian: Let $a$ be a generator of $C$, then every element is of the form $ax$ for some $x \in Z(G)$, which implies that $A$ is abelian since $a^ix a^j y = a^j y a^i x$.
