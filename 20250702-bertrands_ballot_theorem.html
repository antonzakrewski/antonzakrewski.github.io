bertrands ballot theorem

Proposition

Let $m > n$ and $\mathcal{B}_{m,n}$ the set of lower lattice paths. Then

\begin{align*} \vert \mathcal{B}_{m,n} \vert = \frac{m-n}{m+n} \vert \mathcal{L}_{m,n} \vert \end{align*}

where $\mathcal{L}_{m,n}$ is the set of lower lattice paths

Proof

Let

  1. $\overline{A_{m,n}}$ be the set of lattice paths where $y_2 > x_2$, i.e. start in the wrong direction
  2. $A_{m,n}$ be the set of lattice paths where $x_2 > y_2$, i.e. start in the direction such that they still can be lower lattice paths after the first step.

Then $\vert \overline{A_{m,n}} \vert = \vert \mathcal{B}_{m,n-1} \vert = \binom{m+n-1}{m}$ as $\overline{A}_{m,n} \simeq \mathcal{B}_{m,n-1}$.

Then there exists a bijection

\begin{align*} \overline{A}_{m,n} \simeq A_{m,n} \setminus \mathcal{B}_{m,n} \end{align*}

given by sending a lattice path to the mirrored path up until the first intersection with the diagonal:

Any path in $\mathcal{A}_{m,n}$ must intersect the diagonal since $m > n$ and any path in $A_{m,n} \setminus \mathcal{B}_{m,n}$ must also intersect the diagonal, since otherwise it would be a lower lattice path.

Note that

\begin{align*} \mathcal{B}_{m,n} = \mathcal{L}_{m,n} \setminus \left(\overline{A}_{m,n} \coprod (A_{m,n} \setminus \mathcal{B}_{m,n})\right) \end{align*}

where $\mathcal{L}_{m,n}$ denotes all lattice paths since $\overline{A}_{m,n} \cap A_{m,n} = \emptyset$.

Hence

\begin{align*} \vert \mathcal{B}_{m,n} \vert =& \vert \mathcal{L}_{m,n} \vert - \vert \overline{A}_{m,n} \vert - \vert A_{m,n} \setminus \mathcal{B}_{m,n} \vert \\ =& \binom{m+n}{m} - 2 \cdot \binom{m+n-1}{m} \\ =& \binom{m+n}{m} - 2 \cdot \binom{n+m-n}{n+m} \binom{m+n}{n} \\ =& 1 - \frac{2n}{m+n} \binom{m+n}{m} \\ =& \frac{m - n}{m + n} \binom{m+n}{m} \end{align*}
