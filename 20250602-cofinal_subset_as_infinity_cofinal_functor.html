cofinal subset as infinity cofinal functor

Proposition

Let $S \subseteq T$ be a subset of a partially ordered set $T$.

TFAE:

  1. $S$ is a cofinal subset.
  2. the inclusion
\begin{align*} \iota: N(S) \rightarrow N(T) \end{align*}

on nerves is cofinal

i think equivalent (under at least mild assumptions like well order; use colimit of identity as terminal object, )

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

2) $\implies$ 1) - messy argument :/

consider the inclusion

\begin{align*} T \hookrightarrow \overline{T} \end{align*}

which adds a terminal object if it does not exist.

Then the colimit

\begin{align*} T \rightarrow \overline{T} \end{align*}

is given by the terminal object (or supremum as colimit).

By assumption

\begin{align*} S \rightarrow T \rightarrow \overline{T} \end{align*}

also has the same colimit, so the supremum of

\begin{align*} S \subsetq T \end{align*}

is given by the terminal object $1$.

So we may assume that $1 \in T, 1\not\in S$ (otherwise the subset is cofinal by the argument above).

Then consider

\begin{align*} T \rightarrow& \mathrm{Set} \\ i \mapsto \{1,2\} \\ 1 \mapsto \{*\} \end{align*}

then the colimit of $T \rightarrow \mathrm{Set}$ is given by $\{*\}$ but the colimit of $S \rightarrow T \rightarrow \mathrm{Set}$ is given by

\begin{align*} \coprod_{\pi_0(S)} \{1,2\} \end{align*}

which has different cardinality.
