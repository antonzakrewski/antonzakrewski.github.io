equivalent characterization of a bounded/continuous operator

Proposition

Let $T: X \rightarrow Y$ be a linear operator. TFAE:

  1. $T$ is continuous
  2. $T$ is continuous in some $x_0 \in X$
  3. there exists some $c < \infty$ such that $\| T x \| \leq c \| x \|$ for every $x \in X$
  4. $T$ is a bounded operator.

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

special case

2) $\implies$ 3)

Assume that for each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists an $y_n$ such that $\| T y_n \| > \| y_n \| \geq n$. Then we define for $n \geq 1$ the sequence $x_n \coloneqq \frac{1}{n \| y_n \|} y_n \in X$. Observe that $\| x_n \| = \frac{1}{n} \xrightarrow{n \rightarrow \infty} 0$ and hence $(x_n + x_0)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \xrightarrow{n \rightarrow \infty} x_0$. But $\| T(x_n) \| = \frac{1}{n \| y_n \|} \| T(y_n) \| > 1$. Hence $T(x_n + x_0)$ does not converge to $T(x_n)$. This is a contradiction to the statement, that $T$ is continuous in $x_0$

3) $\implies$ 4)

special case

4) $\implies$ 1)

Suppose $T$ is bounded. We show that $T$ is sequentially continuous.

Let $x_n \rightarrow x$ be a converging sequence. Then $\|x_n - x \| \xrightarrow{n \rightarrow \infty} 0$. But as $T$ is bounded, we get

\begin{align*} \| T(x_n - x) \| \leq c \cdot \| x_n - x \| \xrightarrow{n \rightarrow \infty} \end{align*}

hence $T$ is (sequentially) continuous in $x$. As $X$ is a metric space and $x$ was arbitary, this shows that $T$ is continuous
