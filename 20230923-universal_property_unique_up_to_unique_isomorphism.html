universal property unique up to unique isomorphism

hypothesis

A universal property defines an object - if it exists - up to unique isomorphism

Proofsketch

Consider a category $\mathcal{C}$ and some universal property, that both $M$, and $M'$ satisfy. Then by assumption there exist unique morphism $\varphi: M \rightarrow M'$ and $\psi: M' \rightarrow M$, usually such that following diagram commutes

Furthermore, $M$ itself satisfies the universal property and $\mathrm{id}_{M}$ makes the following diagram commute

Thus by composition of morphism and uniqueness of the mediating morphism $\mathrm{id}_{M}$, it follows, that

\begin{align*} \varphi \circ \psi =& \mathrm{id}_{M} \\ \psi \circ \varphi =& \mathrm{id}_{M'} \end{align*}

Hence $\varphi, \psi$ are isomorphisms and furthermore the isomorphism between $M,M'$ is by construction uniquely defined
