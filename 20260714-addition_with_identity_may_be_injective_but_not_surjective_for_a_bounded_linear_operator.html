addition with identity may be injective but not surjective for a bounded linear operator on a hilbert space

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a Hilbert space, then there exist bounded operators $f: \mathcal{H} \rightarrow \mathcal{H}$ such that

  1. $(f - z \mathrm{id})$ is injective for all $z$
  2. $(f - z \mathrm{id})$ is never surjective

Proof

Let $\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{L}^2([0,1])$ and consider the map

\begin{align*} M: \mathcal{L}^2([0,1]) \rightarrow& \mathcal{L}^2([0,1]) \\ [\psi] \mapsto& [- \cdot \psi(-)] \end{align*}

which is defined almost everywhere.

Now we claim that $M - \mathrm{id}$ is injective: suppose there exists an $z$ such that $(M - z)(\psi) = 0$. Then almost everywhere, $(x - z)(\psi(x)) = 0$ which shows $\psi(x) = 0$ almost everywhere.

Now we show that $M - \mathrm{id}$ is not surjective: Let $\psi \in \mathcal{H}$ with continuous representative and $\psi(z) \neq 0$. Then $\psi \not\in \mathrm{im}(M - z)$ becaushe continuous represantitives in $\mathrm{im}(M - z)$ vanish at $x = z$ by some $L^2$ calculation.
