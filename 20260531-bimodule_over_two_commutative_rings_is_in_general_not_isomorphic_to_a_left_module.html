bimodule over two commutative rings is in general not isomorphic to a left module

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring. Then an $R,R$ bimodule is in general not uniquely determined by just the left action (or just the right action)

Proof

Recall that the category of bimodules is the category of modules over the tensor proudct with opposite ring So let e.g. $R = \mathbb{Z}[X]$, then an $\mathbb{Z}[X], \mathbb{Z}[X]$ bimodule is precisely a module over $\mathbb{Z}[X] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}[X]^{\mathrm{op}} \cong \mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$. Hence we may take an abelian group with two endomorphisms (e.g. any nonzero module) and find a suitable map $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y] \rightarrow \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M)$ where the right action does not coincide with the left action That is, it suffices to find an abelian group $M$ and e.g. $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]$.
