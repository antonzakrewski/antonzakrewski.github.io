n-truncated category is not an (infinity,1)-topos

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an n-truncated category not equivalent to the terminal category. Then $\mathcal{C}$ is not an $(\infty,1)$-topos.

Proof

Now w.l.o.g. assume that there exist objects $X,Y$ such that $\pi_{n-1}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)) \neq 0$, otherwise decrease the $n$. w.l.o.g. we may assume that $X = Y$ (cf. category with coproducts admits endomorphism with homotopy groups in maximal degree). Let $S^n$ and $*$ be the images of the unique colimit preserving functor $\mathrm{An} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$ (cf. presheaf category arises by freely adding colimits).

Now $S^n \cong \Sigma^n S^0 \cong *$ (cf. n-fold suspension is trivial in an n-truncated category).

Now assume that $\mathcal{C}$ is a topos. Then we have descent, i.e. induction shows that $\mathcal{C}_{/S^l} \simeq \mathrm{Fun}(S^l, \mathcal{C})$ for $l$. (cf.topos over the sphere)

so in particular, $\mathrm{Fun}(S^n, \mathcal{C}) \simeq \mathcal{C}_{/S^n} \cong \mathcal{C}_{/*}$. But the evaluation functor cannot be an equivalence, because for $Z$ as above, there exists a nonconstant map $S^n \rightarrow \{Z\} \subseteq \mathcal{C}$ which does not lie in the constant diagram functor.
