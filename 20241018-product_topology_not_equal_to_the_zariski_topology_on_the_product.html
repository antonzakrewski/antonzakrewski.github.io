product topology not equal to the zariski topology on the product

Proposition

Let $F$ be an algebraically closed field. Then the zariski topology $\mathbb{A}_K^n$ does not in general coincide with the product topology $\prod \mathbb{A}_K^1$.

Proof

counter example

Consider the zero locus of $Z(X - Y)$. Then it follows that the complement is open.

Thus let $(a,b) \in \mathbb{A}_K^2 \setminus Z(X - Y)$.

Assume that $\mathbb{A}_K^2 \setminus Z(X - Y)$ is open in the cofinite topology. Then there exists an open neighbourhood of $(a,b)$ of the form $U \times V$.

it follows that an $(a,b) \in U \times V$ if and only if $a \in U$ and $b \in V$. As $U,V$ are cofinite, there exist only finitely many $a'$ resp. $b'$ such that $(a',-) \not \in U, (-,b') \not\in V$.

But for each $x$ it follows that $(x,x) \not\in \mathbb{A}_K^2 \setminus Z(X - Y)$
