induced group homomorphism

Definition / Proposition

Let $G,H$ be groups, $N \trianglelefteq G$ a normal subgroup with $N \subseteq \mathrm{ker}(\varphi)$ for a homomorphism $\varphi: G \rightarrow H$ and $\pi_N: G \rightarrow G/N$ the canonical projection Then there exists a unique homomorphism such that following diagram commutes:

Proof

Let

\begin{align*} \overline{\varphi}: G/N \rightarrow& H \\ gN \mapsto& \varphi(g) \end{align*}

welldefined

Let $g'N = gN$ for $g,g' \in N$. Then there exists an $n \in N$ such that $g'n = g$. therefore

\begin{align*} \varphi(g) =& \varphi(g'n) \\ =& \varphi(g') \varphi(n) \\ =& \varphi(g') e \\ =& \varphi(g') \end{align*}

homomorphism

Let $gN, hN \in G/N$. Then

\begin{align*} \overline{\varphi(gN \cdot hN)} =& \varphi(g \cdot h) \\ =& \varphi(g) \cdot \varphi(h) \\ =& \overline{\varphi(gN)} \cdot \overline{\varphi(hN)} \end{align*}
