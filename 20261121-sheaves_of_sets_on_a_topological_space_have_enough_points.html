sheaves of sets on a topological space have enough points

Proposition

Let $X$ be a topological space. Then $\mathrm{Shv}(X)$ has enough points.

Proof

Let $\varphi: \mathcal{F} \rightarrow \mathcal{G}$ be an effective epimorphism of sheaves. In particular, effective epimorphism is an epimorphism in a 1,1 category shows that $\varphi$ is an epimorphism. Let $\tilde{C}(f)$ be the cech nerve and $\mathrm{colim} \tilde{C}(f) \rightarrow Y$ be the canonical map.

Then passing to stalks shows that $\mathrm{colim} \tilde{C}(f)_x \simeq \mathrm{colim} \tilde{C}(f_x) \rightarrow Y_x$ is an isomorphism: This follows, as taking stalks preserves epimorphism (cf. functor preserving pushouts preserves epimorphisms) and effective epimorphism in Set.

Now stalkwise characterization of isomorphisms in sheaves of sets shows the desired claim.
