category theory for commutative algebra

List

motivation

  • a huge part of pure math aims to understand mathematical structure
  • category theory structurully studies mathematical structure
  • hence many arguments/constructions can be done once in the general framework and the concrete statements follow as corollary (e.g. Isomorphism theorems).
  • category theory can also be guidance for mathematical questions:
    • it often removes unneccesary technicalities and highlights the structure
    • I like that having intuiton for category theory already always gives me (some) intuition for the special case

fields

  • essential:
    • algebraic topology
    • algebraic geometry
    • algebraic k-theory
    • higher algebra
    • homotopy theory
  • at least useful: for pure math:
    • although strictly speaking not necessary, many propositions/statements can be done categorically.
    • for example commutative algebra can be taught without categories. But if one is willing to introduce it, then there exist shortcuts for certain arguments/constructions
    • technically for everything we want to use it would be faster to do the specific case
    • but theorems from category theory can be applied to other arguments, so at least we will develop very useful theory

regarding intuition

  • category theory is sometimes viewed as devoid of intuiton and dry
  • in particular: it is normal to lack intuition when first learning it
  • We face the dilemma:
    • do We want to introduce commutative algebra first and then do category theory with examples from commutative algebra
    • do We want to introduce category theory first and then do commutative algebra which (hopefullyr provides intuition for category theory
  • typically when given a statement it first is completely unclear until one stares long enough at some diagrams and then eventually it will be trivial
  • the proofs are "formal nonsense":
    • most of the time "you do the only thing that you can" and it will work

convention

I will use category synonymously with (1,1)-Category. Whenever some link maps to some more general concept, e.g. (\infty,1)-categories - just ignore it.
