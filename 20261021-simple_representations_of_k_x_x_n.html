simple representations of k[x]/x^n

Proposition

Let $A = k[x]/(x^n)$. Then $A$ admits only one simple representation, namely $k$ with $x$ acting by zero.

Proof

Then $(x) \subseteq A$ is a maximal ideal (look at the quotient) and it is nilpotent So $\mathrm{Rad}(A) = (x)$ (one inclusion as it is maximal, the other as the radical contains all nilpotent ideals).

So artin-wedderburn implies that $A$ has 1-simple representation of dimension 1
