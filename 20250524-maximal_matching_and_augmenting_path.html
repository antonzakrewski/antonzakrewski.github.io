maximal matching and augmenting path

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph and $M$ a matching

TFAE:

  1. $M$ is a maximal matching
  2. there does not exist an augmenting path for a matching for $M$

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Suppose there exists an augmenting path for a matching $E$ Then in particular choosing the edges which dont lie in the matching $M$ provides a matching $M'$. Consider the symmetric difference

\begin{align*} \overline{M} \coloneqq M \Delta M' \end{align*}

which is a matching $\overline{M}$ with $\vert \overline{M} \vert = \vert M \vert + 1$ (todo)

2) $\implies$ 1)

Suppose there exists a bigger matching $M'$.

Let $A = M \cup M'$. w.l.o.g. assume that $A$ is connected, otherwise choose a $C_i \subseteq A$ which contains more edges of $M'$ than $M$, which has to exist since $\vert M' \vert > \vert M \vert$ (cf. Pigeonhole principle). Then choose an edge $v_1v_2 \in M' \setminus M$, where $v_1 \not\in M$, which has to exist since $M'$ contains more edges and more vertices than $M$.

Then if $v_2 \not\in M$, then $v_1v_2$ is an augmenting path. otherwise choose the unique vertex $w_2$ with $v_2w_2 \in M$ and $v_3 \in M'$ with $v_2v_3 \in M'$. Note that such a $v_3$ has to exist: there exists a vertex $v_l \neq v_1,v_2,v_3, v_l \in M$ which is connected to $v_1$ (since $\vert M' \vert > \vert M \vert$ and $A$ is connected.). In particular $v_1$ has by construction degree $1$, each other vertex has at most degree $2$ (since it has at most degree $1$ in $M$ and $M'$) and our choices for the path $v_1 \rightarrow v_l$ have been unique so far. Hence there exists an edge outgoing from $v_2$ to $v_3$ which necessarily lies in $M'$ (otherwise $M$ wouldn't be a matching).

Then repeat this algorithm, which has to terminate (when we cant choose an edge in $M$) since we only have finitely many edges and at each step our constructed path has to be circlefree (otherwise some vertex would have degree 3).

Then the constructed path provides an augmenting path, since by construction we alternate between $M$ and $M'$ and we start and end in $M' \setminus M$
