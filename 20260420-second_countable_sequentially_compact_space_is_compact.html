second countable, sequentially compact space is compact

Proposition

Let $X$ be a secondcountable, sequentially compact topological space. Then $X$ is also compact.

Proof

messy proof - sorry Suppose $X$ is not compact, i.e., there exists an open cover $X = \bigcup_{\alpha} U_\alpha$ such that every finite collection $U_{\alpha_1}, ...,U_{\alpha_n}$ does not cover $X$. We may w.l.o.g. assume that $\bigcup_{\alpha} U_{\alpha}= \bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} U_n$ is countable (second countable space is countably compact). w.l.o.g. we may assume that $B_{n} \coloneqq \bigcup_{i=1}^n U_n \cap X \setminus U_{n+1} \neq \emptyset$ after removing suitable $U_i$ and renumbering inductively and appropriately. If $\bigcup_{i=1}^n U_n \cap X \setminus U_{n+1} = \emptyset$, then we may remove $U_{n+1}$ (and renumber $U_{n+2},...$) and we may assume that we still have an infinite amount of $U_n$'s, as otherwise $U_1,...,U_n$ is a finite cover of $X$.

Now we choose for each $U_{n+1} \setminus B_n$ an element $a_n$ (with the convention $B_{-1} = \emptyset$). Then this gives a sequence $a_n$ which has a convergent subsequence $b_m$, which converges to some $b$. But $b$ lies in some $U_n$ and moreover $b_m$ converges to $b$, hence for $m >> 0$ it follows that $b_m \in U_n$. But this shows, that infinitely many $a_l$ lie in $U_n$, which is a contradiction for $l > n$, since we have choosen $a_l$ as element in $U_l \setminus B_{l-1} \subseteq U_l \setminus U_n$.
