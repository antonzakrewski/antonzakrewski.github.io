alternating dimensions of a long exact sequence

Proposition

Let $k$ be a field, $V_i$ be vector spaces and

an exact sequence.

Then

\begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i} \mathrm{dim}(V_i) = 0 \end{align*}

Proof

w.l.o.g. we may assume that $V_n, V_1 = 0$ by extending

where $V_n \rightarrow 0 \rightarrow 0$ is still exact at $0$ and adding/subtracting $\mathrm{dim}(0) = 0$ does not change the sum.

This follows as

\begin{align*} \mathrm{im}(0) = \mathrm{ker}(0 \rightarrow V_0) = 0 \end{align*}

and

\begin{align*} \mathrm{im}(V_n \rightarrow 0) = 0 = \mathrm{ker}(0) \end{align*}

Exactness shows that

\begin{align*} \mathrm{im}(f_{i-1}) = \mathrm{ker}(f_i) \end{align*}

where the fundamental theorem of vectorspace homomorphisms & rank theorem shows

\begin{align*} \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(f_i)) =& \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{im}(f_{i-1})) \\ =& \mathrm{dim}(V_{i-1}/\mathrm{ker}(f_{i-1})) \\ =& \mathrm{dim}(V_{i-1}) - \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(f_{i-1}) \end{align*}

or

\begin{align*} \mathrm{dim}(V_{i-1}) =& \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(f_i)) + \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(f_{i-1}) \end{align*}

Thus

\begin{align*} \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i} \mathrm{dim}(V_{i}) =& \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i} \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(f_i)) + \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(f_{i+1}) \\ =& \pm \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}(f_0)) \pm \mathrm{ker}(f_n) \\ =& \pm 0 \pm 0 \\ =& 0 \end{align*}
