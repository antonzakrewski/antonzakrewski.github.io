multiplier algebra of C_0

Proposition

Let $X$ be in CH_*. Then the multiplier algebra of the C_0 of $X$ is given by $C_b(X)$

Proof

$C_0(X) \subseteq C_b(X)$ essential ideal

Let $f \in C_0(X)$ with $f \neq 0$. Then choose an $x \in X$ such that $f(x) \neq 0$ and then use Tietze to get a function $h: X \rightarrow [0,1]$ with $h(x) = 1$_ Thne $(f \cdot h)(x) \neq 0$ which shows that $C_0(X)$ is essential.

surjective

so it suffices to show that the canonical map $C_b(X) \rightarrow C_0(X)$ is surjective. Let $(L,R) \in M(C_0(X))$ be arbitrary, then $L = R$ (cf. multiplier algebra of a commutative algebra). Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the collection of compact subsets of $X \setminus \{\infty\}$, ordered by inclusion and filtered.

For $K \in \mathcal{K}$ let $1_{K}$ be a function with $(1_{K}_{\vert K} = \mathrm{const}_1$ when restricted to $K$ and vanishing elsewhere.

Then consider $f \coloneqq \lim_{\mathcal{K}} L(1_{K})$ which exists:

First we show that for $K \subseteq K'$, we have $L(1_{K}) = L(1_{K'})$ when restricted to $K$. So let $k \in K$, then choose a function $h$ with $h(k) = 1$ and $\mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq K$, which exists by some nesting of closed subsets and Tietze.

Then $(L(1_{K}) \cdot h)(x)= L(1_{K} \cdot h )(x) = L(1_{K'} \cdot h )(x) = (L(1_{K'}) \cdot h)(x)$ which shows the claim.

It is a continuous function: let $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be open, then $f^{-1}[U] = \bigcup_{O \subseteq K} O \cap 1_{K}[U^{-1}]$ is the union of opens. Moreover, $\lim_{\mathcal{K}} L(1_{K})$ is bounded by $\| L \|$ where $L$ is bounded.

Now for any $g \in C_0(X)$, we have that multiplication with $g$ is continuous. Hence

\begin{align*} \lim_{\mathcal{K}} (L(1_{K})) \cdot g =& \lim_{\mathcal{K}} (L(1_{K}) \cdot g) \\ =& \lim_{\mathcal{K}} L(1_{K} \cdot g) \\ =& L(g) \end{align*}

where we use that $1_{K}$ is an approximate unit, which can be shown by some $\frac{\varepsilon}{n}$ estimate.
