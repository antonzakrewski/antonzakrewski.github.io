localization of a module homomorphism

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $S \subseteq R$ multiplicatively closed and $f: M \rightarrow N$ a module homomorphism for $R$-modules $M,N$. Then there exists unique \(S^{-1}R\)-homomorphism

\begin{align*} S^{-1}f: S^{-1}M \rightarrow S^{-1}N \end{align*}

for the localization of $M,N$ such that following diagram commutes

Proof

Let

\begin{align*} S^{-1}: S^{-1}M \rightarrow& S^{-1}N \\ \frac{m}{s} \mapsto \frac{f(m)}{s} \end{align*}

This map - if welldefined - is induced by assumption of commuting

welldefined

Let $\frac{m_1}{s_1} = \frac{m_2}{s_2}$, then there exists an $t \in S$ such that

\begin{align*} t \cdot (s_2 \cdot m_1 - s_1 \cdot m_2) = 0 \end{align*}

Hence applying $f$ we get

\begin{align*} 0 =& f(t \cdot (s_2 \cdot m_1 - s_1 \cdot m_2)) \\ =& t \cdot (s_2 \cdot f(m_1) - s_1 \cdot f(m_2)) \end{align*}

or equivalently

\begin{align*} \frac{f(m_1)}{s_1} = \frac{f(m_2)}{s_2} \end{align*}
