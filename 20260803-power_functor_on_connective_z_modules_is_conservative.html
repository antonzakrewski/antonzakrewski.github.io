n-th power functor on Z-modules detects zero objects

Proposition

The \(n\)-th power functor

\begin{align*} (-)^{\otimes n}: \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow& \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}) A \mapsto& A \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} A \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} ... \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} A \end{align*}

is conservative.

Proof

Let $A \in \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})$ be an object, then it splits as $A \simeq \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} H_n(A)$ (cf. object in the derived category of a hereditary ring splits). Hence it suffices to show this for an abelian group $A = \pi_0(A)$. Now assume that $A \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} A = 0$.

Here we may use that infinite extension of scalars is symmetric monoidal, hence

commutes.

In particular, the functor $(-)^{\otimes n}: \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{F}_p) \rightarrow \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{F}_p)$ detects the zero object (cf. derived category for a field, derived tensor product over a field), which shows that $A \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{F}_p$ is the zero object. Hence $A$ is uniquely p-divisible, which shows that $A$ is uniquely-p divisible for all primes or in other words, $A$ lies in $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q})$ (cf. category of rational spectra category of rational spectra is the derived category of Q)

But here the same argument shows that $A$ must be zero (cf. n-th power functor in the derived category over a field detects zero objects)
