Rad of k[x]/f

Proposition

Let $k$ be a field and $f \in k[x]$ a polynomial of the form $f = c \prod p_i^{e_i}$ for irreducible $p_i \in k[x]$, w.l.o.g. monic polynomial. Let $d = \sum_{i} e_i \mathrm{deg}(p_i)$ Then

  1. $\mathrm{Rad}(k[x]/f)$ is the ideal generated by $\prod p_i$
  2. has dimension $d - \sum \mathrm{deg}(p_i) = \sum (e_i - 1) \mathrm{deg}(p_i)$
  3. has nilpotence degree $\mathrm{max}(e_i)$

Proof

using the [[id:b86c4c1a-17c6-4c68-af9c-5d012e397ad1][classification of simple representations of k[x]/f]] & Artin-Wedderburn implies that we are interested in the kernel of the map

\begin{align*} A \rightarrow \bigoplus_{p_i} K[x]/p_i \end{align*}

where an element $f \in A$ lies in the kernel if and only if $p_i \mid f$ for all $i$. Hence it is of the form $\prod p_i \cdot c$ since each $p_i$ are irreducible, non associated element.

The dimension calculation then follows from the rank theorem.

To show the nilpotence degree we first observe that $k[x]/f$ is a commutative ring $\mathrm{Rad}(A)$ is a principal ideal domain. Then $(c \cdot p_1 \cdot ... \cdot p_n)^{\mathrm{max}(e_i)} = c \cdot p_1^{\mathrm{max}(e_1) \cdot ... p_n^{\mathrm{max}(e_n)}$ and this element lies in the ideal generated by $f$.

Moreover, $\mathrm{max}(e_i)$ is the minimal number since for $n < \mathrm{max}(e_i)$ we have that $p_{j}^{\mathrm{max}(e_i)} \not\mid (p_1 \cdot ... \cdot p_n)^{n}$ and $p_j^{\mathrm{max}(e_i)} \mid f$ for $e_j = \mathrm{max}(e_i)$.
