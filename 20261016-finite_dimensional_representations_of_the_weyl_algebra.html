finite dimensional representations of the weyl algebra in characteristic zero

Proposition

Let $\mathrm{char}(k) = 0$, then there exist only zero dimesnional representation of the Weyl algebra as finite dimensional representation.

Proof

Observe that a representation is equivalently given by two matrices, which satisfies $A \cdot B = B \cdot A + 1$. But the trace is a vectorspace-homomorphism and is invariant under cyclic permutation, so $\mathrm{tr}(A \cdot B) = \mathrm{tr}(B \cdot A +1) = \mathrm{dim}_k(V) + \mathrm{tr}(B \cdot A) = \mathrm{dim}_k(V) + \mathrm{tr}(A \cdot B)$. This implies $\mathrm{dim}_k(V) = 0$ (or that $V$ is not a finite dimensional/dualizable).

Proposition

Let $A$ be the Weyl algebra over $k$ for $\mathrm{char}(k) = 0$. Then there does not exist a nontrivial finite dimensional representation of $A$

Proof

Recall that $A = k \langle x,y\rangle/ \langle yx - xy - 1\rangle$. So in particular, we would have

\begin{align*} 0 =& \mathrm{tr}(0) \\ =& \mathrm{tr}(yx - xy - 1) \\ =& \mathrm{tr}(yx) - \mathrm{tr}(xy) - \mathrm{dim}_k(V) \\ =& - \mathrm{dim}_{k}(V) \end{align*}

which implies that $V$ is the zero space.
