fully faithfulness for adjoint triplets

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{L} \dashv \mathcal{F} \dashv \mathcal{R}$ be an adjoint pair. TFAE:

  1. $\mathcal{L}$ is fully faithful
  2. $\mathcal{R}$ is fully faithful

Proof

follows from:

  • fully faithful adjoint and abstract equivalence of unit/counit

where we have natural equivalence

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{F} \mathcal{L}(-), -) \simeq \mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{L}(-), \mathcal{R}(-)) \simeq \mathrm{Hom}(-, \mathcal{F }\mathcal{R}(-)) \simeq \end{align*}

and any unit / counit, which is an equivalence, induces an natural equivalence of the other with the identity functor.

Here we have used yoneda argument.

alternatively

Let $c,c' \in \mathcal{C}$ for $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$. Let $\eta_{\mathcal{L}}: \mathrm{id} \Rightarrow \mathcal{FL}$ be the unit of $\mathcal{L} \dashv \mathcal{F}$ and $\epsilon_{\mathcal{R}}: \mathcal{FR} \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ the counit of $\mathcal{F} \dashv \mathcal{R}$. Then we have a diagram 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{L}(c), \mathcal{R}(c')) \arrow[]{rd}{\mathcal{F}} \arrow[bend left]{rrd}{\simeq} \arrow[bend right]{ddr}{\simeq}& &  \\
& \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{FL}(c), \mathcal{FR}(c')) \rar{\eta_{\mathcal{L}}^*} \dar{\epsilon_{\mathcal{R}}_*} & \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c, \mathcal{FR}(c')) \dar{\epsilon_{\mathcal{R}}_*} \\
& \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c, \mathcal{FR}(c')) \rar{\eta_{\mathcal{L}}^*} & \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c,c') \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

so in particular 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{L}(c), \mathcal{R}(c')) \rar{\simeq} \dar{\simeq} & \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c, \mathcal{FR}(c')) \dar{\epsilon_{\mathcal{R}}_*} \\
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c, \mathcal{FR}(c')) \rar{\eta_{\mathcal{L}}^*} & \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c,c') \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

commutes.

Now by yoneda, $\eta_{\mathcal{L}}^*$ is an equivalence if and only if $\eta_{\mathcal{L}}$ is an equivalence (and similarly for $\epsilon_{\mathcal{R}}$. Since equivalences satisfy a two out of three principle, we get that $\eta_{\mathcal{R}}_*$ is an equivalence if and only if $\eta_{\mathcal{L}}^*$ is one.
