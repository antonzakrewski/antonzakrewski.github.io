Proof

consider the quotient star algebra $B \coloneqq \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x(1 + y^2) - 1)$ for $x^* = x, y^* = y$. Then let $A = \mathbb{C}[x] \subseteq B = \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x(1 + y^2) - 1)$. Then maximal seminorm of a star algebra is in general not non infinity shows that $\| x \|_{\mathrm{max}, A} = \infty$ but we claim that $\| x \|_{B, 1}$. It is at least $1$ since $x \rightarrow \mathrm{id}, y \mapsto 0$ is a map of star algebras satisfying the relation and mapping self adjoint operators to self adjoint operators. To show that it is at most $1$, it suffices to prove that for any self-adjoint operator $y$, $\| (1 + y^2) x \| \geq \| x \|$. Then the inverse must have norm $\leq 1$.

w.l.o.g. assume $\| x \| = 1$, then we have

\begin{align*} \langle x, (1 + y^2) x\rangle =& \langle x,x\rangle + \langle x,y^2 x\rangle \\ =& \| x \| + \langle yx, yx\rangle \\ =& \| x \|^2 + \| y x \|^2 \end{align*}

Then $\| y x \|^2$ is nonnegative, which implies $\| x \| \leq \langle x, (1 + y^2) x\rangle$. Now using cauchy schwarz shows

\begin{align*} \| x \| \leq \langle x, (1 + y^2)x\rangle \leq \| x \| \cdot \| (1 + y^2) x \| \end{align*}

which shows $\| (1 + y^2) x \| \geq 1$.
