projection theorem

Proposition

Let $X$ be a Hilbert space and $A$ a closed subset Then for all $x \in X$ there exists a unique $a \in A$ and a unique $w \in A^{\bot}$ such that $x = a + w$

Proof

Let $x \in X$ let $a \in A$ be such that $d(x,a) = \mathrm{dist}(x,A)$ (cf. distance in a hilbert space takes minimum). We claim that $w \coloneqq x - a$ lies in $A^{\bot}$.

Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{K}$ and $y \in A$. Then

\begin{align*} \| w \|^2 \leq \| x - (a + \lambda y) \|^2 = \| w h|^2 + \|\lambda y\|^2 - 2 \mathrm{Re}( \langle w, \lambda y\rangle) \end{align*}

where $(a + \lambda y)$ lies in $A$ (and $w = x - a$ is chosen such that it is minimal) and $x - (a + \lambda y) = w - \lambda y$. So for $0 \neq y \in A$ we have (after subtracting $\| w \|^2$ and dividing by $\| y \|$)

\begin{align*} \vert \lambda \vert^2 - \frac{2 \mathrm{Re}( \langle w, \lambda y\rangle)}{\| y \|^2} \geq 0 \end{align*}

For $\lambda = t \in \mathbb{R}$ we obtain

\begin{align*} t^2 - \frac{2 \mathrm{Re}( \langle w, y\rangle)}{\| y \|^2} t \geq 0 \end{align*}

for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$ which implies $\mathrm{Re}( \langle w,y\rangle = 0$ for sufficiently small $t > 0$.

So this shows (if $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{R}$) that $\langle w,y\rangle = 0$ for all $y \in A$ or $w \in A^{\bot}$. So now it remains to prove the case $\mathbb{K} = \mathbb{C}$. Then let $\mathbb{K} = \mathrm{i} t$ and we get

\begin{align*} t^2 + \frac{2 \mathrm{Im}( \langle w, y\rangle)}{\| y \|^2} t \geq 0 \end{align*}

which shows $\mathrm{Im}( \langle,y \rangle) = 0$ for all $y \in A$. This also shows -- together with the previous argument -- that $\langle w,y\rangle = 0$ for all $y \in A$ or $w \in A^{\bot}$.

uniqueness

Assume that $x = w + a = w' + a'$ with $w,w' \in A^{\bot}$ and $a,a' \in A$. Then $w - w' = a' - a$ where the LHS lies in $A^{\bot}$ and the RHS lies in $A$. But $A \cap A^{\bot} = \{0\}$ and thus $w = w', a = a'$.
