separable dual space implies original space is separable

Proposition

Let $X$ be a normed vector space, such that the dual space $X^*$ is a separable. Then so is $X$.

Proof

Let $A = \{f_n \in X^* : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ be dense and countable (which exists by hypothesis). For $n \in \mathbb{N}$ chooes an $x_n \in X$ such that $\| x_n \| = 1$ and $\vert f_n(x_n) \vert \geq \frac{\| f_n \|_*}{2}$ (e.g. choose any element $k$ in the kernel and add it if required).

Let $D \coloneqq \mathrm{span}(x_n) \subseteq X$ and we claim that $D$ is dense and contains the dense subset $\mathbb{Q} + i \mathbb{Q} \{x_n\}$.

Suppose that $D$ is not dense, then there exists an $z \in X$ such that $\mathrm{dist}(z,D) > 0$. Then there exists an $z \in X$ such that $\mathrm{dist}(z,D) > 0$. By separating a point from a subspace by a linear functional there exists a linear functional $f \in X^*$ with $D \subseteq \mathrm{ker}(f)$ and $f(z) > 0$.

Now $A$ is dense in $X^*$, hence there exists a sequence $(f_{n_k})_k \subseteq A$ such that $\| f_{n_k} - f \|_* \xrightarrow{k \rightarrow \infty} 0$

But meanwhile

\begin{align*} \| f_{n_k} - f \|_* \geq \vert f_{n_k}(x_{n_k}) - f(x_{n_k}) \vert = \vert f_{n_k}(x_{n_k}) - 0 \vert \geq \frac{1}{2} \| f_{n_k} \|_* \end{align*}

This shows $\| f_{n_k} \|_* \xrightarrow{k \rightarrow \infty} 0$ as the LHS goes to $0$, which implies $f_{n_k} \xrightarrow{k \rightarrow \infty} 0$ in $X^*$ so $f = 0$.

This is a contradiction.
