yoneda n-extension is a special k-invariant

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an abelian category, $\mathcal{D}^b(\mathcal{C})$ the bounded derived category with t-structure . Then there exists a bijection between

  1. n extensions of $N$ by $M$ up to equivalence
  2. equivalence classes of object $X$ with exactly two nontrivial homotopy/homology groups in degree $1,n$: $\pi_1(X) \cong N$ and $\pi_{n}(X) = M$.
  3. $\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{C}}^n(N,M) = \pi_0(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(N,M[n])$

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2), 2) $\implies$ 1)

Recall that two n extensions -- i.e. exact sequences $0 \rightarrow M \rightarrow E_n \rightarrow ... \rightarrow E_1 \rightarrow N \rightarrow 0$ -- are equivalent if and only if there exists a zig-zag of morphisms of morphisms, which induce an isomorphism (resp. equality after identifying with the isomorphism) on $M$ and $N$. Now we may regard $C \coloneqq 0 \rightarrow ... \rightarrow E_n \rightarrow ... \rightarrow E_1 \rightarrow 0 \rightarrow 0 \rightarrow ...$ as chain complex and then exactness above implies that this chain complex has nontrivial homology exactly in degrees $n$ and $1$: $H_n(C) \cong M, H_1(C) \cong N$. Moreover, a morphism between such chain complexes -- which induces an isomorphism on the kernel / cokernel -- is a quasi-isomorphism, since every other homology is trivial.

3) $\implies$ 2), 2) $\implies$ 3)

Let $f: N \rightarrow M[n]$ be an element in $\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{C}}^{n}(N,M)$, then we may take the cofiber and get a cofiber sequence $N \rightarrow M[n] \rightarrow \mathrm{cofib}(f)$. then the long exact sequence in the heart for a t-structure shows that $\pi_1(\mathrm{cofib}(f)) \cong \pi_1(N[1]) \cong N, \pi_n(\mathrm{cofib}(f)) \cong \pi_n(M[n)) \cong M$ since $N$ and $M[n]$ have heavily concentrated homology (in fact - only homology in one degree)

Remark

This only works in $\mathcal{D}^b(\mathcal{A})$ of abelian categories $\mathcal{A}$ (maybe other 1-categories ?): The reason is, that an n-extension only sees morphisms in the heart and e.g. spectra has more objects and morphisms than D(Z).
