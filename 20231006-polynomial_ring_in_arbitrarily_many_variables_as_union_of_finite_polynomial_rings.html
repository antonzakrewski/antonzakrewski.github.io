polynomial ring in arbitrarily many variables as union of finite polynomial rings

Proposition

Let \(R\) be a ring and $R[X_i \vert i \in I]$ a polynomial ring for arbitrarily many variables. Then

\begin{align*} R[X_i] = \bigcup_{J \subseteq I} R[X_j \vert j \in J] && J \text{ finite set} \end{align*}

Proof

a)

for each $R[X_j]$, since $J \subseteq I$, we conclude, that $R[X_j] \subseteq R[X_i]$ Hence

\begin{align*} R[X_i] \supseteq \bigcup_{J \subseteq I} R[X_j \vert j \in J] && J \text{ finite set} \end{align*}

b)

Let $f \in R[X_i]$. Then by definition there exist only finitely many monomials with finitely many occurences of $X_i$ Thus there only exist finitely many occurences of $i \in I$, hence there exists a finite set $J$ with $

\begin{align*} f \in R[X_j \vert j \in J] \end{align*}
