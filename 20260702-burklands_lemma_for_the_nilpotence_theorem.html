Burklunds lemma for the nilpotence theorem

Proposition

There is a sequence $C_i \in \mathrm{Syn}_\mathrm{MU}^{\omega}$ a with maps $\mathbb{S}^{0,0} \rightarrow C_i$ such that

  1. $\mathrm{slope}(C_i) \rightarrow 0$ as $i \rightarrow \infty$
  2. the underlying spectrum of $C_i$ splits as sum of spheres with $\mathbb{S}^{0,0} \rightarrow C_i$ the inclusion of one summand

Proof

Achims PhD theorem for the slope define inductively $C_{i+1} = C_i/\nu$ for an appropriate self map $\nu: \Sigma^{n,w} C_i \rightarrow C_i$.

Inductively, the nuderlying spectrum of $C_i$ is a sum of spheres, so $\nu$ acts on it by a matrix of entries in $\pi_*(\mathbb{S})$.

Then nishidas nilpotence theorem, the matrix is nilpotent (one can show that each entry is of positive degrees; todo)
