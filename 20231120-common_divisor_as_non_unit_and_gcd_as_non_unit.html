common divisor as non-unit and gcd as non-unit

Proposition

Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $a,b \in A$ such that $c \in A \setminus A^{\times}$ is a common divisor of $a,b$. Then the greatest common divisor $\mathrm{gcd}(a,b)$, if it exists, is not a unit

Proof

let $g \coloneqq \mathrm{gcd}(a,b)$, then by assumption

\begin{align*} c \mid g \end{align*}

Suppose $g$ is a unit, then it follows, that $c$ is also a unit, as for $c \cdot c' = g$, it follows that

\begin{align*} (c \cdot c') \cdot g^{-1} = 1 \end{align*}
