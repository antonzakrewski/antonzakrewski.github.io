equicontinuous collection of maps is uniformly equicontinuous for a compact space

Proposition

Let $X$ be a compact metric space. Suppose $\mathcal{F} \subseteq C(X)$ is a collection of continuous maps.

TFAE:

  1. $\mathcal{F}$ is equicontinuous
  2. $\mathcal{F}$ is uniformly equicontinuous

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Suppose that there exists an $\varepsilon > 0$, such that for $\delta > 0$, then collection $\mathcal{F}$ is not uniformly equicontinuous for this combination $\epsilon$ and $\delta$. Then choose a sequence $\delta_n \rightarrow 0$, and corresponding $f_n$ and points $x_n$, where $f_n(B_{\delta_n}(x_n)) \subsetneq B_{\varepsilon}(f_n(x_n))$. Now choose a convergent subsequence $x_{n_k} \rightarrow x$ (since $X$ is sequentially compact / compact) and then by assumption, there exists an $\delta > 0$ such that $f_i(B_{\delta}(x)) \subseteq B_{\varepsilon}(f_i(x))$ for all $f_i \in \mathcal{F}$. This is a contradiction: choose $n >> 0$ such that $d(x,x_n) < \frac{\delta}{2}$ and $\delta_n < \frac{\delta}{2}$, which exists as both sequences converge to $x$ and $0$ respectively. Now $B_{\delta_n}(x_n) \subseteq B_{\delta}(x)$ (triangle inequality) and hence $f_n[B_{\delta_n}(x_n)] \subseteq f_n[B_{\delta}(x)] \subseteq B_{\varepsilon}(f_n(x))$

2) $\implies$ 1)
