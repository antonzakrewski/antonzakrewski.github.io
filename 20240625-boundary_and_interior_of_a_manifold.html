boundary and interior of a manifold as disjoint union

Proposition

Let $W$ be a \(n+1\)-dimensional topological manifold with boundary Then $W$ is a disjoint union of the boundary of a manifold and the interior of a manifold

Proof

Note that each point admits a chart, hence is at least in one of the boundary or the interior, as

\begin{align*} \mathbb{R}^n \times (\infty,0) \cup \mathbb{R}^n \times [0] = \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \end{align*}

It remains to show disjointness, i.e. that a point $x$ in a boundary is not an interior point:

Consider the local singular homology and excision for local homology: Then

\begin{align*} H_n(W, W \setminus \{x\}) \simeq H_n(U, U \setminus \{x\}) \simeq H_n(\mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}, \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \setminus \{\varphi(x)\}) \end{align*}

Then by relative homology of two contractible spaces this is $0$.

But $H_n(W, W \setminus \{x\})$ for an interior point is given by

\begin{align*} H_n(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}, \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \setminus \varphi(x)) \end{align*}

after possibly restricting $\mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ to a neighbourhood isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.
