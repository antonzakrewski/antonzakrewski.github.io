noetherian space and minimal element of finite intersections of closed subsets

Proposition

Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a topological space

  1. $X$ is a noetherian space
  2. Let $S_i \subseteq X$ be family of subsets and $\mathcal{S}$ the set of all finite intersections.

Then there exists a minimal element

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Proof by contraposition: Let $S_i \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ be a subset. Suppose there doesn't exist a minimal element for $\mathcal{S}$. Then we can inductively construct a sequence $\mathcal{A}_1 \supseteq \mathcal{A}_2 ...$ with $\mathcal{A}_1 = S_{i(1)}$ and $\mathcal{A}_{n+1} = \mathcal{A}_n \cap S_{i(n+1)}$ Therefore, the chain $(\mathcal{A}_{n})$ can't stabilize, since then $\mathcal{A}_m$ would be a minimal element

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let $S_1 \supseteq S_2 \supseteq ...$ be a Chain. Then $\mathcal{S}$ contains a minimal element $S = \bigcap_{j = 1}^n S_{i(j)}$ and w.l.o.g. we can assume $S = S_{\mathrm{max}(i(j))}$, since $S_{j(i)} \supseteq S_{\mathrm{max}(i(j))}$ Therefore we conclude

\begin{align*} S_{\mathrm{max}(i(j))} \subseteq S_n \forall n \in \mathbb{N} \end{align*}

because otherwise, $S_n \cap S \subseteq S_{\mathrm{max}(i(j))}$ and hence $S$ wouldn't be a minimal element.
