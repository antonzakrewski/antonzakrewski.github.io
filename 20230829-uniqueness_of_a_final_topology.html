uniqueness of a final topology

Proposition

Let $X$ be a set, $(X_i,\mathcal{T}_i)$ be a class of topological spaces and $f_i: X_i \rightarrow X$ be a class of set-theoretic maps Then the final topology is unique.

Proof

Suppose $(X, \mathcal{T})$ and $(X, \mathcal{T}')$ are final topologies Then, since $f_i: (X_i, \mathcal{T}_i) \rightarrow (X,\mathcal{T}')$ is continuous, we conclude that $\mathrm{id}_{X}: (X, \mathcal{T}) \rightarrow (X,\mathcal{T}')$ is by universal property also continuous 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
X_i \arrow[]{rd}{f_i} \arrow[]{r}{f_i} & (X, \mathcal{T}) \arrow[]{r}{\mathrm{id}_{X}}\\
& (X,\mathcal{T}') \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

Hence by reasons of symmetrie, we get $\mathcal{T} \subseteq \mathcal{T}', \mathcal{T}' \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ (see: Feinere Topologie und Identitätsabbildung) and hence $\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{T}'$
