limits in a bousfield localization

Proposition

Let $\iota: \mathcal{C} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}$ be a bousfield localization Then the inclusion reflects and preserves limits and $\mathcal{C}$ contains all limits which exist in $\mathcal{D}$

Proof

preserves

follows as right adjoint preserves limits

reflects

Let

\begin{align*} I: \mathcal{I} \rightarrow \mathcal{C} \end{align*}

be an diagram and $\iota(I): \mathcal{I} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ the composition with the inclusion. Suppose furthermore that the limit $\mathrm{lim} \iota(I(-))$ in $\mathcal{D}$ exists.

By assumption there exists a collection of morphisms $S$ such that $\mathcal{C}$ is the subcategory of S-local objects (cf.S-local object and localization), i.e.

\begin{align*} \mathrm{map}(f,c) \end{align*}

is an equivalence for $f \in S$ and $c \in \mathcal{C}$.

it suffices to show that

\begin{align*} \mathrm{map}(-,\mathrm{lim} \iota(I(-))): \mathrm{An} \rightarrow \mathrm{An} \end{align*}

maps $f \in S$ to an equivalence.

But mapping anima functor preserves limits, so

\begin{align*} \mathrm{map}(f,\mathrm{lim} I(-) ) \simeq \mathrm{lim} \mathrm{map}(f,I(-)) \end{align*}

where by assumption $I(-)$ restricts to $S$ local objects, hence the $\mathrm{map}(f,I(-))$ is a diagram consisting only of equivalences. in particular using natural equivalence between infinity functors and objectwise equivalence this is a natural equivalence between infinity functors, so using functorality of the limit functor this maps to an equivalence.

Since $f$ was arbitrary, this holds for each element in $S$. Hence $\mathrm{lim} I(-)$ is S-local and thus lies in $\mathcal{C}$. In particular fully faithful functor reflects limits
