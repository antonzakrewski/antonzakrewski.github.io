algebraic tensor product lemma for c star algebras

Proposition

Let $A,B,C \in \mathrm{C}^*-\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{nu}}$. Assume that there exists a map

\begin{align*} \rho: A \otimes^{\mathrm{alg}} B \rightarrow C \end{align*}

such that it has dense image.

  1. Then there exist homomorphism $\alpha: A \rightarrow M(C)$, $\beta: B \rightarrow M(c)$ such that
\begin{align*} \rho(a \otmies b) = \alpha(a) \beta(b) \end{align*}
  1. In particular, $\| \rho(a \otimes b) \| \leq \| a \| \| b \|$.
  2. Moreover, $[\alpha(a), \beta(b)] = 0$
  3. Furthermore, $\alpha,\beta$ are nondegenerate

Proof

uniqueness

assume $\alpha, \beta$ exist. Then

\begin{align*} \alpha(a) \cdot \rho(a' \otimes \beta) =& \alpha(a) \alpha(a') \beta(b) \\ =& \alpha(a a') \beta(b) \\ =& \rho(a a' \otimes b) \end{align*}

Therefore define $\alpha(a) = (L_a,R_a)$ for $L_{\alpha}(\rho(\sum_i \alpha_i \otimes b_i)) = \sum_{i} \rho(a \alpha_i \otimes b_i)$.

We need to show that this is well-defined, so let $v_{\nu}$ be an approximate unit for $B$

\begin{align*} \sum_{i} \rho(a a_i \otimes b_i) =& \lim_{\nu} \sum \rho( a a_i \otimes v_{\nu} b_i) \\ =& \lim_{} \end{align*}

Here we use that $b \mapsto \rho(a \otimes b)$ is continuous: If $a$ is self-adjoint, then it preserves $*$ homomorphisms. If $a \<eq 0$, then the function is positive function, hence also continuous (cf. linear, positive function is continuous).

commute

Consider

Consider

\begin{align*} \alpha(a) \beta(b) \rho(c \otimes d) =& \alpha(a) \cdot \rho(ac \otimes d) \\ =& \rho(ac \otimes bd) \\ =& \rho(a \otimes b) \rho(c \otimes d) \end{align*}

which shows $\alpha(a) \beta(b) = \rho(a \otimes b)$

But we also have

\begin{align*} \rho(ac \otimes bd) =& \beta(b) \rho(ac \otimes d) \\ =& \beta(b) \alpha(a) \rho(c \otimes d) \end{align*}

which shows the claim, as the image is dense.

non degenerate

Ltet $\beta(v_{\nu})$ for an approximate unit $v_{\nu}$, then consider

\begin{align*} \beta(v_{\nu}) \rho(c \otimes d) =& \rho(c \otimes v_{\nu} d) \rightarrow \rho(c \otimes d) \end{align*}

which shows that every $\rho(c \otimes d)$ can be approximated. But the span of $\rho(c \otimes d)$ is dense by assumption.
