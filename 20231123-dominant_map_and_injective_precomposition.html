dominant map between affine varieties and injective precomposition

Proposition

Let $K$ be a field, $\mathbb{A}_{K}^n$ the affine space and $W \subseteq \mathbb{A}_{K}^n, V \subseteq \mathbb{A}_K^m$ be affine varieties. Suppose $f: V \rightarrow W$ is a rational map

TFAE:

  1. $f$ is a dominant rational map
  2. the precomposition $f^{ \rightarrow}: A(Y) \rightarrow k(X)$ is injective, where $A(Y)$ is the affine coordinate ring and $k(X)$ the function field

Proof

Let $g \in A(Y)$. Then $g \in \mathrm{ker}(f^*)$ is equivalent to $g \circ f = 0$ in $K(X)$. This holds if and only if $f(\mathrm{def}(f)) \subseteq Z(g)$ maps to the zero locus of $g$

hence $f^*$ is not injective if and only if there exists an $0 \neq g$ such that

\begin{align*} f(\mathrm{def})) \subseteq Z(g) \subsetneq Y \end{align*}

i.e.

\begin{align*} \overline{f(\mathrm{def}(f))} \subsetneq Z(g) \end{align*}
