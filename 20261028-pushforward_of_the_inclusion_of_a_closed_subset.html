stalks direct image sheaf of the inclusion of an closed subset

Proposition

Let $\iota: Z \hookrightarrow X$ be a closed subset and $j: O \hookrightarrow X$ the open complement Then for any presheaf $\mathcal{F}$, we have

\begin{align*} \iota_*( \mathcal{F}))_{x} \cong \begin{cases} \mathcal{F}_{x} & \mbox{if } x \in Z \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

Proof

good

$x \in Z$

Here we have a map of posets (for the $\supseteq$)

\begin{align*} \{O \vert x \in O \subseteq X\} \rightarrow \{V \vert x \in V \subseteq Z \} \end{align*}

which is coinitial morphism of directed posets (even surjective). Now the colimit over the LHS computes $\iota_*(\mathcal{F}_{x})$ and the colimit of the RHS computes $\mathcal{F}_{x}$

Hence coinitial morphism of directed posets is coinitial shows this.

$x \in U$

Here the map

\begin{align*} \{O \vert x \in O \subseteq U\} \rightarrow \{O \vert x \in O\} \end{align*}

is again coinitial, since for any $O$, we have $O \supseteq O \cap U$. Now $j_*(\mathcal{F})$ is the constant, $\{*\}$ valued sheaf or in other words, the terminal object. Moreover, taking stalks is left exact, hence preserves the terminal object.

elementary
