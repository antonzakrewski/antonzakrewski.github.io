ring structure on the power set

Satz

Sei $X$ eine Menge, dann ist $(\mathcal{P}(X), \Delta, \cap)$ ein Ring mit

Beweis

good proof

Consider $\mathbb{F}_2$ as ring, in particular as Ring Object in category set. Then $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Set}}(X,\mathbb{F}_2)$ inherits a ring structure (cf. functor preserving limits preserves ring objects) as $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Set}}(X,-)$ preserves limits (cf. covariant hom-functor preserves small limits). Furthermore there exists a canonical isomorphism

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Set}}(X,\mathbb{F}_2) \cong& \mathcal{P}(X) \\ f \mapsto& f^{-1}[1] \end{align*}

where one can check that

  1. $\cap$ corresponds to addition in $\mathbb{F}_2$
  2. the symmetric difference corresponds to multiplication

1)

here we multiply pointwise, i.e. for $f_1,f_2 \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Set}}(X,\mathbb{F}_2)$

\begin{align*} (f_1 \cdot f_2)(x) =& f_1(x) \cdot f_2(x) \\ =& \begin{cases} 1 & \mbox{if } f_1(x), f_2(x) = 1 \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

Hence we get $x \in (f_1 \cdot f_2)^{-1}[1]$ if and only if $x \in f_1^{-1}[1], x \in f_1^{-1}[1]$ which corresponds to the intersection.

2)

Addition is again pointwise, i.e.

\begin{align*} (f_1 + f_2)(x) =& f_1(x) + f_2(x) \\ =& \begin{cases} 1 & \mbox{if } f_1(x) \neq f_2(x) \\ 0 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

which is precisely the symmetric difference.

coherences

In particular we get all coherences (associativity, commutativity, distributivity) for free
