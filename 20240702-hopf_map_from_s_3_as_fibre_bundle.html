hopf map from S^3 as fibre bundle

Proposition

The hopf map from S^3

\begin{align*} S^3 \rightarrow& \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^1 \simeq S^2\\ (z_0,z_1) \mapsto& [z_0 : z_1] \end{align*}

where $S^3 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^4 \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$

is a Fibre bundle with fibres homeomorphic to $S^1$

Proof

Let $[z_0 : z_1] \in \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^1$. Then w.l.o.g. we may assume that $z_0 \neq 0$.

Define

\begin{align*} \pi: S^1 \times [- \varepsilon, \varepsilon] \times [- \varepsilon, \varepsilon] \rightarrow& \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^1 \\ (x,y,z) \mapsto& [\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} 2\pi x} \cdot (z_0 + y) : \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} 2\pi x} \cdot (z_1 + z)] \end{align*}

Define

\begin{align*} \varphi: S^1 \times [- \varepsilon, \varepsilon] \times [- \varepsilon, \varepsilon] \rightarrow& S^3 \\ (x,y,z) \mapsto& (\mathrm{e}^{i 2 \pi x} (z_0 + y), (\mathrm{e}^{i 2 \pi x} (z_1 + z))/\lambda \end{align*}

where $\lambda = \|((z_0 + y), (z_1 + z))\|$ is the factor such that the point lies in the sphere.

Then as $\pi$ factors through $\varphi$ it follows that $\varphi$ is continuous.

Furthermore we get the evident diagram

\begin{align*} U \coloneqq \mathrm{im}(S^1 \times (- \varepsilon, \varepsilon) \times (-\varepsilon, \varepsilon)) \end{align*}

Note that $U$ is open (cf. surjective map from a compact space to a hausdorff space as quotient map). Hence restriction to $\mathrm{hopf}^{-1}[U]$ is welldefined blabla
