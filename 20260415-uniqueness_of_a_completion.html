uniqueness of a completion of a metric space

Proposition

Let $X$ be a metric space, then a completion is unique up to unique isometry.

Proof

Let $X \hookrightarrow M, X \hookrightarrow M'$ be completions. Then we need to show that there exists a unique isometry $M \rightarrow M'$ over $X$. In particular:

  1. $X$ is dense in $M,M'$
  2. $M,M'$ are complete
  3. the inclusions $X \xhookrightarrow{\iota} M, X \xhookrightarrow{\iota'} M'$ are 1-lipschitz

By extension of a lipschitz map from a dense subspace to a complete space & restriction of a map to dense subspace in a Hausdorff space there exist unique 1-lipschitz maps

But the unique extension to $X \xhookrightarrow{\iota} M$ is given by the identity

and analogously for $X \xhookrightarrow{\iota'} M$.

Hence by uniqueness, the compositions $\overline{\iota} \circ \overline{\iota'}, \overline{\iota'} \circ \overline{\iota}$ are the respective identities.

In particular, $\overline{\iota}, \overline{\iota'}$ are 1-lipschitz bijections, so it remains to show that both are isometries: This follows, as for $m_1,m_2 \in M$

\begin{align*} d_M(m_1,m_2) \leq& 1 d_{M'}(\overline{\iota'}(m_1), \overline{\iota}(m_2)) \\ \leq& 1^2 d_{M}(\overline{\iota \circ \iota'}(m_1),\overline{\iota \circ \iota'}(m_2)) \\ =& d_M(m_1,m_2) \end{align*}

hence $d_M(m_1,m_2) = 1 d_{M'}(\overline{\iota'}(m_1), \overline{\iota}(m_2))$. Thus $\overline{\iota}$ and analogously $\overline{\iota'}$ are isometries.
