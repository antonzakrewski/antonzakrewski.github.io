finite coproduct in the category of propositions as disjunction

Proposition

Given the category of propositions $\mathrm{Prop}$ and Propositions $A_1,...,A_n$, the categorical coproduct is the disjunction

\begin{align*} A_1 \lor ... \lor A_n \end{align*}

Proof

inclusion

By definition, it holds, that

\begin{align*} A_i \vdash A_1 \lor ... \lor A_n \end{align*}

Therefore it follows from the introduction rule for the disjunction, that there exist morphisms

universal property

Suppose there exists a proposition $B$, such that for all $A_i$, $A_i \vdash B$. Then by elimination rule for the disjunction, there exists a morpishm $A_1 \lor ... \lor A_n \rightarrow B$

commuting

furthermore, the diagram commutes, because $\mathrm{Prop}$ is by definition a posetal category (cf. commuting in a posetal category)
