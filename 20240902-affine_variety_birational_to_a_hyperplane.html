affine variety birational equivalent to a hyperplane

Proposition

Let $K$ be a algebraically closed field, $\mathbb{A}_{K}^n$ the affine space and $X \subseteq \mathbb{A}_{K}^n$ be an affine variety. Then $X$ is birational equivalent to an affine Hyperebene

Proof

Let $K(X) = k(x_1,...,x_n)$ be the function field of an affine variety. Then $K(X)$ is a finitely generated field extension over $k$.

Then as algebraically closed field are perfect, it follows that $k(x_1,...,x_n)/k$ is a separable field extension. By existence of a maximal purely transcendental field extension we may find a purely transcendental field extension $k(y_1,...,y_m)/k$ and an algebaric field extension $k(x_1,...,x_n)/k(y_1,...,y_m)$.

By Satz vom primitiven Element this is generated by a single element $z$, i.e. $k(x_1,...,x_n) = k(y_1,...,y_m)(z)$

Sei $k(y_1,...,y_m) \coloneqq L$

Sei $H \in L[x]$ das Minimalpolynom von $z$. Sei $G$ das Produkt der Polynome, welche bei den keffizienten im Nenner von $H$ vorkommen (cf. Polynomring über einem Körper als faktorieller Ring) Dann ist

\begin{align*} GH \in K[y_1,...,y_m][T] \end{align*}

Da die $\{y_1,...,y_m\}$ algebraisch unabhängig ist, ist $K[y_1,...,y_m][T]$ auch faktoriell.

By Gauss-Lemma we may conclude that $G \cdot H$ is irreducible in $K[y_1,...,y_m][T]$ and $G\cdot H$ defines an affine variety via

\begin{align*} Y \coloneqq Z(G \cdot H) = \mathbb{A}_k^{d+1} \end{align*}

By construction we have

\begin{align*} k(Y) \cong L(z) \end{align*}

Using equivalence between the category of affine varieties and dominant rational maps and finitely generated field extension we may find a unique isomorphism between $Y$ and $X$, i.e. a birational equivalence
