fundamental theorem of ring homomorphism

Proposition

Let $R$ be a ring, $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq R$ an ideal and $\pi: R \rightarrow R/\mathfrak{a}$ the canonical projection to the quotient ring. Given another ring $S$ and a ring-homomorphism $\varphi: R \rightarrow S$ such that $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathrm{ker}(\varphi)$, there exists a unique homomorphism $h: R/\mathfrak{a} \rightarrow S$, such that following diagram commutes

Proof

uniqueness

Suppose we have $\psi_1,\psi_2$ satisfying the condition. then we get

\begin{align*} \psi_1 \circ \pi = \psi_2 \circ \pi \end{align*}

Since $\pi$ is surjective, by surjective map as epimorphism we conclude, that $\pi$ is an epimorphism. Hence it follows, that

\begin{align*} \psi_1 = \psi_2 \end{align*}

existence

Let

\begin{align*} \psi: R/\mathfrak{a} \rightarrow& S \\ r + \mathfrak{a} \mapsto& \varphi(r) \end{align*}

welldefined map

Let $r + \mathfrak{a} = r' + \mathfrak{a}$. Then there exists an $a \in \mathfrak{a}$ such that $r + a = r'$. Therefore,

\begin{align*} \psi(r + \mathfrak{a}) =& \varphi(r) \\ =& \varphi(r) + 0 \\ =& \varphi(r) + \varphi(a) \\ =& \varphi(r + a) \\ =& \varphi(r') \\ =& \psi(r' + N) \end{align*}

additive
\begin{align*} \psi( r + \mathfrak{a} + r' + \mathfrak{a}) =& \psi(r + r' + \mathfrak{a}) \\ =& \varphi(r + r') \\ =& \varphi(r) + \varphi(r') \\ =& \psi(r + \mathfrak{a}) + \psi(r' + \mathfrak{a}) \end{align*}
multiplication
\begin{align*} \psi((r + \mathfrak{a}) \cdot (r' + \mathfrak{a})) =& \psi(rr' + \mathfrak{a}) \\ =& \varphi(rr') \\ =& \varphi(r) \cdot \varphi(r') \\ =& \psi(r + \mathfrak{a}) \cdot \psi(r' + \mathfrak{a}) \end{align*}
