classification of simple representations of k[x]/f

Proposition

Let $k$ be a field and $f \in k[x]$ a polynomial of the form $f = \prod p_i^{e_i}$ for irreducible $p_i \in k[x]$, w.l.o.g. monic polynomial. Then $\{k[x]/p_i\}$ is the set of isomorphism classes of simple representations (where both are pairwise nonisomorphic)

Moreover, $\mathrm{dim}_{k}(k[x]/p_i) = \mathrm{deg}(p_i)$.

Proof

Recall that simple representations are cyclic modules, so isomorphic to $k[x]/(f)/(\mathfrak{a})$ for an ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq k[x]/(f)$. Now the correspondence theorem for ideals and quotient rings implies that $\mathfrak{a}$ can be identified with an ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq k[x]$ which contains $(f)$ and as $k[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, it is of the form $(g)$ for a $g \mid f$. We claim that $k[x]/g$ is simple if and only if $g$ is irreducible. If it is irreducible, then $k[x]/g$ is field extension and hence simple (as each element generates this simple field extension) and if it is not irreducible then any factor $p_i$ induces a nontrivial submodule.

Suppose we have a $k[x]$ linear isomorphism $f: k[x]/p_i \rightarrow k[x]/p_j$, then for any preimage $v = f^{-1}[v]$ we have $0 = p_i \cdot v$ and $p_i \cdot 1 = f(p_i \cdot v) = 0$ hence $p_i \mid p_j$. A similar argument implies $p_j \mid p_i$ or that $p_i = p_j$.

The fact about dimensions is a standard fact from galois theory.
