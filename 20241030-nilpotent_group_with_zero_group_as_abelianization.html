nilpotent group with zero group as abelianization

Proposition

Let $G$ be a group

TFAE:

  1. $Q = 1$
  2. $Q^{\mathrm{ab}} = 0$ and $Q$ is nilpotent

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

follows from

  • abelian group as nilpotent group

and the abelianization is $0$

2) $\implies$ 1)

Proof by induction over the length of the series

induction basis

Assume we have a length $0$ filtration, i.e. $Q = Q_0 = 1$ Then it follows $Q = 0$ by assumption.

induction step

Assume $1 \trianglelefteq ... \trianglelefteq N \trianglelefteq Q$ is a (nilpotency) series of minimal length. (as $\mathbb{N}$ is well ordered) Consider the short exact sequence 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
1 \arrow[]{r}{} & N \arrow[]{r}{} & Q \arrow[]{5}{} & Q/N \arrow[]{r}{}& 1
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

where $N$ is a normal and $Q/N$ is abelian as $N$

Note that here $Q/N$ is nilpotent group with smaller length of filtration (cf. subgroup of a nilpotent group, quotient group of a nilpotent group)

After abelianizing we get the short exact sequence (cf. abelianization as right exact functor)

Here by exactness we conclude that $(Q/N)^{\mathrm{ab}} = 0$ so by induction we conclude that $Q/N = 1$. This shows that the normal closure of $N$ resp. $N$ itself (as $N$ is a normal subgroup) is $Q$.

As the series was chosen with minimal length, we conclude that $1 = N = Q$, otherwise we may remove $N$ from the series
