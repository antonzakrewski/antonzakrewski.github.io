localization of an intersection of modules

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $M$ an \(R\)-module and $N, L \subseteq M$ submodules. Then for the localization functor it holds, that

\begin{align*} S^{-1}(N \cap L) = S^{-1}N \cap S^{-1}L \end{align*}

Proof

$S^{-1}(N \cap L) \supseteq S^{-1}N \cap S^{-1}L$

Let $\frac{m}{s} \in S^{-1}N \cap S^{-1}L$, then there exist $m_1 \in N, m_2 \in L$ and $s_1,s_2 \in S$ such that

\begin{align*} \frac{m}{s} =& \frac{m_1}{s_1} \\ =& \frac{m_2}{s_2} \\ =& \frac{s_2 \cdot m_1 }{s_1 \cdot s_2} \\ =& \frac{s_1 \cdot m_2 }{s_1 \cdot s_2} \end{align*}

Hence there exists an $s \in S$ such that

\begin{align*} s \cdot (s_1 \cdot m_2 - s_2 \cdot m_1) =& 0 \\ s \cdot s_1 \cdot m_2 =& s \cdot s_2 \cdot m_1 \in N \cap L \end{align*}

Hence

\begin{align*} \frac{m}{s} =& \frac{m_2}{s_2} \\ =& \frac{s \cdot s_1 \cdot m_2}{s \cdot s_1 \cdot s_2} \in S^{-1}(N \cap L) \end{align*}

$S^{-1}(N \cap L) \subseteq S^{-1}N \cap S^{-1}L$

Suppose $\frac{m}{s} \in S^{-1}(N \cap L)$, then there exist $m_1 \in N \cap L$ and $s_1 \in S$ such that

\begin{align*} \frac{m_1}{s_1} = \frac{m}{s} \end{align*}

Furthermore $\frac{m_1}{s_1} \in S^{-1}N \cap S^{-1}L$
