infinite dimensional representation in general does not admit an irreducible subrepresentation

Proposition

Let $A$ be an k-Algebra over a field $k$ and $V$ an infinite dimensional representation. Then $V$ in general does not a admit a nontrivial irreducible representation

Proof

consider $A = k[x], V = k[x]$ regarded as module over itself. Let $M \subseteq V$ be a nonzero submodule, then there exists a nontrivial element $f \in M$. In particular, we may consider the submodule generated by $x \cdot f$, which is a proper submodule of $\langle f\rangle \subseteq M$ and therefore also a proper submodule of $M$.
