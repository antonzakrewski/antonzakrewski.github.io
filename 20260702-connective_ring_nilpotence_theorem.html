connective ring nilpotence theorem

Proposition

Let $R$ be a connective E_1-ring spectrum and $\alpha \in \pi_n(R)$ an element such that the image in $\mathrm{MU}_n(R)$ is $0$. Then $\alpha$ is a nilpotent element.

Proof

observe that the map $f$ can be lifted to am pa

\begin{align*} \mathbb{S}^{n,n+1} \xrightarrow{\tilde{\alpha}} \nu(R) \end{align*}

for the <functor $\nu: \mathrm{Sp} \rightarrow \mathrm{Syn}_{\mathrm{MU}}$.

pick an $C_i$ such that $\mathrm{Slope}(C_i) < \frac{1}{n}$ (cf. Burklands lemma for the nilpotence theorem). Then $\nu(R) \otmies C_}$ also has a vanishing line of the same slope (some blackboxed argument).

Hence some $\tilde{\alpha}^k$ must lie above the vanishing line, hence $\tilde{\alpha}^k$ is zero in $\pi_{*,*}(\nu(R) \otimes C_i)$.

Now we may pass to the underlying spectrum which shows that $\alpha^k$ becomes $00$ in the underlying spectrum $\nu(R) \otimes C_i$.

Since the map $\nu(R) \rightarrow \nu(R) \otimes C_i$ becomes a split mono on underlying spectra, this implies that $\alpha^k = 0$ in $\pi_*(R)$.
