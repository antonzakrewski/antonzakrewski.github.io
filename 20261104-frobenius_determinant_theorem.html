frobenius determinant theorem

Proposition

Let $G$ be a finite group Then for the frobenius determinant

\begin{align*} \mathrm{det}(X_G) = \prod_{j=1}^r P_j(x)^{\mathrm{deg} P_j} \end{align*}

Proof

Let $V = \mathbb{C}[G]$ be the0regular representation. Let $L(x) = \sum_{g \in G} x_{g} p(g)$. Now

\begin{align*} L(x) h =& \sum_g \in G} x_g p(g) h \\ =& \sum_{g \in G} x_g g h \\ =& \sum_z \in G X_{zh^{-1}} z \\ \end{align*}

So the matrix of $L$ agrees with $X_G$ up to permuting columns.

Hence

\begin{align*} \mathrm{det}(X_G) = \pm \mathrm{det}(L) \end{align*}

Here one of these will always be the one dimensional, constant representation, so we can put the sign in there.

Then, by Maschke, $\mathrm{det}(L(x)) = \prod_{i=1}^r (\mathrm{det}(L_i)(x))^{\mathrm{dim}(V_i)$ for $L_i = L_{\vert V_i}$

Let $\{e_m^i\}$ be a absis of $V_i$ and write $E_{j,k}^i$ to be the basis of $\mathrm{End}_{k}(V_i)$ coming from $e_{i,m}$. Then $\{E_{j,k}^i\}$ is a basis of $\mathbb{C}[G]$ and $L_{i}(x) = \sum y_{i,j,k} E_{j,k}^i$

Now we note that $y_{j,k}^i$ are a linear isomorphism of the standard generators. So $\mathrm{det}(L_i(x)) = \mathrm{det}(y_{j,k}^i)$ is irreducible by the next lemma and nonproportianal, since each involves different $y_{j,k}^i$.
