theory building reduces the necessity to be clever

Statement

The more theory you have at your disposale, the less clever you have to be:

Suppose you have a linear equation -- without solutions -- and want to show that you can't solve it. If you don't know about linear algebra, then you (presumably) have to be clever and find a specific, ad hoc argument, why it is not possible to solve it. If you know linear algebra, then you can write down an algorithm to check if there exists a solution (use ranks of matrices).
