vector bundle and locally free for finitely many elements

Proposition

Let $R$ be commutative ring and $M$ any \(R\)-module. TFAE:

  1. $M$ is an vector bundle
  2. there exist $f_1,...,f_n$ such that $(f_i) = R$ and that $M_{f_i}$ is a free $R_{f_i}$ module.

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Let $M \oplus N \simeq R^n$. Then w.l.o.g. assume that $R$ is not a local ring, as vector bundle over a local ring are free. Then for each prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$, choose an element $f_{\mathfrak{p}} \in R \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ Moreover, the collection $f_{\mathfrak{p}}$ generates the unit ideal, since it is not contained in any prime / maximal ideal by construction.

Hence it suffices to show that for each prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$, that there exists an $f \in R \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ with $M_f$ free.

Moreover, $R_{\mathfrak{p}} \simeq \mathrm{colim}_{f \not\in \mathfrak{p}} R_{f}, M_{\mathfrak{p}} \simeq \mathrm{colim}_{f \not\in \mathfrak{p}} M_{f}$ are isomorphic, e.g. by comparing universal property. Now choose an isomorphism $\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}}: R_{\mathfrak{p}}^n \rightarrow M_{\mathfrak{p}}$, which is given by choosing $n$ elements $\frac{r_i}{g_i} \in R_{\mathfrak{p}}$. Now this map lifts to $\varphi_g: R_g^n \rightarrow M_g$ for $g = g_1 \cdot ... \cdot g_n$. Then we have an exact sequence (since localization commutes with almost everything & filtered colimits are exact in category RMod).

Here $\mathrm{coker}(\varphi_g)$ is finitely generated by $c_1,...,c_m$ where $c_i$ vanishes after inverting $g_{c_i}$. Hence $\mathrm{coker}(\varphi_g)$ vanishes after inverting $g' = g_{c_1} \cdot ... \cdot g_{c_n}$.

Now $\varphi_{g \cdot g'}: R_{g \cdot g'}^n \rightarrow M_{g \cdot g'}$ is a surjection, hence split epi since $M_{g \cdot g'}$ is a projective module. furthermore, we want to show that $\varphi_{g \cdot g'}$ is injective after passing to $\varphi_{g \cdot g' \cdot g''}$ for some $g'' \not\in \mathfrak{p}$. But injectivity of a split epi is equivalent to surjectivity of the split mono (cf. monic split epi as isomorphism). Hence the same argument as before suffices to find an $g \cdot g' \cdot g''$ which does not lie in $\mathfrak{p}$ since $R \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ is multiplicatively closed.

2) $\implies$ 1)

follows from:

  • vector bundle is a zariski local property
  • where finite free modules are of course vector bundles.
