anti-derivation is a bounded linear operator

Proposition

Let

\begin{align*} T: C([0,1]) \rightarrow& C([0,1]) \\ f \mapsto& (x \mapsto \int_{0}^x f(t) \mathrm{d}t) \end{align*}

be the anti-derivation Then it is a bounded operator (with respect to $\| - \|_{\infty}$) with Operatornorm $\| T \| = 1$

Proof

follows from the estimate

\begin{align*} \| T f \| \leq& \mathrm{sup}(f) \cdot \int_{0}^1 1 \mathrm{d}t =& \| f \|_{\infty} \end{align*}

moreover, $\| T 1 \| = \int_{0}^1 1 \mathrm{d}t = 1$ and $\| 1 \|_{\infty} = 1$, hence $\| T \| \geq 1$
