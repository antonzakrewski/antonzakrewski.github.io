disjointification of countably many subsets

Proposition

Let $X$ be a set, $A_n \in \mathcal{P}(X)$ countably many subsets Then there exist $B_n \subseteq A_n$ with

  1. $B_n \subseteq A_n$
  2. $B_n$ are pairwise disjoint
  3. $\bigcup A_n = \coprod B_n$

using

  1. complements
  2. and finite intersections / finite unions

Proof

w.l.o.g. assume that $A_n \neq A_m$ for $n \neq m$, otherwise us e.g. $\emptyset$ Let

\begin{align*} B_0 \coloneqq A_0 \end{align*}

and define

\begin{align*} B_{n+1} \coloneqq A_{n+1} \setminus (\coprod_{j=1}^n B_j) \end{align*}

Then for $B_i,B_j$ with w.l.o.g. $i > j$ it follows that

\begin{align*} B_i \cap B_j =& A_{i} \setminus (\coprod_{l=1}^i B_l) \cap B_j \\ \subseteq& A_i \setminus B_j \cap B_j \\ =& \emptyset \end{align*}

hence they are pairwise disjoint.

Let $a \in \bigcup A_i$, and let $n$ be the smallest natural number such that $a \in A_n$ (note that $\mathbb{N}$ is well-ordered). Then it follows that $a \not\in \bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1} A_i \supseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1} B_i$ and thus $a \in A_{n} \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1} B_i$
