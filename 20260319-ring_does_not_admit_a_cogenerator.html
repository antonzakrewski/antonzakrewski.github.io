ring does not admit a cogenerator

Proposition

The category Ring does not admit a cogenerator.

Proof

Let $C$ be a cogenerator. Then consider the ring-homomorphisms

\begin{align*} \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}[X] \xrightarrow{0,1} \mathbb{Z}/p \end{align*}

In particular there exists two different ring homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}[X] \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}$. Hence $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}, C) \neq \emptyset$ as

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(\mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}[X], \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Set}}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(\mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}[X], C), \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(\mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}, C)) \end{align*}

is injective

This shows that there exist ring homomorphisms

\begin{align*} \mathbb{Z}/2 \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow& C \\ \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow& C \end{align*}

But this would imply $\mathrm{char}(C) \mid 2, \mathrm{char}(C) \mid 3$, which is impossible (cf. characteristic and ring homomorphism from Z/p)
