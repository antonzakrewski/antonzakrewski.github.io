first countable, compact space in general not second countable

Proposition

A first-countable, compact space is in general not secondcountable

Proof

consider $\mathbb{R}$ with the discrete topology and $\overline{\mathbb{R}} = \mathbb{R} \cup \{*\}$ where a set containing $\{*\}$ is open if and only if it is $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$. Then one checks that it is a valid topology. It is compact since it is coarser than the one point compactification of $\mathbb{R}$ (cf. gröbere topologie und Kompaktheit). It is first countable, since for $x \in \mathbb{R}$ we may choose $\{x\}$. But it is not second countable, since it would otherwise contain $\{ \{x\} \vert x \in \mathbb{R}\}$
