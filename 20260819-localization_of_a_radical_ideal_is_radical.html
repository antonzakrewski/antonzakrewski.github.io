localization of a radical ideal is radical

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $I$ a radical ideal and $S$ a multiplicatively closed subset Then $I \otimes_{R} S^{-1}R \hookrightarrow S^{-1}R$ is also a radical ideal.

Proof

Let $r \otimes_{R} \frac{r'}{s}$ be an element (w.l.o.g. $r' = 1$) which is nilpotent, that is $(\frac{r}{s})^n = 0$ for some $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Then there exists an $s'$ such that $s' \cdot r^n = 0$. In particular, $(s' \cdot r)^n = (s')^n \cdot r^n = 0$ and hence $(s' \cdot r) \in I$ lies in the radical ideal. Now $r \otimes_{R} \frac{r'}{s}$ is equal to $s' \cdot r \otimes_{R} \frac{r'}{s' \cdot s}$ and hence lies in the image.
