two sided ideals of finite dimensional semisimple algebras

Proposition

Let $A$ be a finite dimensional semi-simple algebra. Then every two-sided ideal $I \subseteq A$ is of the form $A e$ for a unique central, idempotent element $e$

Proof

Let $I$ be an ideal, i.e. an \(A\)-submodule $I \subseteq A$. Now $A \simeq \bigoplus_{i \in I} A_i$ for finititely many terms and simple \(A\)-modules $A_i$. Now subobject of a semi-simple object is semi-simple implies that the inclusion $I \hookrightarrow A$ identifies as summand inclusion (up to isomorphism, but for ideals this even becomes strict).

\begin{align*} \bigoplus_{j \in J \subseteq I} A_i \rightarrow \bigoplus_{i \in I} A_i \end{align*}

Hence let $e$ be $(\chi_{J}(i))$ where $\chi_{J}(i)$ is the characteristic function, i.e. $\chi_{J}(i) = 1$ if $i \in J$ and $\chi_{J}(i) = 0$ else. Then for any element $a = (a_i) \in A$, we have $e a = (\chi_{J}(i) \cdot a_i) = (a_i \cdot \chi_{J}(i)) = (a) \cdot e$ since $0,1$ are central in $A_i$.

In particular, $a$ lies in $I$ if and only if $a_i = 0$ for $i \not\in I$ and hence $e a = a$.
