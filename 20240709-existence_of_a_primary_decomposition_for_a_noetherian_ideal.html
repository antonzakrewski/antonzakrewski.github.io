existence of a primary decomposition for a noetherian ring

Proposition

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, then each strict ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subsetneq A$ admits a primary decomposition.

Proof

reduction

using the

  • primary ideal and quotient
  • correspondence theorem for ideals and quotient rings
  • quotient ring of a noetherian ring as noetherian ring

it suffices to show that

\begin{align*} (0) \subseteq A/\mathfrak{A} \end{align*}

has a primary decomposition.

proof

Let

\begin{align*} S \coloneqq \{\mathfrak{a} \vert \subsetneq A \mathrm{ ideal}, \mathfrak{a}\mathrm{ has no primary decomposition}\} \end{align*}

and assume that $S \neq \emptyset$.

Then by noetherian ring and existence of a maximal ideal for any set of ideals there exists an ideal $\mathfrak{a} \in S$ such that the zero ideal

\begin{align*} \{0\} = A/\mathfrak{a} = A' \end{align*}

is the maximal ideal, which is not primary.

Hence there exists $x,y^n \neq 0$ for all $n \geq 0$ such that

\begin{align*} x \cdot y^n = 0 \end{align*}

Let

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Ann}(y) = \{x \in A \vert x \cdot y \} \subseteq A \end{align*}

be the annihilator we get an ascending chain of ideals

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Ann}(y) \subseteq \mathrm{Ann}(y^2) \subseteq \mathrm{Ann}(y^3)} \subseteq ... \end{align*}

which stabilizes, as $A'$ is noetherian.

Hence

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Ann}(y^n) = \mathrm{Ann}(y^{n+1}) \end{align*}

Consider

\begin{align*} (x), (y^n) \end{align*}

By assumption, as $(0)$ is the maximal ideal without a primary decomposition. Hence there exists a primary decomposition of $(x), (y^n)$.

Then it follows that

\begin{align*} (x) \cap (y^n) \end{align*}

has a primary decomposition (primary decomposotiion of an intersection of ideals).

But then

\begin{align*} (x) \cap (y^n) = (x \cdot y^n) = (0) \end{align*}

as ???

contradiction
