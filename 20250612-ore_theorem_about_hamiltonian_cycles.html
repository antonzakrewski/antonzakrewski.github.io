ore theorem about hamiltonian cycles

Proposition

Let $G = ([n],E)$ be a graph. Suppose for different, disconnected vertices $x,y \in [n]$ with $x y \not\in E$ the sum of degrees is

\begin{align*} d_G(x) + d_G(y) \geq n \end{align*}

Then $G$ is a hamiltonian graph.

Proof

Proof by contradiction:

Suppose $G$ satisfies the condition but is not hamiltonian. Then w.l.o.g. assume that $G$ is the graph with maximal amount of edges among graphs without hamilton cycle but satisfying the condition above.

Furhtermore $G \neq K_n$, since $K_n$ is clearly hamiltonian. So let $x,y\in[n]$ with $xy \not\in E$, w.l.o.g. assume that $x = v_1, y = v_n$

Then $G' \coloneqq G \cup \{xy\}$ contains a hamiltonian cycle $C$ which necessarily contains $xy$. Now assume w.l.o.g. that the hamiltonian cycle is given by $x = v_1,v_2,...,v_{n-1},v_n = y$

Let $T = N_G(y)$ denote the neighbourhood of $y$ in $G$ and Let $S = \{v_i : xv_{i+1} \in E, i \in \{1,...,n-1\}\}

Then

\begin{align*} \vert T \vert =& \mathrm{deg}_G(y) \\ \vert S \vert =& \mathrm{deg}_G(x) \\ \end{align*}

Furhtermore

\begin{align*} \vert S \vert + \vert T \vert = d_G(x) + d_G(y) \geq n \end{align*}

since $xy \not\in E(G)$.

But $\vert S \cup T \vert \leq n-1$ since $v_n = y \not\ni S,T$. Hence there exists a vertex $i \in S \cap T$ or

  1. $yv_i \in E(G)$
  2. $xv_{i+1} \in E(G)$

But then $x, v_{i+1},...,y,v_i,...,x$ is a hamilton cycle
