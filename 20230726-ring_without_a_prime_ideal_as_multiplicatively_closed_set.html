Ring without a prime ideal as multiplicatively closed set

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $\mathfrak{a}$ an ideal TFAE:

  1. $R \setminus \mathfrak{a}$ is multiplicatively closed
  2. $\mathfrak{a}$ is a prime ideal

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Let $a \cdot b \in \mathfrak{a}$, then by assumption of multiplicative closure w.l.o.g. $a \not\in R \setminus \mathfrak{a}$ or equivalently $a \in \mathfrak{a}$ Therefore $\mathfrak{a}$ is prime.

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let $a,b \in R \setminus \mathfrak{a}$, then $a \cdot b \in \mathfrak{a}$ would be a contradiction to $\mathfrak{a}$ prime, hence $a \cdot b \in R \setminus \mathfrak{a}$
