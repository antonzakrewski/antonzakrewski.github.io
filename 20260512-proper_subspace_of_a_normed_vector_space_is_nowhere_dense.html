proper subspace of a normed vector space is nowhere dense

Proposition

Let $F \subsetneq X$ be a proper subspace of a normed vector space. Then $F$ is nowhere dense

Proof

Let $x \not\in F$, then we claim that $\alpha \cdot x$ does not lie in $F$ for each $\alpha \in \mathbb{K} \setminus \{0\}$. If there exists some $\alpha \neq 0$ with $\alpha \cdot x \in F$, then as $F$ is closed under multiplication, so does $\frac{1}{\alpha} \cdot \alpha \cdot x = x$.

We first claim that $0$ is not an interior point, as for any $\epsilon > 0$, $\frac{\varepsilon}{2 \cdot \| x \|} \cdot x$ does not lie in $F$ but in $B_{\varepsilon}(0)$, i.e. there does not exist an open neighbourhood $U$ of $0$ such that $U \subseteq F$. So let $f \in F$ arbitrary, then observe that the map

\begin{align*} + f: X \rightarrow& X \\ x \mapsto& x +f \\ \end{align*}

is a homeomorphism (with inverse $ + (-f)$,) so preserves the notions of interior points etc. In other words, $0 + f$ is not an interior point of $F + f = F$ where we have used that $F$ is closed under sums.
