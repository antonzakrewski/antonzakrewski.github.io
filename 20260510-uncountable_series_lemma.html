uncountable series lemma

Proposition

Let $J \neq \emptyset$ be an index set and $0 \leq c_{\alpha} < \infty$ for all $\alpha \in J$. Then

\begin{align*} \sum_{\alpha \in J} \coloneqq \mathrm{sup}_{J_0 \subseteq J, \vert J_0 \vert < \infty} \sum_{\alpha \in J_{0} c_{\alpha} < \infty \end{align*}

implies $c_{\alpha} = 0$ for all but countably many $\alpha$

Proof

Let $J_{> 0} = \{c_{\alpha} > 0\}$. Assume that $J_{> 0}$ is uncountable Let $(\frac{1}{n+1}, \frac{1}{n}] \coprod [1,\infty)$ be a countable partition of $\mathbb{R}^+$. Then the pigeonhole principle for uncountable sets implies that there exists some $(\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n}]$ (or mutatis mutandis $[1,\infty)$) such that uncountably many $c_{\alpha}$ lie in $(\frac{1}{n+1}, \frac{1}{n}]$. But this shows that $c_{\alpha} > \frac{1}{n}$ for infinitely many $\alpha$ or $\mathrm{sup}_{J_0 \subseteq J, \vert J_0 \vert < \infty} \sum_{\alpha \in J_{0} c_{\alpha} = \infty$
