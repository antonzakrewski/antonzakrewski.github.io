convolution for a finite group

Proposition

Let $G$ be a finite group and $R$ a ring Then $\mathrm{Fun}(G,R)$ admits a convolution product, given by

\begin{align*} (f \star h)(g) = \sum_{g' \in G} f(g') \cdot h((g')^{-1} g) \end{align*}

This endows $\mathrm{Fun}(G,R)$ with a ring structure.

Proof

neutral element for multiplication

consider $\partial_{e}$. Then $(\partial_{e} \star h)(g) = \sum_{g' \in G} \partial_{e}(g') h((g')^{-1} g) = \partial_{e}(e) h(e^{-1} g) = h(g)$. similarly $(h \star \partial_{e})(g) = h(g)$

distributivity

follows from the distributivity: We show left distributivity, right follows analogously

\begin{align*} ((f_1 + f_2) \star h)(g) =& \sum_{g' \in G} (f_1 + f_2)(g') \cdot h((g')^{-1} g) \\ =& \sum_{g' \in G} f_1(g') h((g')^{-1} g) + f_2(g') h((g')^{-1} g) \\ =& \sum_{g' \in G} f_1(g') h((g')^{-1} g) + \sum_{g' \in G} f_2(g') h((g')^{-1} g) \\ =& (f_1 \star h)(g) + (f_2 \star h)(g) \end{align*}

associativity

\begin{align*} ((f_1 \star f_2) \star f_3)(g) =& \sum_{g_1 \in G} (f_1 \star f_2)(g_1) \cdot f_3(g_1^{-1} g) \\ =& \sum_{g_1 \in G} \sum_{g_2 \in G} (f_1(g_2) \cdot f_2(g_2^{-1} g_1) \cdot f_3(g_1^{-1} g) \\ =& \sum_{g_2 \in G} f_1(g_2) \cdot (\sum_{g_1 \in G} f_2(g_2^{-1} g_1) \cdot f_3(g_1^{-1} g) \\ =& \sum_{g_2 \in G} f_1(g_2) \cdot (f_2 \star f_3)(g_2^{-1} g) \\ =& f_1 \star (f_2 \star f_3)(g) \end{align*}
