casorati-weierstrass

Proposition

Assume that $f: B_r(z_0) \setminus \{z_0\} \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ is holomorphic with essential singularity $z_0$. Then $\mathrm{im}(f)$ is dense

Proof

Assume the image is not dense. Then there exists an $w \in \mathbb{C}$ such that

\begin{align*} B_{\delta}(w) \cap \mathrm{im}(f) = \emptyset \end{align*}

Hence define

\begin{align*} g: D_r(z_0) \setminus \{z_0\} \rightarrow& \mathbb{C} \\ z \mapsto& \frac{1}{f(z) - w} \end{align*}

the function is holomorphic (cf. quotient of holomorphic functions as holomorphic function) and bounded by $\frac{1}{\delta}$ and therefore has a removable singularity (cf. theorem) at $z_0$.

If $g(z_0) \neq 0$ then $f - w$ is holomorphic at $z_0$, so $z_0$ is a removable singularity of $f$, contradiction

If $g(z_0) = 0$, then $f-w$ has a pole at $z_0$ (cf. pole of a holomorphic function and infinity) so $f$ as well.
