stone-cech compactification

Definition

The stone-cech compactification $\beta: \mathrm{Top} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH}$ is defined as left adjoint to the inclusion

\begin{align*} \mathrm{CH} \rightarrow \mathrm{Top} \end{align*}

Existence

using pointwise construction of left adjoints

Let $C(X) \coloneqq \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Top}}(X, [0,1])$ be the set of continuous maps into the interval. Let $\prod_{f \in C(X)} [0,1]$ be the product, which by Tychonoff's Theorem is compact Then consider the map

\begin{align*} (f): X \rightarrow& \prod_{f \in C(X)} [0,1] \\ x \mapsto& (f(x))_{f \in C(X)} \end{align*}

and define

\begin{align*} \beta X \coloneqq \overline{\mathrm{im}(e)} \end{align*}

with the map

\begin{align*} X \rightarrow \mathrm{im}(f) \subseteq \beta(X) \end{align*}

is unit

continuous

Note that the map $e$ is induced by the universal property of the product for

\begin{align*} f: X \rightarrow& [0,1] \\ x \mapsto f(x) \end{align*}

compact, hausdorff

follows from:

  • closed subset of a compact space as compact set
  • subspace of a hausdorff space as hausdorff space
  • product of hausdorff spaces as hausdorff space

Then we may consider the map

\begin{align*} Y \rightarrow& \prod_{f \in C(Y)} [0,1]\\ y \mapsto& (f(y))_{f \in C(Y)} \end{align*}

similar to the stone cech compactification.

This map is injective: for $y \neq y'$ we may find a map extending

\begin{align*} \{y,y'\} \rightarrow& [0,1]\\ y \mapsto& 0 \\ y' \mapsto& 1 \end{align*}

using Urysohnsches Lemma.

Then we may find the dashed arrows via the argument of the reduction to $[0,1]$ and the universal property.

Note that $F: \beta X \rightarrow \prod_{f \in C(Y)} [0,1]$ now extends the map $X \rightarrow \prod_{f \in C(Y)} [0,1]$

Now $Y \rightarrow \prod_{f \in C(Y)} [0,1]$ is a topological embedding (cf. injective map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space as embedding) so it suffices to show that the map

\begin{align*} F: \beta X \rightarrow \prod_{f \in C(Y)} [0,1] \end{align*}

restricts to $Y$, i.e. $\mathrm{im}(F) \subseteq Y$

Here we use closure and continuous maps which shows that

\begin{align*} \mathrm{im}(F) =& F[\beta X] \\ =& F[\overline{X}] \\ \subseteq& \overline{F[X]} \\ \subseteq& \overline{Y} = Y \end{align*}

where $X$ is by construction dense in $\beta X$ and $F[X] \subseteq Y$ holds as $F$ is an extension of $f: X \rightarrow Y$

Now $Y \subseteq \prod_{f \in C(Y)} [0,1]$ is compact, so in particular closed since the RHS is hausdorff (cf. closedness of compact sets in Hausdorff spaces). Thus $\overline{Y} = Y$ and we are done.

uniqueness

follows from restriction of a map to dense subspace in a Hausdorff space where any extension of $\mathrm{im}(X \rightarrow \beta X) \rightarrow Y$ is unique.
