tree contains at least two leafs

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a tree with $\vert V \vert \geq 2$. Then $G$ contains at least 2 leafs

Proof

circular proof

assume $\vert V \vert \geq 2$, then in particular the minimal degree of a graph is given by at least $1$ (even precisely one) since $G$ is connected. Then using sum of degrees of a graph and tree and connected graph and edges we get

\begin{align*} \sum_{v \in V(G)} d_G(v) =& 2 \cdot E(G) \\ =& 2 \cdot V(G) - 2 \end{align*}

where this equality shows, that there exist at least $2$ vertices with $d_G(v_1) = d_G(v_2)$ as otherwise

\begin{align*} \sum_{v \in V(G)} d_G(v) > 2 \cdot V(G) - 2 \end{align*}

proof by contradiction

Assume that $G$ contains precisely one leaf (the other case follows from graph with minimal degree 2 contains circle). Then let $v_0$ denote the leaf and follow the algorithm of graph with minimal degree 2 contains circle. This provides a cycle, hence contradiction to the assumption.

Here the algorithm can be done by the following way:

  • choose an adjacent vertex $v_{i+1}$ to $v_i$, such that the edge $\{v_i,v_{i+1}\}$ has not been chosen before
  • repeat this until you can't anymore
  • since $G$ contains finitely many edges, this algorithm has to terminate.

Then we are stuck in some $v_n$. If $\mathrm{d}_G(V_n) = 1$, then we got another leaf (note that it has to be distinct, since we have already marked the only edge connecting $v_0$) Otherwise for $\mathrm{d}_G(V_n) \geq 2$ this implies that we have already marked an edge $v_n \rightarrow v_l$. showing that $v_n = v_{l+1}$ or $v_n = v_{l-1}$.

This in particular provides a walk $v_n \rightarrow v_n$ where by construction each edge was distinct, hence the graph is not acyclic. Contradiction to our definition
