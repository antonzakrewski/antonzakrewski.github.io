integral domain and nonzero product of ideals

Proposition

Let \(R\) be a commutative ring. TFAE:

  1. for ideals $\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}$ with $\mathfrak{p} \cdot \mathfrak{q} = 0$ we conclude that $\mathfrak{p} = 0$ or $\mathfrak{q} = 0$
  2. $R$ is an Integral domain

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Let $a,b \in R$, then there exist generated Ideals $(a),(b)$ and furthermore $(a) \cdot (b) = \{r \cdot a \cdot b \vert r \in R\}$. If $(a) \cdot (b) = \{0\}$, then we conclude w.l.o.g. that $(a) = 0$ and because of $a \in (a)$ we also get $a = 0$

2) $\implies$ 1)

Proof by contraposition Suppose there exist zero divisors $a \cdot b = 0$. Then $(a), (b) \neq \{0\}$ and furthermore

\begin{align*} (a) \cdot (b) =& \{r \cdot a \cdot b \vert r \in R\} \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

By assumption, $a \in (a), b \in (b)$ hence contradiction
