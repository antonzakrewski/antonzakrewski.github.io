finitely generated module and localization at a prime ideal as zero module

Proposition

Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $M$ a finitely generated module and $\mathfrak{p} \in \mathrm{Spec}(A)$ a prime ideal Suppose that the localization

\begin{align*} M_{\mathfrak{p}} = 0 \end{align*}

is the zero module

Then there exists an $f \in A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ with

\begin{align*} M_{f} = 0 \end{align*}

see: localization along an element

Proof

choose generators $m_1,...,m_r$ of $M$ as \(A\)-module. Then it follows that

\begin{align*} \frac{m_1}{1} = \frac{0}{1} \end{align*}

hence there exists an $s_1 \in S$ such that

\begin{align*} s_1 \cdot m_1 = 0 \end{align*}

Let

\begin{align*} s \coloneqq \prod s_i \end{align*}

As $A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ is multiplicatively closed, we conclude that $s \in A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$

Furthermore as $A$ is commutative we get for the localization at $s$

\begin{align*} \frac{m_j}{1} = 0 \end{align*}

since

\begin{align*} s \cdot m_j =& (\prod_{i \neq j} s_i \cdot) s_j \cdot m_j \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

Since

\begin{align*} \frac{m_i}{1} = 0 \end{align*}

generate $S^{-1}M$ as $S^{-1}M$ module, we are done
