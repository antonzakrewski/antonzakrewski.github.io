CW structure on the real projective space

Proposition

The real projective space $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ is a finite CW-Complex.

Proof

Proof by induction

induction basis

Note that

\begin{align*} S^0 / \simeq = * \end{align*}

hence $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^0$ is a trivial cw complex.

induction step

Let

\begin{align*} \pi: S^n \rightarrow& \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n \\ (s_0,...,s_n) \mapsto& [s_0 : ... : s_n] \end{align*}

be the projection.

Then define

\begin{align*} \iota: S^n \rightarrow& D^{n+1} \\ (s_0,...,s_n) \mapsto& (s_0,...,s_n,0) \end{align*}

where $D^{n+1}$ is parametrized as closed upper hemisphere of $S^{n+1}$.

Let

\begin{align*} \pi: D^{n+1} \rightarrow& \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n+1} \\ (s_0,...,s_n,s_{n+1}) \mapsto& [s_0 : ... : s_{n+1}] \end{align*}

for $D^{n+1} = \{(s_1,...,s_n) \vert \sum s_i^2 = 1\}$

Then it follows that

is a pushout.

Let 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
|[label={[label distance=-2mm]-45:\ulcorner}]| S^n \arrow[]{r}{} \arrow[]{d}{\iota}& \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n \arrow[]{d}{} \arrow[bend left]{ddr}{\iota^{\mathbb{P}}}& \\
D^{n+1} \arrow[bend right]{rrd}{\pi} \arrow[]{r}{}& \mathrm{Push} \arrow[dashed]{rdd}{} & \\
& & \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n+1} \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

be the induced map

By bijective continuous map from a compact space to a hausdorff space as homeomorphism it suffices to show that this is a bijection. Note that $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ is Hausdorff (cf. projective space as hausdorff space) and that the pushout is given by

\begin{align*} (D^{n+1} \coprod \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)/\simeq \end{align*}

where both $D^{n+1}, \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ are compact.

surjective

Note that $D^{n+1} \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{Push}$ is an epimorphism (cf. epimorphisms mirrored by pushouts, epimorphism in category top)

injective

Now suppose $(s_0,...,s_{n+1}), (t_0,...,t_{n+1}) \in D^{n+1}$ and $[u_0 : .... : u_n : 0] \in \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ are such that they agree pairwise in $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$

case $(s_0,...,s_{n+1}), [u_0:...:u_n:0]$

Then by construction of a colimit in Top and underlying Set we may take a preimage of $[u_0 : .... : u_n : 0]$, scale it appropriatly to get $(s_0,...,s_{n+1}) = (s_0,...,0)$ and hence conclude that $(s_0,...,0) \sim [u_0 : .... : u_n : 0]$

For $(s_0,...,s_{n+1}), (t_0,...,t_{n+1})$ it follows that they differ by $\cdot \pm 1$, as we have constraints on $\sum s_i^2$ w.l.o.g. we may assume that $s_i = - t_i$ and hence $t_{n+1} = s_{n+1} = 0$. Then we may take a preimage of $s_i, t_i$ in $S^n$ and map it via $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ to the pushout.

Therefore it follows by construction of a pushout that $(s_0,...,s_{n+1}) \sim (t_0,...,t_{n+1})$
