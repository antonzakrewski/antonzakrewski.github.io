explicit description of the continuous calkin category of dualizable category

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a dualizable stable category. Then the continuous calkin category $\mathrm{Calk}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C})$ is identified with the idempotent completion of the essential image of the functor

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Cone}(\widehat{よ}(-) \Rightarrow よ(-)): \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}) \end{align*}

Here the natural transformation $\widehat{よ} \Rightarrow よ(-)$ is the counit of $\widehat{よ} \dashv よ$.

Proof

Let $\mathcal{L} \coloneqq \mathrm{Cofib}(\widehat{よ}(\mathrm{colim}) \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}(-)): \mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})$ be the cofiber of the counit. Then the inclusion $\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Calk}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C})) \rightarrow \mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})$ has $\mathcal{L}$ as left adjoint after restricting $\mathcal{L}$ to the subcategory.

Now $\mathcal{L}$ is colimit preserving functor, since colimits commute with colimits and $\widehat{よ}$ as well as $\mathrm{colim}$ are left adjoints. For representables $よ(x)$, we also have $\mathrm{colim} よ(x) \simeq x$ and hence $\mathcal{L}(x) = \mathrm{cofib}(\widehat{yo}(x) \Rightarrow よ(x))$. We claim that

Now $\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Calk}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C}))$ is the fiber of the colimit preserving functor $\mathrm{colim}$, hence closed under colimits in $\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})$. This shows that $\mathcal{L}$ is strongly continuous, so in particular also preserves compact objects. Now $\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})$ is generated by the compact representables under filtered colimits, hence $\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Calk}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C}))$ is generated by $\mathcal{L}(よ(\mathcal{C}))$ under filtered colimits as $\mathcal{L}$ is colimit preserving and the image of $\mathcal{L}$ generates $\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Calk}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C}))$. Hence any compact object in $\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{colim})$ is a retract of $\mathcal{L}(よ(-))$ (cf. compact object as filtered colimit and retract).
