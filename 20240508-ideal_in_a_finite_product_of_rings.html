ideal in a finite product of rings

Proposition

Let $R_i$ be rings for $i \in \{1,...,n\}$ and $I$ an ideal. Then

\begin{align*} I = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n I_{i} \end{align*}

for ideals $I_i \subseteq R_i$

Proof

Let

\begin{align*} \pi_i: \bigoplus R_i \rightarrow R_i \end{align*}

be the projection and $I_i \coloneqq \pi_i[i]$. By image of an ideal under an epimorphism as ideal

$\subseteq$

Then it follows that

\begin{align*} I \subseteq \pi_{i}^{-1}[\pi_i[I]] = \bigoplus_{j\neq i}^n R_j \oplus I \end{align*}

hence also

\begin{align*} I \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^n \pi_i^{-1}[\pi_i[I]] = \bigoplus I_i \end{align*}

$\supseteq$

Let $(a_i) \in \bigoplus_{i=1}^n I_{i}$.

Then w.l.o.g. for $a_1$ there exists an element $(a_1,b_2,...,b_n)$. multiplication with $(1,0,...,0)$ results in $(a_1,...,0)$.

Hence by repeating and taking the finite sum we obtain that $(a_i) \in I$
