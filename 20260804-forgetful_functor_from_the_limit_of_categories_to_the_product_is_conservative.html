forgetful functor from the limit of categories to the product is conservative

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}_{-}:I \rightarrow \mathrm{Cat}$ be a diagram. then the canoncial functor from the limit to the product

\begin{align*} \lim_{I} \mathcal{C}_i \rightarrow \prod_{\pi_0(I)} \mathcal{C}_i \end{align*}

is a conservative functor

Proof

Recall that a map is an equivalence if and only if the square

is a pullback (cf. equivalence is detected by the fiber of the inclusion into the arrow category).

Now both $\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^1, -), \mathrm{id} \cong \mathrm{Fun}(*,-)$ preserve limits (cf. functor category functor preserves limits) and there exists a natural transformation $\mathcal{C} \simeq \mathrm{Fun}(*, \mathcal{C}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^1, \mathcal{C}) \simeq \mathrm{Ar}(-)$ obtained by precomposition So let $f: * \rightarrow \mathrm{Ar}(\lim_{I} \mathcal{C}_i) \simeq \lim_{I} \mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C}_i)$ be a morphism in the limit. then we need to show that there exists a pullback diagram

here it follows from limits commute with limits and limit of terminal objects is the terminal object that the pullback $\lim_{I} \mathcal{C}_i \times_{\lim_I \mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C}_i)} *$ is the limit of $\lim_{I} (\mathcal{C}_i \times_{\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C}_i)} *)$. Here $(\mathcal{C}_i \times_{\mathrm{Ar}(\mathcal{C}_i)} *)$ is equivalent to the point if the \(i\)-th component of $f$ is an equivalence and the empty category else.

Hence by limit containing the empty category is empty, the limit is nonempty if and only if it is nonempty for all $i \in I$. This shows that the functor to the product category is conservative.
