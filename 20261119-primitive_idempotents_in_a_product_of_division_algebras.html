primitive idempotents in a product of division algebras

Proposition

Let $A \simeq \prod_{i=1}^n D_i$ be a product of division algebras of characteristic $\neq 2$ Then the only primitive idempotent elements are given by $e_i$.

Proof

Observe that idempotent elements are precisely the pointwise idempotent elements, so elements of the form $\chi_{J \subseteq I}$. So suppose $\chi_{J} \neq 0$ and that there exist two different $i,i' \in J$. Then $\chi_{J \setminus i} + e_{i} = \chi_{J}$ and $\chi_{J \setminus i} \cdot e_i = 0$ show that $\chi_{J}$ is not primitive (use that $\chi_{J \setminus i}$ is nonzero).

Now we show that $e_i$ is primitive: Suppose $e_i = \chi_{J} + \chi_{J'}$, then w.l.o.g. $i \in J, i \not\in J'$ and since $\mathrm{char}(D_j) \neq 2$, this implies $\chi_{J'} = 0$ since the only idempotents in $D_i$ are $0$ and $1$.
