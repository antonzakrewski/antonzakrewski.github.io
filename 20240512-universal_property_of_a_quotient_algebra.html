universal property of a quotient algebra

Proposition

Let \(A\) be a ring, $B,C$ A-Algebras and $\mathfrak{b} \subseteq B$ an ideal Then for a morphism of A-Algebras

\begin{align*} f: B \rightarrow C \end{align*}

with $\mathfrak{b} \subseteq \mathrm{ker}(f)$ there exists a unique morphism of A-Algebras

\begin{align*} \overline{f}: B/\mathfrak{b} \rightarrow C \end{align*}

Proof

follows from the fundamental theorem of ring homomorphism where there exists a unique ring-homomorphism 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
B \arrow[]{rr}{} \arrow[]{rd}{\pi} & C
& B/\mathfrak{b} \arrow[]{ru}{} &  \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

which then also lets 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
& \arrow[]{ld}{} A \arrow[]{rd}{} & \\
B \arrow[]{rr}{} \arrow[]{rd}{\pi} & C
& B/\mathfrak{b} \arrow[]{ru}{} &  \\
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

commute
