linearly independent sets over the endomorphisms of a simple representation

Proposition

Let $V$ be a simple, finite dimensional representation and consider $V$ as a module over $\mathrm{End}_{A}(V)$ by left multiplication. Then let $v_i,...,v_n$ be a set of linearly independent elements linearly independint over the division algebra $D$. Then for any $\{w_1,...,w_n\}$ in $V$ (not neccesseraliy linearly independent), there exists an $a \in A$ such that $a v_i = w_i$.

Proof

Suppose the contrary. Then the map

\begin{align*} \varphi: A \rightarrow \bigoplus_{i=1}^n V a \mapsto (a v_i,...,a v_n) \end{align*}

is not surjective. Then the image of $\varphi$ is a proper subrepresentation. by subobject of a semi-simple object is semi-simple, this is a proper direct summand. Hence there exists some map $\pi: \bigoplus_{i=1}^n V \rightarrow V$ such that $\mathrm{im}(\varphi) \subseteq \mathrm{ker}(\pi)$ (i.e. killing the direct sum and including it back). Here $\pi(v) = \sum f_i v$.

But then we conclude that $\Pi(\varphi(i)) = \sum f_i v_ = 0$, which contradicts the linear independence of $v_i$ over $D$.
