restriction of left bousfield localization induces equivalence

Proposition

Let $\begin{tikzcd} \mathcal{C} \arrow[r, shift left=1ex, "\mathcal{F}"{name=F}] & \mathcal{D}\arrow[l, shift left=.5ex, "\mathcal{G}"{name=G}] \arrow[phantom, from=G, to=F, , "\scriptscriptstyle\boldsymbol{\bot}"] \end{tikzcd}$ be an adjunction. Suppose that $\mathcal{G}$ is fully faithful, then the restriction $\begin{tikzcd} \mathrm{im}(\mathcal{G}) \arrow[r, shift left=1ex, "\mathcal{F}"{name=F}] & \mathcal{D}\arrow[l, shift left=.5ex, "\mathcal{G}"{name=G}] \arrow[phantom, from=G, to=F, , "\scriptscriptstyle\boldsymbol{\bot}"] \end{tikzcd}$ is an equivalence.

Proof

use:

  • fully faithful essentially surjective functor is an equivalence
  • infinity equivalence as adjoint
  • left adjoint unique up to contractible choice
