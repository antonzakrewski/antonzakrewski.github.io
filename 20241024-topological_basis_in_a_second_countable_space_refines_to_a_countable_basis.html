topological basis in a second countable space refines to a countable basis

Proposition

Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a secondcountable topological space, $\mathcal{B}$ a basis Then there exists a countable subset $\mathcal{B}' \subseteq \mathcal{B}$ which is itself a basis.

Proof

https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/65425/bases-having-countable-subfamilies-which-are-bases-in-second-countable-space Choose a countable basis $\mathcal{C} = \{C_n\}$

Now for each pair $(n,m)$ pick an $B_i \in \mathcal{B}$ such that $C_n \subseteq B_i \subseteq C_n$ if such exists. This then $B_{i(n,m)}$ is countable.

Let $x \in U$ be an open subset, then choose an open $C_m \subseteq U$ with $x \in C_m$. Then choose an open $B_i \subseteq C_m$ containing $x$ and again an $C_n \subseteq B_i$. Thus there exists an $B_i'$ satisfying this so $B_{i(m,n)}$ exists. Furthermore by $x \in C_n \subseteq B_{i(m,n)} \subseteq C_m \subseteq U$ is welldefined.
