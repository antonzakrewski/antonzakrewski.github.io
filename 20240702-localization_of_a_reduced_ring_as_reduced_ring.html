localization of a reduced ring as reduced ring

Proposition

Let $R$ be a reduced ring and $S^{-1}R$ the localization

Proof

Let $\frac{a}{s}$ be a nonzero nilpotent element, then there exists an $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that

\begin{align*} \frac{a^n}{s^n} = 0 \end{align*}

w.l.o.g. we may assume that $s = 1$ by multiplication with $s$ as $s$ is a unit and $A$ is commutatitve. Then we get

\begin{align*} \frac{a^n}{1} = \frac{0}{1} \end{align*}

or there exists an $s \in S$ with

\begin{align*} s \cdot a^n = 0 \end{align*}

This shows that $s \cdot a$ is nilpotent, hence $s \cdot a$ is zero in $R$. But then

\begin{align*} 0 \cdot s =& \frac{a}{s} \cdot s \\ =& \frac{a}{1} \end{align*}
