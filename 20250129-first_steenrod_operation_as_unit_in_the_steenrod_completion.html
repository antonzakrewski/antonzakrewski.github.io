Sq as unit in the steenrod completion

Proposition

The $\mathrm{Sq}$ is a unit in the steenrod completion Sq in the steenrod completion

Proof

a) abstractly

  • completion of a ring for a maximal ideal as local ring
  • maximal ideal and addition with 1 as unit and local ring
  • here $\mathrm{Sq} = 1 + ... \not\in \mathcal{A}_{\geq 1}$ hence a unit

b) constructive

we have to solve

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Sq} \cdot ( 1 + x) = 1 \end{align*}

etc.

Then an inverse is given by defining

\begin{align*} \Chi(\mathrm{Sq}^0) \coloneqq 1 = \mathrm{Sq}^0 \end{align*}

and inductively

\begin{align*} \Chi(\mathrm{Sq}^n) = \sum_{i=1}^n \mathrm{Sq}^i(\Chi(\mathrm{Sq}^{n-1})) \end{align*}

where

\begin{align*} \Chi(\mathrm{Sq}) =& \sum_{n \geq 0} \Chi(\mathrm{Sq}^n) \\ =& \mathrm{Sq}^{-1} \end{align*}

Then

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Sq} \cdot \Chi(\mathrm{Sq}) =& (\sum_{i \geq 0} \mathrm{Sq}^i) \cdot (\sum_{j \geq 0} \chi(\mathrm{Sq}^jj)) \\ =& \sum_{i,j \geq 0} \mathrm{Sq}^i \Chi(\mathrm{Sq}^{j}) \\ =& \sum_{n \geq 0} \sum_{i=0}^n \mathrm{Sq}^i \Chi(\mathrm{Sq}^{n-i}) \\ =& \mathrm{Sq}^0(\Chi(\mathrm{Sq}^0)) \\ =& 1 \cdot 1 \\ =& 1 \end{align*}

Then by left inverse of an isomorphism as right inverse this has to be a two sided inverse
