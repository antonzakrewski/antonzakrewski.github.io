metric separable space is second countable

Proposition

Let $X$ be a separable metric space. Then $X$ is secondcountable.

Proof

Choose a countable dense subset $Y$ and for each point $y \in Y$ the standard countable, neighbourhood basis $\mathcal{N}_y := \{B_{\frac{1}{n}}(y) \vert n \in \mathbb{N}^+\}$.

We claim that $\bigcup_{y \in Y} \mathcal{N}_y$ is a countable neighbourhood basis. It is countable, as it is the countable union of countable sets.

So let $O$ be an open neighbourhood, then it remains to show that for each point $x \in O$, there exists an open subset $U_x$ contained in $O$ with $x \in U$. By the self indexing trick, it then follows that $\bigcup_{x \in O} U_x = O$.

Now $\mathcal{N}_x$ is a neighbourhood basis of $x$, so in particular it follows that there exists an $n \in \mathbb{N}^+$ such that $B_{\frac{1}{n}}(x) \subseteq O$. Moreover, $B_{\frac{1}{10n}}(x) \cap Y$ is nonempty, as $Y$ is dense. Hence choose a $y$ in $B_{\frac{1}{10n}}(x) \cap Y$ and it follows that $x \in B_{\frac{1}{5n}}(y)$. Moreover, $B_{\frac{1}{5n}}(y) \subseteq B_{\frac{1}{n}}(x)$ since $d(x,y) < \frac{1}{10n}$ and therefore for $z \in B_{\frac{1}{5n}(y)}$ we may estimate by the triangle inequality

\begin{align*} d(x,z) \leq \frac{1}{10n} + \frac{1}{5n} < \frac{1}{n} \end{align*}

In particular, $B_{\frac{1}{5n}(y)} \subseteq O$.
