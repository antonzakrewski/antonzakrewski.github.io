smash product of pointed anima preserves suspensions

Proposition

There exists a natural equivalence

\begin{align*} (\Sigma^{m} -) \wedge (\Sigma^{n} -) \simeq& \Sigma^{n+m}(- \wedge -) \end{align*}

see:

  • smash product of pointed anima_
  • suspension functor on pointed Anima

Proof

we show that for a fixed $Y$

\begin{align*} (\Sigma^m -) \wedge Y \simeq& (\Sigma^m - \wedge Y) \end{align*}

Then by symmetry / similar arguments we get

\begin{align*} (\Sigma^m X) \wedge (\Sigma^n Y) \simeq& \Sigma^m (X \wedge (\Sigma^n Y)) \\ \simeq& \Sigma^m \Sigma^n (X \wedge Y) \\ \simeq& \Sigma^{m +n} X \wedge Y \end{align*}

But the first claim follows from

  • smash product of anima preserves colimits
  • smash product on pointed anima as pointed functor
  • infinity functor preserving terminal object and colimits preserves suspensions
