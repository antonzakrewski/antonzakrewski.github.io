infinity subcategory induces subcategory of the homotopy category

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an infinity category, $\mathcal{C}' \hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}$ a sub quasicategory. Then $h\mathcal{C}' \hookrightarrow h \mathcal{C}$ is canonically a subcategory of the homotopy category with

  1. $\mathrm{Ob}(h \mathcal{C}') = \mathcal{C}'_0$
  2. $\mathrm{Mor}(h \mathcal{C}') = \{[f] \vert f \in \mathcal{C}_1\}$

Proof

objects

follows as $\mathrm{Ob}(h X) = X_0$ holds for any simplicial set $X$

morphisms

We want to show that the map

\begin{align*} h \mathcal{C}' \rightarrow h \mathcal{C} \end{align*}

is injective on morphism.

Given $[f], [g] \in h \mathcal{C}'$ such that

\begin{align*} [f] =_{h \mathcal{C}} [g] \end{align*}

we conclude that there exists a simplex

Note that by simplex in a sub quasicategory this simplex is also in $\mathcal{C}'$, hence

\begin{align*} [f] =_{h\mathcal{C}'} [g] \end{align*}

The description of the morphism follows from the definition.
