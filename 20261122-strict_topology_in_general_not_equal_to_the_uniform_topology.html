strict convergence in general not equal to convergence on compact subsets

Proposition

Let $X$ be a locally compact, hausdorff space. Then uniform convergence on compact subspaces of $X$ in general does not imply strict convergence on $C_b(X) \simeq M(C_0(X))$ (cf. multiplier algebra of C_0).

Proof

Consider $X = [1,\infty)$ and $f_n = \mathrm{max}(x^2,n)$. Then on any compact $K \subseteq [1,\infty)$, the sequence $f_n$ is eventually constant.

But for $\frac{1}{x^2}$, the sequence $\frac{1}{x^2} \cdot f_n$ does not converge: If it would, then it would converge pointwise, but this function would be pointwise given by $x \mapsto x$, which is not bounded (c.f convergence in the strict topology of C_b implies pointwise convergence).
