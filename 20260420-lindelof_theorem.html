second countable space is countably compact

Proposition

Let $X$ be a secondcountable topological space. Then $X$ is countably compact space

Proof

Let $X = \bigcup_{\alpha} U_{\alpha}$ an open cover and $\{B_n \vert n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ a countable Basis. Let $\mathcal{N} = \{n \in \mathbb{N} \vert B_n \subseteq U_{\alpha} \text{ for some }\alpha\} \subseteq \mathbb{N}$.

Now every $U_{\alpha}$ is a union of $B_n$'s, i.e., $U_{\alpha} = \bigcup_{m_{\alpha}} B_{m_{\alpha}}$ since $\{B_n\}$ is a basis.

Then $X = \bigcup_{\alpha} U_{\alpha} = \bigcup_{\alpha} \bigcup_{m_{\alpha}} B_{m_{\alpha}}$ where the LHS $\bigcup_{\alpha} \bigcup_{m_{\alpha}} B_{m_{\alpha}}$ is countable (after eliminating redundant copies). Now for each $B_n$ which appears as some $B_{m_{\alpha}} \subseteq U_{\alpha}$ we may choose $U_{\alpha} = U_{\alpha_n}$ and hence $X = \bigcup_{\alpha} \bigcup_{m_{\alpha}} B_{m_{\alpha}} \subseteq \bigcup_{n} U_{\alpha_n}$. In particular, $X \subseteq \bigcup_{n} U_{\alpha_n}$ where the RHS is a countable cover.
