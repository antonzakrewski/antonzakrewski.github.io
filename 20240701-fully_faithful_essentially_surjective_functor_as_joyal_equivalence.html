fully faithful essentially surjective functor is a joyal equivalence

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}$ be infinity categories and $f: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ an essentially surjective and fully faithful infinity functor. Then $f$ is a joyal equivalence

Proof

For all $X \in \mathrm{sSet}$ the induced map

\begin{align*} f_*: (C^X)^{\simeq} \rightarrow (\mathcal{D}^X)^{\simeq} \end{align*}

is a joyal equivalence

Consider $X = \mathcal{D}$. Then $\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}} \in \mathrm{Ob}((\mathcal{D}^\mathcal{D})^{\simeq})$. Thus there exists an $g \in (\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{D}})_0^{\simeq}$ such that $f g \simeq \mathrm{id}$.

It suffices to show that $gf \simeq \mathrm{id}$.

Consider the map

\begin{align*} f_x: (\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{C})^{\simeq} \rightarrow (\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}})^{\simeq} \end{align*}

under this map $gf$ gets mapped to $fgf$. Now as $fg \simeq \mathrm{id}$ we conclude that $fgf \simeq f$ Then observe that the identity maps to $f$.

As it is a joyal equivalence, we get a path $gf \rightarrow \mathrm{id}$ which by bijection of path compennets is doned
