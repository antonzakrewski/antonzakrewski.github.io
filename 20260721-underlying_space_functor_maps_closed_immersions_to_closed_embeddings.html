underlying space functor maps closed immersions to closed embeddings

Proposition

Let $Y \hookrightarrow X$ be a closed immersion. Then $\vert Y \vert \rightarrow \vert X \vert$ is also a closed immersion of topological spaces, that is $\vert Y \vert$ is a closed subspace of $\vert X \vert$.

Proof

first see that $\vert Y \vert \hookrightarrow \vert X \vert$ is a monomorphism (cf. underlying space functor preserves monomorphisms). Now let $\mathrm{Spec}(R) \rightarrow X$ be an R-point Then consider the cartesian square

\[\begin{tikzcd Y_R \arrow[hookrightarrow, ]{r}{} \dar & \mathrm{Spec}(R) \dar \\ Y \rar & X \\ \end{tikzcd}\]

where $Y_R \cong \mathrm{Spec}(R/I)$ for some ideal $I$ (cf. classification of closed subfunctors of affine schemes).

By the explicit description of the quotient topology, a subset $U \subseteq \vert X \vert$ is closed if and only if it is closed in $\mathrm{Spec}(R) \rightarrow X$ for all R-points. Moreover, colimits are universal in a 1-presheaf category, hence $Y_R$ is the colimit indexed over $\mathrm{El}(X)$ Hence we have a commutative diagram

\[\begin{tikzcd \vert \mathrm{Spec}(R/I) \vert \arrow[hookrightarrow, ]{r}{} \dar & \vert \mathrm{Spec}(R) \vert \dar \\ \vert Y \vert \rar & \vert X \vert \\ \end{tikzcd}\]

and a subset $U \subseteq \vert Y \vert$ is closed if and only if it is closed in $\mathrm{Spec}(R/I) \rightarrow X$ for all R-points.

Thus this statement reduces to zariski spectrum of a quotient ring, which shows that $\vert \mathrm{Spec}(R/I) \vert \rightarrow \vert \mathrm{Spec}(R) \vert$ is a closed immersion.
