Proposition

Let $X \in \mathrm{CH}$ be compact hausdorff. Then convergence in the strict topology implies pointwise convergence

Proof

Let $f_n \rightarrow f$ converge strictly. Then for any $x \in X$, choose a function $h: X \rightarrow [0,1]$ which vanishes at $\infty$ and (e.g.: choose a compact neighbourhood of $x$, take the constant function there and extend it in $X^+$ where the point at $\infty$ is required to map it to $0$ - this exists by intervall is a injective cogenerator in CH / T3a / locally compact hausdorff). Then $f_n \cdot h \rightarrow f \cdot h$ with respect to the sup norm which implies that $f(x) = f_n h (x) \rightarrow (f h)(x) = f(x)$.
