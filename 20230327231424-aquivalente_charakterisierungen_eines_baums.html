Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph.

TFAE:

  1. $T$ is a tree, i.e. a circlefree and connected graph
  2. for each $u,v$ there exists precisely one path $u \rightarrow v$ (cf. path)
  3. $T$ is minimally connected, i.e. after removing any edge the graph is not connected anymore
  4. $T$ is maximally circlefree

1) $\implies$ 2)

By connectedness there exists a path $u \rightarrow v$. Suppose there exist two different paths $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$. Then $\gamma_1 \ast \gamma_2^{-1}$ is a provides circle: Choose a vertex $v \in \gamma_1$ which does not occur in $\gamma_2$. Then choose the highest vertex $z \in \gamma_1$ with $z < v$ and $z \in \gamma_2$. Note that such a vertex exists (by well-ordered and $v \in \gamma_1,\gamma_2$). Let $e = \{v_1,v_2\} \in E$ be any edge, then it is in particular the unique path from $v_1$ to $v_2$. After removing $e$ there does not exist a path anymore (since $e$ was unique)
