existence of small enough neighbourhoods which restrict and continuous map

Proposition

Let $(X,\mathcal{T}_X)$ and $(Y,\mathcal{T}_Y)$ be topological spaces. Suppose $f: X \rightarrow Y$ is a map. TFAE:

  1. $f$ is continuous
  2. for each $x \in X$ and each open subset $U_{f(x)}$ of $f(x)$, there exists a neighbourhood $U_{x}$ such that $f[U_x] \subseteq U_{f(x)}$.

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

choosing $f^{-1}[U_{f(x)}]$ suffices.

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let $U$ be an open subset, then we need to show that $f^{-1}[U]$ is open or equivalently a neighbourhood of all its points. w.l.o.g. we may assume that $f^{-1}[U_{f(x)}]$ is nonempty, so choose a point $x$ with $f(x) \in U$. If we can find an open subset $U_{x}$ such that $f[U_x] \subseteq U_{f(x)}$, then this shows that $U_{x} \subseteq f^{-1}[U]$ or that $f^{-1}[U]$ is a neighbourhood of $x$. Since $x$ was arbitrary, this shows that $f^{-1}[U]$ is open.
