base space acts trivially on cohomology classes pulled back from total space

Proposition

Let

be a fiber sequence in the category of Anima. Let $E \in \mathrm{Sp}$ be a spectrum.

Let $x \in E^*(F)$ be an element in cohomology which is pulled back from some $y \in E^*(B)$. Then the action of $B$ on $F$ becomes trivial applied to $x$

Proof

follows from the Lurie theorem about colimits in Anima which shows that the map

\begin{align*} F \rightarrow E \end{align*}

is \(B\)-equivariant.

So in particular the action on $y$ is trivial. Then furthermore the map on cohomology

\begin{align*} E^*(E) \rightarrow E^*(F) \end{align*}

is \(B\)-equivariant, so the action on the image of $y$ - namely $x$ - is trivial
