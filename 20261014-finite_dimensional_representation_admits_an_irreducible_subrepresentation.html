finite dimensional representation admits an irreducible subrepresentation

Proposition

Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a K-Algebra and $V$ a representation. Then $V$ contains an irreducible representation.

Proof

Let $V$ have dimension $n$ over $k$. Then if $V$ has a nontrivial $A$ submodule $V_1 \subsetneq V$, then it is still a proper sub vector space, hence has dimension $\mathrm{dim}_k(V_1)< n$. We can proceed to create a chain of proper \(A\)-submodules $V \supseteq V_1 \supseteq ...$ which has to stop at some point, since passing to dimension induces a decreasing sequence $n < \mathrm{dim}_{k}(V_1) < ...$.

Then the last submodule of a maximal chain is an irreducible submodule: It is irreducible, because the chain is maximal.
