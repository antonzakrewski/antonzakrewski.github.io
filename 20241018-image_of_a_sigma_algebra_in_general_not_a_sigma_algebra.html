image of a sigma algebra in general not a sigma algebra

Proposition

Let $(\mathcal{F}, X)$ be a sigma-algebra and $f: X \rightarrow Y$ a map Then the family of sets

\begin{align*} \{B \subseteq Y \vert \exists A \in \mathcal{F} \vert f[A] = B \} \end{align*}

is in general not a sigma algebra

Proof

consider a finite set $[n]$ with the powerset and the inclusion into an infinite set, e.g. $\mathbb{N}$.

Then for example $\mathbb{N} \not\in f[\mathcal{F}]$
