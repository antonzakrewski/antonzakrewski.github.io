dual of l infinity splits as dual of l0 and kernel

Proposition

there exists a canonical splitting

\begin{align*} (l^{\infty})^* \cong (l_0)^* \end{align*}

Proof

Let $f \in (l^{\infty})^*$, we need to show that there exists a unique representation

\begin{align*} f = f_1 + f_2 \end{align*}

for $f_1 \in l_0^*$ and $f_2_{\vert l_0} = 0$.

Observe that the restriction of a bounded linear operator is again bounded, so define So define $f_1 \coloneqq J(f_{\vert l_0})$ for $J: l_{0}^* \rightarrow (l^\infty)^*$ from dual of l0 embeds into dual of l infinity.

Now define $f_2 \coloneqq f - f_1$ which is also a bounded operator. Recall that continuous maps into a hausdorff space - like $\mathbb{K}$ - are determined by a dense subset. It follows that $f_2$ is zero on $e_n$ - which is a schauder basis of $l_0$. Therefore, it is zero on a dense subset - namely $\mathrm{span}(e_n)$ - and hence zero when restricted to $l_0 \subseteq \overline{\mathrm{span}(e_n)}$.

So it remains to show that this is unique: It suffices to show that one of these summands - in our case $f_1$ - is unique: So let $f_1 + f_2 = f = f_1' + f_2'$ be two different representations of the desired kind, then it follows that

\begin{align*} f_1(e_n) = (f_1 + f_2)(e_n) = f(e_n) = (f_1' + f_2')(e_n) = f_1'(e_n) \end{align*}

which shows $f_1$ and $f_2$ agree on a dense subset (namely $\mathrm{span}(e_n)$) and hence coincide on $\mathrm{span}(e_n)$
