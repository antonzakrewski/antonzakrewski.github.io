L infinity for N is not separable

Proposition

Let $\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}$ be the space of compelx valued sequences with the L unendlich norm. Then this space is not separable

Proof

Suppose $X = \{x_n^m \vert m \in \mathbb{N}\} \subseteq l^{\infty}$ is a countable dense subset. Then it suffices to construct a sequence $y_n \in l^{\infty}$ with $\| (x_n)^m - y_n \| \geq 1$ for each $m \in \mathbb{N}$ (then $B_{1}(y_n) \cap X = \emptyset$): If such an $y_n$ exists, then $y_n \not\in \overline{X}$ as there can't exist a convergent sequence $(z_n)^m \xrightarrow{m \rightarrow \infty} (y_n)$ for $(z_n)^m \in X$).

For that we may inductively define

\begin{align*} y_n \coloneqq \begin{cases} -1 & \mbox{if } x_n^n > 0 \\ 1 & \mbox{if } x_n^n \leq 0 \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

and then $(y_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is evidently a bounded sequences ($\| y_n \| = 1$) with $\| x_n^m - y_n \| \geq \vert y_m - x_m \vert \geq 1$ for all $x_n^m$.
