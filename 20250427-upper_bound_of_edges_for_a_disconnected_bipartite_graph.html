upper bound of edges for a disconnected, bipartite graph

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a bipartite graph with $\vert V \vert = n$ and $\vert E \vert > \mathrm{floor}(\frac{n-1}{2} \cdot \mathrm{ceil}(\frac{n-1}{2})$. Then $G$ is a connected graph.

Proof

optimal

Consider

\begin{align*} K_{\mathrm{floor}(\frac{n-1}{2}), \mathrm{ceil}(\frac{n-1}{2})} \coprod K_1 \end{align*}

Then it has

\begin{align*} \mathrm{floor}(\frac{n-1}{2} \cdot \mathrm{ceil}(\frac{n-1}{2} \end{align*}

many edges

bound

Let $G$ be a maximal, disconnected bipartite graph. Assume that $A \coprod B = V$ are disjoint with $A \times B \subseteq V$. Then consider the intersection with the connected compenents, which forms a partition $A_1,...,A_l, B_1,...,B_l$. w.l.o.g. we may assume that $l = 2$ as otherwise we could connect $A_{l-1}, A_l$ with $B_{l-1},B_{l}$ etc. Thus we get $K_{n_1,n_2} \coprod K_{m_1,m_2}$ with $n = n_1 + n_2 + m_1 + m_2$.

Here $K_{n_1,n_2}$ contains $n_1 \cdot n_2$ many edges, hence

\begin{align*} \vert E_{K_{n_1,n_2} \coprod K_{m_1,m_2}} \vert = n_1 \cdot n_2 + m_1 \cdot m_2 \end{align*}

which is maximal for $m_1 = 1,m_2 = 0$ and $n_1 = \mathrm{ceil}(\frac{n-1}{2}), n_2 = \mathrm{floor}(\frac{n-1}{2})$
