Module Homomorphism for a non-commutative ring in general not a module

Proposition

Let \(R\) be a non-commutative ring, $M,N$ \(R\)-modules then the set of module-homomorphisms $\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,N)$ with

\begin{align*} r \cdot \varphi(m) \mapsto r \cdot m \end{align*}

is in general not a module.

Proof

For example take $M = N$ and possibly any other "sufficiently injective" module homomorphism $\mathrm{id}: M \rightarrow N$ Let $\mathrm{id} \in \mathrm{End}_{R}(M)$ and $m \in M$, $r,r' \in R$. Then $r' \cdot -$ is a module endomorphism (cf. scalar multiplication as endomorphism) Thus

\begin{align*} (r \cdot r') \cdot \mathrm{id}(m) =& r \cdot (r' \cdot \mathrm{id}(m) \\ =& r' \cdot r \cdot \mathrm{id}(m) \end{align*}

This requires in general commutativity
