compact subset of an open space for a metric space

Proposition

Let $M$ be a metric space, $K \subseteq M$ a compact set and $U \subseteq M$ an open neighbourhood, such that $K \subseteq U$. Then there exists an $\varepsilon > 0$ such that the epsilon-neighbourhood of $K$ is a subset of $U$

Proof

follows from:

  • allgemeiner Satz vom Minimum und Maximum
  • distance to a subset as continuous map for
\begin{align*} d(-, X \setminus U) \end{align*}

where each point $k \in K$ also lies in $U$, hence by definition of an open set there exists an $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $U_{\varepsilon}(k) \subseteq U$, therefore showing

\begin{align*} d(-, X \setminus U) \geq \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \end{align*}
