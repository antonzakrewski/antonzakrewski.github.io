bounded continuous functions are a complete metric space

Proposition

Proof

metric space / Metrik

nonnegative

symmetric

follows as $\vert f(x) - g(x) \vert = \vert g(x) - f(x) \vert$ and hence it is also symmetric after applying $\mathrm{sup}(-)$ this shows $d_{\infty}(f,g) = d_{\infty}(g,f)$

positive definite

Assume that $d_{\infty}(f,g) = 0$, then for each $x$ in $X$ it holds that $\vert f(x) - g(x) \vert \leq \mathrm{sup}_{x} \vert f(x) - g(x) \vert = 0$, i.e., $f(x) = g(x)$ or $f = g$. Moreover, $d_{\infty}(f,f) = 0$ follows as $\mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} \vert f(x) - f(x) = 0$.

triangle

observe that for $f,g,h$ and $x$ in $X$: $\vert f(x) - h(x) \vert \leq \vert f(x) - g(x) \vert + \vert g(x) - h(x) \vert$. Hence applying $\mathrm{sup}(-)$ preserves this relation, i.e., $\mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} \vert f(x) - h(x) \vert \leq \mathrm{sup}_{x \in X}( \vert f(x) - g(x) \vert + \vert g(x) - h(x) \vert) \leq \mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} \vert f(x) - h(x) \vert \leq \mathrm{sup}_{x \in X}( \vert f(x) - g(x) \vert + \mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} ( \vert g(x) - h(x) \vert)$

complete

Observe that the map

\begin{align*} \mathrm{ev}_x: C_b(X) \rightarrow& \mathbb{K} \\ f \mapsto f(x) \end{align*}

is continuous/ 1 lipschitzs for $x$ in $X$. Let $f_n$ be a Cauchy sequence. Then $f_n(x) = \mathrm{ev}_x(f)$ is also a cauchy sequnce, since $\mathrm{ev}_x$ is 1-lipschitz. Moreover, $\mathbb{K}$ is complete, hence $f_n(x) \rightarrow f(x)$ for some $f(x) \in \mathbb{K}$. We claim, that $f: x \mapsto f(x)$ is a continuous, bounded function.

bounded

Observe that $\mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} \vert f_n(x) \vert$ converges, as it is a Cauchy sequence in $\mathbb{R}$. Hence $\mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} \vert f_n(x) \vert$ is bounded by some $C$, i.e. for all $x \in X$ and all $n$ we get $\vert f_n(x) \vert \leq C$. Now we may apply the limit (and use that $\vert - \vert$ is continuous) and get, that for all $x \in X$ $\vert f(x) \vert = \vert \mathrm{lim} f_n(x) \vert \mathrm{lim} \vert f_n(x) \vert \leq C$, hence $\mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} \vert f_n(x) \vert \leq C$.

continuous

Let $x \in X$ and $U$ be an open neighbourhood of $f(x)$. Then we need to show that there exists a neighbourhood $U_x$ of $x$ such that $f[U_x] \subseteq U$ (cf. existence of neighbourhoods and continuous map) w.l.o.g. we may assume that $U = B_{\varepsilon}(f(x))$.

Then $f_n(x) \rightarrow f(x)$, hence for almost all $n$: $\mathrm{sup}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}( \vert f(x) - f_n(x) \vert ) < \frac{\varepsilon}{5}$. Moreover, $f_n$ converges to $f$ (in the metric space $C_b(X) \cup \{f\}$ with the supremum norm) so for almost all $n$: $\mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} \vert f(x) - f_n(x) \vert < \frac{\varepsilon}{5}$.

Now choose $n >> 0$ such that both conditions above hold and consider the open subset $O \coloneqq f_n^{-1}[B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{5}}(f_n(x))]$. We claim that $f[O] \subseteq U$ and $x \in f_n^{-1}[B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{5}}(f_n(x))]$. The second claim is evident, so it remains to show the first.

So let $x' \in O$, then

  1. $\vert f(x') - f_n(x') \vert < \frac{\varepsilon}{5}$ as $\mathrm{sup}_{x \in X} \vert f(x) - f_n(x) \vert < \frac{\varepsilon}{5}$
  2. $\vert f_n(x') - f_n(x) \vert < \frac{\varepsilon}{5}$ as $x' \in f_n^{-1}[B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{5}}(f_n(x))]$
  3. $\vert f_n(x) - f(x) \vert < \frac{\varepsilon}{5}$ as $\vert f(x) - f_n(x) \vert < \frac{\varepsilon}{5}$.

Therefore, the triangle inequality shows

\begin{align*} \vert f(x') - f(x) \vert \leq& \vert f(x') - f_n(x') \vert + \vert f_n(x') - f_n(x) \vert + \vert f_n(x) - f(x) \vert \\ <& \varepsilon \end{align*}

and hence $f(x') \in f[B_{\varepsilon}(f(x))]$. This shows - as claime
