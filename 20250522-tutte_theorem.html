tutte theorem

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph. TFAE:

  1. there exists a perfect matching
  2. for each $S \susbeteq V$ the graph $G \setminus S$ contains at most $\vert S \vert$ many connected components with odd amount of vertices.

Denote with

\begin{align*} q(G \setminus S) = \mathrm{amount of connected components with odd amount of vertices} \end{align*}

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Suppose there exists a perfect matching and let $S \subseteq G$ be arbitrary. Then for each connected component of $G \setminus S$ there exists at least one vertex which connects to a unique vertex in $S$:

  1. uniqueness
  2. existence follows by Pigeonhole principle where

2) $\implies$ 1)

Proof by contraposition

Suppose $G$ does not contain a perfect matching.

condition 2) invariant under addition of edges

We claim that if we add edges to $G$, then the resulting $G' \supseteq G$ still satisfies the condition: If we add an edge with vertices in $S$, then this does not change $G \setminus S$. If we add an edge with vertices in one of the resulting connected components of $G \setminus S$, then we still have the same amount of vertices in the connected component. Furthermore if we add an edge with vertices in two different connected components of $G \setminus S$ then we either have the same $q(G \setminus S)$ (connecting even + even or odd + odd) or $q(G \setminus S) - 1$ (connecting even + odd).

connected component

Now choose a maximal $G' \supseteq G$ which does not contain a matching. Then $G'$ still satisfies the condition 2).

If we add any arbitrary edge, then by assumption $G'$ contains a perfect matching.

Let $S = \{v \in V : \mathrm{deg}_S(v) = \vert v \vert - 1\}$ i.e. the vertices which are connected to each other vertex via a direct edge. We claim that $G \setminus S$ contains complete graphs as connected components.

construction of the matching

Let $C_1,...,C_l$ be the connected components of $G \setminus S$, which are by the arguments above complete graphs.. Now choose a perfect matching on each connected component. The matchings on the components with even amount of vertices are perfect, the matchings on the components with odd amount of vertices miss only one vertex.

Now by assumption $q(G \setminus S) \leq \vert S \vert$, hence we may choose for each vertex $c_i$ of $C_1,...,C_l$ which is not contained in a perfect matching a pairwise different vertex $s_i \in S$ and the edge $c_i - s_i$ (which exists, since $s_i$ is by assumption connected to each vertex in $G$)

Then we may match those vertices in $S$ which are not already matched with some $c_i$. We claim that $S \setminus \{s_i\}$ has an even amount of vertices:

We have

\begin{align*} \vert V \vert =& \sum_{i=1}^l v(C_i) + \vert S \vert \\ =& \sum_{v(G) \mathrm{even}} v(G) + \sum_{V(G) \mathrm{od}} (v(C_i) + 1) + \vert S \vert - q(G \setminus S) \end{align*}

where the first two summands are even.

Furthermore $\vert V \vert$ is even, since

\begin{align*} q(g) =& q(G \setminus \emptyset) \\ =& \vert \emptyset \vert =& 0 \end{align*}

which shows that $\vert S \vert - q(G \setminus S)$ is even, which is precisely the cardinality of $S \setminus \{s_i\}$.

Hence we have a contradiction to the assumption, that $G'$ does not contain a mathcing.
