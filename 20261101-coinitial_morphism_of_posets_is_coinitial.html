coinitial morphism of directed posets is coinitial

Proposition

Let $P,Q$ be filtered partially ordered sets and $g: P \rightarrow Q$ a coinitial morphism of posets. Then for any functor $\mathcal{F}: Q \rightarrow \mathrm{Set}$, there exists a canonical equivalence

\begin{align*} \mathrm{colim}_{P} \mathcal{F} \circ g \rightarrow \mathrm{colim}_{Q} \mathcal{F} \end{align*}

Proof

First observe that there exists a canonical map $h: \mathrm{colim}_{P} \mathcal{F} \circ g \rightarrow \mathrm{colim}_{Q} \mathcal{F}$: It is induced by the universal porperty of $\mathrm{colim}_{P} \mathcal{F}$ by the maps $(\mathcal{F} \circ g)(p) \rightarrow \mathcal{F}(g(p)) \rightarrow \mathrm{colim}_{Q} \mathcal{F}$.

We show that this map is a Bijection. For this we use the explicit description of the filtered colimit in $\mathrm{Set}$: It is given by $(\coprod_{q \in Q} \mathcal{F}(q))/\sim$ where two elements $(x,q), (x',q')$ are equivalent if and only if there exists a $\tilde{q}$ and maps $f: q \rightarrow \tilde{q}, f': q' \rightarrow \tilde{q}$ such that $\mathcal{F}(f)(x) = \mathcal{F}(f')(x')$.

Let $[x] \in \mathrm{colim}_{Q} \mathcal{F}$ be an element, then it is represented by an element $(x_q,q)$. In particular, we can find an $p \in P$ such that $q \leq g(p)$ or there exists an $\tilde{f}: q \rightarrow g(p)$.

Hence the equivalence class of $x_p$ is also represented by $\mathcal{F}(f)(x_p) =: (x_{g(p)}, g(p))$, which lies in $\mathcal{F}(g(p)) = (\mathcal{F} \circ g)(p)$. This shows that $(x_{g(p)}, g(p))$ lifts to $\mathrm{colim}_{P} (\mathcal{F} \circ g)$ and maps to the equivalence class of $[x] \in \mathrm{colim}_{Q} (\mathcal{F} \circ g)$.

Now suppose there exist $[(y_1,p_1)], [(y_2,p_2)]$ such that $h([(y_1,p_1)]) = h([(y_2,p_2)])$. Then there exists an $q \in Q$ and morphisms $f_1: g(p_1) \rightarrow q, f_2: g(p_2) \rightarrow q$ such that $f_1(y_1) = f_2(y_2)$. We may then find an $\tilde{p}$ and a morphism $\tilde{f}: q \rightarrow g(\tilde{p})$.

In particular, since every diagram commutes here, we have for $\tilde{f}_1: p_1 \rightarrow \tilde{p}, \tilde{f}_2: p_2 \rightarrow \tilde{p}$ that

\begin{align*} \tilde{f}_2(y_2) =& (\tilde{f}) (f_1(y_1)) \\ =& \tilde{f} (f_2(y_2)) \\ =& \tilde{f}_2(y_2) \end{align*}

This shows that $[(y_1,p_1)] = [(y_2,p_2)]$ or that the map was injective.
