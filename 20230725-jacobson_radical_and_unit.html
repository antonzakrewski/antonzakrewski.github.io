Jacobson radical and unit

Proposition

Let $A$ be a ring and $x \in A$ TFAE:

  1. $x \in \mathcal{J}_A$ (see: Jacobson radical)
  2. $\forall y \in A : 1 - xy$ is <right invertible>

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Proof by contraposition: Assume there exists an $y \in A$ such that $1 - xy \in A \setminus A^{\times}$. Then consider $(1 - xy) R \subsetneq R$. By Lemma von Zorn there exists a maximal right ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ with $(1 - xy) R \subseteq \mathfrak{m}$. Now $1 \not\in \mathfrak{m}$ implies that $x \not\in \mathfrak{m}$ hence $x \not\in \mathcal{J}_A$

2) $\implies$ 1)

Assume there exists a maximal right ideal with $x \not\in \mathfrak{m}$, then

\begin{align*} x + \mathfrak{m} \neq 0 \in A/\mathfrak{m} \end{align*}

and because of the characterization of maximales Ideal eines kommutativen Rings und Faktorring als Körper we get that there exists a $y \in A$ such that

\begin{align*} 0 + \mathfrak{m} = 1 - xy + \mathfrak{m} \Rightarrow 1 - xy \in \mathfrak{m} \end{align*}

Therefore it also holds, that

\begin{align*} 1 - xy \in \mathfrak{m} \subseteq A \setminus A^{\times} \end{align*}

(see: von einer Einheit erzeugtes Ideal und triviales Ideal)
