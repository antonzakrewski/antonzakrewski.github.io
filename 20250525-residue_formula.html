residue formula

Proposition

Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be open and $f: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ be a holomorphic function Then integration is a group-homomorphism

\begin{align*} \int_{-} f \mathrm{d}x: H_1(\Omega, \mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow (\mathbb{C}, +) \end{align*}

where a circle $\gamma$ around poles $z_1,...,z_n$ maps to

\begin{align*} \int_{\gamma} f(-) \mathrm{d}z = 2\pi i \sum_{i=1}^n \mathrm{res}_{z_k}(f) \end{align*}

see:

  • residue of a pole of order n

Proof

reduction to classic residue formula

follows as

\begin{align*} H_1(\Omega, \mathbb{Z}) = \pi_1(\Omega)^{\mathrm{ab}} \end{align*}

on connected components, where $\mathbb{C},+$ is abelian

classic formula

reduction to single pole

follows from homotopy invariance

single pole

Then

\begin{align*} f(z) = \sum_{k=-n}^{-1} a_k (z - z_0)^k + G(z) \end{align*}

for $G$ holomorphic in a neighbourhood of $z$.

Then linearity of integration gives

\begin{align*} \int_{\gamma} f(z) \mathrm{d}z =& \sum_{k=-n}^{-1} \alpha_k \int_{\gamma} (z - z_0)^k \mathrm{d}z + \int_{\gamma} G =& 2\pi \mathrm{i} a_{-1} \end{align*}

where the last follows from cauchy integral formula
