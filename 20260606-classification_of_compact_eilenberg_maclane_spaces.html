classification of compact Eilenberg-MacLane spaces

Proposition

Let $G$ be a nontrivial abelian group and $K(G,n)$ an Eilenberg-MacLane space, which is compact. Then one of the following happens

  1. $G$ is finite, $n = 0$
  2. $G = \bigoplus_{i=1}^m \mathbb{Z}$ and $n =1$

Proof

w.l.o.g. assume that $G \neq 0$.

$n = 1$

Observe that first hurewicz shows $K(G,1) \simeq \pi_1(K(G,1)) \simeq H_1(K(G,1))$ and compact anima has finitely generated homology so $G$ is finitely generated abelian group. Now the classification of finitely generated abelian groups shows $G \simeq \bigoplus \mathbb{Z}/(m_i)$ and hence by product of Eilenberg-MacLane anima is an Eilenberg-MacLane anima

\begin{align*} K(G,1) \simeq \prod_{i=1}^l K(\mathbb{Z}/(m_i),1) \end{align*}

where each $K(\mathbb{Z}/(m_i),1)$ is a retract of $K(G,1)$. Hence it suffices to observe that $K(\mathbb{Z}/n \mathbb{Z},1) \simeq B \mathbb{Z}/n \mathbb{Z}$ is not compact for $n \neq 0$ (e.g. integral homology of BZ/nZ) and hence $K(G,1)$ is compact at most if $m_i = 1$.

If each $m_i = 0$, then $K(G,1) \simeq \prod_{i=1}^l S^1$ is compact (cf. finite product of compact anima as compact anima).

$n \geq 2$

use:

  • finite anima with finite pi_1 contains infinitely many nontrivial homotopy groups
