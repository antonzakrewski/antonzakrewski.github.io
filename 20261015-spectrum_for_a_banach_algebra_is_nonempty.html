spectrum for a banach algebra is nonempty

Proposition

Let $A$ be a unital Banach Algebra. Then the unital spectrum is nonempty.

Proof

Part of some spectral theorem (???) implies that for any linear functional $l: A \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$, the partial map

\begin{align*} l(R_{a}): \mathbb{C} \rightarrow A \xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev}_{v_i}} \mathbb{C} \\ \lambda \mapsto l((a - \lambda)^{-1}) \end{align*}

is a holomorphic function whereever defined.

We want to show that if $R_a$ is defined on $\mathbb{C}$, that $R_a$ is then constant. Then $R_{a}$ has to be the constant zero map, since $R_{a}(n) \rightarrow 0$ as $\vert R_{a}(n) \vert \leq \frac{1}{n - \| a \|}$. This will be an evident contradiction with $R_a(-) \in A^{\times}$. It suffices to show that $l(R_{a})$ is constant for all $l$ since $\mathbb{C}$ is a coseparator in the category of banach spaces. Suppose $R_{a}(\lambda) - R_{a}(\lambda') \neq 0$, then there exsits a linear functional $l: A \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ such that $l(R_{a}(\lambda) - R_{a}(\lambda')) \neq 0$ and hence $l(R_{a}(-))$ can't be constant.

To show that $l \circ R_{a}(-)$ is constant, it suffices to show that it is bounded by Liouville's Theorem. Now the map $l \circ R_a$ is in particular continuous and hence bounded on compacts like $\overline{B_{\| a \| + 1}(0)}$. Outside $\overline{B_{\| a \| + 1}(0)$ we have $\vert R_{a}(-) \vert \leq 1$ since for $\lambda$ with $\vert \lambda \vert \geq \| a \| + 1$, we get $\| a - \lambda \| \geq 1$ by the reverse triangle inequality and hence $ \| R_{a}(\lambda) \| < 1$ and hence in particular bounded after applying $l$.
