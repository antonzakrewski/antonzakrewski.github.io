trivial intersection of vector spaces and intersection with direct sum in general not trivial

Proposition

Let $V$ be a vector space and $U_1,U_2,U_3 \subseteq V$ subspaces. Suppose $U_i \cap U_j = \{0\}$ for $i \neq j$.

Then in general $(U_1 \oplus U_2) \cap U_3\neq \{0\}$.

Proof

consider $V = \mathbb{R}^2, U_1 = \left \langle \begin{pNiceMatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pNiceMatrix} \rangle \right, U_2 = \left \langle \begin{pNiceMatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pNiceMatrix} \rangle \right, U_3 = \left \langle \begin{pNiceMatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pNiceMatrix} \right \rangle$. Then $U_1 \oplus U_2 = \mathbb{R}^2$ so $(U_1 \oplus U_2) \cap U_3 = U_3$. But $U_i \cap U_j = \{0\}$ for $i \neq j$
