extension of a sheaf on a basis of a topological space (bad proof)

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ a basis of a topological space $X$. Let $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}} \rightarrow \mathrm{Set}$ be a functor, which satisfies the sheaf condition.

Then the right kan extension, pointwise given by

\begin{align*} \overline{\mathcal{F}}(V) \cong \lim_{U \subseteq V, U \in \mathcal{B}} \mathcal{F}(U) \end{align*}

Uniqueness:

Let $s,t$ be two sections in $\overline{\mathcal{F}}(V)$, which are of the form $(s_{U})_{\vert U}$ and $(t_{U})_{\vert U}$ for $U \subseteq V, U \in \mathcal{B}$. Assume that they agree on a cover $V_i$ of $V$, i.e. for all $U_{i,j} \subseteq V_i$ and $U_{i,j} \in \mathcal{B}$, we have $(s_{U}_{\vert U_{i,j}}) = (t_{U}_{\vert U_{i,j}})$. Now for any $U \subseteq V, U \in \mathcal{B}$ we need to show that $s$ and $t$ agree on $U$ (by the formula of the limit). We get that $U \cap V_{i,j}$ is an open cover of $U$. In particular, after refining to an $W_{i,j,k} \subseteq U \cap V_{i,j}, W_{i,j,k} \in \mathcal{B}$ we may assume that $W_{i,j,k} \in \mathcal{B}$ is an open cover of $U$. Then $s$ and $t$ agree on $W_{i,j,k}$, so as $\mathcal{F}$ is a sheaf, we get that $s$ and $t$ agree on $U$.

Hence $s$ and $t$ agree on all $U$ or agree in $\overline{\mathcal{F}}(V)$.

existence of lifts

Let $V$ be an open and $V_i \subseteq V$ an open covering. Assume that we have $s_{V_i} \in \overline{\mathcal{F}}(V)$ which agree on intersections: Then for each $U \subseteq V$ we have that $U \cap V_i$ is an open covering of $U$. Passing to $V_{i,j} \subseteq U \cap V_i$ for $V_{i,j} \in \mathcal{B}$ we may assume that the covering consists of elements in $\mathcal{B}$.
