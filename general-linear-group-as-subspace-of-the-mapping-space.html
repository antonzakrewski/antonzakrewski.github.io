general linear group as subspace of the mapping space

Proposition

The general linear group $\mathrm{Gl}_n(\mathbb{R})$ (cf. real general linear group as manifold) is a subspace of the mapping space

\begin{align*} \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R}) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Map}(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n) \end{align*}

Proof

Consider the map

\begin{align*} \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R}) \rightarrow& \mathbb{R}^n \\ (v,A) \mapsto& A \circ v \\ \end{align*}

it is continuous, as it is the restriction of the multiplication of $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R}^n) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$.

by adjunction - $\mathbb{R}$ is locally compact (cf. mapping space product adjunction for topological spaces) - this induces a map

\begin{align*} \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R}) \rightarrow& \mathrm{Map}(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n) \\ A \mapsto& A \end{align*}

which is injective as can be seen by element chasing.

by right adjoint preserves products we get

\begin{align*} \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Map}(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n) \cong \mathrm{Map}(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R})^n \end{align*}

using openness of a finite product of open maps it suffices to show that

\begin{align*} \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Map}(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}) \end{align*}

is an open map.

By open injective map and subbasis we may look at the set of subsets

\begin{align*} \{B \in \mathrm{Gl}_n(\mathbb{R}) \vert \vert A e_i - B e_i \vert < \varepsilon\} \end{align*}

for $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ as subbasis around $A$.
