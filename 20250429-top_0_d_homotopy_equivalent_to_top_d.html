Top_0(d) homotopy equivalent to Top(d)

Proposition

The maps

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Top}_0(d) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Top}(d) \\ \end{align*}

and

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Top}(d) \rightarrow \mathrm{Top}_0(d) f \mapsto f - f(0) \end{align*}

are homotopy equivalences for Top(d) & Top_0(d)

Proof

a) $\mathrm{Top}_0(d) \rightarrow \mathrm{Top}(d) \rightarrow \mathrm{Top}_0(d)$

note that the composition is already the identity

b)

consider the map

\begin{align*} [0,1] \times \mathrm{Top}(d) \rightarrow& \mathrm{Top}(d) \\ (t,f) \mapsto f - f(0) \cdot t \end{align*}

which restricts to the identity resp. the composition on the boundary of $[0,1]$.

Hence it remains to show that this map is welldefined (i.e. continuous and restricts appropriately) For that use the adjunction (and use that maps restrict to $\mathrm{Top}(d) \subseteq \mathrm{Map}(\mathbb{R}^d, \mathbb{R}^d)$)

\begin{align*} [0,1] \times \mathrm{Top}(d) \times \mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow& \mathbb{R}^d \\ (t,f,d) \mapsto& f(d) - t \cdot f(0) \end{align*}

and use

  1. $\mathbb{R}^d$ as topological vector space (link), hence addition and scalar multiplication is continuous
  2. the evaluation morphisms for $f(d), f(0)$ where we get $f(0)$ for $* \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ indicating the zero vector.
