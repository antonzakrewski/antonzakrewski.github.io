dimension of singularities of a normal quasi-projective variety

Proposition

Let $X$ be a normal quasi-projective variety Then

\begin{align*} \mathrm{dim}(X^{\mathrm{sing}}) \leq \mathrm{dim}(X) - 2 \end{align*}

see: dimension

Proof

w.l.o.g. let $X$ be affine and $d = \mathrm{dim}(X)$.

Then $X^{\mathrm{sing}} \subseteq X$ is closed with $\mathrm{dim}(X^{\mathrm{sing}}) \leq d - 1$.

Suppose $X^{\mathrm{sing}}$ has an irreducible component $Z$ of dimension $d - 1$.

Let $I(Z) \subseteq A(X)$ the vanishing ideal of $Z$ - it is a prime ideal since $Z$ is irreducible.

Consider

\begin{align*} O_{X,Z} = A(X)_{I(Z)} \end{align*}

be the corresponding local ring (cf. ring of regular functions, localization at a prime ideal as local ring)

dimension of a quotient finite type k-algebra

Then

\begin{align*} \mathrm{ht}(I(Z)) = \mathrm{dim}(X) - \mathrm{dim}(Z) \end{align*}

and hence $\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{O}_{X,z}) = \mathrm{ht}(I(Z)) = 1$.

By assumption $X$ is normal and hence $\mathcal{O}_{X,Z} = A(X)_{I(Z)}$ is normal.

Then $\mathrm{dim} \mathcal{O}_{X,Z} = 1$ and by <> is a regular local ring.

Thus the ideal $M_{X,Z} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X,Z}$ is a Principal ideal with $M_{X,Z} = (\pi) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X,Z}$

There exists an open subset $U \subseteq X$ with $Z \cap U \neq \emptyset$

w.l.o.g. we may assume that there exists an $V = D(f) \subseteq X$ <elementar offen> (i.e. affine open) with $Z \cap V \neq \emptyset$ and

\begin{align*} I(Z \cap V) = (\pi) \subseteq A(V) \end{align*}
