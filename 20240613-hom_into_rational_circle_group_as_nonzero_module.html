co-extenison for the rational circle group as nonzero module

Proposition

Let \(A\) be a ring, then

\begin{align*} \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(R,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}) \end{align*}

as \(R\)-module via co-extension of scalars is a non zero module

Proof

Let

\begin{align*} 1: \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow R \end{align*}

be the initial morphism (cf.Initial Object in Ring), especially also an group-homomorphism.

Let

\begin{align*} \frac{1}{2}: \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow& \mathbb{Q} / \mathbb{Z} \\ 1 \mapsto \frac{1}{2} \end{align*}

(cf. universal property of a ring as module)

If $1$ is injective, then there exists an extension 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathbb{Z} \arrow[rightarrowtail, ]{r}{} \arrow[dashed]{rd}{\varphi}& R \arrow[]{d}{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

with $1_R \mapsto \frac{1}{2}$, hence $\varphi \neq 0$ is a nontrivial morphism.

Suppose $1$ is not injective.

Then using the Homomorphiesatz für Gruppen there exists an injection

\begin{align*} \mathbb{Z}/k \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z} \end{align*}

and furthermore the map

\begin{align*} \mathbb{Z}/k \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow& \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \\ 1 \mapsto& \frac{1}{k} \end{align*}

thus using injectivity for

ensures the existence of a nontrivial morphism.
