continuous function induces chain map

Proposition

Let $X,Y$ be topological spaces and $f: X \rightarrow Y$ a continuous map. Then the map induced by

\begin{align*} f_n: C^{\mathrm{sing}_n}(X) \rightarrow& C^{\mathrm{sing}_n}(Y) \\ (g: \Delta_{\mathrm{Top}}^n \rightarrow X) \mapsto& (f \circ g: \Delta_{\mathrm{Top}}^n \rightarrow Y) \end{align*}

on the abelian group of singular n-simplices is a chain morphism for

Proof

element chasing for an simplex $\sigma: \Delta^n_{\mathrm{Top}} \rightarrow X$ results in:

\begin{align*} f_{n-1} \circ d_n(\delta) =& f_{n-1} \circ (\sum_{j=0}^{n} (-1)^{j} \cdot (\sigma \circ \partial_j) \\ =& \sum_{j=0}^{n} (-1)^{j} (f_{n-1} \circ \sigma \circ \partial_j ) \\ =& \sum_{j=0}^{n} (-1)^{j} (f_n \circ \sigma \circ \partial_j) \\ =& d_n \circ f_n(\sigma) \\ \end{align*}

singular boundary map

\sum_{j=0}^{n} (-1)^{j} \cdot (f \circ \sigma_{n}^{(j)}) \in C_{n-1}(X)
