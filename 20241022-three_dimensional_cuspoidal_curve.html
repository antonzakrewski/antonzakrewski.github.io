three dimensional cuspoidal curve

Definition / Proposition

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and $\mathbb{A}_{K}^3$ the affine space. Then the three dimensional cuspoidal curve is defined as

\begin{align*} C \coloneqq \{(t,t^2, t^3 \vert t \in K\} \end{align*}

and an affine variety generated by $X^2 - Y, X^3 - Z$

Proof

Note that $C \subseteq Z(X^2 - Y, X^3 - Z)$ follows trivially. Let $(X,Y,Z)$ be satisfying the above identities.

Then it follows that $X^2 = Y$ and $X^3 = Z$ showing that $(X,Y,Z) = (X,X^2,X^3)$

vanishing ideal

consider the map

\begin{align*} \varphi: K[X,Y,Z] \rightarrow& K[t] \\ X \mapsto& t \\ Y \mapsto& t^2 \\ Z \mapsto& t^3 \end{align*}

As the map is surjective, it suffices to show that $\mathrm{ker}(\varphi) = I(C)$ Here we get trivially $\mathrm{ker} \supseteq I(C)$ by checking the relations - and therefore also $\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}) \geq \mathrm{dim}(I(C))$

Note that by dimension of a quotient finite type k-algebra we get that $\mathrm{dim}(K[X,Y,Z]) = \mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{ker}) + \mathrm{dim}(K[t]$ or that $\mathrm{dim}(ker) =2$ Thus it remains to show that $(0) \subseteq (X^2 - Y) \subseteq (X^3 - Z, X^2 - Y)$ is a chain of prime ideals.

Here this follows by

  1. Satz von Eisenstein (regarding as $(K[Y,Z])[X]$
  2. some iso theroem for rings where
\begin{align*} K[X,Y,Z]/(X^2 - Y, X^3 - Z) \cong (K[X,Y,Z]/(X^2 - Y))(X^3 - Z) \end{align*}

where the quotient by a single polynomial eliminates one variable.
