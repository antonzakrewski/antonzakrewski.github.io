Proposition

Let $R$ be a ring. Then $R$ in general does not satisfy the invariant basis property

Proof

Let $k$ be a field, $k^{\mathbb{N}}$ the vector space with countable basis and $\mathrm{End}_{k}(k^{ \mathbb{N}})$ the endomorphism ring. Then choose an vectorspace-isomorphism $\psi: k^{\mathbb{N}} \cong k^{\mathbb{N}} \oplus k^{\mathbb{N}}$. Applying the hom-functor induces an isomorphism of abelian groups

\begin{align*} R =& \mathrm{End}_{k}(k^{\mathbb{N}}) \\ =& \mathrm{Hom}_{k}(k^{\mathbb{N}}, k^{\mathbb{N}}) \\ \cong& \mathrm{Hom}_{k}(k^{\mathbb{N}}, k^{\mathbb{N}} \oplus k^{\mathbb{N}}) \\ \cong& \mathrm{Hom}_{k}(k^{\mathbb{N}}, k^{\mathbb{N}}) \oplus \mathrm{Hom}_{k}(k^{\mathbb{N}}, k^{\mathbb{N}}) \\ \cong& R \oplus R \end{align*}

Furthermore the map $\psi_*$ is also a module-homomorphism of right \(R\)-modules, where the module structure is given by

\begin{align*} f \cdot \varphi = f(\varphi) \end{align*}

for $f \in R, R^2$
