cone of a metrizable space is in general not metrizable

Proposition

Let $X$ be a metrizable space, then the Cone $CX$ is in general not.

Proof

Consider $X = \mathbb{Z}$ with the subspace metric (or topologically equivalent the discrete metric). Then the cone is given by $\mathbb{Z} \times [0,1]/\sim$ or $\coprod_{z \in \mathbb{Z}} [0,1]/\sim$. We claim that the cone is not first-countable at the cone point $0$ and hence $CX$ can't be metrizable (cf. metric spaces are first countable):

Recall that a subset of $\mathbb{Z} \times [0,1]/\sim$ is a neighborhood of the cone point if and only if it contains a subset of the form $\coprod_{z \in \mathbb{Z}} [0, \varepsilon_z)$ for $\varepsilon_z > 0$. Now assume that there exists a countable neighbourhood basis $\mathcal{N} = \{O_n\}$ where each $O_n$ is -- w.l.o.g. -- of the form $O_n = \coprod_{z \in \mathbb{Z}} [0, \varepsilon_{z,n})$. Then we can -- using a diagonal argument -- construct a neighbourhood of the cone point, which is not a superset of any $O_n$:

Indeed, define

\begin{align*} U := \coprod_{z \in \mathbb{Z}} [0, \frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_{z,z}) \end{align*}

where $U \not\supseteq O_n$ since $[0, \frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_{z,z}) \not\supseteq [0, \varepsilon_{z,z})$.
