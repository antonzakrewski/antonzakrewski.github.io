degree of maps from S^n to RP^n

Proposition

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ be an odd number. Then $D(S^n, \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n) = 2 \mathbb{Z}$

Proof

Observe that $D(S^n, \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)^{\mathbb{F}_2} = 0$ (cf. mapping degree with coefficients in $\mathbb{F}_2$). This follows as degree of a map between closed oriented manifolds is determined by cohomology and for any $f: S^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$, the map $f^*: H^n(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n, \mathbb{F}_2) \rightarrow H^n(S^n, \mathbb{F}_2)$ is the zero map. This follows as $\mathbb{F}_2 \simeq H^1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n,\mathbb{F}_2) \rightarrow H^1(S^n,\mathbb{F}_2) = 0$ must be the zero map and the nontrivial element $x$ in $H^1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n,\mathbb{F}_2)$ is a generator of the graded ring, i.e. $f(x^n) = f(x)^n = 0^n$ shows that the top map is the zero map.

Alternatively: degree of maps and index of pi_1.
