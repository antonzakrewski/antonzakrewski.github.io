distance in a hilbert space takes minimum

Proposition

let $X$ be a Hilbert space, $A \subseteq X$ a closed subspace and $x \in X$. Then there exists a unique $a \in A$ such that $\mathrm{dist}(x,A) = \| x - a \|$

Proof

existence

Let $d \coloneqq \mathrm{dist}(x,A) = \mathrm{inf}_{y \in A} \| x - y \|$. Then there exists a sequence $(y_n) \in A$ such that

\begin{align*} d = \mathrm{lim}_{n \rightarrow \infty} \| x - y_n \| \end{align*}

with $\| x - y_n \| \geq d$. The parallelogram identity -- $\| u + v \|^2 = 2 \cdot (\| u\|^2 + \|v\|^2 ) - \| u - v \|^2$ shows

\begin{align*} \| y_n - y_m \|^2 =& \|(y_n - x) + (x - y_m) \|^2 \\ =& 2 \cdot \left(\| y_n - x \|^2 + \| y_m - x \|^2 \right) - \| 2 x - y_m - y_n \|^2 \end{align*}

We may further calculate

\begin{align*} \| 2 x - y_m - y_n \|^2 = 4 \cdot \| x - \frac{y_m - y_n}{2} \| \geq 4 d^2 \end{align*}

since $y_m - y_n \in A$ and equivalently $\frac{y_m - y_n}{2} \in A$.

this shows

\begin{align*} \| y_n - y_m \|^2 \leq& 2 \cdot \left(\| y_n - x \|^2 + \| y_m - x \|^2 \right) - 4d^2 \xrightarrow{m,n \rightarrow \infty} 0 \end{align*}

Hence $y_n$ is a Cauchy sequence and $X$ is complete, that is $y_n \rightarrow a$ for some $a \in X$. Moreover, $A$ is closed, which shows that $a \in A$ and by continuity of the norm we get $d = \| x - a \|$.

uniqueness

Assume that there exists another $a'$, then the estimate

\begin{align*} \| a - a' \|^2 \leq& 2 \cdot \left(\| a - x \|^2 + \| a' - x \|^2 \right) - 4d^2 \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

shows that $\| a - a' \| = 0$ or equivalently $a = a'$.
