lifting of idempotent elements of I-adically complete

Proposition

Let $R$ be an I-adically complete ring for $I \subseteq R$. Let $e \in R/I$ be an idempotent element.

Then there exists an element $\overline{e} \in R$ which maps to $e$ under the projection

\begin{align*} R \rightarrow R/I \end{align*}

Proof

$I^n = 0$

Let $\overline{x} \in R/I$ be an element. Then choose a (naive) lift $x \coloneqq \overline{x} \in R$.

Let $y \coloneqq 1 - x$. Then it follows that

\begin{align*} 1 = x + y \end{align*}

and furthermore

\begin{align*} xy =& x \cdot (1 - x) \\ =& x - x^2 \\ =& (1 - x) \cdot x \\ =& yx \end{align*}

Consider

\begin{align*} (1)^n =& (x + y)^{2n} \\ =& \sum_{i=0}^{2n} \binom{2n}{i} x^i y^{2n-i} \\ =& \sum_{i=0}^{n} \binom{2n}{i} x^i y^{2n-i} + \sum_{i=n+1}^{2n} \binom{2n}{i} x^i y^{2n-i}\\ \end{align*}

Let

\begin{align*} y' \coloneqq& \sum_{i=n+1}^{2n} \binom{2n}{i} x^i y^{2n-i}\\ x' \coloneqq& \sum_{i=0}^{n} \binom{2n}{i} x^i y^{2n-i} \end{align*}

Then it follows by explicit calculation that

  1. $x' \cdot y' \in I^n = 0$
  2. $x' + y' = 1$

Hence $x'$ is an idempotent element.

reduction to $I^n = 0$

Note that the map

\begin{align*} R \rightarrow \mathrm{lim} R/I^n \end{align*}

is injective (even iso), as is

\begin{align*} R \rightarrow \mathrm{lim} R/I^n \hookrightarrow \prod R/I^n \end{align*}

In particular for any element $x \in R$ there exists an $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $x \not\in I^n$
