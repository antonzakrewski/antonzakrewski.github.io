Image of a strongly continuous functor out of a dualizable stable category is dualizable

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}$ be stable presentable categories, where $\mathcal{C}$ is a dualizable category. Suppose $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ is a strongly continuous functor. Then $\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{F})$ is a dualizable category.

Proof

Observe that $\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{F}) \subseteq \mathcal{D}$ is closed under colimits and also presentable (cf. image of a left adjoint between stable presentable categories is the fiber), hence using adjunctions restrict to full subcategories we have that $\mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}$ has a right adjoint computed by $\mathrm{im}(\mathcal{F}) \rightarrow \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$, where both functors preserve colimits, thus $\mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{im}(\mathcal{F})$ is strongly continuous. This together with presentable stable category generated by the image of strongly continuous functors is dualizable implies that $\mathrm{im}(\mathcal{F})$ is dualizable.
