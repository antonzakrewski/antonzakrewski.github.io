compact set in general not closed

Proposition

Let $X$ be a topological space and $Y \subseteq X$ be a compact set. Then $Y$ is in general not closed

Proof

Consider $\mathbb{R}$ with the cofinite topology. Then $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ is not closed, as it is not finite. But given an open cover $U_i$ of $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ we may choose any nonempty open set $U$. This $U$ already contains almost all $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$. Hence there remain at most finitely many elements in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\} \setminus U$. For those we may choose finitely many $U_i$, each containing one missing element.

Then $U, U_1,...,U_n$ is a finite covering of $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$
