classification of irreducible representations of k[x] over an algebraically closed field

Proposition

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field. Then any irreducible representation $V$ of $k[x]$ is one dimensional and hence up to isomorphism classified by $\mathbb{C}$

Proof

$V$ infinite dimensional

Suppose $V$ is infinite dimensional, then choose a nontrivial element $v \in V$. Now consider the submodule generated by $v$, which is either finite dimensional or isomorphic to $k[t]$. Then it suffices to show that $k[t]$ is not irreducible, since any proper ideal - e.g. $(t)$ is an $k[t]$ submodule.

$V$ finite dimensional

We can triagonalize any matrix over an algebraically closed field. So in particular, it contains an eigenvector which forms a 1 dimensional subspace.

isomrophism claim

Observe that a map of representations from $f: (k,\lambda_1) \rightarrow (k,\lambda_2)$ must satisfy $\lambda_2 f(v) = f(t^n \cdot 1) = \lambda_1 f(v)$ by $k[t]$ linearity, so if there exists an nontrivial image, then $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2$.
