monic polynomial of an integral element in general not irreducible

Proposition

Let \(B\) be an Integral domain, $A \hookrightarrow B$ a subring. Suppose $\alpha \in B$ is integral over $A$.

Then in general there does not exist a monic irreducible polynomial with $\alpha$ as root

Proof

consider

\begin{align*} A \coloneqq \mathbb{Z}[ \sqrt{5}] B \coloneqq \mathbb{R} \end{align*}

Then

\begin{align*} b = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \end{align*}

is integral over $\mathbb{Z}$, as it is a zero of

\begin{align*} X^2 - 2x - 2 \in \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{5}][X] \end{align*}

But $b$ is also the zero of the linear polynomial

\begin{align*} 2X - (1 + \sqrt{5}) \end{align*}

which is not monic.
