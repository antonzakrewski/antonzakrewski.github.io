derived category of the integers is smallest stable subcategory closed under limits and containing integers

Proposition

Let $\mathbb{Z}$ be the integers and $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})$ the derived category. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the smallest subcategory which

  1. contains $\mathbb{Z}$
  2. is stable
  3. is closed under limits

Then $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{D}(R)$.

Proof

Let $M$ be an object in $\mathcal{D}(R)$. Then $M \simeq \prod_{n} H_n(M)$ (cf. object in the derived category of a hereditary ring) so it suffices to show that $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})^{\heartsuit}$ lies in $\mathcal{C}$.

It suffices to show that $\mathbb{Q}$ lies in $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})$: If $\mathbb{Q}$ lies in $\mathcal{C}$, then so does the cofiber $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ and the rational circle group is an injective cogenerator (cf. rational circle group is a cogenerator + rational circle group is an injective group). Hence any abelian group $A$ embedds into $\prod \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ and the cokernel is again an injective group (cf. quotient of a divisible group is a divisible group, Baer's criterion). Moreover, any injective group is a retract of $\prod \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ as we can embedd $A$ into $\prod \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ (cf. injective map and split mono). Therefore, our abelian group $A$ is the fiber of a $\prod \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \rightarrow (\prod \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})/A$ (cf. short exact sequence in the heart is a bifiber sequence in a stable category).

Now it suffices to show that there exists a $\mathbb{Q}$ vector space $M$ in $\mathcal{C}$, since $\mathbb{Q}$ is a retract of $M$.

Recall that rational vector spaces are precisely uniquely divisible abelian group and hence it suffices to show that there exists a nontrivial uniquely divisible object in $\mathcal{C}$ (we even show that it is a group). Consider $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{Z}) \simeq \mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{Z})[1]$ (cf. Ext(Q,Z) is the additive group of real numbers) which is nontrivial (cf. proof of derived hom(-,Z) is conservative). moreover, it is the limit $\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\cdot n} \mathbb{Z} ...$ and hence by an sequential limit of a morphism inverts the morphism argument (or $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathbb{Q},-)$ is infinite co-extension of scalars) shows that it uniquely divisible.
