minimal separator and neighbourhood of a knot intersecting all components

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph and $X \subseteq V$ a minimal separator. Let $C_i$ be the connected components of $G \setminus X$ and $x \in X$. Then

\begin{align*} N_G(x) \cap C_i \neq \emptyset \end{align*}

for all $i$

Proof

assume otherwise, i.e.

\begin{align*} N_G(x) \cap C_i = \emptyset \end{align*}

let $C_j$ be a different connected component.

Then by minimality $G \setminus X \cup \{x\}$ is connected. Hence there exists a path $\gamma: c_i \rightarrow c_j$ for some $c_i \in C_i, c_j \in C_i$ which necessarily goes through $x$, w.l.o.g. exactly once. Thus the path splits into two paths $\gamma_1 \in C_i,\gamma_2 \in C_j$ where the endpoint of $\gamma_1$ lies in $C_i$ (since it connects $c_i \in C_i$) and is a neighbour to $x$ in $G$. Thus the intersection is nonempty.
