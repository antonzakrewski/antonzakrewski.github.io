Maschkes theorem

Proposition

Let $G \rightarrow \mathrm{GL}_n(V)$ be a finite dimensional (???) unitary representation of a finite group and $U \subseteq V$ an invariant subspace. Then htere exists an invariant subspace $W$ such that the representation splits

\begin{align*} V \cong U \oplus W \end{align*}

Proof

finite dimensional

Let $U \oplus U^{\bot} \cong V$, then we need to show that $U^{\bot}$ is invariant. I.e. for each $w \in U^{\bot}$ is $\rho_g(w) \in U^{\bot}$.

Now by \(G\)-equivariance of the

\begin{align*} \langle \rho_g(w), u\rangle =& \langle w, \rho_{g^{-1}}(u)\rangle \\ =& 0 \end{align*}

which shows that $\rho_g(w) \in U^{\bot}$ since by assumption $\rho_{g^{-1}}(u) \in U$
