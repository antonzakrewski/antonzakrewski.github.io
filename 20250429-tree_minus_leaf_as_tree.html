tree minus leaf as tree

Proposition

Let $G = (V,E)$ be a tree and $v \in V$ a leaf. Then $G \setminus \{v\}$ is also a tree

Proof

connected

Let $v_1,v_2 \in G \setminus \{v\}$ be edges. We have to show that there exists a path in $G \setminus \{v\}$. For this consider a path $\gamma$ form $v_1$ to $v_2$ in $G$ (note that this exists, since $G$ is connected). Then we claim that $v \not\in \gamma$, hence $\gamma$ restricts to $G \setminus \{v\}$.

Assume $v \in \gamma$, then we would get $v_i \rightarrow v \rightarrow v_{i+1}$ with distinct edges. Contradiction to $d_{G}(v) = 1$ where $v$ is a leaf.

acyclic

note that a subgraph of an acyclic graph has to be also acyclic, as any cycle in the subgraph would provide a cycle in the bigger graph
