Proposition

Let $n \geq 2$, then the homology of $\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ is additively by

\begin{align*} H_*(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n) =& H_*(\Omega S^n) \oplus H_*(\Omega S^n)\\ =& \mathbb{Z}[z_{n-1}] \oplus \mathbb{Z}[z_{n-1}] \end{align*}

together with the following ring structure

  1. $\mathbb{Z} \langle x,g\rangle/(g^2-1)$ with $\vert x \vert = n-1, \vert g \vert = 0$ if $n$ is odd (that is essentially the group ring $R[C_2]$ for $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]$),
  2. $\mathbb{Z} \langle x,g \rangle /(g^2-1, gx + xg)$ if $n$ is even, that is the graded ring generated by $x,g$ with $\vert x \vert = n-1, \vert g \vert = 0$.

Ring structure

Observe that the inclusion $\Omega S^n \rightarrow \Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ is a map of \(\mathbb{E}_1\)-groups (cf. recognition principle for anima valued E_n groups) with image given by the connected component of $\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ corresponding to the multiplicative unit (i.e. the constant path at the basepoint). Hence the map $H_*(\Omega S^n) \hookrightarrow H_*(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$ is an injective map of graded rings and therefore we can identify the subring of the unit component as $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ (cf. integral homology ring of the loop space of the sphere).

Now let $g$ be image of the generator $\gamma \in \pi_0(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)\cong \pi_1(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n) \cong C_2$ under the map $\pi_0(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n) \rightarrow H_0(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}$. Then $g^2 = 1$ in both $\pi_0(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$ and $H_0(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$ (cf. zeroth homology ring structure of an anima valued monoid).

Now multiplication with $\gamma$ is an equivalence on the underlying anima $\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ and hence so is multiplication with $g$ on the additive group of $H_*(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$. Hence we can identify each element in the second unit component with $g \cdot l \cdot x^m$ for a unique $l \cdot x^m \in H_*(\Omega S^n) \hookrightarrow H_*(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$ (here we use the inclusion of the non-unit connected component):

Now we want to show that $g x = (-1)^{n-1} x g$, i.e. that they commute in the case $n$ odd or anticommute in the case $n$ even. Here it suffices to compute $g x g^{-1}$, which has to live in the unit component of $H_*(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$ in degree $n-1$, i.e. is of the form $l \cdot x$ for some $l$, since we get a lift

Here the multiplication $g - g^{-1}$ comes from the the multiplication $G \times G \times G \rightarrow G$ and the fact that $\mathbb{Z}[-]$ is a symmetric monoidal functor (cf. lax monoidal functor induces functor on Algebras, free R-module spectrum functor is symmetric monoidal to module spectra).

Moreover, naturality of the absolute hurewicz homomorphism induces a split mono $\pi_{n-1}(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n) \rightarrow H_{n-1}(\Omega S^n)$ (cf. absolute Hurewicz theorem) since the inclusion of the connected component is connected enough. Therefore, it suffices to compute the action on $\pi_{n-1}(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$.

But the proof of RP^n is simple iff n is odd shows that the action of $g$ on $\pi_n(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is the identity if $n$ is odd and $- \mathrm{id}$ else (alternatively, one can use that $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{F}_2$, so if the action is nontrivial, then it has to be $- \mathrm{id}$). Hence $g x = (-1)^{n-1} x g$.

This shows that there exists a ring-homomorphism $\mathbb{Z} \langle x,g \rangle/(g^2-1) \rightarrow H_*(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$ if $n$ is odd and a ring homomorphism $\mathbb{Z} \langle x,g \rangle /(g^2-1, gx-xg) \rightarrow H_*(\Omega \mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n)$. In both cases, the map is surjective, since it hits a generator of the unit component ($x$) and multiplication with $g$ induces an equivalence of anima between the unit component and the non-unit component. Furthermore, the map is also injective in both cases: There exists a canonical grading on $\mathbb{Z}\langle x,g \rangle/(g^2-1)$ (or $\mathbb{Z}\langle x,g \rangle/(g^2-1, gx-xg)$) with $\vert x \vert = n-1, \vert g \vert = 0$ such that the map is one of graded rings. Hence it suffices to observe that we have - in each nontrivial compononent - a surjective map $\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}$, which therefore has to be an isomorphism (cf. surjective endomorphism for a noetherian module as isomorphism).
