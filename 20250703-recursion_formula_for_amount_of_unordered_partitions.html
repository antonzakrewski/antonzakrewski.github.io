recursion formula for amount of unordered partitions

Proposition

Let $p_{n,k} = \mathcal{P}_{n,k} = \{\mathrm{ unordered partition of } n, k\}$ (cf. unordered partition of a number) Then

\begin{align*} p_{n,k} = p_{n-1,k-1} + p_{n-k,k} \end{align*}

Proof

Consider the partition (i.e. disjoint union) of unordered partitions consisting of

  1. $A$ as all parts at least $2$
  2. $B$ at least one part is given by $1$ (note that by assumption each number is positive)

Then subtraction of $1$ induces a bijection

\begin{align*} A \rightarrow& \mathcal{P}_{n-k,k} \\ [(x_1,...,x_a)] \mapsto& [(x_i - 1)] \end{align*}

hence $\vert A \vert = p_{n-k,k}$.

But furthermore forgetting the $1$ induces a bijection

\begin{align*} B \rightarrow& \mathcal{P}_{n-1,k-1} \\ [(x_i),1] \mapsto& [(x_i)] \end{align*}

and thus

\begin{align*} \vert B \vert = p_{n-1,k-1} \end{align*}

Hence

\begin{align*} p_{n,k} =& A \coprod B \\ =& p_{n-1,k-1} + p_{n-k, k} \end{align*}
