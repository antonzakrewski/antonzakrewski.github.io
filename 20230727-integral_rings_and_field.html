integral rings and field

Proposition

Let $A \subseteq B$ rings such that $B$ is integral ring over $A$ and $B$ is an Integral domain

TFAE:

  1. $A$ is a field
  2. $B$ is a field

Proof

1) $\implies$ 2)

Let $b \in B \setminus \{0\}$, then there exists an equation

\begin{align*} \sum \alpha_{i} b^i = 0 \end{align*}

w.l.o.g. we may assume that $\alpha_0 \neq 0$, as otherwise we may cancel $b$ sufficiently often.

this shows that

\begin{align*} b \cdot (\sum \alpha_{i} b^{i-1}) = - \alpha_0 \end{align*}

where the RHS is a unit, hence also $b$ with inverse

\begin{align*} (- \alpha_0)^{-1} \cdot (\sum \alpha_{i} b^{i-1}) \end{align*}

2) $\implies$ 1)

Let $\alpha \in A \setminus \{0\}$ then there exists an inverse $\alpha^{-1} \in B$. By assumption $\alpha^{-1}$ is integral over $A$ showing

\begin{align*} \alpha^{-n} + ... + \alpha_0 = 0 \end{align*}

or w.l.o.g. after cancelling $\alpha^{-n}$ sufficiently often

\begin{align*} \alpha^{-1} ( \alpha^{n-1} + ... + \alpha_1) = - \alpha_0 \neq 0 \end{align*}
