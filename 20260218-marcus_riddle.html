marcus chessboard riddle

Proposition

Proof

Choose a bijection between $\prod_{i=1}^6 \mathbb{F}_2$ and the chessboard. In particular we want to encode an element $x_0 \in \prod_{i=1}^6 \mathbb{F}_2$ Then a chessboard constellation is given precisely by a a map $f: \prod_{i=1}^n \mathbb{F}_2 \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_2$ where $f(x) = 1$ if and only if the coin on position $x \in \prod_{i=1}^6 \mathbb{F}_2$ is on heads. In particular we may consider the sum

\begin{align*} \sum_{x \in \prod_{i=1}^6 \mathbb{F}_2} f(x) x \in (\prod_{i=1}^6 \mathbb{F}_2) \end{align*}

Then it suffices to flip the coin $x - x_0$ and then the sum described above decodes the position $x_0$.
