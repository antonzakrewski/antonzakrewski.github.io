dual of l0 embeds into dual of l infinity

Proposition

There exists a isometric embedding $l_0^* \rightarrow (l^{\infty})^{*}$.

Proof

Let

\begin{align*} J \colon l_0^{*} \rightarrow& (l^\infty)^{*} \\ g \mapsto& \left( x \mapsto \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} g(e_n) x_n \right) \end{align*}

We claim that $\left( x \mapsto \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} g(e_n) x_n \right)$ is a well-defined bounded operator.

bounded

Let $x \in l^{\infty}$, then we need to show that $\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} g(e_n) x_n$ converges. It suffices to show that $\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \vert g(e_n) x_n \vert = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \vert g(e_n) \vert \cdot \vert x_n \vert$ converges. Recall that $\mathrm{sup}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} x_n \eqqcolon b$ exists so it suffices to show that $\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \vert g(e_n) \vert$ converges since $\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \vert g(e_n) \vert \cdot \vert x_n \vert \leq \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \vert g(e_n) \vert \cdot b$.

Let

\begin{align*} h_n \coloneqq \begin{cases} \frac{\| g(e_n) \|}{g(e_n)} & \mbox{if } g(e_n) \neq 0 \\ 1 & \mbox{else } \\ \end{cases} \end{align*}

and $y = \left(h_n e_n \right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, $y_n = (y_1,...,y_n,0,0,...)$. Observe that $\| y \|_{\infty} = \| y_n \|_{\infty} = 1$ and hence $y \in l^{\infty}, y_n \in l_0$.

Then

\begin{align*} g(y_n) =& \sum_{i=1}^n h_i g(e_i) \\ =& \sum_{i=1}^n \vert g(e_i) \vert \leq& \| g \|_{\infty} \end{align*}

and passing to limits shows that $\sum_{i=1}^\infty g(e_i) \leq& \| g \|_{\infty}$.

This shows that $x \mapsto \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} g(e_n) x_n$ is a well-defined map and - if $J$ is linear, then $Jg$ is a bounded operator: For $x \in l^\infty$ we may estimate

\begin{align*} \| Jg \| =& \frac{\| Jg(x) \|}{\| x \|} \\ =& \frac{\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \vert g(e_n) \vert \cdot \vert x_n \vert }{\| x \|} \\ \leq& \frac{\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \vert g(e_n) \vert \cdot \| x \|_{\infty}}{\| x \|_{\infty}} \\ \leq& \| g \| \end{align*}

linear operator

Let $g_1,g_2 \in (l^0)^{\infty}$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb{K}$. Then it follows immediately that

\begin{align*} J(g_1 + \lambda g_2)(x) =& \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (g_1 + \lambda g_2)(e_n) \cdot x_n \\ =& \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( g_1(e_n) + \lambda g_2(e_n) \right) \cdot x_n \\ =& \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} g_1(e_n) \cdot x_n + \lambda \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} g_2(e_n) \cdot x_n \\ =& J(g_1)(x) + \lambda J(g_2)(x) \end{align*}

isometry

Recall that we have showed that $\| Jg \| \leq \| g \|$, so it suffices to show $\| Jg \| \geq \| g \|$. but this follows immediately, as $l^0 \subseteq l^{\infty}$ is a subspace and by construction $Jg_{\vert l^0} = g$. Hence

\begin{align*} \| J g \| =& \mathrm{sup}_{x \in l^{\infty}} \frac{\| Jg(x) \|}{\| x \|_{\infty}} \\ \geq& \mathrm{sup}_{x \in l_0} \frac{\| Jg(x) \|}{\| x \|_{\infty}} \\ \geq& \mathrm{sup}_{x \in l_0} \frac{\| g(x) \|}{\| x \|_{\infty}} \\ =& \| g \| \end{align*}
