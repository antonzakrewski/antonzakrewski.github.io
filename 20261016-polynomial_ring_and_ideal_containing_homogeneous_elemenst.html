polynomial ring and ideal containing homogeneous elements and irreducible representation

Proposition

Let $A = k[x_1,...,x_n]$ and $I \neq A$ any ideal which contains all homogeneous elements of degree $\geq N$. Then $A/I$ is an indecomposable $A$ module.

Proof

Assume there exists $M_1/I \oplus M_2/I \cong A/I$. Then we may assume that $I \not\supseteq \langle x_1,...,x_n\rangle$ as otherwise $A/I \cong k$ or $A/I \cong 0$ and both are generated by a single nontrivial or any element. So choose a monomial $g$ with maximal total degree $\geq 1$ such that $g \not\in I$.

We first claim that w.l.o.g. after renumbering $M_1$ or $M_2$, each polynomial in $M_2$ does not contain a constant part. Suppose there exist polynomials $f_i \in M_i$ with a nonzero monomial with degree $0$, then $g \cdot f_i = \lambda g \cdot f_i \in A/I \setminus \{0\}$ since $g$ kills any other monomial term (by maximality of degrees). This is a contradiction to $M_1/I \cap M_2/I = 0$

Now choose an $f$ with $f + 1 \in M_1/I, f \in M_2$ and observe that $f$ does not contain any constant term. We may w.l.o.g. assume that $f \not\in I$ as otherwise $M_1/I$ would contain $1$ (and then the sum would be trivial).

Then there exsits an $n$ such that $f^{n+1} \in I$ and $f^n \not\in I$: In particular, $f^n \in M_1 \cap M_2$ since $f^n \cdot (f + 1) = f^{n-1} \cdot f = f^n$.
