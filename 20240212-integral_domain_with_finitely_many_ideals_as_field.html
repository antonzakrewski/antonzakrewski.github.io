integral domain with finitely many ideals as field

Proposition

Let $R$ be an Integral domain with finitely many ideals. Then $R$ is a field

Proof

Let $a \in R \setminus \{0\}$, then consider

\begin{align*} (a^1), (a^2), (a^3), ... \end{align*}

by Pigeonhole principle, there exist $n,k \in \mathbb{N}^+$ such that

\begin{align*} (a^n) =& (a^{k \cdot n}) \\ =& (a^k) \cdot (a^n) \end{align*}

hence by $a^n \in (a^{n}) \cdot (a^{k})$ there exist $\alpha = r \cdot a^k, \beta = r' \cdot a^n$ such that

\begin{align*} a^n =& \alpha \cdot \beta \\ =& r \cdot a^{k} \cdot r' \cdot a^n \\ \end{align*}

hence since $R$ is integral, we can cancel and get

\begin{align*} 1 = (r \cdot r') \cdot a^k \end{align*}

and therefore $a^k$ is a unit resp. also $a$ for

\begin{align*} a \cdot (a^{k-1} \cdot r \cdot r') \end{align*}
