general linear group scheme

Definition / Proposition

Let $k$ be a commutative ring, then the general linear group functor is also a group scheme

Proof

a) affine scheme

follows as the determinant function $\mathrm{det}: \mathrm{Mat}_n(k) \simex k[x_{i,j}] \rightarrow k$ is some polynomial, i.e. we may regard $\mathrm{det}$ as polyonmial. Then the functor $\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$ is represented by $A \coloneqq k[x_{i,j}]_{\mathrm{det}}$, i.e. maps $A \rightarrow R$ are precisely $n^2$ entries, such that the determinant is a unit

b) group scheme

observe that matrix multiplication induces a coalgebra structure
