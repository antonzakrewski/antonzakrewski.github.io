cocountable topology on an uncountable set not first countable

Proposition

Let $X$ be an uncountable set and $\mathcal{T}$ the cocountable topology on $X$. Then $X$ is not first-countable

Proof

By

  1. feinere Topologie und Frechet-Topologie
  2. cofinite topology coarser than cocountable topology
  3. Äquivalente Charakterisierung eines T1-Raums (3)

we conclude, that

\begin{align*} \bigcap_{O \ni x, O \text{ open}} O = \{x\} \end{align*}

by <allgemeine de Morgansche Gesetz>, we conclude that

\begin{align*} X \setminus \bigcap O = \bigcup (X \setminus O) \end{align*}

By assumption, each $X \setminus O$ is countable, thus it's a contradiction to the countability of a countable union of countable sets
