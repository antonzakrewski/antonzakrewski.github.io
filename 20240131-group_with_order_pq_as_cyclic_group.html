classification of groups of order pq

Proposition

Let $p,q$ be prime numbers with $p < q$ and $p \nmid q - 1$. Suppose $G$ is a group with group order $p \cdot q$. Then

\begin{align*} G \cong \mathbb{Z}/pq \mathbb{Z} \end{align*}

Proof

Because

\begin{align*} s_q \equiv 1 \mod{q} \end{align*}

and $s_q \mid p < q$ it follows, that $s_q \leq p$ and hence

\begin{align*} s_q = 1 \end{align*}

Since

\begin{align*} s_p \mid q \end{align*}

it follows, that $s_p = 1$ or $s_p = q$, as $q$ is prime. Since by assumption $p \not\mid q - 1$ or equivalently $q \not\equiv 1 \mod p$ it follows $s_p = 1$ Thus by sole group of an order as normal subgroup, $U_q,U_p \trianglelefteq G$ is a normal subgroup Therefore, by product of a normal subgroup and subgroup as subgroup $U_p \cdot U_q$ is a group Note that by order of the product of coprime subgroups it follows, that

\begin{align*} \vert U_p \cdot U_q \vert = p \cdot q \end{align*}

Then by group of order p as cyclic group it follows, that $U_p \cong \mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z}$ and $U_q \cong \mathbb{Z}/q \mathbb{Z}$. Choose generators $a \in U_p, b \in U_q$. Then it follows from $U_p,U_q \trianglelefteq G$, that

\begin{align*} z \coloneqq a b a^{-1} b^{-1} \in U_p \cap U_q \end{align*}

Since order of an element divides group order it follows, from $\mathrm{ord}(z) \mid p, \mathrm{ord}(z) \mid q$ that $\mathrm{ord}(z) = 1$ and hence $z = 1$.

Therefore

\begin{align*} a \cdot b = b \cdot a \end{align*}

and hence $G = \langle a, b\rangle$ is abelian, as the generators commute

Applying classification of finitely generated abelian groups and resp. general chinese remainder theorem results in

\begin{align*} G \cong \left(\mathbb{Z}/p \mathbb{Z} \right) \otimes \left(\mathbb{Z}/q \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/pq \mathbb{Z} \end{align*}
