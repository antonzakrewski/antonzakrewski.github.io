union of algebraic sets

Proposition

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field and $\mathbb{A}_{K}^n$ the affine space. then for algebraic sets $Z(S_1),...,Z(S_k)$ it follows, that

\begin{align*} \bigcup_{i = 1}^{k} Z(S_i) =& Z(\prod_{i =1}^k S_i) =& Z(\bigcap_{i = 1}^{k} S_i) \end{align*}

Proof

$\bigcup_{i = 1}^{k} Z(S_i) \subseteq Z(\bigcap_{i =1}^k S_i)$

Suppose $(p_1,...,p_n) \in \bigcup_{i = 1}^k Z(S_i)$, then there exists an $j$ such that $(p_1,...,p_n) \in Z(S_j)$. Hence for all $f \in S_j$, it holds that $f(p_1,...,p_n) = 0$, thus especially also for all $f \in \bigcap_{i=1}^n S_i \subseteq S_j$

$Z(\bigcap_{i = 1}^{k} S_i) \subseteq Z(\prod_{i =1}^k S_i)$

corollary of

  • algebraic set and order

$Z(\prod_{i = 1}^{k} S_i) \subseteq \bigcup_{i = 1}^{k} Z(S_i)$

Let $(p_1,...,p_n) \in Z(\prod_{i = 1}^{k} S_i)$, then for all $f = \sum \alpha_j \prod_{i=1}^n f_i$ with $f_i \in it holds, that $f(p_1,...,p_n) = 0$. Suppose for all $S_i$ there exists an $g_i \in S_i$ such that $g_i(p_1,...,p_n) \neq 0$, then $(\prod_{i=1}^n g_i)(p_1,...,p_n) \neq 0$. Hence by contraposition, there exists an $S_j$ such that for all $f \in S_j$ it holds, that $f(p_1,...,p_n) = 0$. Therefore $(p_1,...,p_n) \in Z(S_j) \subseteq \bigcup_{i = 1}^{k} Z(S_i)$
