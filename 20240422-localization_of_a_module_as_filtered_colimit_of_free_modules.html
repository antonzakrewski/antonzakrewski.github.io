localization of a module as filtered colimit of modules

Proposition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $S \subseteq R$ a multiplicatively closed subset and $M$ an \(R\)-module Then the localization $S^{-1}M$ is a filtered colimit for

with $M_{i,n} \coloneqq M$ and $i,n$ are only used for indexing

Proof

using

  • composition of filtered colimits as filtered colimit

sloppy

for a localization along an element $f$ we claim that $f^{-1}M$ is the sequential colimit

with $M_i \coloneqq M$ and

\begin{align*} \iota_i: M_i = M \rightarrow& f^{-1}M \\ m \mapsto& \frac{m}{f^i} \end{align*}

This diagram evidently commutes.

Suppose there exists an natural transformation

\begin{align*} \tau: M_i \Rightarrow \underline{N} \end{align*}

to the constant functor for some module $N$.

Then define

\begin{align*} t: f^{-1}M \rightarrow& N \\ \frac{m}{f^n} \mapsto& \iota_n(m) \end{align*}

It is welldefined as

\begin{align*} t(\frac{m f^k}{f^{n+k}}) =& \iota_{n+k}(f^k m} \\ =& \iota_{n+k}(\cdot f^k m} \\ =& \iota_n(m) \end{align*}

It is furthermore the unique map making the whole diagram commute, as each element $\frac{m}{f^n}$ has a preimage $m$ under $\iota_n$, hence $t( \frac{m}{f^n}) = \iota_n(m)$
