real projective space is a smooth manifold

Proposition

The projective space $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ is an \(n\)-dimensional Manifold.

Proof

2-countable etc.

  • follows from:
    • manifold with compact exhaustion implies second countability

where $\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^n$ is the image of $S^{n}$, so in particular compact.

hausdorff

overkill

  • real projective space as CW-Complex
  • CW complex as hausdorff space

charts

Let

\begin{align*} U_i \coloneqq \{[x_0 : ... : x_n] \vert x_0 \neq 0\} \end{align*}

for $i \in \{0,...,n\}$

Then $U_1,...,U_n$ is an open cover as

\begin{align*} U_i = \pi[\prod_{j \neq i} \mathbb{R} \times (\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\})] \end{align*}

where $\pi[\pi^{-1}[U_i]] = U_i$ and $\pi^{-1}[U_i] = \prod_{j \neq i} \mathbb{R} \times (\mathbb{R}\setminus \{0\})$ is open, hence $U_i$ is open in the quotient topology

Let

\begin{align*} \varphi_i: U_i \rightarrow& \mathbb{R}^n \\ [x_0 : ... : x_n] \mapsto& \frac{1}{x_i} (x_0,...,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},...,x_n) \end{align*}

resp.

\begin{align*} \psi_i: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow U_i (x_0,...,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},...,x_n) \mapsto& [x_0: ... : x_{i-1} : 1 : x_{i+1} : ... : x_n] \end{align*}

Note that both are inverse to each other (once we show that $\varphi_i$ is welldefined)

welldefined

Let $[x_0 : ... : x_n] = [y_0 : ... : y_n] \in U_i$. Then by assumption, $y_i, x_i \neq 0$ and furthermore, there exists an $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^\times$ such that

\begin{align*} x_j = \lambda y_j \end{align*}

Hence

\begin{align*} \varphi_i([x_0 : ... : x_n]) =& \frac{1}{x_i} (x_j)_{j \neq i} \\ =& \frac{1}{\lambda y_j} \cdot (\lambda y_j)_{j \neq i} \\ =& \frac{1}{y_j} \cdot (y_j)_{j \neq i} \end{align*}

surjective - not necessary :/

Let $(\alpha_1,...,\alpha_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Then $\varphi_i(\alpha_1,...,\alpha_{i-1},1,\alpha_{i+1},...,\alpha_n) = \frac{1}{1} (\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_n) = (\alpha_1,...,\alpha_n)$

injective - not necessary :/

Let

\begin{align*} \varphi_i([x_0,...,x_n]) =& \frac{1}{x_i} (x_j)_{j \neq i} \\ =& \frac{1}{y_i} (y_j)_{j \neq i} \\ =& \varphi_i([y_0,...,y_n]) \end{align*}

Then

\begin{align*} [x_0,...,x_n] \cdot \frac{y_i}{x_i} = [y_0,...,y_n] \end{align*}

hence $[x_0,...,x_n] = [y_0,...,y_n]$ which shows that the map is injective

smooth

Let $U_{i,j} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : x_i,x_j \neq 0\}$ Then the transition maps are given by

\begin{align*} \varphi_i \circ \varphi_j^{-1}: (x_0,...,x_i,...,x_j,...,x_n \mapsto ( \frac{x_0}{x_i},...,\frac{1}{x_{i}},...,1,...,\frac{x_n}{x_i}) \end{align*}

which is smooth on the domain since it $x_i\neq 0$.
