complex numbers are a semiprojective c star algebra

Proposition

The C star algebra $\mathbb{C}$ is a semiprojective c star algebra.

Proof

We need to find a lift 

\begin{displaymath}
\begin{tikzcd}
& B/J_i \dar
\mathbb{C} \arrow[dashed]{ru}{}\rar & B/J
\end{tikzcd}
\end{displaymath}

Choose a self-adjoint lift $b_i$ to some $J_i$ (cf. lifting of self-adjoint elements) . Then we claim that $\| b_i^2 - b_i\| \rightarrow 0$:

For every $\frac{1}{n}$ we can find an $j_n \in J$ such that $b_i^2 - b_i = j_n$ with $\| j_n \| < \frac{1}{n}$. so w.l.o.g. after passing to a sufficiently large $i$, we can assume that $\| b_i^2 - b_i \| < \frac{1}{4}$. Then pertubation of the spectrum implies that $\sigma(b_i) \subsetq [0,\frac{1}{2} - \delta) \coprod ( \frac{1}{2} - \delta, 1]$ since

\begin{align*} (x^2 - x) \sigma(b_i) \subseteq (- \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{4}) \end{align*}

So let $f = \chi_{[\frac{1}{2}, \infty)}: \sigma(b_i) \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ be continuous, self-adjoint and apply function calculus of $b_i$ to it: Then we get an element $f(b_i)$ whose spectrum is given by $f(\sigma(b_i)) \subseteq \{0,1\}$, so $f(b_i)$ is a projection (cf. projection and spectrum)
