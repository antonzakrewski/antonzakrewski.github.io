Arzela-Ascoli

Proposition

Let $K$ be a compact metric space and $f_n$ a sequence of continuous, complex-valued functions $f_n: K \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ that is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. Then $f_n$ has a uniformly convergent subsequence.

Proof

using compact metric space as separable space: Let $E$ be a countable, dense subset. Then there exists a subsequence $f_{n_k}$ such that $f_{n_k}(x)$ converges for $x \in E$.

it suffices to show that $f_{n_k}$ is uniformly cauchy and then apply uniformly cauchy sequence as uniformly convergent sequence

Let $\varepsilon > 0$. By equicontinuity there exists a $\delta > 0$ such that for all $x,y\in K$ and all $n$ if $d(x,yr \leq \delta$ then $\vert f_n(x) - f_n(y) \vert \leq \frac{\varepsilon}{3}$. Since $E$ is dense, the sets $B_{\delta}(x)$ for $x \in E$ form an open cover of $K$. By compactness there exist $x_1,...,x_J\in E$ with $K = \bigcup_{j=1}^J B_{\delta}(x_j)$.

Since for any $j = 1,...,J$ the sequence $f_{n_k}(x_j)$ converges, there exists an $K_j$ such that if $k,k' \geq K_j$ then

\begin{align*} \vert f_{n_k}(x_j) - f_{n_k'}(x_j) \vert \leq \frac{\varepsilon}{3} \end{align*}

Now let $x \in K$ and let $k,k' \geq \mathrm{max}_{K_1,...,K_j) \eqqcolon K$.
