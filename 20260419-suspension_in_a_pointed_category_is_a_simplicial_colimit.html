suspension in a pointed category is a simplicial colimit

Proposition

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a pointed category, then suspension (if it exists) is a simplicial colimit.

Proof

w.l.o.g. we may assume that (e.g. after using opposite yoneda embedding) $\mathcal{C}$ is a cocomplete (cf. cocomplete category is tensored over Anima). Then the suspension of $X$ is given by

\begin{align*} S^1 \otimes X \end{align*}

since $- \otimes X: \mathrm{An} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}$ is the unique colimit preserving functor mapping $* \mapsto X$ and $\emptyset \otimes X \simeq 0$. But $S^1$ is a simplicial colimit of coproducts consisting of objects in $\mathrm{Set}$ (cf. anima as geometric realization).

Hence $S^1 \simeq \mathrm{colim}_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}} \mathcal{F}$ for some $\mathcal{F}: \Delta^{\mathrm{op}} \rightarrow \mathrm{Set}$ and therefore

\begin{align*} S^1 \otimes X \simeq& (\mathrm{colim}_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}} \mathcal{F}) \otimes X \\ \simeq& \mathrm{colim}_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}} (\mathcal{F} \otimes X) \\ \simeq& \mathrm{colim}_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}} \coprod_{\textrm{some amount}} X \end{align*}
