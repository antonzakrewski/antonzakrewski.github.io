inclusion of sum of integers into product of integers is universally injective

Proposition

The inclusion $\bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}$ is a universally injective map.

Proof

observe that the map $\bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}$ is the filtered colimit of the maps

\begin{align*} \bigoplus_{J \subseteq \mathbb{N}, \vert J \vert < \infty} \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z} \end{align*}

but $\bigoplus_{J} \mathbb{Z} \simeq \prod_{J} \mathbb{Z}$ are isomorphic as $\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}}$ is an additive category and moreover the inclusion $\prod_{J} \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \prod_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \mathbb{Z}$ is a split mono, hence universally injective (cf. split mono is universally injective )

filtered colimit of universally injective maps is universally injective
